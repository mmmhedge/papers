# SCORE: Subject Coordinate Recovery for Label-Free Cross-Subject EEG-to-Image Retrieval

**Authors:** Zhenyao Cui, Siyuan Kan, Siyang Li, Ziwei Wang, Dongrui Wu
**Published:** 2026-08-19 | **arXiv:** [2608.19134v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19134v1)

## Summary
This paper addresses a critical gap in brain-computer interfaces: making EEG-to-image retrieval work across different people without labeled training data for each new user. The key insight is that different brains encode visual concepts in similar ways but along different "coordinate directions" — like rotating a map. SCORE solves this by learning to recover each person's unique brain coordinate system at deployment time using only unlabeled EEG and images, achieving state-of-the-art cross-subject performance.

## Theoretical Background
This work builds on visual decoding — the field of reconstructing what someone sees from their brain activity (measured via EEG, a non-invasive neural signal). Prior approaches treat each person independently, requiring extensive labeled calibration data per subject. The paper leverages the insight that neural representations are structurally similar across people but rotated in high-dimensional space. This connects to domain adaptation literature, where the core challenge is aligning feature spaces across different data distributions. The method also borrows from alignment techniques in multimodal learning — matching EEG embeddings to image embeddings — and uses orthogonal transformations, a classical tool for recovering rotations between coordinate systems without requiring ground-truth correspondences.

## Core Contributions
1. **Empirical discovery**: Demonstrates that inter-subject variability in EEG decoding stems primarily from rotations in feature space rather than fundamental differences in how brains represent images.

2. **Recovery-aware training**: Introduces a source-only training procedure that simulates cross-subject generalization by synthetically creating subject-shift scenarios, preparing the model for deployment-time adaptation.

3. **Label-free coordinate alignment**: Proposes a deployment algorithm that estimates the optimal orthogonal transformation (rotation) from source to target subject EEG space using only unlabeled EEG-image pairs, without updating frozen encoders.

4. **Hubness-corrected landmark selection**: Addresses the hubness problem (where certain images become artificially similar to many EEG samples), enabling robust selection of reliable EEG-image correspondences for transformation estimation.

## Connections to Other Work
This relates to [[domain adaptation]] and [[transfer learning]] by solving the problem of adapting neural models to new domains (subjects) without retraining. It shares methodology with [[alignment]] approaches in multimodal learning (e.g., CLIP-style EEG-image matching). The orthogonal Procrustes problem — finding the best rotation between two point clouds — is a classical technique reapplied here in a novel neuroscience context. The work also connects to [[mechanistic-interpretability]] by revealing structural properties of neural representations across subjects.

## Practical Applications
1. **Brain-computer interfaces for locked-in patients**: Enable paralyzed individuals to communicate thoughts/images without extensive personalized calibration, reducing setup time from hours to minutes.

2. **Neurofeedback systems**: Clinicians could personalize real-time visual feedback therapies (e.g., for PTSD or attention disorders) without per-patient label collection.

3. **Cognitive neuroscience research**: Portable EEG-based experiments could scale across populations without collecting individual training datasets, accelerating discovery of how vision is encoded.

4. **Wearable EEG devices**: Consumer brain-sensing devices could add image-search or thought-recognition features without per-user training.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **Universal Brain-Search**: A wearable EEG headset + app that lets users retrieve images from their mind in real-time. "Think of what you saw, get the image." Deploy SCORE at inference to work immediately on any user.

2. **EEG-Powered Accessibility**: A keyboard alternative for locked-in patients using thought-to-image-to-text pipelines. Train once, deploy across diverse neurology patients without individual calibration.

3. **Personalized Neurofeedback-as-a-Service**: SaaS platform for therapists delivering visual therapy (anxiety, PTSD, attention). SCORE reduces setup time and cost by eliminating per-patient EEG labeling.

4. **Brain-Data Analytics for Neuromarketing**: Rapid deployment of EEG-based visual perception testing at scale (e.g., test ad responses across 100s of users without individual training).

5. **Research Platform for Neuroscience Labs**: Subscription service providing pre-trained SCORE models and APIs for academic researchers to run EEG decoding studies without ML expertise or per-subject data collection.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Rotation assumption**: The paper assumes inter-subject variability is primarily orthogonal (rotation-only). If some subjects have fundamentally different feature scales or non-linear distortions, the approach will fail.

2. **Landmark quality dependence**: Hubness correction helps, but the method still relies on finding enough reliable EEG-image correspondences. In very small deployment sets or with noisy EEG, the transformation estimate may be unstable.

3. **Frozen encoders**: The approach cannot adapt the EEG or image encoders at deployment. If a subject has unusual anatomy or electrode placement, pre-trained encoders may be poorly suited.

4. **Limited to retrieval**: Works for matching EEG to images from a known gallery. Generating novel images from EEG (synthesis) remains an open problem.

5. **Scalability of 200-way retrieval**: Performance is measured on 200-image galleries. Real-world scales (millions of images) may require approximate nearest-neighbor search and could degrade accuracy.

6. **EEG signal quality**: Assumes reasonable EEG quality and artifact removal. In mobile/real-world settings with poor signal, performance likely degrades significantly.

## Open Questions
1. **Non-linear alignment**: Can the approach extend to non-orthogonal transformations (e.g., learned affine maps)? Would this improve generalization for subjects with atypical neural representations?

2. **Minimal deployment data**: How few unlabeled EEG-image pairs are needed to reliably estimate the coordinate transformation? Where does performance saturate?

3. **Cross-dataset generalization**: Do source and target subjects need to be drawn from the same EEG dataset? Can SCORE generalize across different EEG hardware, sampling rates, or electrode montages?

4. **Temporal dynamics**: Current work uses static image-EEG pairs. How does the approach handle dynamic visual sequences or temporal EEG dynamics?

5. **Encoder robustness**: Why are the pre-trained encoders so robust across subjects? Understanding this could improve design of EEG encoders for other tasks.

6. **Scaling to synthesis**: Can SCORE's coordinate recovery enable EEG-to-image generation for new subjects without retraining generative models?

## Key Takeaway
Brain imaging works across people by recovering each person's unique neural coordinate system without any labeled data at deployment.
