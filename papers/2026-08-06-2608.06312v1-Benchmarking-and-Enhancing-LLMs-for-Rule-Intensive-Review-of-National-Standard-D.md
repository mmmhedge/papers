# Benchmarking and Enhancing LLMs for Rule-Intensive Review of National Standard Documents

**Authors:** Tao Wang, Qihao Yang, Rongjiao Liang, Lianghong Lin, Haitao Wang et al.
**Published:** 2026-08-06 | **arXiv:** [2608.06312v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06312v1)

## Summary
Large language models (LLMs) increasingly support complex professional tasks, yet their capabilities in rule-intensive document review remain insufficiently evaluated. National standard documents, such as China GB/T standards, offer a representative testbed: they are lengthy, highly structured, and governed by explicit rules for scope, terminology, normative wording, and cross-section consistency. Existing benchmarks focus on domain knowledge and question answering, largely overlooking intrinsic quality review for professional documents.

## Theoretical Background
Multi-agent systems study how autonomous participants coordinate, compete, and form collective outcomes. Their behavior can depend on interaction structure and feedback dynamics rather than the capability of any single agent.

The paper is positioned against prior approaches described in its abstract and asks whether a more targeted representation, evaluation method, or optimization procedure can improve the relevant trade-off.

## Core Contributions
1. National standard documents, such as China GB/T standards, offer a representative testbed: they are lengthy, highly structured, and governed by explicit rules for scope, terminology, normative wording, and cross-section consistency.
2. To bridge this gap, we introduce GB/T-Bench, the first benchmark for the structured review of national standard documents.
3. We also develop a diagnosis-oriented evaluation protocol requiring exact matches on error location, review dimension, and error type, plus document-level coverage metrics.

## Technical Limitations
This note is based on the title, metadata, and abstract rather than a full-paper audit. The abstract cannot establish implementation completeness, statistical robustness, or whether all baselines were tuned comparably. Reported gains may depend on the selected datasets, models, task definitions, and compute environment. Deployment would require independent validation on out-of-distribution inputs, measurement of total runtime and cost, and analysis of failure cases. Any high-stakes use would also need human oversight and domain-specific governance.
