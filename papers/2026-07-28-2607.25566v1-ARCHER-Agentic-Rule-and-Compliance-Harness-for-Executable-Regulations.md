# ARCHER: Agentic Rule and Compliance Harness for Executable Regulations

**Authors:** Chiraag Singh Anand, Xue Wen Tan, Lionel Teo, Eric Tan
**Published:** 2026-07-28 | **arXiv:** [2607.25566v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25566v1)

## Summary
ARCHER is a system that automatically generates auditable code for checking whether building designs comply with regulatory rules, using multiple AI agents working together. This matters because building compliance checking currently requires expensive manual work, and existing automated systems are inflexible, proprietary, and hard to adapt — ARCHER makes the process transparent, scalable, and cost-effective even with open-source models.

## Theoretical Background
Building Information Modeling (BIM) is a digital representation of physical and functional characteristics of buildings. Automated Compliance Checkers (ACCs) have long attempted to verify designs against rule sets, but traditionally these are hard-coded for specific contexts and rarely generalize. The paper builds on two conceptual pillars: (1) multi-agent systems — programs where independent agents collaborate, often via orchestration strategies, to solve problems too complex for single components; and (2) program synthesis — the automated generation of code that satisfies a specification. The key insight is that regulatory language can be interpreted as a specification, and LLMs (large language models — neural networks trained to predict sequences of text) can generate executable verification code when guided by structured orchestration rather than simple prompting.

## Core Contributions
- **ARCHER harness**: A deterministically orchestrated multi-agent system that converts Codes of Practice (plain-language regulations) into executable, auditable verification code — not proprietary black boxes but transparent logic users can inspect.
- **Taxonomy of six harnesses**: Systematic comparison across increasing levels of agentic sophistication, revealing which agent coordination strategies actually improve compliance checking accuracy.
- **Empirical validation across model tiers**: Demonstration that a fully on-premise open-weights model using ARCHER reaches 97.8% of frontier-API performance at 25% of the cost, solving the data-sovereignty problem for regulated organizations.
- **82% accuracy improvement**: Over naive single-pass prompting on a novel real-world compliance dataset, showing that deterministic orchestration (not just scale) drives performance.

## Connections to Other Work
This relates to [[program synthesis]] literature, which has explored LLM-guided code generation, but applies it to a domain (regulatory compliance) rarely treated this way. It connects to broader [[multi-agent-systems]] research on orchestration strategies and agent coordination, showing concrete benefits of structured workflows over emergent behavior. The emphasis on transparent, auditable code echoes concerns in [[alignment]] (ensuring systems behave as intended) and [[mechanistic-interpretability]] (understanding what systems actually do), though the paper frames this pragmatically as regulatory accountability rather than theoretical safety. It also complements recent work on cost-accuracy trade-offs in LLM deployment — moving from proprietary APIs to on-premise models without catastrophic accuracy loss.

## Practical Applications
- **Building permit automation**: Architects and engineers upload BIM files; ARCHER automatically validates against local/national building codes, returning audit trails instead of hiring compliance consultants.
- **Real estate due diligence**: Investment firms verify regulatory compliance of properties at scale before acquisition, reducing legal risk and accelerating deals.
- **Retrofit and renovation planning**: Contractors query ARCHER to identify which rules a proposed renovation might violate before design, reducing expensive rework.
- **Regulatory harmonization**: Organizations operating across jurisdictions automatically map designs against multiple regional codes simultaneously, flagging conflicts.
- **Insurance underwriting**: Insurers use ARCHER to quickly assess whether buildings meet relevant safety standards, informing premiums.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **BuildCheck SaaS**: A subscription platform where architects and developers upload BIM files and select jurisdiction; ARCHER returns compliance reports with specific violations flagged, AI-generated remediation suggestions, and audit-ready evidence trails. Target: mid-market architecture/engineering firms.
2. **Compliance-as-a-Service (CaaS) for governments**: Local authorities license ARCHER to automatically pre-screen permit applications, reducing review backlogs and ensuring consistent interpretation of codes. Revenue: per-submission or per-jurisdiction-per-year.
3. **Real estate investment accelerator**: An API product that real estate funds integrate into deal evaluation pipelines; instant compliance risk scoring for properties. Monetize per API call or via tiered subscription.
4. **Regulatory codebook digitization service**: Help municipalities and standards bodies (e.g., ISO, ASHRAE) convert plain-language codes into machine-readable specifications, then syndicate those specs to ARCHER users. Recurring licensing model.
5. **Safety-critical compliance auditing (pharma/manufacturing)**: Extend ARCHER beyond buildings to FDA, GMP, and other regulated manufacturing compliance; generate auditable evidence for inspections and certifications.

## Technical Limitations
- **Regulatory language ambiguity**: Codes often contain vague or context-dependent language ("appropriate," "substantial," "in good faith") that an LLM may interpret inconsistently; deterministic orchestration helps but doesn't eliminate this.
- **Domain-specific knowledge**: The system still requires human experts to curate or validate the mapping from regulations to executable rules; it doesn't read raw legislation and automatically produce correct verification code without errors.
- **Limited to rule-based compliance**: Compliance that requires subjective judgment, negotiation, or exemptions (common in practice) falls outside the system's scope.
- **Scalability of multi-agent orchestration**: As the number of rules grows and interactions between them become more complex, the orchestration strategy may need manual tuning; no evidence the approach scales to tens of thousands of rules.
- **Model-dependent failure modes**: Even at 97.8% accuracy, 2.2% of checks may fail; for safety-critical regulations, this residual error rate may be unacceptable and require human review anyway.
- **BIM data quality**: Verification accuracy depends on the quality and completeness of the input BIM model; garbage in, garbage out applies.

## Open Questions
- How does ARCHER handle conflicting or contradictory rules across different regulatory tiers (local, national, international)?
- Can the orchestration strategy be learned or automatically tuned from labeled compliance examples, rather than hand-designed?
- What is the human effort required to map a new Codes of Practice into the ARCHER framework, and can that be further automated?
- How do explanations generated by ARCHER compare to human compliance auditors in terms of correctness and legal defensibility?
- Does the system generalize to non-building domains (manufacturing, pharma, finance) or is the approach BIM-specific?
- How sensitive is performance to the choice of backbone model, and is there a model-agnostic upper bound on achievable accuracy?

## Key Takeaway
Multi-agent orchestration makes LLMs generate transparent, auditable building compliance code at 97% accuracy and one-quarter the cost of proprietary APIs.
