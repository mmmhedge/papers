# CANDO: Cooperative Agentic Network for Layout Design Optimization

**Authors:** Athanasios Masouris, Zheng Jing, Benjamin Sam Chandler, Hadi Jamali-Rad
**Published:** 2026-10-07 | **arXiv:** [2610.10044v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10044v1)

## Summary
This paper introduces CANDO, a framework where multiple specialized AI agents collaborate to generate facility layouts that satisfy real-world constraints like irregular boundaries and access requirements. The authors also release ALPS-Bench, a benchmark of 1,000 professionally designed facility layouts with domain-specific evaluation metrics. CANDO outperforms existing methods by reasoning about spatial decisions iteratively rather than trying to generate layouts in a single pass.

## Theoretical Background
Layout generation sits at the intersection of generative modeling (creating new designs from learned patterns) and constraint satisfaction (ensuring designs meet practical requirements). Traditional approaches use neural networks trained on large datasets of layouts, measuring success via distributional metrics like Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) and Intersection over Union (IoU) — these reward similarity to training data but don't capture whether a layout actually works in practice. Multi-agent systems involve independent agents with specialized roles that coordinate to solve problems; here, agents focus on different aspects of layout design (e.g., one verifies spatial feasibility, another optimizes access routes). This paper leverages [[large-language-models]] and verification loops — repeated cycles where designs are checked against rules and refined — to guide agent interactions without requiring expensive training.

## Core Contributions
1. **ALPS-Bench**: A new benchmark with 1,000 real facility layouts annotated professionally, paired with instance-specific scoring rules derived from actual design manuals rather than generic pixel-level metrics. This shifts evaluation from "does it look like training data?" to "does it satisfy design principles?"

2. **CANDO framework**: A training-free, multi-agent architecture where specialized agents iteratively refine layouts through a verification loop. No neural network training needed — instead, agents use reasoning and structured feedback to improve designs step-by-step.

3. **Empirical validation**: Demonstrates that cooperative agents beat both learned baselines and LLM-based methods on existing benchmarks (PubLayNet, RICO, PKU-PosterLayout) and the new ALPS-Bench, suggesting the approach generalizes across layout types.

## Connections to Other Work
This work bridges [[generative-models]] (creating new layouts) with [[multi-agent-systems]] (distributed problem-solving). It relates to constraint-aware generation and formal verification in AI, as well as to recent work applying [[large-language-models]] to structured reasoning tasks. The verification-grounded loop echoes ideas from self-correction and iterative refinement in language models, but applies them to visual-spatial domains. The emphasis on domain-specific metrics rather than distributional similarity connects to broader critiques of benchmark design in generative AI.

## Practical Applications
- **Architectural and facility planning**: Automatically generate floor plans for offices, hospitals, retail spaces, or factories that meet code requirements and optimize traffic flow.
- **Urban design and site planning**: Create layouts for building complexes or parking structures that respect irregular lot boundaries and zoning constraints.
- **Interior design tools**: Software that helps designers explore layout alternatives while enforcing accessibility requirements and ergonomic principles.
- **Emergency response planning**: Generate evacuation routes and facility layouts that prioritize safety and access for first responders.
- **Real estate development**: Speed up site planning workflows by generating multiple compliant layout options for client review.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **LayoutAI for Architects**: A cloud-based tool that takes site boundaries, building codes, and design preferences as input and generates 10+ compliant layout options in minutes. Architects refine via natural language feedback; the platform learns domain conventions over time.

2. **Facility Management Optimizer**: SaaS for FM teams to re-optimize existing layouts (offices, warehouses) for new workflows or constraints (post-COVID density, accessibility upgrades, equipment moves) without hiring consultants.

3. **White-label API for CAD/BIM software**: Embed CANDO as a plug-in for AutoCAD, Revit, or Archicad so designers get real-time layout suggestions as they draw, grounded in code compliance.

4. **Fast-track permitting platform**: Help municipal planners quickly validate submitted layout designs against local codes using automated agent-based verification; reduce approval cycles from weeks to days.

5. **E-commerce store layout service**: Optimize retail floor plans for foot traffic, product visibility, and accessibility by combining agent reasoning with real-world sales data and customer flow patterns.

## Technical Limitations
- **Agent reasoning bottlenecks**: The iterative refinement process relies on agents' ability to understand and fix violations; if an agent fails to recognize a constraint violation or makes a poor fix, the loop may not converge or may settle on a suboptimal layout.
- **Scalability of verification**: Checking real-world constraints (motion planning, line-of-sight, fire code) is computationally expensive; the approach may not scale to very large or complex facilities without optimized verification.
- **Domain transfer**: ALPS-Bench focuses on specific facility types; it's unclear how well CANDO generalizes to fundamentally different layout domains (e.g., microchip design, warehouse racking) without retraining verification agents.
- **Handling ambiguity**: Real design decisions often involve trade-offs (aesthetics vs. efficiency, cost vs. safety) that aren't fully captured by design manuals; the method may struggle with subjective or creative preferences.
- **Dependency on manual rules**: CANDO requires hand-crafted design manuals and verification logic; adapting to new domains or evolving codes requires manual specification.

## Open Questions
- How sensitive is CANDO to the quality and completeness of design manuals? What happens when rules are missing or contradict each other?
- Can the agent framework learn or adapt its verification logic over time, or does it always require manual specification?
- How does CANDO perform on layouts with novel constraints not seen in ALPS-Bench or training data?
- Can you combine CANDO's verification loop with learned components (e.g., a neural network that proposes layouts faster) for better speed/quality trade-offs?
- Does the multi-agent framework naturally support collaborative human-AI design workflows, where architects and agents iteratively refine layouts together?

## Key Takeaway
Specialized agents collaborating through verification loops generate better facility layouts than single-pass learned models, without requiring training.
