# Accurate but Not Humble: Evaluating Epistemic Humility in LLM Agents under Knowledge Conflict

**Authors:** Kaiser Sun, Bernal Jimenez Gutierrez, Hongjun Liu, Jingyu Zhang, Jie Gao et al.
**Published:** 2026-10-08 | **arXiv:** [2610.12360v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12360v1)

## Summary
This paper evaluates whether LLM-based agents recognize and communicate uncertainty when they encounter contradictory information. The authors propose "epistemic humility" (EH)—an agent's ability to identify conflicts, revise beliefs, and admit uncertainty—as a key evaluation dimension separate from task accuracy. They find that agents can be accurate without being humble: many high-performing agents detect conflicts internally but fail to communicate doubt in their final answers.

## Theoretical Background
The work builds on established concepts in epistemology (the study of knowledge and justified belief) and behavioral AI evaluation. It assumes agents operate through sequential reasoning steps, retrieving evidence and updating beliefs—a framework common in [[retrieval-augmented generation]] (RAG) systems and agentic workflows. The paper extends traditional task-success metrics (common in NLP benchmarks) by adding a behavioral lens: how agents *handle* uncertainty, not just whether they get answers right. This connects to broader work on [[alignment]] and behavioral safety in autonomous systems, where the reasoning process matters alongside outcomes.

## Core Contributions
1. **Epistemic humility framework**: Operationalizes EH through three behavioral dimensions—Identify (detect conflicts), Solve (resolve them), and Escalate (communicate remaining uncertainty).
2. **Knowledge conflict evaluation methodology**: Proposes two evaluation settings—controlled synthetic conflicts and naturally occurring conflicts in multi-step execution—each with matched no-conflict controls for clean causal interpretation.
3. **Accuracy-humility decoupling**: Empirically demonstrates that task accuracy and epistemic humility are orthogonal: high-accuracy agents often fail to communicate uncertainty or maintain conflict resolution across execution steps.
4. **Trajectory-level analysis**: Shows agents frequently detect conflicts early but lose track in later steps, revealing failure modes invisible in final-answer-only evaluation.

## Connections to Other Work
This relates to [[RLHF]] and broader alignment work on making models more honest and calibrated. It complements mechanistic interpretability research by focusing on behavioral outputs rather than internal representations. The work also touches [[uncertainty quantification]] in NLP and [[truthfulness]] evaluation (e.g., TruthfulQA), but uniquely emphasizes *agentic* behavior under conflict rather than static model responses. It challenges the assumption that accuracy alone measures agent quality—a perspective relevant to [[reinforcement learning]] from feedback, where reward signals may not capture all desirable behaviors.

## Practical Applications
1. **Enterprise AI assistants**: Identifying when business intelligence systems should escalate to humans rather than confidently serving contradictory data.
2. **Medical/legal decision support**: Detecting when systems encounter conflicting guidelines or evidence and signaling uncertainty to domain experts.
3. **Fact-checking and misinformation detection**: Agents that acknowledge when sources conflict rather than arbitrarily choosing one.
4. **Autonomous research workflows**: Systems that flag contradictions in the literature and highlight areas needing human review.
5. **Regulatory compliance**: Auditing whether AI systems handling financial or safety-critical tasks properly communicate uncertainty to supervisors.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **"ConfidentAI" — Epistemic Audit Tool**: SaaS platform that automatically tests enterprise LLM applications for epistemic humility; flags high-accuracy models that mask uncertainty; generates compliance reports.
2. **"HumbleRAG" — Uncertainty-Aware Document AI**: A retrieval-augmented generation system optimized for conflict detection; integrates with enterprise knowledge bases; surfaces contradictions before generating answers.
3. **"TrustScore for Agents"**: A B2B evaluation service that rates LLM agents on multiple dimensions (accuracy, humility, calibration); feeds into procurement/vendor selection for enterprises.
4. **"ConflictDetect"**: Safety middleware for chatbot platforms that intercepts agent outputs and warns users when the system detected but didn't resolve internal contradictions.
5. **"AgentInsurance" — Risk Mitigation for AI Workflows**: Insurance product that requires EH certification; companies using humble agents pay lower premiums because they catch and flag edge cases.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Evaluation circularity**: How an agent "should" behave when encountering conflicts depends on the domain and use case—the paper's ISE framework assumes a particular philosophy of epistemic virtue that may not generalize.
2. **Scalability of human annotation**: Assessing trajectory-level behavior requires annotating agent execution logs; this is labor-intensive and may not scale to large-scale model evaluation.
3. **Model-specific findings**: Evaluation tested only four agents; unclear how findings generalize across model families, sizes, or architectures (e.g., retrieval mechanisms vary widely).
4. **Accuracy-humility tradeoff is assumed but not analyzed deeply**: The paper shows the tradeoff exists but doesn't deeply investigate *why* or under what conditions it's unavoidable. Is it fundamental or a training artifact?
5. **Synthetic vs. natural conflicts**: Controlled conflicts are cleaner for analysis but may not reflect real-world ambiguity, where conflict is often subtle and graded rather than binary.

## Open Questions
1. Can we train agents to improve epistemic humility *without* sacrificing accuracy? What loss functions or RL rewards achieve this?
2. Do certain model sizes or architectures naturally exhibit more epistemic humility? Is there a scaling law?
3. How do different types of conflict (parametric vs. contextual, recent vs. foundational knowledge) affect agent behavior differently?
4. Can agents learn to *resolve* conflicts rather than just escalate them? What strategies work?
5. How do epistemic humility and calibration relate quantitatively? Is humble uncertainty honest (well-calibrated) or just deferent?

## Key Takeaway
Accurate LLM agents often hide uncertainty instead of communicating it—epistemic humility and task accuracy are decoupled goals.
