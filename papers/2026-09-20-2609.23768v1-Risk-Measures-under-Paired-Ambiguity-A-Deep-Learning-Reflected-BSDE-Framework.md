# Risk Measures under Paired-Ambiguity: A Deep Learning Reflected BSDE Framework

**Authors:** Nacira Agram, Jan Rems, Emanuela Rosazza Gianin
**Published:** 2026-09-20 | **arXiv:** [2609.23768v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23768v1)

## Summary
This paper solves the problem of deciding when to exercise options (like American options) when there is uncertainty about both the true probability model and the discount rate used for valuation. The authors use deep learning to approximate solutions to complex mathematical equations (reflected BSDEs) that describe optimal stopping under this "paired ambiguity." The work matters because real-world financial decisions involve both model risk and parameter uncertainty, and this framework provides a practical computational method to handle both simultaneously.

## Theoretical Background
The paper builds on several foundational ideas. **Backward stochastic differential equations (BSDEs)** are a mathematical tool for pricing and hedging in finance — they reverse time to work backward from a payoff to compute its value today. **Reflected BSDEs** are a variant where the solution is constrained to stay above a lower boundary (like an option's intrinsic value), encoding the right to exercise early. **Dynamic risk measures** generalize the idea of risk quantification beyond simple variance — they allow risk preferences to evolve over time and handle ambiguity about which probability model is correct. The paper combines two sources of uncertainty: **Girsanov model uncertainty** (ambiguity about the true drift of asset prices) and **cash subadditive risk** (ambiguity about the discount rate or risk aversion). Prior work studied these separately; this paper couples them in a unified framework called "paired ambiguity."

## Core Contributions
1. **Paired-ambiguity framework**: Formally combines simultaneous uncertainty in both the probability model (via Girsanov) and the discount rate (via cash subadditive risk measures), characterized by an upper reflected BSDE with explicit structural properties.

2. **Explicit stopping rules**: For quadratic drivers associated with entropic risk measures, the paper derives closed-form or semi-explicit optimal stopping rules in benchmark cases, providing intuition for how ambiguity affects exercise decisions.

3. **Convergence-guaranteed deep learning scheme**: Develops a neural network algorithm for solving reflected quadratic BSDEs with rigorous convergence analysis. The key technical contribution is a proof strategy using discrete reflection and truncation to reduce to a globally Lipschitz system, combining BSDE discretization theory with neural network approximation bounds.

4. **Numerical validation on American options**: Empirically demonstrates how both discount-rate ambiguity and entropic ambiguity change optimal stopping boundaries and exercise values, with concrete examples.

## Connections to Other Work
This work extends prior research on [[BSDE]]s and option pricing under model uncertainty. It relates to classical **optimal stopping theory** (e.g., optimal exercise of American options) but under non-probabilistic uncertainty. The deep learning approach for BSDEs connects to recent work on solving high-dimensional PDEs via neural networks (e.g., [[Physics-Informed Neural Networks]], [[Solving High-Dimensional PDE]]s). The paper also engages with **robust optimization** and **ambiguity-averse decision-making** — frameworks that hedge against parameter uncertainty. The entropic risk measure connection links to **information-theoretic** approaches in finance and the study of how agents with ambiguity aversion behave.

## Practical Applications
1. **American option pricing and hedging**: Banks and traders pricing American-style derivatives when uncertain about volatility models and funding rates; the framework helps compute exercise boundaries under ambiguity.

2. **Real options and investment timing**: Corporate finance decisions (e.g., when to invest in a project) under both technological uncertainty and cost-of-capital uncertainty.

3. **Optimal liquidation**: High-frequency trading firms managing position exits when uncertain about market microstructure models and the true discount rate.

4. **Portfolio rebalancing**: Determining when to rebalance portfolios under simultaneous model uncertainty and uncertainty about the true cost of capital.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **Ambiguity-aware derivatives pricing engine**: A SaaS platform for investment banks that computes American option prices and exercise recommendations under paired ambiguity, with model parameter uncertainty quantified.

2. **Real options valuation tool for corporate finance**: Software helping CFOs and strategic planners value investment decisions (R&D, M&A, capacity expansion) under both market and discount-rate uncertainty, with visual sensitivity analysis.

3. **Quantified model risk dashboard**: A risk monitoring system that estimates the impact of probability model ambiguity and discount-rate ambiguity on portfolio values, flagging when ambiguity is high.

4. **Adaptive trading execution algorithm**: An algorithmic trading system that adjusts liquidation schedules and execution speed based on real-time estimates of market model uncertainty and ambiguous funding costs.

5. **Regulatory capital calculator**: A tool for banks to compute risk-sensitive capital requirements (e.g., under Basel III or stress-testing regimes) that properly account for joint ambiguity in models and discount rates.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Quadratic driver assumption**: The deep learning scheme works well for quadratic BSDEs; more general nonlinear drivers may require different approximation strategies, and convergence guarantees may not extend.

2. **Curse of dimensionality**: While neural networks help, pricing in very high dimensions (e.g., baskets with 100+ assets) remains computationally challenging; the method is practical for moderate dimensions but not unlimited.

3. **Training data and offline nature**: The approach requires solving offline for a given set of model parameters; if parameters shift (e.g., volatility regime change), retraining may be needed.

4. **Entropic risk measure focus**: Explicit stopping rules are derived for entropic risk; other risk measures may not yield closed form and require pure neural network approximation.

5. **No model misspecification recovery**: The framework assumes ambiguity is correctly specified (e.g., the true model lies within the Girsanov ambiguity set); if the actual model is outside, performance may degrade.

6. **Computational cost vs. traditional methods**: For low-dimensional American options, finite-difference or tree methods may be faster; neural approaches shine only when dimension or complexity is high.

## Open Questions
1. **Non-reflected case and partial exercise constraints**: Can the framework handle partial exercise (e.g., Bermudan options or options on multiple correlated underlyings with constraints)?

2. **Adaptive ambiguity sets**: What if the ambiguity set itself evolves with new information? Can the framework update stopping rules online?

3. **Convergence rates and sample complexity**: How many training samples and network parameters are needed for given accuracy? Is there a lower bound?

4. **Extension to other risk measures**: Can the theory and algorithm be extended beyond entropic risk (e.g., CVaR, distortion measures)? Do explicit rules exist?

5. **Coupled ambiguities**: What if probability and discount-rate ambiguities are correlated or interacting (beyond independence)? Does the paired framework decompose nicely?

6. **Empirical calibration**: How do practitioners estimate the true ambiguity set sizes? Can data-driven methods learn ambiguity parameters from market prices?

## Key Takeaway
Deep learning solves optimal stopping under simultaneous uncertainty in both asset models and discount rates, with convergence guarantees and practical option-pricing applications.
