# Underwater C3-JEPA: An Object-Centric Cross-View World Model for ROV Salvage

**Authors:** Yuncong Yang, Jinlong Li, Yulong Xue, Feng Wu, Chunwen Zhang et al.
**Published:** 2026-09-24 | **arXiv:** [2609.30214v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30214v1)

## Summary
This paper introduces Underwater C³-JEPA, a world model that learns to predict how underwater objects move and interact from multiple camera views, without needing touch sensors. The model combines multi-view observations with robot control signals to forecast future states in a compressed latent space, enabling autonomous underwater robots (ROVs) to plan salvage operations through model-predictive control and learned behaviors. The key innovation is handling the unique challenges of underwater environments—hydrodynamic lag, multi-view fusion, and heavy-load manipulation—while keeping the predictor lightweight enough for real-time deployment.

## Theoretical Background
This work builds on joint-embedding predictive architectures (JEPA) — a framework where models learn representations by predicting future latent states rather than reconstructing raw pixels. The paper extends JEPA with three key components: cross-view fusion (leveraging multiple synchronized cameras), control conditioning (incorporating vehicle commands), and object-centric tokenization (focusing representation on task-relevant entities rather than entire scenes).

The foundation draws from world models in robotics—systems that learn environment dynamics to enable planning and control. Prior work on multi-view learning uses attention mechanisms to align evidence across cameras; this paper applies "held-out-view attention" to learn representations robust to camera occlusion. The approach also incorporates weak binding (connecting gripper and target objects with minimal annotation) and geometric sharpening (SIGReg) to anchor spatial understanding without expensive per-frame labeling.

## Core Contributions
1. **Object-centric latent prediction under hydrodynamic dynamics**: Explicitly models the lag and momentum effects in underwater environments, moving beyond standard kinematic prediction.
2. **Cross-view world model with held-out-view attention**: Fuses multi-camera evidence in a way that generalizes to missing or occluded views, tested by recovering withheld camera observations.
3. **Weak binding and SIGReg**: Reduces annotation overhead by using loose spatial anchors (gripper-object pairings) and regularization to sharpen geometric representations without dense labels.
4. **Transferability demonstration**: Shows the learned representation encodes more task-relevant information than reconstruction-free baselines and generalizes from simulation to real underwater video.
5. **Lightweight architecture for real-time MPC**: Enables both model-predictive-control candidate ranking and behavior-agent training on resource-constrained underwater platforms.

## Connections to Other Work
This work sits at the intersection of several research threads:
- **[[World Models]]** and forward modeling for robotics planning
- **[[JEPA]]** architectures, which use latent prediction rather than pixel reconstruction
- **Multi-view learning** and self-supervised vision from multiple perspectives
- **Model-predictive control** in robotics, enabling planning without reward learning
- **Object-centric representations** (similar to [[MONet]], [[IODINE]]), which factor scenes into discrete entities
- **Sim-to-real transfer** in robotic manipulation under visual feedback

The paper contrasts reconstruction-free latent approaches (which may lose spatial detail) with its geometry-aware variant, positioning it between pure learned abstractions and explicit geometric models.

## Practical Applications
1. **Autonomous underwater salvage**: Recovering lost cargo, wreckage, or archaeological artifacts from the seafloor using ROVs without manual teleoperation.
2. **Subsea inspection and maintenance**: Planning precise gripper trajectories around offshore infrastructure (pipelines, cables, platforms) in zero-visibility or poor-visibility conditions.
3. **Deep-sea research**: Collecting delicate biological or geological samples in harsh deep-ocean environments where direct human control adds latency and risk.
4. **Port and harbor cleanup**: Autonomous removal of debris, nets, or hazardous materials from underwater zones.
5. **Military/defense applications**: Autonomous mine detection, disposal, or underwater vehicle recovery in contested or dangerous waters.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **Autonomous Subsea Salvage-as-a-Service**: Rent pre-trained ROV systems for insured cargo recovery, charged per successful item recovered or per dive hour, targeting shipping insurance and marine logistics firms.
2. **ROV Predictive Planning Software**: Sell a lightweight MPC stack (based on this architecture) to existing ROV operators and manufacturers, improving mission success rates and reducing operator fatigue.
3. **Underwater Inspection AI for Infrastructure**: Deploy multi-camera ROVs to oil/gas and renewable energy platforms with onboard world-model inference for autonomous structural defect detection and geometric mapping.

5. **Autonomous Aquaculture Monitoring & Maintenance**: Small underwater drones with this architecture for fish farm net inspection, debris removal, and autonomous feeding arm control without human presence, improving disease control and efficiency.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Simulation-to-reality gap**: While tested on real underwater video, full end-to-end real-world validation of MPC closed-loop control and multi-step planning is limited; hydrodynamic model fidelity in simulation may not capture all environmental variability.
2. **Scalability to extreme depth**: Paper focuses on near-field salvage; ultra-high pressure, minimal lighting, and extreme hydrodynamic conditions (deep trench environments) are not addressed.
3. **Generalization to novel objects**: Weak binding and SIGReg assume task objects and grippers are roughly similar to training data; performance on unseen morphologies (e.g., very small or very large targets) is unclear.
4. **Multi-body interaction**: Assumes single rigid-body task object; complex deformable materials (nets, fabric, organic matter) or multi-object entanglement are not explicitly handled.
5. **Latency and hardware constraints**: Requires synchronized multi-view cameras and onboard compute; deployment on older or weight/power-constrained ROVs may be infeasible.
6. **Limited ground truth for long-horizon predictions**: The paper validates state prediction against held-out views but does not extensively validate multi-step rollouts against actual physical outcomes.

## Open Questions
1. **How far into the future can the model predict accurately?** What is the prediction horizon before drift accumulates beyond MPC utility, and how does this scale with water current variability?
2. **Can this architecture handle dynamic environments** (e.g., moving currents, other marine animals, shifting debris) beyond the controlled salvage scenarios tested?
3. **How does performance degrade with fewer cameras** or in monocular settings? Is there a minimum camera configuration for robust cross-view attention?
4. **Can the learned dynamics transfer across different ROV platforms** (e.g., different thrusters, gripper types, sensor suites), or is retraining required?
5. **What role does contact forces play in prediction?** Can the model learn to infer grasp success or failure risk from visual cues alone, or does it need explicit force feedback?
6. **How does the model perform in murky or low-light conditions?** Real-world underwater visibility is often far worse than simulation; is domain randomization or additional synthetic data sufficient?

## Key Takeaway
Lightweight visual world models enable underwater robots to plan manipulation tasks from multi-camera views without contact sensors, transferring from simulation to real underwater salvage.
