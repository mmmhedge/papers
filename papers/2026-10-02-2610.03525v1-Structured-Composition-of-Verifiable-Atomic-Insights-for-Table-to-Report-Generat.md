# Structured Composition of Verifiable Atomic Insights for Table-to-Report Generation

**Authors:** Teng Lin, Xinyu Liu, Nan Tang
**Published:** 2026-10-02 | **arXiv:** [2610.03525v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03525v1)

## Summary
This paper tackles automatic report generation from data tables — turning raw numbers and relationships into readable analytical narratives. The key innovation is breaking down insight discovery into verifiable atomic building blocks (simple, pattern-based facts) that are then systematically combined into complex insights, ensuring every claim in the final report can be traced back to specific data and SQL queries.

## Theoretical Background
Table-to-report generation sits at the intersection of natural language generation and data analytics. Prior work used two main approaches: (1) sequential data agents — LLM-driven systems that query tables step-by-step but get stuck in local patterns, and (2) direct LLM generation — passing tables to language models and hoping they produce correct summaries. The field builds on insights about compositional reasoning (combining simple elements into complex structures) and the need for verifiable, explainable AI outputs. This work assumes tables are relational (structured with rows, columns, and schemas) and that insights should be grounded in executable queries, not just plausible-sounding text.

## Core Contributions
1. **Atomic insight formalism**: Defines verifiable "atoms" as minimal analytical units matching predefined patterns (e.g., "maximum value in column X"). Automatically enumerates all valid atoms from schema and data.
2. **Multi-relational insight graph**: Organizes atoms as nodes with edges encoding logical, temporal, or hierarchical relationships — a structured representation that avoids premature commitment to specific analyses.
3. **Composition operators**: Systematic rules to fuse atomic insights into higher-order composite conclusions (e.g., combining "A is growing" and "B is declining" into "A outpaced B").
4. **End-to-end verifiability**: Every generated insight comes with executable SQL and fine-grained provenance traces, enabling humans to audit claims.
5. **Empirical validation**: Demonstrates improvements in factual correctness, novelty, and completeness across three benchmarks (InsightBench, DDR-Bench, T2R-Bench).

## Connections to Other Work
ComInsight combines ideas from [[agents|data agents]] with explicit graph-based reasoning, similar to how [[knowledge graphs]] structure facts before reasoning. It resembles work on compositional semantics in NLP and addresses the "exploration bias" problem seen in sequential decision-making agents (related to concerns in [[reinforcement-learning]] about greedy local choices). The emphasis on verifiability echoes concerns in AI safety and interpretability — ensuring systems produce auditable outputs rather than black-box predictions. The pattern-based atomic unit design parallels template-driven NLG approaches but with stronger logical grounding.

## Practical Applications
1. **Financial reporting automation**: Banks and investment firms automatically generate quarterly analysis reports from balance sheets, trade data, and market tables.
2. **Data science report generation**: Analytics platforms auto-produce executive summaries of A/B test results, user cohort analysis, or product metrics without manual write-ups.
3. **Scientific data analysis**: Automated narratives for experimental results, clinical trial summaries, or epidemiological reports that require full traceability.
4. **Business intelligence dashboards**: Dynamic textual narratives accompanying visualizations to explain what the numbers mean and why changes matter.
5. **Regulatory compliance**: Auto-generate required audit reports and risk summaries with full SQL provenance for compliance review.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Pattern predefinition**: Requires analysts to manually define atomic insight patterns upfront; domain-specific patterns may be missed or over-specified, limiting generalization to novel analytical questions.
2. **Schema dependency**: Heavily relies on high-quality schema and table relationships; poorly structured, sparse, or siloed data will reduce atom quality and discoverable insights.
3. **Scalability of atom enumeration**: Enumerating all valid atoms grows combinatorially with table count and column count; real-world data warehouses with hundreds of tables may face computational bottlenecks.
4. **Composition operator limitations**: Fixed composition rules may miss creative or cross-domain insights that don't fit predefined logical/temporal/hierarchical templates.
5. **Data quality assumptions**: Assumes table data is clean and labeled; missing values, encoding errors, or unlabeled columns will corrupt atomic facts and downstream reasoning.
6. **LLM still required for narrative**: The final text generation likely still uses an LLM; if the LLM hallucinates or misinterprets the composed insights, verifiability is undermined.

## Open Questions
1. Can atomic patterns be learned automatically from data and user feedback, rather than hand-authored? Would a meta-learning approach help systems adapt to new domains?
2. How does ComInsight scale to truly massive tables (billions of rows) or high-dimensional data? Are there sampling or approximation strategies that preserve verifiability?
3. Can the composition operators be extended to causal reasoning (e.g., "A caused B") rather than just logical/temporal relations? What additional validation would that require?
4. How do users interact with the multi-relational graph? Can we show users alternative insight paths and let them explore competing narratives?
5. Does this approach extend to unstructured data (e.g., combining table insights with text or images)? Would that still preserve SQL-level verifiability?
6. Can we measure or define "novelty" more rigorously, and ensure the system avoids obvious or redundant insights?

## Key Takeaway
Decompose table-to-report generation into verifiable atomic facts, compose them systematically, and attach SQL traces to every claim for full auditability.
