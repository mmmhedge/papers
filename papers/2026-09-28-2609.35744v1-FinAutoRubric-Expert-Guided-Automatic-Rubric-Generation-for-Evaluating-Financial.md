# FinAutoRubric: Expert-Guided Automatic Rubric Generation for Evaluating Financial Research Agents

**Authors:** Hoyoung Lee, Suyeol Yun, Jack Haverty, Yunju Cho, Meesong Kim et al.
**Published:** 2026-09-28 | **arXiv:** [2609.35744v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35744v1)

## Summary
This paper presents FinAutoRubric, a system that automatically generates evaluation rubrics for assessing financial research agents by combining expert guidance with AI-driven workflows. Rather than manually creating fixed rubrics for each financial benchmark task, experts provide reusable evaluation principles that agents use to generate query-specific scoring criteria, review them, and validate results—solving a critical problem in building trustworthy evaluations for AI systems operating in high-stakes financial domains.

## Theoretical Background
The work builds on two foundations. First, the challenge of **benchmark evaluation**: measuring AI agent performance reliably requires clear, consistent rubrics (scoring criteria), but creating them by hand is expensive and doesn't scale. Second, the **agentic AI paradigm** — systems where language models execute multi-step reasoning loops to solve complex tasks. FinAutoRubric combines these by treating rubric generation itself as a multi-agent problem: one agent researches values, another reviews them, and human experts remain in the loop when ambiguity arises. The approach is grounded in financial domain expertise, where correctness depends on precise institutional standards and fixed information cutoffs (e.g., balance sheet data as of a specific date).

## Core Contributions
- **Expert-guided rubric generation**: A framework where domain experts encode evaluation standards once (as prompts and code rules), and those standards propagate across hundreds of queries without per-task manual work.
- **Multi-agent validation pipeline**: A writer agent researches expected values while a reviewer agent verifies them, with escalation to humans on disagreement—reducing expert effort while maintaining quality.
- **Task Bank abstraction**: A reusable library of evaluation criteria that generalizes across tasks (78 tasks, 8 asset classes) rather than siloing criteria per benchmark.
- **FinAutoRubric Benchmark**: A new 100-query evaluation dataset across 78 financial tasks, with evidence that rubrics generated by earlier models still show improvement headroom for later ones (enabling continuous evaluation).
- **Empirical validation**: Rubric quality matches hand-authored rubrics from financial experts; generated scores correlate closely with human grading; in-house analysts prefer them in blind review.

## Practical Applications
- **Regulatory compliance & audit**: Financial institutions need defensible evaluations of their AI trading or research agents; FinAutoRubric provides audit trails and expert-aligned scoring.
- **Fund manager oversight**: Hedge funds and asset managers can scale evaluation of internal AI systems across asset classes without hiring evaluators for each strategy.
- **Third-party benchmarking**: Financial data vendors (Bloomberg, Refinitiv, FactSet) could use this to evaluate competing AI agents on standardized, expert-validated criteria.
- **Institutional AI governance**: Banks and insurance companies can embed their own risk and compliance standards into rubric generation, ensuring AI agents reflect house rules.
- **Grad student / researcher tool**: Academic finance labs and quant teams can quickly evaluate research agents on bespoke questions without manual grading overhead.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **FinAutoRubric-as-a-Service**: Cloud platform where asset managers upload their internal evaluation questions and expert guidelines; the system auto-generates rubrics and grades agent outputs, with a dashboard for tracking performance over time.
2. **Compliance Rubric Engine**: Specialized SaaS for banks and insurance firms that auto-generates risk, regulatory, and operational rubrics aligned to their policies and audit frameworks; integrates with internal AI monitoring.
3. **Quant Research Hiring/Screening**: Use FinAutoRubric to auto-grade candidate solutions to standardized finance problems; institutions pay for automated, expert-aligned candidate evaluation in recruiting pipelines.

## Technical Limitations
- **Expert guidance overhead**: System quality still depends on high-quality expert input; if experts provide vague or inconsistent guidance, rubrics degrade. Scaling across institutions with different standards requires re-tuning.
- **Information cutoff rigidity**: Financial data changes constantly; the rubric assumes fixed cutoff dates, which may not suit live trading or real-time analysis scenarios.
- **Model generation lag**: Rubrics from earlier LLM generations are shown to leave headroom for later ones—but no clear mechanism for automated rubric improvement or continuous refinement without expert intervention.
- **Escalation cost**: The human-in-the-loop on disagreement is presented as a feature but could become a bottleneck at scale; the paper doesn't quantify escalation rates or human effort required.
- **Domain specificity**: Evaluation criteria for finance are highly institutional and asset-class-specific; transfer to other domains (healthcare, legal) or other financial institutions may be limited.
- **Validation scope**: Tested on 100 queries and 8 asset classes; unclear how well the approach generalizes to novel, out-of-distribution financial questions or emerging asset types.

## Open Questions
- How does rubric quality degrade as financial data or regulations change? Can the system detect and flag stale rubrics?
- What is the minimum amount of expert guidance needed to achieve good rubric quality? Is there a learning curve where less expert input becomes viable over time?
- Can the Task Bank be shared across institutions without per-institution fine-tuning? What is the cost/benefit tradeoff?
- How do agent-generated rubrics perform on adversarial or deliberately misleading financial queries?
- Does the multi-agent validation loop (writer + reviewer) actually outperform a single agent or simpler baseline (e.g., direct expert scoring + LLM critique)?
- Can the framework extend to real-time or forward-looking financial metrics (e.g., earnings forecasts) where ground truth is uncertain even for humans?

## Key Takeaway
Automatic rubric generation with expert guidance enables scalable, trustworthy evaluation of financial research agents without per-task manual scoring.
