# The Missing Primitive: Diagnosing and Repairing Mathematical Reasoning in Large Language Models

**Authors:** Shuo Xing, Zilin Dai, Chengyuan Qian, Fangzhou Lin, Wenjing Chen et al.
**Published:** 2026-10-01 | **arXiv:** [2610.02191v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02191v1)

## Summary
This paper diagnoses why large language models (LLMs) struggle with mathematical reasoning despite solving hard problems, introducing "Mathematical Primitives"—foundational reasoning capabilities—and a benchmark (LEAP) that breaks down math understanding into four dimensions: Discovery, Generation, Digestion, and Execution. The authors then propose a training method that improves mathematical reasoning by explicitly teaching these primitives, showing that fixing "Discovery" (finding the right strategy) is the highest-leverage improvement.

## Theoretical Background
Recent work shows LLMs can solve frontier math problems, yet their errors suggest gaps in structural reasoning rather than mere knowledge gaps. The paper builds on interpretability research exploring what internal capabilities drive model behavior. It treats mathematical understanding as decomposable—separate from raw problem-solving accuracy—similar to how cognitive science breaks down human reasoning into distinct processes (planning, execution, verification). Prior work in [[mechanistic-interpretability]] has shown that breaking down complex reasoning into interpretable components helps both diagnose failures and improve training.

## Core Contributions
1. **Mathematical Primitives framework**: A novel conceptual lens defining four orthogonal dimensions of mathematical reasoning (Discovery, Generation, Digestion, Execution) rather than treating math as a monolithic capability.
2. **LEAP benchmark**: A diagnostic tool that isolates failures along each primitive dimension, revealing that solution accuracy masks distinct capability imbalances.
3. **Discovery as bottleneck**: Empirical finding that the discovery primitive (identifying the right approach) is the dominant failure mode, not execution.
4. **Primitive-privileged self-distillation (ALPS)**: A post-training framework that selectively transfers primitive-guided reasoning from a teacher to student model, improving performance across model scales.

## Connections to Other Work
This relates to [[mechanistic-interpretability]] work on understanding model reasoning, similar to probing studies that decompose capabilities. It connects to [[reinforcement-learning]] and [[alignment]] via post-training refinement methods (echoing ideas from [[RLHF]] and recent self-distillation approaches). The diagnosis-then-repair pattern mirrors root-cause debugging in system design. The work sits alongside other mathematical reasoning benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH) but uniquely decouples *what* a model knows from *how* it reasons.

## Practical Applications
- **Math tutoring systems**: Diagnostic reports identifying which reasoning dimension a student struggles with (discovery vs. execution) enable targeted intervention.
- **Automated theorem proving**: Debugging failures in formal math systems by isolating which primitive fails (e.g., does the model find the lemma but fail to apply it?).
- **Code generation for STEM**: Improving math-heavy code generation (scientific computing, finance) by training on primitive-aligned data.
- **Educational AI**: Adaptive systems that adjust problem difficulty based on which primitive the learner is weak in.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **Math Reasoning Debugger**: Browser extension / IDE plugin that breaks down why an LLM-powered homework solver failed—showing which primitive(s) broke—helping students learn the gap.
2. **Primitive-aligned Math Tutoring Platform**: Online tutoring that profiles student reasoning along the four primitives and auto-generates targeted exercises (e.g., "you're weak at Discovery, here are 10 strategy-finding problems").
3. **Enterprise Math AI Quality Dashboard**: SaaS that audits math-heavy API outputs (financial calculations, engineering simulations) by running primitive-level diagnostics before shipping to customers.
4. **Primitive-Optimized LLM Fine-tuning Service**: Managed service that fine-tunes open-source models on discovery-heavy datasets, selling improved math reasoning as a premium API.
5. **Competitive Math AI Coach**: App that trains users on competition math by feeding their attempts to a primitive-diagnostic model, providing human-like feedback on *which reasoning skill* to improve.

## Technical Limitations
- **Benchmark coverage**: LEAP may not capture all mathematical reasoning styles (e.g., geometric intuition, numerical approximation); four dimensions might oversimplify some domains.
- **Scalability of annotation**: Creating primitive-aligned training data requires careful human labeling; unclear if this scales to very large, diverse datasets.
- **Generalization across domains**: The four primitives are derived from algebraic/symbolic math; applicability to combinatorics, probability, or applied math is untested.
- **Causality vs. correlation**: Showing Discovery is a bottleneck doesn't guarantee that fixing it will scale improvements linearly; improvements may plateau due to other hidden factors.
- **Compute cost of self-distillation**: ALPS requires a teacher model; the efficiency gain vs. standard fine-tuning on scaled data is unclear.

## Open Questions
- How do the four primitives interact? Can a model be weak in one without being weak in others, or are they entangled?
- Do these primitives transfer across domains (algebra → geometry → probability)? Are domain-specific primitive profiles needed?
- Can we automatically generate discovery-rich training data without human annotation? What properties of text correlate with high discovery signal?
- How does model scale affect the relative importance of each primitive? Do larger models shift which primitive is the bottleneck?
- Can we use primitive diagnostics to improve reasoning in other domains (planning, legal reasoning, scientific hypothesis generation)?

## Key Takeaway
LLMs fail math not by bad execution but by poor strategy discovery; training them to find the right approach works better than teaching them execution.
