# RynnValue: Scaling Robotic Value Foundation Models with Temporal Distance

**Authors:** Dongchi Huang, Hongyin Zhang, Bohan Hou, Siteng Huang, Zhian Su et al.
**Published:** 2026-08-10 | **arXiv:** [2608.09853v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09853v1)

## Summary
This paper introduces RynnValue, a foundation model that learns to predict how far a robot is from reaching language-specified goals by using temporal distance (time-to-goal) as a training signal instead of human preferences. The key insight is that temporal labels can be extracted automatically from video timestamps at scale, allowing the model to train on 7,000+ hours of robotic manipulation data without expensive preference annotations. When converted into reward signals, RynnValue improves real-world robot task success rates from 52.5% to 72.5%.

## Theoretical Background
This work builds on two established threads in robot learning. First, reward modeling — the problem of learning what task success looks like from data — has traditionally relied on human preferences or explicit task progress signals. Second, value functions in reinforcement learning predict cumulative future reward; here, the authors reframe this as predicting temporal distance (steps remaining to goal). The paper also draws on recent foundation model scaling, which shows that training on massive heterogeneous datasets can produce generalizable representations. The temporal distance signal is inspired by inverse models in robotics, which predict action consequences, but inverted: instead of predicting "what action led here," it predicts "how many steps to the goal."

## Core Contributions
1. **Temporal distance as a supervision signal**: Replaces preference labels and progress annotations with automatically-derived temporal labels from video timestamps. This scales reward learning to 3M clips without manual annotation.

2. **Training techniques for temporal robustness**: Introduces random temporal sampling, temporal-order shuffling, and value-isolation attention — mechanisms that prevent the model from learning shortcuts (e.g., ignoring failures) and ensure value predictions remain sensitive to task state changes.

3. **Zero-shot transfer and empirical gains**: Demonstrates that the learned value function generalizes to unseen tasks, robot embodiments, and camera viewpoints without retraining. On RBM-EVAL-OOD benchmark, achieves Kendall's tau_a of 0.675, exceeding fully preference-supervised baselines (0.655) while using no preference data. Real-world policy improvements reach +20 percentage points (online) and +18.7 points (offline).

## Connections to Other Work
This advances [[Reward Modeling]] for robotics by finding a scalable, annotation-free supervision signal. It relates to work on [[Inverse Models]] and self-supervised robot learning, which also exploit raw video structure. The foundation model angle connects to recent [[Generative Models]] and large-scale representation learning (e.g., video transformers), but focuses on value prediction rather than generation. The potential-based reward shaping conversion links to classical [[Reinforcement Learning]] theory. The approach also sidesteps some challenges in [[Alignment]] (ensuring learned rewards reflect intent) by using an objective signal (time) rather than subjective preferences.

## Practical Applications
1. **Scalable policy training**: Robots can be trained on large datasets of teleoperated or exploratory manipulation without hiring annotators for preference judgments, reducing data preparation bottlenecks.

2. **Transfer to new tasks**: A single RynnValue model can reward entirely new goal-reaching tasks specified in language, enabling rapid deployment of multi-task manipulation systems.

3. **Cross-embodiment learning**: The same value model works across different robot designs and cameras, allowing companies to pool data across hardware variants without task-specific fine-tuning.

4. **Data monetization**: Organizations can collect and share raw robot video at lower cost (no annotation needed) while using RynnValue to extract reward signals, lowering barriers to large-scale robot datasets.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **RynnValue as a cloud API**: Offer a pre-trained value model accessible via API. Robotics labs upload video clips and language goals; the service returns reward predictions or fine-tuned policies. Monetize on inference volume.

2. **Robot Learning Platform**: Build an end-to-end platform (like Hugging Face for robots) where teams upload manipulation videos, define goals, and the system auto-labels with temporal-distance rewards, trains policies, and evaluates on simulation. Charge per task or per training hour.

3. **Sim-to-Real Reward Transfer**: Extend RynnValue to output rewards that transfer from simulation to real robots. Sell to manufacturers who have abundant sim data but limited real-world budget.

4. **Multi-Robot Fleet Orchestration**: Use RynnValue to learn a shared value function across a fleet of heterogeneous robots; enable task load-balancing by predicting which robot will reach a goal fastest. SaaS for warehouse/factory operators.

5. **Human-Robot Collaboration Scoring**: Deploy RynnValue to score partial task progress during human-robot teaming; provide real-time feedback to workers on trajectory quality without human raters. Useful for training and safety.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Temporal signal assumption**: The method assumes task demonstrations contain clear goal-reaching episodes. Exploratory or unstructured data without a defined end state will produce noisy temporal labels and may degrade generalization.

2. **Language grounding**: The model still requires language descriptions of goals at test time. Robustness to varied language phrasing, typos, or abstract goal descriptions is not thoroughly tested.

3. **Generalization bounds unclear**: While zero-shot transfer to unseen embodiments is shown, the paper does not characterize failure modes—e.g., which embodiments or viewpoint shifts break the model? What is the distribution shift threshold?

4. **Potential-based reward conversion**: Converting value predictions to dense rewards via potential-based shaping is a standard technique, but the paper does not compare alternative shaping strategies or discuss sensitivity to reward scaling.

5. **Real-world scale**: Experiments span offline and online settings, but online runs are relatively short. Long-horizon deployment robustness and sample efficiency during online RL are not deeply analyzed.

6. **Computational cost**: The paper does not report inference latency or model size, which matters for real-time robot control.

## Open Questions
1. **Can temporal distance scale to long-horizon manipulation?** Most clips in the dataset likely have durations of minutes; how does the model perform on hour-long tasks or multi-step procedures?

2. **How sensitive is the model to clock drift or asynchronous data collection?** Real robot datasets often have timestamp jitter or multiple unsynchronized cameras. Does shuffling + sampling robustness degrade?

3. **Does the approach work for goals without a clear end state?** What about maintenance tasks, exploration, or open-ended objectives? Can the model assign meaningful temporal distance?

4. **Can we distill RynnValue into smaller, edge-deployable models?** For on-robot inference, what is the accuracy/latency trade-off?

5. **How does the approach combine with other reward signals (e.g., safety constraints, human feedback)?** Can temporal distance be blended with preference or IRL-based rewards?

6. **What is the theoretical sample complexity?** How much data is truly needed to match preference-based methods? Is there a scaling law?

## Key Takeaway
Robots can learn value functions from raw video timestamps without manual annotations, scaling to millions of clips and improving real-world task success by 20 points.
