# When and Where to Trust the Teacher: Unifying On-Policy Distillation and GRPO through Entropy-Calibrated Credit Assignment

**Authors:** Jie Zhang, Jingxiao Yang, Zhehao Huang, Yuhang Liu, Xiaolin Huang
**Published:** 2026-09-23 | **arXiv:** [2609.28385v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28385v1)

## Summary
This paper tackles a fundamental problem in training language models for mathematical reasoning: how to effectively combine two sources of feedback — a verifier (which knows if the final answer is correct) and a teacher (which provides token-level guidance). The authors propose UECR-GRPO, a unified framework that integrates both signals intelligently, improving performance on math benchmarks by carefully deciding when and where to trust each feedback source.

## Theoretical Background
The paper builds on recent advances in reinforcement learning for language models. **On-policy distillation (OPD)** provides dense feedback at the token level by having a student model learn from a teacher's preferences, but teacher preferences may not correlate with correctness. **GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization)** is a RLVR approach that uses verifiable rewards (from checking final answers) with group normalization for stable training. The key tension is that verifiers give sparse, correct signals (right/wrong answer) but offer no guidance on intermediate steps, while teachers offer rich intermediate feedback that may be unreliable. The authors draw on **KL-regularized objectives** (penalizing divergence from prior policies) and **PPO clipping** (a standard technique for stable policy gradient updates) as foundational techniques.

## Core Contributions
1. **Path-Utility Unification (PUU)**: A principled way to combine verifier rewards and teacher signals before group normalization and clipping (rather than sequentially), allowing teacher evidence to influence response ranking. Uses length-normalized teacher scores in a single KL-regularized objective.

2. **Entropy-Calibrated Redistribution (ECR)**: A token-level reweighting mechanism that redistributes verifier-derived credit using the gap between teacher and old-policy predictions, weighted by full-vocabulary teacher entropy. Crucially, it preserves total task credit per response and respects token-wise sign information through a zero-sum projection.

3. **Empirical validation**: Demonstrates consistent gains (0.56–0.89 pp) over strong baselines across five math reasoning benchmarks at two model scales (1.7B and 4B parameters).

## Connections to Other Work
This work unifies two important recent directions: [[RLVF]] (learning from verifiable feedback) and [[on-policy distillation]]. It extends [[GRPO]] by integrating teacher signals more tightly. Related to [[RLHF]] and [[preference learning]], but for the mathematical reasoning domain where ground truth is computable. The entropy calibration parallels uncertainty quantification in [[active learning]] and [[confidence estimation]]. The credit assignment problem is central to all policy gradient methods, touched by prior work on [[reward shaping]] and [[temporal credit assignment]].

## Practical Applications
- **AI tutoring systems**: Math problem solvers that learn both from correctness (verifier) and pedagogical quality (teacher), improving reasoning step reliability.
- **Automated theorem proving**: Systems that combine proof verification with heuristic guidance from human-written or expert proofs.
- **Code generation with correctness checks**: Models trained on both passing test cases and style/clarity feedback from curated code.
- **Scientific computation**: Models trained to produce correct numerical results while following interpretable derivation steps preferred by domain experts.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **Math homework solver with explainability**: A B2C app that solves math problems and shows working steps, using UECR-GRPO to balance correctness with clarity — targeting parents, students, and tutors.

2. **Enterprise proof assistant for finance/legal**: Automated reasoning engine for contract logic, tax code interpretation, or financial modeling that integrates formal verification with domain expert guidance.

3. **Adaptive tutoring platform**: White-label system that fine-tunes a student model on both test correctness and teacher quality signals, personalizing to learner weaknesses.

4. **API for verifiable AI reasoning**: Managed service offering LLM endpoints trained with UECR-GRPO, optimized for STEM domains, sold to EdTech, research, and enterprise customers.

5. **Reinforcement learning consulting for LLM teams**: Consulting/framework service helping companies apply entropy-calibrated credit assignment to their custom domains beyond math.

## Technical Limitations
- **Verifier availability**: Assumes access to a reliable verifier (checkable final answer). Many domains (e.g., open-ended writing, creative tasks) lack this.
- **Teacher quality dependency**: Performance still hinges on teacher quality; garbage-in–garbage-out if the teacher is weak. No principled way to detect and reject systematically poor teachers.
- **Computational cost**: Full-vocabulary teacher entropy computation adds overhead; scalability to very large vocabularies or long sequences not discussed.
- **Limited domain scope**: Evaluated only on mathematical reasoning; generalization to other structured reasoning tasks (code, logic) is unclear.
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity**: Multiple components (entropy attenuation, normalization, projection) likely require tuning; sensitivity analysis is sparse.

## Open Questions
- How does UECR-GRPO perform when the teacher and verifier substantially disagree? Can we detect and recover from systematic teacher bias?
- Does the method scale to longer sequences or larger vocabularies, and what is the computational overhead relative to standard GRPO?
- Can the entropy-calibration idea extend beyond math to domains without verifiable ground truth (e.g., open-ended reasoning)?
- How robust is the zero-sum credit projection? What happens if verifier signals are themselves noisy or miscalibrated?
- Does this framework apply to non-language domains (vision, robotics) with verifiable rewards and intermediate guidance?

## Key Takeaway
Intelligently mixing verifiable rewards and teacher feedback by calibrating trust via entropy improves math reasoning models more than either signal alone.
