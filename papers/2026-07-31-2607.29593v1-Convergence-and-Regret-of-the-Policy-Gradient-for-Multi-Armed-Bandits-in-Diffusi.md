# Convergence and Regret of the Policy Gradient for Multi-Armed Bandits in Diffusion Environment

**Authors:** Yanwei Jia, Du Ouyang
**Published:** 2026-07-31 | **arXiv:** [2607.29593v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29593v1)

## Summary
This paper analyzes policy gradient algorithms for multi-armed bandits (decision problems with multiple choices) in continuous-time environments governed by stochastic differential equations (SDEs — equations with random noise). The authors prove that the algorithm converges to the optimal choice and achieves logarithmic regret (a measure of how much worse the algorithm performs than the ideal), improving on prior work through novel mathematical techniques.

## Theoretical Background
Multi-armed bandits are a foundational problem in reinforcement learning where an agent repeatedly chooses among K actions (arms), each returning stochastic rewards, balancing exploration (trying new arms) versus exploitation (using known good arms). Policy gradient methods — algorithms that directly improve a stochastic policy (a probability distribution over actions) by following gradients of expected reward — are a central RL technique. This work extends these ideas to continuous-time environments modeled by stochastic differential equations, which allow reward streams to evolve smoothly over time rather than in discrete steps. The paper uses the logit parameterization (a standard way to ensure probabilities stay valid through exponential/softmax transformations) for the policy and analyzes convergence using Lyapunov functions (mathematical tools that measure stability in dynamical systems).

## Core Contributions
- **Convergence guarantee**: Shows policy gradient with logit parameterization converges almost surely to the optimal arm under any constant learning rate (no rate tuning required).
- **Regret bound**: Derives a non-asymptotic O(log T) regret bound when learning rates stay below a fixed threshold—matching or improving the best known rates for this setting.
- **Novel Lyapunov analysis**: Constructs a new Lyapunov function specifically for policy gradient in SDEs, providing cleaner proofs than prior work (Lattimore 2026a) and bridging continuous and discrete-time analyses.

## Connections to Other Work
This builds on the continuous-time RL framework from [[Wang et al. 2020]] and [[Jia and Zhou 2022b]]. It directly improves [[Lattimore 2026a]], which studied the same SDE setting. The work is conceptually related to classical bandit theory (Lai-Robbins lower bounds) and modern policy gradient theory (e.g., convergence of softmax policies), but innovates by combining SDE analysis tools with RL. The Lyapunov approach is a rigorous alternative to martingale concentration bounds often used in discrete-time RL proofs.

## Practical Applications
- **Adaptive experimental design**: Sequentially optimizing over choices in real-time systems (drug dosing, resource allocation) where outcomes evolve continuously.
- **Algorithmic trading**: Dynamically selecting among trading strategies in markets with continuous price evolution.
- **Robotics control**: Real-time motor command selection in environments with continuous state dynamics.
- **Network routing**: Continuous-time selection of communication paths in networks with time-varying latencies.

## Technical Limitations
- **Constant learning rate assumption**: Requires pre-specifying a learning rate below a problem-dependent threshold; the threshold itself may be hard to compute in practice.
- **Logit parameterization requirement**: Results specific to this choice; generalization to other parameterizations (e.g., neural network policies) is unclear.
- **SDE model realism**: Assumes rewards truly follow SDEs; real systems may have hidden state, regime changes, or model mismatch that violates this.
- **Exploration via noise**: Convergence relies on inherent randomness in the SDE; in fully deterministic or poorly explored settings, arm discovery may stall.
- **Scalability unclear**: Paper focuses on K arms; extension to high-dimensional action spaces or function approximation is not addressed.

## Open Questions
- Does the Lyapunov approach extend to contextual bandits (where actions depend on side information) in continuous time?
- Can the regret bound be improved below O(log T), or is this tight for policy gradient in this setting?
- How does the method perform with neural network parameterizations of the policy, beyond logit?
- What happens if the SDE model is misspecified or has unknown parameters?
- Can the theory extend to exploration-exploitation tradeoffs in non-stationary (time-varying reward) SDEs?

## Key Takeaway
Policy gradient converges to optimal choices in continuous-time bandits with logarithmic regret and any learning rate, proved via novel SDE-based Lyapunov analysis.
