# AaLLM: An End-to-End Analog Circuit Design Framework from Topology Generation to Sizing Using Large Language Models

**Authors:** Mohammed Ayman Habib, Rylan Hart, Morteza Fayazi
**Published:** 2026-08-13 | **arXiv:** [2608.13472v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13472v1)

## Summary
This paper presents AaLLM, an end-to-end system that uses large language models to design analog circuits automatically — both choosing circuit topologies (the structure) and sizing components (the values). Rather than requiring experts to manually iterate between different design tools, AaLLM takes user specifications as input and outputs a complete circuit design, achieving designs as good as or better than hand-crafted ones while running 40x faster than existing approaches.

## Theoretical Background
Analog circuit design traditionally requires expert intuition navigating a high-dimensional, nonlinear design space with conflicting trade-offs (e.g., power vs. speed). Recent work has applied LLMs — neural networks trained on vast text to predict and generate sequences — to engineering tasks. Prior LLM-based circuit work has been fragmented: some focus only on topology generation (which components to connect), others only on sizing (what values to use). Knowledge representation in LLMs is typically shallow, prone to hallucinations (confident false outputs). The paper builds on retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) — a technique that grounds LLM outputs in retrieved external documents — and multi-agent reasoning, where specialized agents collaborate to solve problems better than a single agent could.

## Core Contributions
1. **End-to-end automation**: First system to handle both topology generation and sizing in a unified workflow, eliminating fragmented manual steps.
2. **Automated knowledge base construction**: Automatically extracts and structures circuit design knowledge from papers and textbooks via RAG, removing tedious manual data collection.
3. **Tri-agent feedback architecture**: Introduces Designer (proposes component values) → Critic (validates) → Evaluator (arbitrates) loop that minimizes expensive SPICE simulation calls.
4. **Novel topology generation**: Demonstrates the system can generate previously-unseen circuit topologies with competitive or superior figures of merit (up to 3x better on certain circuits).
5. **Empirical efficiency gains**: 3–4.5x fewer SPICE calls and 40x wall-clock speedup versus prior multi-agent LLM circuit pipelines.

## Connections to Other Work
AaLLM extends [[Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)]] by applying it to technical domain knowledge for circuit design. It relates to broader work on [[multi-agent-systems]] where specialized agents collaborate (e.g., similar collaborative patterns in code generation and scientific discovery). The work bridges LLMs and [[optimization]] — circuit sizing is fundamentally a constrained optimization problem. It differs from [[reinforcement-learning]] based circuit design (which learns policies through simulation feedback) by using symbolic reasoning and explicit domain knowledge. The tri-agent loop echoes debate-based or ensemble reasoning patterns in recent LLM work, though applied to a novel domain (EDA rather than NLP or reasoning tasks).

## Practical Applications
1. **Rapid prototyping for analog IP designers**: Small teams can iterate circuit designs in minutes rather than days, reducing time-to-market for mixed-signal chips.
2. **Democratizing circuit design**: Engineers without deep analog expertise can generate reasonable designs, lowering barriers to entry in analog electronics.
3. **Automated design exploration**: Generate hundreds of candidate topologies quickly to find novel trade-offs (power, noise, area) without manual iteration.
4. **Educational tools**: Students learn circuit design principles by interacting with an AI that explains its choices using retrieved textbook/paper excerpts.
5. **Custom analog blocks in SoC design**: SoC designers can specify power budgets and performance targets, receive netlists, and integrate them into larger chips faster.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **AaLLM-as-a-Service SaaS**: Cloud platform where analog designers upload specs (bandwidth, power, noise targets), receive optimized netlists, and integrate with cloud-based SPICE simulators. Subscription per design or per month.
2. **Interactive Analog Design Co-pilot**: IDE plugin (for Cadence, Synopsys) that suggests circuit improvements in real-time, generates variants, and explains trade-offs as designers edit schematics.
3. **Analog IP Marketplace**: Pre-trained AaLLM models fine-tuned on specific domains (op-amps, filters, ADCs, PLLs); customers pay per download or royalty-free licensing. Certification of generated designs.
4. **Startup: Analog AutoML for IoT**: Automated design of ultra-low-power analog front-ends for IoT sensors; target companies that currently hire expensive analog consultants.
5. **Design Knowledge Extraction SaaS**: Automatically mine and structure analog design knowledge from patent databases and research papers, sell as curated knowledge bases to EDA tool vendors.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Limited to known design classes**: While the system generates novel topologies, it remains anchored to patterns in its training knowledge base; truly exotic or unconventional designs may be overlooked.
2. **SPICE accuracy bottleneck**: The system still relies on SPICE for validation; parasitics, layout effects, temperature/process variation, and aging are not captured well by circuit simulation alone.
3. **Hallucinations in component selection**: Despite RAG grounding, LLMs can still confidently suggest non-existent components or incorrect parameter ranges; manual human review is required before fabrication.
4. **Scalability to complex systems**: Testing is on specific isolated circuits; scaling to full mixed-signal SoCs with thousands of components and strong interdependencies is unclear.
5. **Knowledge base staleness**: Automated extraction from papers works, but may miss cutting-edge research or vendor-specific optimizations; manual curation burden doesn't disappear, only shifts.
6. **Specification clarity assumption**: The system assumes user specs are unambiguous and complete; real-world customers often have implicit, conflicting, or evolving requirements.

## Open Questions
1. **How does performance degrade with ambiguous or contradictory specs?** What happens when speed, power, and area cannot all be optimized simultaneously?
2. **Can the system handle process/voltage/temperature variation and aging?** Does it generate designs robust across PVT corners?
3. **How well does the tri-agent feedback generalize to entirely new circuit families not seen during training?** Is there a domain shift problem?
4. **What is the human verification burden in practice?** How many generated designs pass first-time layout and fabrication?
5. **Can this extend to mixed-signal and digital circuits?** The paper focuses on analog; what breaks when there are digital control loops or state machines?
6. **How sensitive is performance to the quality and scope of the knowledge base?** What is the ROI of expanding the training corpus?

## Key Takeaway
LLMs with multi-agent feedback can automatically design analog circuits faster and sometimes better than experts, solving both topology and sizing in one unified workflow.
