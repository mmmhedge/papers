# Denoising Multi-Robot Trajectories

**Authors:** Yuhao Zhang, Keisuke Okumura, Ajay Shankar, Amanda Prorok
**Published:** 2026-09-28 | **arXiv:** [2609.35651v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35651v1)

## Summary
This paper presents D4orm, a diffusion-denoising framework that solves multi-robot trajectory planning by iteratively refining candidate paths to be collision-free and dynamically feasible. Rather than using traditional optimization, it leverages massive parallel sampling on GPUs to efficiently generate high-quality solutions, scaling from small robot teams to 100+ robots and real-world quadrotor deployments.

## Theoretical Background
Multi-robot trajectory planning is a classic coordination problem: given N robots with dynamics constraints, find collision-free paths for all of them simultaneously. This is nonconvex and exponential in complexity. The paper builds on **D4orm** (a prior dynamics-aware diffusion framework) and applies diffusion-denoising — a generative sampling technique where a neural network learns to iteratively "denoise" noisy samples toward valid solutions. The key insight is treating trajectory planning as deformation optimization: rather than computing paths from scratch, the method takes candidate trajectories and incrementally improves them through sampling-based optimization, which maps naturally to GPU parallelism. This contrasts with classical solvers (RRT, trajectory optimization) that use deterministic search or gradient-based methods.

## Core Contributions
1. **Architectural extensions** of D4orm: decoupled planners (for scalability), receding-horizon online planners (with feedback control), and fully distributed planners (for resource-constrained robots).
2. **Empirical validation** at multiple scales: 2–10 robots in simulation with full real-world deployment on quadrotors; 100-robot deconfliction; continuous "lifelong" operation on ground robots.
3. **Comparative analysis** showing diffusion-denoising outperforms MPPI (cross-entropy path integral sampling) and learned diffusion baselines in solution quality and reliability.
4. **Zero-shot transfer**: trained policies generalize to unseen real-robot scenarios without retraining.

## Connections to Other Work
This extends [[multi-agent coordination]] and [[motion planning]] by applying generative diffusion models—techniques popularized in image generation—to a robotics domain. It relates to prior sampling-based planners like RRT* and MPPI, but replaces hand-crafted sampling with learned denoising. The distributed variant connects to [[decentralized control]] and [[onboard planning]] for swarm robotics. The receding-horizon formulation echoes [[model predictive control]] but uses diffusion sampling instead of gradient-based optimization.

## Practical Applications
1. **Autonomous warehouse systems**: coordinating dozens of mobile robots for sorting/transport without infrastructure-level collision avoidance.
2. **Drone swarm operations**: real-time collision-free flight for aerial inspection, surveillance, or delivery fleets.
3. **Search-and-rescue coordination**: multiple robots navigating dynamically to reach targets in unknown environments with onboard compute.
4. **Formation control in manufacturing**: robots assembling or manipulating objects in shared workspaces.
5. **Lifelong autonomous transit**: continuously operating ground robots in shared spaces (e.g., hospitals, factories) that adapt to obstacles in real time.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **Warehouse robotics middleware**: sell the D4orm planner as an add-on layer for off-the-shelf mobile robots, enabling orders-of-magnitude higher throughput without rewiring infrastructure.
2. **Drone swarm-as-a-service**: deploy managed drone teams for inspection/surveying tasks that require real-time coordination; license the distributed planner to operators.
3. **Robot fleet orchestration SaaS**: cloud/edge platform that accepts robot fleet geometry, obstacles, and goals; returns optimized distributed plans; targets small manufacturers and logistics hubs.
4. **Autonomous mobile robot (AMR) controller upgrade**: retrofit existing AMRs with real-time D4orm planning to reduce collision detection latency and improve throughput in dense environments.
5. **Simulation-to-reality transfer service**: offer simulation, training, and zero-shot deployment services for multi-robot systems, lowering deployment friction for research labs and companies.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Generalization scope**: all experiments use relatively simple robot morphologies (differential-drive, holonomic, quadrotors). Complex dynamics (legs, manipulators) or nonholonomic constraints beyond tested cases remain unclear.
2. **Scalability ceiling**: 100-robot experiments are impressive but still far from thousand-robot swarms; computational cost per robot and memory overhead at larger scales need clarification.
3. **Training data requirement**: the method relies on a learned diffusion model; how sensitive is performance to training distribution? What happens with robot types unseen in training?
4. **Real-time latency**: receding-horizon plans require fast GPU inference. Deployment on resource-constrained robots (small drones, ground vehicles without GPU) is demonstrated but may not generalize to even tighter compute budgets.
5. **Obstacle assumption**: static or slowly changing obstacles are assumed; highly dynamic environments (pedestrians, moving obstacles) are not thoroughly tested.
6. **Theoretical guarantees**: no completeness or optimality bounds provided; comparison is purely empirical.

## Open Questions
1. Can diffusion-denoising scale gracefully to 1000+ robots, and what is the actual compute cost per robot added?
2. How does the method perform with heterogeneous robot teams (different dynamics, sizes, capabilities) in the same scene?
3. Can the framework handle soft constraints (e.g., energy-optimal paths) beyond hard collision avoidance?
4. What is the minimum GPU memory / inference latency needed for real-time operation on edge devices?
5. Can the learned diffusion model transfer across significantly different environments (e.g., from simulation to real outdoor scenes), or is retraining necessary?
6. How do communication delays and sensing uncertainty in distributed settings affect plan quality and safety?

## Key Takeaway
Diffusion denoising scales multi-robot coordination efficiently via GPU sampling, outperforming classical and learned alternatives in real deployments.
