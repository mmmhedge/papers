# AskChem: Claim-Centered Infrastructure for Chemistry Literature Synthesis

**Authors:** Bing Yan, Gregory Wolfe, Stefano Martiniani, Kyunghyun Cho
**Published:** 2026-07-30 | **arXiv:** [2607.28618v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28618v1)

## Summary
AskChem transforms chemistry literature search from returning ranked papers to returning specific, source-grounded claims extracted from papers. Scientists and AI agents can now search for atomic facts about chemistry with verified citations and evidence links, rather than manually hunting through documents. This matters because chemistry research relies heavily on synthesizing dispersed findings, and having verifiable, interconnected claims dramatically speeds up literature review and enables better AI agent reasoning.

## Theoretical Background
This work builds on information retrieval (IR) systems that traditionally rank full documents by relevance, and on knowledge base construction methods that extract structured facts from unstructured text. It also draws from work on provenance tracking — maintaining links between claims and their sources — and faceted taxonomy design (hierarchical classification systems that let users drill down through multiple dimensions). The paper treats chemistry papers as sources of extractable, atomic claims rather than opaque monoliths, similar to how modern fact-checking and knowledge graph systems (like those built for medical literature or patent analysis) decompose documents into verifiable units.

## Core Contributions
1. **Claim-centric retrieval paradigm**: Shifts the unit of search from "papers" to "claims" — typed, atomic statements paired with DOI + verbatim quote or explicit evidence locators.
2. **Stabilized faceted taxonomy**: A human-validated hierarchical classification that provides both structured browsing and retrieval.
3. **Evidence graph**: Connects claims through semantic relations, allowing users to explore how findings relate across papers.
4. **Living taxonomy**: An exploratory, community-updatable classification that situates papers under scientific principles.
5. **Operational scale**: Live indexing of 2.4M claims from 147K chemistry papers with multiple access patterns (web, REST, SDK, MCP for AI agents).
6. **Empirical validation**: Demonstrates measurable improvements in citation grounding (100% resolvable DOIs vs. 88.3% baseline) and citation density vs. competing systems.

## Connections to Other Work
AskChem relates to [[RLHF]] and grounding strategies for large language models — by providing a verified claim store, it reduces hallucination when LLMs answer chemistry questions. It also connects to [[agents]] and agent reasoning architectures: the MCP (Model Context Protocol) integration allows AI systems to query grounded chemistry facts, analogous to tool-use in agent frameworks. The work complements recent advances in retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) by moving beyond document-level retrieval to fact-level retrieval. It echoes knowledge graph construction pipelines (e.g., for biomedical literature) but with a focus on chemistry domain specifics and interactive browsing.

## Practical Applications
1. **Drug discovery workflows**: Medicinal chemists can rapidly assemble synthesis routes, reaction conditions, and compound properties across thousands of papers without manual searching.
2. **Regulatory compliance**: Pharmaceutical companies verify material safety data, toxicity claims, and manufacturing standards by tracing claims back to primary sources with one click.
3. **Graduate student onboarding**: New researchers quickly build accurate mental models of established chemistry by browsing the evidence graph and related claims in structured form.
4. **AI-assisted lab notebooks**: Autonomous chemistry agents (e.g., for high-throughput synthesis planning) can retrieve and cite specific conditions, reaction yields, and failure modes with full provenance.
5. **Chemistry education**: Instructors build curricula and assignments by querying claims at multiple abstraction levels (foundational principles vs. experimental protocols).

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **Domain-specific claim indexing as a service**: Extend the AskChem model to materials science, organic synthesis, or biochemistry as white-label backends for research institutions and pharma companies.
2. **Real-time synthesis assistant (mobile/desktop app)**: A lab-bench tool that lets chemists voice-query AskChem ("How do I make ortho-xylene from benzene?") and get instant, cited protocols with linked evidence.
3. **Automated literature review as-a-service for grant proposals**: Generate structured, claim-grounded literature sections for NIH or NSF grant applications, with automatic citation auditing and novelty detection.
4. **Chemistry compliance and patent clearance tool**: Help companies detect prior art and verify regulatory claims by querying and comparing claims across indexed patents and journal literature.
5. **Research collaboration platform**: Build a multi-lab workspace where teams share private claim stores, link to AskChem, and collaboratively curate and debate evidence for disputed claims in chemistry.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Claim extraction bias**: Extracting claims from papers is lossy and subjective — what counts as an "atomic claim" may vary; the system risks missing nuance or context embedded in equations, figures, or prose.
2. **Coverage and quality gaps**: Indexing 2.4M claims from 147K papers is substantial but incomplete; older literature, non-English papers, and preprints may be underrepresented, skewing which findings are discoverable.
3. **Taxonomy brittleness**: A "stabilized" taxonomy may not adapt quickly to emerging subfields or controversy; disagreement about classification could fragment user trust.
4. **Scalability of evidence graphs**: As the claim store grows, maintaining and querying evidence graphs may face computational and consistency challenges.
5. **Provenance granularity**: Linking claims to DOIs and quotes is good, but doesn't capture indirect evidence, inference chains, or conditional applicability (e.g., "this reaction works only in polar solvents").
6. **Domain lock-in**: The system is tuned to chemistry; generalization to physics, materials science, or wet biology will require re-extraction and re-taxonomy design.

## Open Questions
1. Can the claim extraction pipeline be automated end-to-end (e.g., using LLMs) while maintaining quality and avoiding hallucination? What human review burden is necessary?
2. How does citation density or answerability scale as the claim store grows beyond 2.4M? Are there diminishing returns or emergence of new noise?
3. Can the evidence graph be automatically enriched with causal or methodological relations (e.g., "this reaction is a precursor to...")?
4. How do users actually interact with the living taxonomy? Does crowdsourced curation improve discovery, or introduce bias?
5. What is the impact on chemistry AI agents (e.g., molecular design or synthesis planning models) when grounded in AskChem vs. other retrieval sources?
6. Can the system detect and surface contradictory claims (e.g., conflicting reaction yields) to flag literature disputes?

## Key Takeaway
Searchable chemistry claims with DOI verification beat ranked papers for answering cross-paper scientific questions reliably.
