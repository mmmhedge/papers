# A Comparative Explainability Framework for DeBERTa-v3 in Zero-Shot Medical Abstract Classification

**Authors:** Javier Diaz Esteban-Herreros, David Muñoz-Valero, Raquel Martínez-España, Jose M. Juarez, Juan Moreno-Garcia
**Published:** 2026-10-01 | **arXiv:** [2610.02116v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02116v1)

## Summary
This paper evaluates how well five different explanation methods (SHAP, LIME, occlusion, Input x Gradient, and Attention x Gradient) agree with each other when explaining a DeBERTa-v3 model's predictions on medical abstract classification. The authors find that explanations diverge significantly, especially when the model is uncertain, and identify three systematic failure modes—suggesting that auditing AI systems in medicine requires multiple explanation methods rather than trusting any single one.

## Theoretical Background
The paper builds on two foundational concepts. First, **zero-shot classification** — where a model must categorize text into labels it has never explicitly seen during training, using natural language inference to match abstracts against hypothetical descriptions of each class. Second, **Explainable AI (XAI)** — the field addressing how to interpret black-box model decisions. The five explanation methods represent two paradigms: model-agnostic approaches (SHAP, LIME) that work on any model, and method-specific approaches (occlusion, gradients, attention) tuned to deep learning or transformers. The paper confronts the "disagreement problem" — the observation that different XAI methods often produce conflicting explanations for the same prediction.

## Core Contributions
- **Systematic disagreement analysis**: Quantifies pairwise agreement between five explanation methods using the Jaccard index, revealing when and why explanations diverge.
- **Correlation between explainability and predictive certainty**: Demonstrates that explanation stability mirrors the model's confidence — stable in well-defined domains, degraded under semantic ambiguity.
- **Error mechanism taxonomy**: Uncovers three failure modes (lexical hypersensitivity, semantic overlap, loss of attribution coherence) through qualitative auditing.
- **Clinical-domain validation**: Establishes baseline performance on medical abstracts and shows where zero-shot transfer breaks down.
- **Methodological recommendation**: Evidence-based argument for combining multiple explanation methods rather than relying on a single approach.

## Connections to Other Work
This work sits at the intersection of [[mechanistic-interpretability]] (understanding what a model learns) and practical [[alignment]] concerns in high-stakes domains. It relates to broader debates in XAI about **explanation fidelity** — whether different methods capture genuine model behavior or merely different artifact artifacts. The disagreement problem echoes findings in adversarial robustness literature. The zero-shot medical classification task extends work on [[transformers]] for clinical NLP, such as biomedical BERT variants. The paper implicitly critiques single-method auditing practices common in healthcare AI deployment.

## Practical Applications
- **Clinical decision-support validation**: Before deploying AI models for diagnosis screening, hospitals can use this framework to audit which model predictions are truly reliable vs. cases where explanations diverge (high-risk scenarios).
- **Regulatory compliance auditing**: FDA/CE-mark medical device submissions require explainability evidence; this framework provides a quantitative, multi-method audit trail.
- **Model selection in medical labs**: When choosing between competing models, clinicians can use agreement metrics as a proxy for trustworthiness — favoring models with coherent explanations.
- **Training data quality assessment**: High explanation disagreement can signal dataset issues (ambiguous labels, semantic overlap) that need correction before deployment.
- **Domain-specific model fine-tuning**: Identifying failure modes (e.g., lexical hypersensitivity to certain medical jargon) guides targeted retraining.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **ExplainAI for Healthcare**: SaaS platform that runs multi-method XAI audits on customer ML models before clinical deployment, generating compliance reports and risk scorecards.
2. **MedAudit**: Automated explanation disagreement detector—flags when a deployed medical AI system's explanations become incoherent, triggering retraining alerts.

4. **Ontology-Driven Classification Engine**: Domain-specific alternative to zero-shot classification that learns hospital-specific diagnostic hierarchies, reducing semantic ambiguity and explanation breakdown.

## Technical Limitations
- **Scale and generalization**: The study uses only 1,000 balanced texts per class; real clinical datasets are imbalanced and far larger. Findings may not scale.
- **Explanation method selection bias**: Only five methods tested; other attribution techniques (e.g., integrated gradients, attention-flow methods) may show different agreement patterns.
- **Jaccard index sensitivity**: Top-token attribution is coarse; disagreement might vanish if using ranked scores instead of hard thresholding.
- **Zero-shot assumption**: Natural language inference is brittle for medical terminology with overlapping semantics (e.g., "myocardial infarction" vs. "acute coronary syndrome"). Ground truth labels are not validated by clinicians.
- **Model-specific findings**: Results are specific to DeBERTa-v3; other transformers (RoBERTa, GPT, specialized biomedical models) may exhibit different stability patterns.
- **No counterfactual validation**: The paper doesn't verify whether high-agreement explanations actually reflect model internals vs. just correlating with surface features.

## Open Questions
- How does explanation stability correlate with out-of-distribution robustness? Do models with coherent explanations generalize better to novel medical subdomains?
- Can we predict when a model will produce disagreement *before* inference, using meta-features of the input text?
- Do ensemble methods (averaging explanations) outperform any single method? Can disagreement be harnessed as a confidence measure?
- How do structured medical ontologies (SNOMED, ICD) affect explanation agreement compared to flat diagnostic labels?
- Can fine-tuning on "high-agreement" examples improve both accuracy and explainability stability?
- Do clinician-annotated explanations align with any of the five automated methods, or is ground truth itself contested?

## Key Takeaway
Multiple explanation methods must be combined to reliably audit medical AI, as individual methods diverge precisely when model confidence drops.
