# From Corpora to Co-Evolving Capabilities: Capability-Centric Data Design for Generalist Image Generation

**Authors:** Xingjian Wang, Zhao Wang, Taihang Hu, Jun Zheng, Qing Jin et al.
**Published:** 2026-08-18 | **arXiv:** [2608.18076v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18076v1)

## Summary
This paper proposes a capability-driven data infrastructure for training generalist image generation models that can handle both text-to-image synthesis and image editing tasks. Instead of optimizing datasets in isolation, the authors organize heterogeneous training data according to the dependencies between different generative capabilities, using specialized data engines and curriculum learning to co-evolve multiple skills. They curate a massive 440M-image dataset and train 3B and 6B parameter diffusion models that achieve broad visual coverage and effective transfer across tasks.

## Theoretical Background
Image generation has historically progressed through scaling laws — larger datasets and models consistently improve performance. Recent work has focused on data quality, rebalancing (ensuring diverse concepts are represented fairly), and automatic recaptioning to improve text-image alignment. The paper builds on diffusion models — neural networks that generate images by gradually denoising random noise conditioned on text prompts or editing instructions. A key insight from prior work is that different generative capabilities (like composition understanding, style transfer, or object editing) may have different data requirements and learning orders. The paper extends this by treating capability acquisition as an ordered dependency problem, where certain skills should be learned before others.

## Core Contributions
1. **Capability-driven data infrastructure**: Introduces a framework that explicitly models dependencies between generative capabilities rather than treating tasks independently. The system couples capability-specific supervision (how to label/prepare data) with capability-aligned curriculum scheduling (the order in which to present data during training).

2. **Three specialized data engines**: Constructs complementary supervision for (i) text-image grounding (understanding what text describes in images), (ii) inter-image transformation (learning how images relate through edits or style changes), and (iii) image-knowledge association (linking visual content to semantic entities).

3. **Multi-stage curriculum learning**: Jointly evolves task composition, visual-concept distribution, data quality, and image resolution following the dependency order of capability acquisition — not all at once, but sequenced intelligently.

4. **Capability-aware evaluation**: Closes the loop with targeted retrieval and gap-aware resampling — identifying where the model struggles and focusing additional training data there.

5. **Large-scale curation**: Produces 440M text-to-image pairs, 120M editing pairs, and 27M+ image-entity pairs, trained from scratch at 3B and 6B parameter scales.

## Connections to Other Work
This work relates to curriculum learning in deep learning — the idea that presenting training data in a meaningful order (easier to harder, or prerequisite-skills-first) improves final performance. It also connects to [[multi-task learning]] literature, which explores how to train a single model on multiple related objectives. The paper's emphasis on data quality and recaptioning builds on recent work in vision-language pre-training (e.g., CLIP-style models). The diffusion model backbone likely builds on recent advances like latent diffusion and classifier-free guidance. Finally, the notion of "capability gaps" and targeted resampling resembles active learning and hard-example mining strategies used in other domains.

## Practical Applications
- **Commercial image generation services**: Companies offering text-to-image generation (like design tools, e-commerce platforms) can use this infrastructure to support both synthesis and editing in a single, more capable model.
- **Content creation platforms**: Tools for social media creators, graphic designers, and marketers that need both generative and editing capabilities without switching between separate models.
- **E-commerce product imaging**: Automatically generating and editing product images for online retail (changing backgrounds, styles, or orientations).
- **Game and VFX asset creation**: Generating and iteratively editing visual assets for game development or visual effects, where a single model handling multiple related tasks reduces complexity.
- **Accessibility tools**: Models that can generate or modify images based on detailed text descriptions, aiding users who describe visual content for accessibility purposes.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **"EditFlow"** — An all-in-one design platform combining text-to-image generation and editing (background removal, style transfer, object replacement) in a single interface, targeting SME graphic designers who can't afford separate tools.

2. **"DataCraft Labs"** — A B2B data curation and curriculum-learning-as-a-service platform for companies training custom generative models; uses the capability-dependency framework to reduce data annotation costs and improve model quality.

3. **"Brand Asset Generator"** — Specialized tool for enterprises to generate on-brand product imagery and marketing materials; learns brand visual guidelines through editing supervision and generates consistent assets at scale.

4. **"Concept Studio"** — A no-code platform where users define visual concepts (e.g., "modern minimalist kitchen") and the system automatically curates and schedules training data, then fine-tunes a generative model for consistent, controllable synthesis.

5. **"Model Audit & Rebalancing SaaS"** — Service that analyzes trained generative models, identifies capability gaps (via the capability-aware evaluation approach), and recommends targeted data augmentation or retraining schedules.

## Technical Limitations
- **Scalability bottleneck**: Even with 440M images, the paper doesn't clearly explain how to acquire and validate image-entity pairs or ensure concept coverage is complete. This dependency on curated supervision limits generalization to truly novel visual concepts.
- **Curriculum design is manual**: The "dependency order of capability acquisition" is heuristic and human-guided. No principled method is given for discovering or validating this order; different orders might work for different domains.
- **Computational cost**: Training from scratch at 3B and 6B scales is expensive and requires significant infrastructure; reproducibility and accessibility for smaller labs is limited.
- **Evaluation metrics**: CPI-Bench and other metrics measure synthetic quality, but don't directly measure whether the co-evolved curriculum actually transfers better than baseline multi-task learning — this causal claim isn't rigorously validated.
- **Concept drift**: The paper doesn't address whether the learned curriculum generalizes to new data distributions or how to adapt it as new visual concepts or editing styles emerge.
- **Editing fidelity**: Image editing is notoriously hard; the paper doesn't deeply discuss failure modes (e.g., inconsistent object semantics, boundary artifacts) or how editing supervision interacts with T2I capabilities.

## Open Questions
- How sensitive is final performance to the assumed capability dependency order? What if a different order was chosen?
- Can the framework discover the optimal curriculum order automatically (e.g., via reinforcement learning or multi-armed bandits), rather than relying on expert heuristics?
- Does capability-aligned curriculum learning provide a measurable advantage over naive multi-task learning on the same data? What is the performance delta?
- How does the framework scale to much larger models (10B+)? Do the data requirements and curriculum still hold?
- Can editing supervision be fully disentangled from T2I supervision, or do they create conflicting gradients during joint training?
- How does the approach handle long-tail visual concepts or rare editing operations? Is targeted resampling sufficient, or does it lead to overfitting?

## Key Takeaway
Organizing training data by generative task dependencies and curriculum order improves image models' versatility across synthesis and editing at scale.
