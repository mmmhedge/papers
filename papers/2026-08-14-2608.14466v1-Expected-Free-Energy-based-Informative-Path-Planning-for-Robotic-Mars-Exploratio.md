# Expected Free Energy-based Informative Path Planning for Robotic Mars Exploration

**Authors:** Ajith Anil Meera, Pablo Lanillos, Wouter Kouw
**Published:** 2026-08-14 | **arXiv:** [2608.14466v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14466v1)

## Summary
This paper proposes using Expected Free Energy (EFE), a decision-making framework from active inference, to guide robots exploring unknown environments like Mars. The key insight is that robots need to simultaneously gather accurate information AND find valuable locations, while staying within energy/distance budgets — and EFE unifies these competing goals into a single principled objective. The approach outperforms standard information-seeking or reward-seeking methods alone.

## Theoretical Background
This work builds on active inference, a neuroscience-inspired framework where agents choose actions to minimize "free energy" — roughly, the gap between what they expect to observe and what they actually observe, weighted by surprise and goal achievement. Expected Free Energy extends this by planning multiple steps ahead. The paper also uses Gaussian processes (flexible probabilistic models that estimate functions from sparse data) to maintain beliefs about information fields. Classical robotic path planning splits into two camps: information-theoretic methods that maximize data quality (e.g., entropy reduction) and reward-seeking methods that maximize value — this paper argues EFE naturally combines both.

## Core Contributions
1. **EFE as a unifying planning objective**: Shows that Expected Free Energy, not traditionally used in robotic exploration, elegantly balances information gathering and value-seeking under hard resource constraints (path length limits).
2. **Continuous trajectory planning**: Extends EFE-based planning from discrete action spaces to continuous robot trajectories.
3. **Empirical validation**: Demonstrates superior performance versus information-theoretic and reward-seeking baselines on simulated Mars exploration tasks, measured by both map accuracy and speed of locating high-value regions.
4. **Practical deployability**: Emphasizes that EFE is "easy-to-tune" and principled, making it suitable for autonomous systems without extensive task-specific tuning.

## Connections to Other Work
This work sits at the intersection of active inference (e.g., [[Free Energy Principle]]), classical information-seeking methods (entropy maximization, mutual information), and robotic planning under constraints. It differs from standard [[reinforcement-learning]] approaches by grounding the objective in first principles rather than reward functions. Related to [[bandits]] and exploration-exploitation tradeoffs, but with a focus on spatial/information-geometric structure. Also connects to [[Bayesian optimization]] in that it balances learning about the environment with achieving outcomes.

## Practical Applications
- **Planetary exploration**: Autonomous rovers searching for subsurface water or geological targets on Mars, Moon, or other bodies while conserving energy.
- **Environmental monitoring**: Drones or ground robots mapping pollution, radiation, or chemical contamination in hazardous zones while minimizing flight time.
- **Search and rescue**: Autonomous systems exploring disaster zones to locate survivors and map structural integrity under fuel constraints.
- **Precision agriculture**: Robots efficiently surveying crop health and identifying problem areas within battery/fuel limits.
- **Underwater exploration**: AUVs (autonomous underwater vehicles) mapping ocean floors or underwater geology while respecting energy budgets.

## Technical Limitations
- **Gaussian process scalability**: GPs scale poorly (computationally) with large datasets; limiting for long-duration missions with dense sensing.
- **Model mismatch**: Assumes a Gaussian-process belief model is accurate; real Mars terrain and sensor noise may violate this assumption.
- **Local minima in trajectory optimization**: EFE objective may be non-convex; continuous planning may get stuck in local optima, especially in complex environments.
- **Computational budget**: EFE requires planning over horizons; real-time planning on resource-constrained robots (limited CPU, memory) may be infeasible.
- **Validation scope**: Experiments appear simulation-based; real-world deployment (dust, localization error, sensor dropout) untested.
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity**: While claimed "easy-to-tune," the free energy objective has multiple weighting terms; tuning for novel environments unclear.

## Open Questions
- How does EFE planning degrade under model misspecification (e.g., non-stationary information fields, non-Gaussian noise)?
- Can this scale to high-dimensional sensing (e.g., RGB cameras or LIDAR) and larger exploration domains?
- How sensitive is performance to the choice of information field prior and kernel function in the GP?
- Does EFE planning handle dynamic environments (moving targets, changing value fields) or only static ones?
- Can the approach be extended to multi-robot scenarios where robots share information and coordinate?
- What is the empirical comparison against modern deep reinforcement learning-based planning methods?

## Key Takeaway
Expected Free Energy unifies information-gathering and value-seeking into one objective for resource-constrained robotic exploration.
