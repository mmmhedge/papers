# Do LLM Agents Execute the Plans They Declare? From Planning-Mode Declaration to Pattern-Specific Execution

**Authors:** Subba Reddy Oota, Francisco Herrera, Jordi Cabot Sagrera, Marcos López de Prado, Shadab Khan
**Published:** 2026-09-29 | **arXiv:** [2609.38108v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38108v1)

## Summary
This paper examines a critical gap in LLM-based agent systems: agents often fail to execute the plans they declare, even when the plans are sound. The authors propose Planning-as-Routing, where agents explicitly choose a planning mode (Predefined, Sequential, Hierarchical, or Search) and a specialized executor enforces that structure, dramatically improving task success rates across multiple benchmarks.

## Theoretical Background
LLM agents typically operate in a plan-then-execute paradigm: they generate a strategy for solving a task, then carry out steps in an environment. Prior work assumes generic execution frameworks like ReAct (reasoning + action) can handle any declared plan structure. However, this assumes two separate capabilities: planning-mode selection (choosing the right strategy) and faithful execution (following through). The paper builds on the observation that these are distinct failure modes, and that task success alone cannot diagnose which one failed.

## Core Contributions
1. **Empirical identification of the Plan Declaration–Execution Gap**: Demonstrates that only 22–45% of trajectories preserve the declared plan structure across benchmarks, especially for longer plans.
2. **Planning-as-Routing framework**: Introduces a deterministic dispatcher that routes tasks to pattern-specific executors (not generic ReAct) based on the declared mode, enforcing structural integrity.
3. **Quantification of execution vs. selection failures**: Shows that execution improvements (routing) yield dramatic gains (0.48→0.92 on ALFWorld), while mode selection remains unreliable even with few-shot prompting.
4. **Environment and model-specific mode effectiveness**: Demonstrates that the strongest planning mode varies by benchmark (Search wins on ALFWorld, Hierarchical on SWE-bench) and even across models within the same benchmark.

## Connections to Other Work
This work relates to [[ReAct]] (a general agent reasoning framework that lacks plan-structure enforcement), [[chain-of-thought prompting]] (which also relies on faithful execution of declared reasoning steps), and research on [[in-context learning]] and few-shot adaptation. It intersects with mechanistic interpretability questions about whether LLMs actually "understand" the structures they output, and with broader [[alignment]] concerns about agent faithfulness. The routing approach is conceptually similar to mixture-of-experts or hierarchical decision systems.

## Practical Applications
- **Software engineering agents** (SWE-bench): More reliable code generation and debugging by enforcing hierarchical decomposition.
- **Web automation** (ALFWorld): Agents that reliably navigate multi-step e-commerce or information-retrieval tasks.
- **Robotic task planning**: Long-horizon manipulation tasks that require structured, verifiable execution.
- **Autonomous research assistants**: Literature review and hypothesis-testing workflows that must preserve logical structure.
- **Enterprise process automation**: Workflow execution systems where plan fidelity is auditable and critical.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **Agent Monitoring & Audit Dashboard**: SaaS product that tracks plan declaration vs. actual execution for enterprise LLM deployments, flagging execution drifts before they cause failures.
2. **Planning-Mode Optimizer**: Tool that profiles an organization's LLM agents across multiple planning modes and recommends the best mode per task type, with continuous A/B testing.
3. **Structured Agent Execution Layer**: Middleware that sits between any LLM API and the environment, enforcing plan-structure integrity and reducing failure rates for existing agent deployments.
4. **Domain-Specific Agent Templates**: Pre-tuned planning modes and executors for verticals (software engineering, data analysis, customer support) that customers can plug into their workflows.
5. **Agent Fidelity Insurance**: Risk quantification service for enterprises using LLM agents in high-stakes tasks, based on measured execution-gap metrics per mode and model.

## Technical Limitations
- **Limited to four predefined modes**: The framework may not discover novel planning structures suited to domains outside the four categories; the taxonomy itself may be task-agnostic or suboptimal for specialized domains.
- **Weak mode selection**: Even with few-shot examples, LLMs fail to reliably pick the best mode; the paper does not solve this problem and leaves it as "open."
- **Benchmark generalization**: Results come from three specific environments (ALFWorld, SWE-bench, two others); effectiveness on out-of-distribution tasks unknown.
- **Deterministic routing overhead**: Forcing a structured executor may be suboptimal for tasks that genuinely benefit from adaptive, mixed-mode execution.
- **Model scale**: Only three LLMs tested; unclear if larger models self-correct or if the gap persists at frontier scales.
- **No comparison of routing cost**: No analysis of computational overhead, latency, or sample efficiency versus end-to-end LLM generation.

## Open Questions
- **Why do LLMs fail at mode selection?** Is it a training/tokenization issue, or a fundamental limitation of in-context learning? Can a dedicated mode-selector LLM be trained end-to-end?
- **Can planning modes be learned per-task or per-domain?** Rather than fixed modes, could the system adaptively discover modes from task structure?
- **Does scale help?** Do frontier models (GPT-4, Claude-3, etc.) select modes more reliably, or is this independent of model size?
- **Multi-agent routing**: How does this extend to multi-agent systems where agents must coordinate planning modes?
- **Hybrid execution**: Can executors dynamically switch modes mid-task, or does rigid routing sacrifice adaptability?
- **Theoretical characterization**: Under what task properties does each mode provably work best? Can this be predicted without empirical enumeration?

## Key Takeaway
LLM agents declare plans they don't execute faithfully; routing to mode-specific executors fixes execution but leaves mode selection unsolved.
