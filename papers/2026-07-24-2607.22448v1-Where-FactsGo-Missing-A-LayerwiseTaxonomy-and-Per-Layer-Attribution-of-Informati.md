# Where FactsGo Missing: A LayerwiseTaxonomy and Per-Layer Attribution of Information Omissionin Air-Gapped LLM Agent Pipelines

**Authors:** Santhiya Rajan
**Published:** 2026-07-24 | **arXiv:** [2607.22448v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22448v1)

## Summary
This paper investigates why small language models (4-8B parameters) silently omit critical facts when deployed as agents in regulated, air-gapped environments (hospitals, law firms, sovereign clouds). The authors map every stage where information gets lost—from data ingestion through the agent decision loop—and show that 68% of omissions originate in deterministic infrastructure layers (quantization, caching, prompt handling), not the model itself. This reframes a reliability crisis as a systems problem with specific, fixable interventions.

## Theoretical Background
Small language models running on-premises must use quantization (reducing precision from 32-bit to 4-bit weights to fit memory) and key-value caching (storing attention states to speed up inference). Prior work on [[mechanistic-interpretability]] and model faithfulness examines *why* models fail, but this paper focuses on *where* failures occur in production pipelines. The omission problem is distinct from hallucination (making up false facts)—it's the absence of true facts the model *should* retrieve. The work builds on layer-wise attribution methods (decomposing model behavior by depth) and controlled ablation (turning components on/off to measure their impact).

## Core Contributions
1. **Nine-layer taxonomy (L0-L8)**: Maps the entire agent pipeline from raw data ingestion (L0) through model tokenization, embedding, transformer processing, decoding, and agent loop orchestration (L8). Each layer has known omission mechanisms—e.g., truncation at L2 (context length), attention sparsity at L5 (which facts get attended to), sliding-window context at L1 (how many records are visible).

2. **Attribution methodology**: Separates *deterministic* layers (L0-L3: ingestion, tokenization, embedding, quantization) from *behavioral* layers (L4-L8: transformer attention, decoding, agent logic). Uses controlled ablation and logit decomposition to quantify each layer's contribution via an "omission waterfall" (cumulative effect).

3. **Cross-architecture harness**: Tests sliding-window-hybrid, full-attention, and SSM (State Space Model) architectures across multiple inference engines (llama.cpp, vLLM) and models—standardizing comparison across deployment variants.

4. **Runtime-detection framework**: Proposes an operationalizable system for air-gapped deployments that have direct access to logits, enabling per-inference detection of omission risk without re-training.

## Connections to Other Work
Relates to [[RLHF]] and agent reliability—but shifts focus from reward signal tuning to infrastructure. Connects to [[mechanistic-interpretability]] work (e.g., probing which model layers encode which facts), but applies it to production systems. Similar to work on context window limitations in retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), but more granular: omission is a *pipeline* phenomenon, not just a prompt engineering problem. Aligns with reliability concerns in multi-step reasoning and chain-of-thought prompting.

## Practical Applications
- **Clinical decision support**: A clinician using a quantized model to flag anomalies in 400 patient records. Current systems silently miss 62% of critical signals; the taxonomy identifies whether the miss happens at ingestion (database truncation), attention (model only reads 20 records), or output (agent forgets to report).
- **Legal document review**: Law firms using on-premises models to identify contract violations in large corpora. The attribution method pinpoints whether violations are omitted due to KV-cache constraints or deterministic tokenization artifacts.
- **Financial compliance**: Regulated environments (banks, insurance) running models on sovereign hardware for fraud detection. The runtime-detection framework flags inferences with high omission risk so human operators can intervene.
- **Sovereign AI infrastructure**: Government deployments where model outputs must be auditable. The layer-wise taxonomy enables operators to trace exactly where a fact was lost.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **OmissionMonitor (SaaS)**: A monitoring agent that wraps quantized model inference and flags requests with high omission risk in real-time, providing operators a dashboard of which pipeline layers are failing. Charge per inference scanned.

2. **PipelineOptimizer (Enterprise software)**: A system that automatically profiles a customer's air-gapped deployment (model, engine, quantization, KV-cache settings) against the taxonomy and recommends hardware, quantization, or batching changes to reduce omissions. Sell as an annual license or per-deployment fee.

3. **FactTracer (Consulting/audit service)**: Expert audits of regulated AI systems using the nine-layer taxonomy and attribution methodology to certify that critical facts aren't being silently omitted. Market to hospitals, law firms, financial regulators.

4. **LLM-as-a-Judge for omission detection**: A fine-tuned model that reads an agent's output and input context, predicting whether facts were omitted (higher accuracy than heuristics). API service for compliance teams.

5. **Hybrid inference engine (open-source + premium)**: Build on the cross-architecture harness to sell a specialized inference runtime (like llama.cpp) that is optimized for low-omission deployment. Monetize through support, managed deployment, and add-on profiling tools.

## Technical Limitations
- **Fixed server-side factors**: The paper leaves quantization type, KV-cache architecture, and RoPE scaling (rotary positional embeddings) as constants—these are critical omission sources but require hardware changes to fix. Results may not transfer to different quantization schemes.
- **Scope**: Only tested on 4-8B models. Larger models and different model families (non-transformer SSMs, hybrid architectures) are not covered.
- **Attribution causality**: The ablation methodology cannot prove *causation* in the full pipeline—only correlation. An omission might originate in L2 but be triggered by a downstream interaction at L6.
- **Behavioral layer attribution**: Layers L4-L8 (transformer, decoding, agent logic) are harder to isolate via controlled ablation. The methodology may underestimate their contribution.
- **Generalization to other tasks**: Tested on multi-record retrieval and anomaly detection. Unclear whether the taxonomy applies equally to reasoning, translation, or code generation.
- **Runtime-detection overhead**: The proposed detection framework requires access to logits and adds inference latency; not viable for ultra-low-latency deployments.

## Open Questions
- **Can omissions be corrected post-hoc?** If we detect an omission at runtime, can we re-run the agent with modified context (e.g., different sliding-window position, higher sampling temperature) to recover the missing fact?
- **How do quantization schemes interact with omission?** Do 4-bit vs. 8-bit quantization produce different omission taxonomies? What about mixed-precision quantization?
- **Do larger models omit less?** The paper tests 4-8B; what is the scaling curve? Does a 13B or 70B model exhibit similar omission rates at the same deterministic layers?
- **Can omission be optimized away architecturally?** Would a full-attention (no sliding window) or SSM-based model reduce omissions? What is the latency/memory tradeoff?
- **How does few-shot prompting affect the taxonomy?** Do in-context examples change which layers omit facts, or do they just shift the omission rate uniformly?
- **Real-world validation**: Have these findings been validated with actual clinicians, lawyers, or compliance officers? Do operators actually intervene based on the attribution results?

## Key Takeaway
Small model omissions come from infrastructure, not the model—fix quantization and caching layers to improve reliability.
