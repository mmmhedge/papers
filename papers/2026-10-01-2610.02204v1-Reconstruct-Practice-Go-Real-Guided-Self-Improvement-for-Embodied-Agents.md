# Reconstruct, Practice, Go Real: Guided Self-Improvement for Embodied Agents

**Authors:** Yen-Jen Wang, Haozhe Jiang, Shuying Deng, Haoru Xue, Weirui Ye et al.
**Published:** 2026-10-01 | **arXiv:** [2610.02204v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02204v1)

## Summary
This paper presents RPG, a framework that enables robots to autonomously improve their manipulation skills without retraining neural networks. The system identifies what tasks a robot can do from offline data, creates practice scenarios in simulation, diagnoses failures, develops reusable skills, and gradually transfers them to real hardware—achieving 95% success on 22 tasks after iterative practice and 100% on physical deployment. This matters because it sidesteps the costly bottleneck of manual skill engineering and reward design in robotics.

## Theoretical Background
The work builds on several established ideas: offline reinforcement learning (learning from logged data without online interaction), skill libraries (reusable behavioral primitives), and the simulation-to-reality transfer problem. It also leverages multimodal large language models (LLMs)—neural networks trained on diverse text and image data that can reason about and coordinate complex tasks. The paper extends prior work on autonomous curriculum learning and self-correction in embodied agents by coupling error diagnosis with skill synthesis and system-prompt refinement rather than weight updates.

## Core Contributions
- A closed-loop improvement cycle that constructs practice tasks from offline data and uses execution feedback plus privileged simulator state to diagnose failure modes
- A method for automatically synthesizing symbolic skills and refining system prompts (LLM instructions) based on diagnosed issues, without modifying model weights
- Cross-task validation: candidate changes are tested individually and in combination before retention, reducing spurious improvements
- Empirical demonstration of sim-to-real transfer on a real robot (30 physical trials, 100% success on held-out tasks) without explicit hardware adaptation of the learning algorithm itself

## Connections to Other Work
This relates to [[RLHF]] and prompt engineering in LLMs—using structured feedback to improve behavior without retraining. It extends ideas from [[Skill Learning]] and curriculum learning by automating task construction. The work also connects to [[Sim-to-Real Transfer]] in robotics and the emerging paradigm of using LLMs as world models and task coordinators (e.g., [[ReAct]], code-as-policy approaches). Unlike purely gradient-based approaches, RPG operates in the symbolic/interpretability space, making it closer to [[mechanistic-interpretability]] applied to embodied agents.

## Practical Applications
- **Manufacturing assembly lines**: Robots could autonomously improve on complex multi-step tasks (e.g., gear assembly, electronics integration) after initial deployment without stopping production for retraining.
- **Warehouse automation**: Mobile manipulators could adapt to new shelf layouts and object types by practicing in simulation and refining skills without IT intervention.
- **Home robotics**: Service robots (e.g., for elderly care) could self-improve on household manipulation (dishwasher loading, laundry folding) as they encounter new object variations.
- **Surgical robotics**: Teleoperation systems could improve on suturing or knot-tying via offline data analysis and simulated practice, reducing surgeon cognitive load.
- **Satellite servicing**: On-orbit robots could autonomously refine docking and manipulation skills by leveraging logged mission data and simulator feedback without ground retraining cycles.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **RoboCoach**: A SaaS platform where manufacturers upload robot logs and hardware specs; the system automatically generates simulation environments, runs RPG-style improvement, and validates skills before deployment—sold per fleet or as a subscription.
2. **SimSkill Library**: A marketplace of pre-built skill repositories (pick-and-place, pushing, rotating, etc.) that robots can download and fine-tune to their specific morphology and environment using RPG's prompt-refinement mechanism.
3. **RobotOps Console**: Cloud-native ops dashboard for fleet managers to monitor robot performance, trigger autonomous improvement cycles, review synthesized skills, and approve updates—targeting large logistics operators.
4. **HardwareAgnostic Robotics API**: A wrapped interface that abstracts robot morphology and sensors; users write task specs in plain language, and RPG automatically develops and validates skills across different hardware platforms.
5. **Embodied AI Benchmarking Service**: Hosted platform for testing new robot policies and skills on hundreds of simulated task variants with standardized failure diagnostics, leaderboards, and skill-sharing—positioning for research and enterprise adoption.

## Technical Limitations
- **Simulation fidelity**: The method assumes the simulator captures sufficient realism for skills to transfer. Domains with high contact dynamics (e.g., fluid manipulation, deformable objects) may require more careful simulation tuning.
- **Offline data dependency**: Improvement quality depends on the quality and diversity of the initial offline dataset. Tasks not represented in the data are unlikely to be discovered.
- **Skill compositionality**: The paper doesn't deeply address whether synthesized skills compose reliably in novel sequences; performance may degrade with task complexity beyond the training distribution.
- **Multimodal LLM assumptions**: The method relies on a capable LLM for prompt generation and task coordination. Failure modes in LLM reasoning (hallucination, context limits) could propagate to robotics failures.
- **Scalability**: Testing 22 tasks and iterating 15 rounds requires significant simulation compute. Real-time improvement on hundreds of tasks simultaneously is unclear.

## Open Questions
- Can RPG improve on tasks with long horizons and sparse rewards, where failure diagnosis from trajectory logs is harder?
- How does performance scale with task dimensionality and the number of possible skills? Is there a phase transition?
- Can the skill library generalize to *unseen* tasks (zero-shot transfer), or does every new task require new practice rounds?
- What is the minimum amount of offline data needed? Is there a sweet spot, or does more data always help?
- How sensitive is the approach to the choice of LLM? Would it work with smaller, finetuned models, or does it require state-of-the-art LLMs?

## Key Takeaway
Robots can autonomously improve manipulation skills via simulation practice and LLM-driven prompt refinement, achieving real-world success without retraining weights.
