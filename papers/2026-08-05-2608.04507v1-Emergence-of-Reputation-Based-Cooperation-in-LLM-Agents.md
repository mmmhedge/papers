# Emergence of Reputation-Based Cooperation in LLM Agents

**Authors:** Kazuya Horibe, Kenji Itao, Wataru Toyokawa
**Published:** 2026-08-05 | **arXiv:** [2608.04507v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04507v1)

## Summary
Can cooperation among large language model (LLM) agents be evolutionarily stable against free-rider invasion? We study an indirect reciprocity donation game where LLM agents observe behavioral traces and donate on a continuous scale. Strategies, represented as natural language prompts, evolve through cultural transmission across generations.

## Theoretical Background
Multi-agent systems study how autonomous participants coordinate, compete, and form collective outcomes. Their behavior can depend on interaction structure and feedback dynamics rather than the capability of any single agent.

The paper is positioned against prior approaches described in its abstract and asks whether a more targeted representation, evaluation method, or optimization procedure can improve the relevant trade-off.

## Core Contributions
1. Strategies, represented as natural language prompts, evolve through cultural transmission across generations.
2. These findings reveal that LLM agents are confined to Image Scoring-like discrimination and fail to develop the more robust Leading-Eight norms, highlighting a fundamental vulnerability in culturally evolved LLM cooperation and motivating bottom-up approaches to norm construction.
