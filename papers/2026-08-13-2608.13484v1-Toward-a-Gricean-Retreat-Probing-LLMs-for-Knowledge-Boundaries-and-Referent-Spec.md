# Toward a Gricean Retreat: Probing LLMs for Knowledge Boundaries and Referent Specificity

**Authors:** Dananjay Srinivas, Saksham Khatwani, Maria Pacheco
**Published:** 2026-08-13 | **arXiv:** [2608.13484v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13484v1)

## Summary
This paper investigates why large language models (LLMs) fabricate plausible-sounding details about unfamiliar entities instead of admitting uncertainty or giving more general answers. The authors use Gricean pragmatics—a linguistic theory about cooperative communication—as a lens to study this problem, finding that models *internally encode* knowledge boundaries and can predict specificity levels, but fail to use this knowledge during generation. The work bridges interpretability and alignment by showing the mechanisms exist for honest, uncertainty-aware responses; the training objective simply doesn't incentivize them.

## Theoretical Background
The paper draws on two core traditions. First, **Gricean maxims** from philosophy of language describe how cooperative speakers balance informativeness against truthfulness—when uncertain about a referent, they "retreat" from specific to general claims (e.g., "a large dog" instead of "a German Shepherd" if unsure). Second, mechanistic interpretability research investigates what information is encoded in LLM activations and how it flows through layers. The authors combine these by asking whether models encode knowledge uncertainty and specificity signals the way a rational speaker would, and whether generation respects those signals.

## Core Contributions
1. **Diagnostic benchmark**: Constructs a T-REx-based dataset varying entity familiarity and referent specificity to systematically probe models.
2. **Dual-signal finding**: Demonstrates that model activations *do* encode (i) knowledge boundaries (whether an entity is in-distribution) and (ii) anticipated referent specificity, but these signals remain decoupled from generation behavior.
3. **Specificity bias**: Shows models overwhelmingly generate specific referents even for unknown entities, and ignore correct generic alternatives—revealing a misalignment between internal knowledge and output policy.
4. **Gricean alignment framework**: Proposes training or steering objectives that couple knowledge-boundary awareness to generation specificity, framing this as an alignment problem.

## Connections to Other Work
This work sits at the intersection of several research threads:
- **[[Mechanistic Interpretability]]**: Similar to probing studies (e.g., work on whether models encode factual knowledge in activations), but with a pragmatic twist—asking not just *what* is encoded but *how it should be used*.
- **[[Alignment]]**: Related to work on truthfulness and hallucination reduction (e.g., [[RLHF]], constitutional AI), but via an interpretability pathway rather than reward-based approaches.
- **Linguistic grounding**: Echoes work on reference and semantic underspecification in NLP, but applies it to a core failure mode of LLMs.
- **Knowledge boundaries**: Connects to recent work on measuring and improving model calibration and out-of-distribution robustness.

## Practical Applications
1. **Calibrated chatbots**: Deploy LLMs that signal uncertainty explicitly (e.g., "I'm not sure, but a large dog..." vs. fabricating breed details).
2. **Fact-checking and search**: Improve search engines and QA systems to downgrade high-confidence claims about unknown entities.
3. **Domain-specific AI**: In high-stakes domains (legal, medical, financial), steer models toward safe generalizations when knowledge is incomplete.
4. **User trust**: Build interfaces that display confidence alongside specificity, helping humans calibrate trust in model outputs.
5. **Knowledge curation**: Identify weak spots in a model's knowledge base by observing when it retreats to generics.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **Uncertainty-aware QA API**: A querying service that labels responses with specificity levels and knowledge-boundary confidence, helping enterprises filter unreliable claims.
2. **Gricean alignment consultancy**: Tools to audit and fine-tune LLMs to implement pragmatic retreats, targeting enterprise deployments.
3. **Fact-grading for customer support**: A layer that intercepts LLM responses to support tickets, downgrades specificity when entity knowledge is weak, reducing false claims to customers.
4. **Semantic calibration dashboard**: Analytics product showing real-time specificity vs. confidence heatmaps for deployed LLMs, surfacing hallucination risks.
5. **Pragmatic fine-tuning service**: Provide off-the-shelf LoRA or instruction-tuning recipes that teach models to retreat when uncertain, as a plug-in for existing LLM stacks.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Probing vs. causation**: The paper shows that knowledge-boundary and specificity signals *exist* in activations but does not prove they *causally drive* generation or can be reliably extracted at inference time.
2. **Benchmark scope**: T-REx-based evaluation is limited to named entities and factual relations; unclear whether findings generalize to abstract concepts, numerical knowledge, or commonsense reasoning.
3. **Model coverage**: Tested on a limited set of models; behavior may differ across architecture families (e.g., encoder-only, decoder-only, multimodal).
4. **Policy optimization**: No concrete algorithm provided for training models to actually *implement* a Gricean retreat. The paper diagnoses the problem but leaves the solution open.
5. **User study absence**: No evaluation of whether humans actually *trust* retreated responses more, or whether this behavior is pragmatically effective in practice.

## Open Questions
1. **Causal steering**: Can we steer generation toward Gricean behavior via prompting, in-context learning, or lightweight fine-tuning? What's the cost?
2. **Cross-lingual generality**: Do other languages exhibit the same specificity bias, or does Gricean pragmatics differ across cultures?
3. **Emergent deference**: Do larger or better-trained models naturally develop knowledge-aware retreats, or is it fundamentally a training objective problem?
4. **Compositionality**: When uncertainty is about parts of an entity (e.g., uncertain about a person's birthplace but not their profession), can models retreat selectively?
5. **Downstream utility**: Does Gricean alignment improve performance on downstream tasks (e.g., search ranking, QA accuracy, user satisfaction) or only reduce fabrication?

## Key Takeaway
LLMs encode knowledge uncertainty internally but generate confident specifics anyway—a solvable alignment problem, not a fundamental limitation.
