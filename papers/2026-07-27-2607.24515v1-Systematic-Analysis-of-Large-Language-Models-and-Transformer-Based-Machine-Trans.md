# Systematic Analysis of Large Language Models and Transformer-Based Machine Translation for English-Tamil and Tamil-English Across Diverse Datasets

**Authors:** Sriharshaa S, Sangeetha Sivanesan
**Published:** 2026-07-27 | **arXiv:** [2607.24515v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24515v1)

## Summary
This paper evaluates how well state-of-the-art neural translation models handle English-Tamil translation—a genuinely hard problem because Tamil has limited training data, complex grammar, and varied domains. The authors test supervised models (NLLB, mBART) and few-shot prompting with a large language model (TamilLaMA) across four datasets, using both automatic metrics and attention visualization to understand what the models learn and where they struggle.

## Theoretical Background
Machine translation has evolved from rule-based systems to neural machine translation (NMT)—networks trained end-to-end on paired sentences in two languages. Modern approaches use [[Attention Is All You Need|transformers]], which allow models to focus on relevant parts of input text. For low-resource languages like Tamil, three challenges compound: sparse parallel training data, morphological complexity (Tamil has rich inflectional structure), and domain variation (medical texts differ sharply from news). Recent multilingual models like NLLB and mBART — trained on hundreds of language pairs simultaneously — attempt to transfer knowledge across languages, but their effectiveness on morphologically rich, data-scarce pairs remains uncertain.

## Core Contributions
1. **Systematic benchmark** across four diverse, quality-controlled datasets (NTREX, EnTamV2, WikiMatrix, PMIndia) for English-Tamil, explicitly testing domain and data-quality effects.
2. **Attention visualization and interpretability** — detailed token-alignment analysis showing *how* transformers route information between English and Tamil, surfacing model reasoning.
3. **Few-shot LLM translation** — demonstration that in-context prompting with TamilLaMA can produce coherent Tamil translations without fine-tuning, compared qualitatively against supervised baselines.
4. **Dataset quality impact analysis** — empirical evidence that dataset alignment to domain and quality dramatically affects BLEU/chrF scores, not just model architecture.

## Connections to Other Work
This work sits at the intersection of [[mechanistic-interpretability]] (via attention analysis) and practical [[nlp]] for underserved languages. It echoes debates around [[RLHF]] and few-shot adaptation in large models, asking whether scale and in-context learning can overcome data scarcity. Related to multilingual work like mT5 and evaluation frameworks like chrF, but uniquely focuses on a morphologically complex, genuinely low-resource pair and prioritizes interpretability alongside metrics.

## Practical Applications
- **Community translation** — enabling Tamil content creators to auto-draft translations with LLM prompting, then human-in-the-loop refinement.
- **Cross-language healthcare/legal services** — hospitals and courts in Tamil Nadu needing reliable English↔Tamil bridging; data quality findings inform which model to deploy per domain.
- **Educational content localization** — Indian edtech platforms translating English courses into Tamil at scale.
- **Accessibility in government** — Tamil Nadu state services needing bidirectional translation for citizen documents.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **TamilLaMA Translation API** — fine-tuned, domain-specific LLM endpoints (medical, legal, news) with transparent attention visualizations for quality auditing; charge per token or API call.
2. **Dataset Curator for Tamil NLP** — SaaS tool that scores dataset quality and domain fit, then recommends which model/fine-tuning strategy to use; sell to enterprises and research labs.
3. **Human-in-the-Loop Translation Platform** — LLM generates draft, attention maps highlight risky alignments, human translators fix only flagged sections; cut labor cost 40–60% vs. manual translation.

## Technical Limitations
- **BLEU/chrF limitations** — automatic metrics are noisy, especially for morphologically rich languages where many valid translations exist; human evaluation would strengthen claims.
- **Interpretability boundary** — attention visualization shows correlations, not causal mechanisms; doesn't explain *why* a model aligns tokens wrongly or recovers from errors.
- **Few-shot comparison** — qualitative assessment of TamilLaMA outputs is subjective; no side-by-side human evaluation against supervised models.
- **Domain generalization** — testing on four datasets is useful but narrow; Tamil has many subdomains (poetry, colloquial speech, technical jargon) not fully explored.
- **Scalability unknown** — unclear how findings scale to other low-resource + morphologically complex pairs (e.g., Telugu, Kannada, Marathi); results may be Tamil-specific.
- **Fine-tuning gap** — paper doesn't explore fine-tuning NLLB or mBART on in-domain data, which might close performance gaps and would be more practical.

## Open Questions
1. **Can attention alignment predict translation errors?** Train a classifier on attention patterns to detect risky spans before they reach the output; would enable automated quality control.
2. **Does in-context prompting scale to longer documents?** Few-shot works on sentences; what about multi-sentence context, anaphora resolution, discourse coherence?
3. **How do we measure morphological fidelity?** BLEU doesn't capture inflection or agreement errors; design metrics specific to Tamil grammar.
4. **Which dataset characteristics matter most?** Ablation study: vary parallel-pair count, domain purity, annotation quality; rank their impact on model performance.
5. **Can we synthesize or augment Tamil data?** Back-translation or paraphrase generation to grow training sets; does synthetic data help LLMs and supervised models equally?
6. **Cross-lingual transfer from related languages?** Tamil ↔ Telugu or Kannada; can a single multilingual model share representations across Dravidian languages?

## Key Takeaway
Transformer models and few-shot LLMs handle Tamil translation reasonably, but dataset quality and domain alignment matter as much as model size.
