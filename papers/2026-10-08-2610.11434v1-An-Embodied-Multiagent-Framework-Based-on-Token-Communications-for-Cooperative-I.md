# An Embodied Multiagent Framework Based on Token Communications for Cooperative ISAC

**Authors:** Jiahe Guo, Jun Du, Chunxiao Jiang, Jintao Wang
**Published:** 2026-10-08 | **arXiv:** [2610.11434v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11434v1)

## Summary
This paper presents a framework for coordinating multiple UAV (unmanned aerial vehicle) agents in integrated sensing and communication (ISAC) networks using efficient token-based communication. The key innovation is a "state-intent token communication" (SI-TokCom) system where agents exchange compressed messages rather than raw sensor data, allowing them to cooperate on sensing and communication tasks while minimizing energy use. The work matters because it addresses a practical bottleneck in autonomous systems: how to enable fast, coordinated decision-making across many agents without overwhelming communication overhead.

## Theoretical Background
This work combines three established research areas. **Multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL)** — where multiple agents learn policies simultaneously to maximize a shared or individual reward — provides the learning framework. **Integrated sensing and communication (ISAC)** is an emerging paradigm that unifies radar/sensing and wireless communication into a single system, rather than treating them as separate functions. **Token-based communication** draws inspiration from recent advances in discrete representation learning, where agents exchange learned discrete symbols (tokens) from a codebook rather than continuous values, reducing bandwidth. The paper frames the coordination problem as a joint optimization: agents must select tokens to communicate intent and state, while also choosing physical actions (flight trajectory, power levels) to meet sensing and communication fidelity targets under energy constraints.

## Core Contributions
1. **SI-TokCom framework**: A novel approach pairing separate pretrained codebooks for state and intent, allowing agents to compose multi-token messages efficiently rather than using a single monolithic codebook.
2. **Joint embodied learning**: Agents learn token selection and physical control simultaneously through policy gradient methods, linking communication decisions directly to sensing/communication outcomes and energy costs.
3. **Empirical validation**: Demonstrates that token-based cooperation achieves 98.9–99.1% of centralized performance (which has perfect information) while improving over fully decentralized baselines by 5–43.6% on key metrics, without additional propulsion cost.

## Connections to Other Work
This research sits at the intersection of [[multi-agent-systems]] and [[reinforcement-learning]]. It relates to **communication-efficient MARL** (e.g., learned quantization, emergent communication protocols) by using discrete tokens instead of continuous messages. The approach extends **embodied AI** — where agents couple perception and action — by treating communication as part of the embodied loop. It also connects to **ISAC system design**, a growing area in signal processing and wireless networks, by optimizing communication-sensing tradeoffs at the coordination layer rather than only at the signal level. The use of pretrained, composable codebooks echoes techniques from vector quantization and discrete autoencoders.

## Practical Applications
1. **Disaster response networks**: Multiple UAVs equipped with cameras and radars coordinate real-time search and rescue, using token comms to share "person detected at location X" and "I'm moving to sector Y" without saturating bandwidth.
2. **Smart city monitoring**: Fleets of inspection drones survey infrastructure (power lines, bridges) while maintaining contact with ground stations, using energy-efficient token messages to report findings and request tasking.
3. **Autonomous warehouse swarms**: Delivery or inventory drones navigate shared airspace, exchanging tokens like "obstacle at coordinate Z" and "I'm proceeding northbound" to avoid collisions and coordinate handoffs.
4. **Environmental sensing**: Networked aerial sensors monitoring wildfires, pollution, or weather coordinate adaptive flight paths using minimal-bandwidth token exchanges to maximize coverage and detection range.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Codebook pre-training bottleneck**: The approach requires offline codebook generation before deployment. Unclear how codebooks generalize to novel environments, agent counts, or communication constraints not seen during training.
2. **Scalability not addressed**: Experiments likely involve small numbers of agents; unclear whether token communication overhead (selection, composition logic) remains efficient at 10s or 100s of UAVs.
3. **Assumption of synchronous communication**: The paper does not discuss asynchronous message arrival, packet loss, or latency jitter—realistic issues in wireless systems.
4. **Limited sensing fidelity metric**: "Sensing rate" is not clearly defined; unclear whether the framework handles sensor degradation (e.g., occlusion, rain) or sensor fusion across heterogeneous modalities.
5. **Lack of adversarial robustness**: No discussion of Byzantine agents, eavesdropping, or injection attacks on token communication, important for real deployments.
6. **Energy model oversimplification**: Propulsion energy is modeled, but communication energy (transmit power, receiver operation) is implicit in rate constraints; total system energy may be underestimated.

## Open Questions
1. **Generalization and transfer**: Can codebooks trained on one UAV swarm size or environment transfer to new scales or settings? What is the sample complexity of fine-tuning?
2. **Robustness to communication failures**: How does SI-TokCom degrade under packet loss, delay, or adversarial token corruption? Is there a graceful fallback?
3. **Optimal codebook structure**: Is the two-codebook design (state + intent) optimal, or would a hierarchical or task-specific codebook structure be better? How sensitive is performance to codebook size?
4. **Scalability limits**: At what swarm size does per-agent communication overhead become prohibitive? Can graph neural networks or attention mechanisms improve scalability?
5. **Multi-task adaptation**: Can a single trained policy and codebook pair handle diverse mission types (surveillance, delivery, search) without retraining?
6. **Human-in-the-loop**: How can operators inject high-level goals or constraints into a learned policy without full retraining?

## Key Takeaway
UAV swarms can coordinate efficiently by exchanging learned discrete "tokens" instead of raw sensor data, achieving near-centralized performance with minimal energy overhead.
