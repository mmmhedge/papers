# Bilevel optimization for data-driven learning of Koopman embeddings using kernel-based autoencoders

**Authors:** Joel-Pascal Ntwali N'konzi, Feliks Nüske, Stefan Klus
**Published:** 2026-10-08 | **arXiv:** [2610.12370v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12370v1)

## Summary
This paper presents EDMD-kDL, a method for learning compact representations of nonlinear dynamical systems by combining kernel methods with bilevel optimization. Instead of using neural networks (which are opaque and slow to scale), the authors use interpretable kernel dictionaries to approximate Koopman operators—mathematical tools for linearizing nonlinear systems. The work shows comparable or better performance than neural approaches while remaining computationally tractable on large datasets.

## Theoretical Background
**Koopman operator theory** provides a way to analyze nonlinear dynamical systems by finding a change of variables that makes the dynamics linear. The challenge is that true Koopman operators are infinite-dimensional; in practice, finite-dimensional approximations are needed.

**Extended Dynamic Mode Decomposition (EDMD)** is a popular data-driven method for computing these approximations, but it requires specifying a dictionary (basis functions) beforehand—a significant limitation since the "right" dictionary is problem-dependent.

Recent work has used **neural network autoencoders** to learn dictionaries directly from data, but these are black boxes. The authors instead leverage **kernel methods**—a classical machine learning approach that defines similarity between data points using kernel functions. Kernel methods are more interpretable and theoretically tractable, though historically they scale poorly.

**Bilevel optimization** is a nested optimization framework where an inner problem (learning Koopman coefficients) depends on outer problem parameters (the kernel dictionary). This structure allows joint learning without specifying the dictionary in advance.

## Core Contributions
1. **EDMD-kDL**: A novel kernel-based method that learns both the Koopman embedding dictionary and the linear Koopman operator simultaneously via bilevel optimization, avoiding the need to specify the dictionary a priori.

2. **Scalability fix**: By making the kernel matrix size depend on the number of collocation points (chosen flexibly) rather than dataset size, the method scales to large datasets—overcoming the historical weakness of kernel methods.

3. **Empirical validation**: Demonstrated competitive or superior performance compared to neural autoencoder baselines (e.g., on sea-surface-temperature forecasting and video-based learning) while retaining interpretability.

4. **Theoretical grounding**: Combines collocation methods (from numerical PDE solving) with modern bilevel optimization, creating a principled bridge between classical and contemporary approaches.

## Connections to Other Work
This work sits at the intersection of several literatures:

- **Koopman operator methods**: Extends EDMD by automating dictionary selection, addressing a long-standing pain point in the field.
- **Kernel methods**: Revives kernels for a modern problem where neural networks have dominated, showing kernels can be competitive with design.
- **Autoencoder-based discovery**: Related to [[generative-models]] and [[deep-learning]] approaches that learn representations, but trades some expressiveness for interpretability and theoretical guarantees.
- **Bilevel optimization**: Draws from meta-learning and hyperparameter optimization literature, applying it to a new domain.
- **Dynamical systems identification**: Connects to broader work on learning models from time-series and video data.

## Practical Applications
1. **Climate and weather forecasting**: Sea-surface-temperature and broader climate modeling where interpretability and computational efficiency matter for operational systems.

2. **Video-based dynamics**: Learning from raw video (demonstrated in experiments)—useful for robotics, surveillance, and motion prediction without hand-crafted features.

3. **Control systems design**: Engineers need interpretable models of nonlinear plant dynamics; kernel methods provide explainability that neural approaches lack.

4. **Scientific discovery**: In physics and chemistry, discovering governing equations of dynamical systems where the functional form is unknown; kernel dictionaries are more analyzable than neural network weights.

5. **Real-time embedded systems**: Where computational budgets are tight and neural networks are impractical; kernel evaluations are cheaper than deep network inference.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **Interpretable time-series forecasting SaaS**: A platform for industrial users (energy, manufacturing, supply chain) to forecast complex dynamics with explainable models, marketed as "transparent AI for critical systems."

2. **Climate-tech modeling software**: White-label tool for climate scientists and environmental agencies to accelerate sea-level, weather, and ecosystem forecasting with fewer compute resources than current deep-learning approaches.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Kernel selection remains manual**: While the method learns the dictionary weights, the choice of kernel type (RBF, polynomial, etc.) and hyperparameters still requires domain knowledge or cross-validation overhead.

2. **Limited to smooth dynamics**: Koopman theory assumes the underlying system is smooth; highly discontinuous or chaotic regimes may not be well-approximated by finite-dimensional embeddings.

3. **Collocation point placement**: Performance depends on choosing collocation points; the paper doesn't fully address optimal placement strategies or sensitivity to this choice.

4. **Scalability still modest**: While better than standard kernel methods, the bilevel optimization adds computational cost; may still struggle compared to neural methods on datasets with millions of points.

5. **Theoretical guarantees are weak**: No finite-sample complexity bounds or approximation error guarantees are provided; empirical validation is strong but theory lags.

6. **Interpretability claims need validation**: While kernels are "more interpretable" in principle, the learned dictionary remains abstract; truly actionable insights for practitioners are unclear.

## Open Questions
1. **Optimal collocation strategy**: How should collocation points be selected? Could adaptive or learned point selection improve performance further?

2. **Kernel design principles**: Are there systematic ways to choose or learn the kernel family itself (not just weights), rather than fixing it a priori?

3. **Theoretical analysis**: Can one derive generalization bounds or approximation error guarantees for EDMD-kDL under standard assumptions?

4. **Long-horizon forecasting**: Do kernel-based embeddings maintain accuracy over longer prediction horizons compared to neural methods, or do errors accumulate similarly?

5. **Scalability limits**: At what dataset size does the bilevel optimization become prohibitive? How does performance degrade with dataset size?

6. **Comparison fairness**: Were the neural baselines equally tuned and regularized? Could modern neural approaches (e.g., with residual connections or attention) close the gap?

## Key Takeaway
Kernel-based Koopman learning scales to large datasets and rivals neural methods while remaining interpretable—a rare win for classical machine learning in the deep-learning era.
