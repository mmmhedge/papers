# CardioState-JEPA: Delay-Aware Cross-Modal Learning of a Shared Cardiac Representation

**Authors:** Hamza Shafiq, Hung Manh Pham, Bin Zhu, Pan Zhou, Jun Hu et al.
**Published:** 2026-08-13 | **arXiv:** [2608.12944v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12944v1)

## Summary
This paper introduces CardioState-JEPA, a foundation model that learns a single shared representation from three different cardiac sensors—ECG (electrical), PPG (blood flow), and PCG (heart sounds)—by training them jointly rather than separately. The key innovation is handling the natural time delays between these signals and exploiting the fact that they measure the same underlying cardiac cycle, which improves performance on downstream tasks across all three modalities by 8–19 percentage points over single-modality baselines.

## Theoretical Background
The paper builds on several established ideas. **Foundation models** are large self-supervised models trained on diverse data to create general-purpose representations that transfer to many tasks. **Self-supervised learning** (learning from unlabeled data without explicit labels) is the training paradigm used here. The model architecture is based on **JEPA** (Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture), which learns by predicting masked or hidden parts of input data rather than reconstructing raw pixels or waveforms. The paper extends this with **cross-modal learning**—training a single model on multiple different input types (ECG, PPG, PCG signals)—and introduces a **delay aligner**, a learned module that accounts for temporal offsets between signals that measure different physiological events at slightly different times.

## Core Contributions
1. **First cardiac foundation model** spanning three modalities jointly (ECG, PPG, PCG), rather than training separate single-modality models.
2. **Delay-aware cross-modal alignment**: A learned mechanism that explicitly handles temporal offsets between electrical, mechanical, and hemodynamic cardiac events, enabling meaningful cross-modal prediction.
3. **Two-stage pretraining strategy**: Within-modality structure learned from abundant unimodal data first, then modalities aligned using scarce paired recordings—a practical solution to data scarcity.
4. **Substantial empirical improvements**: 8.2–18.8 AUROC points over self-supervised baselines and competitive performance against supervised and privileged-label approaches on 25 downstream tasks.

## Connections to Other Work
This work relates to broader trends in [[self-supervised learning]] and [[foundation models]] for specialized domains. It extends [[JEPA]] principles (as in vision models) to multimodal medical signal processing. The delay alignment mechanism is conceptually similar to temporal synchronization problems in [[multi-modal fusion]] but tailored to physiology. The paper sits at the intersection of [[generative-models]] (pretraining paradigm) and [[computer-vision]]-inspired architectures applied to biomedical signals, contrasting with earlier [[RLHF]]-style or supervised approaches to medical AI.

## Practical Applications
1. **Wearable health monitoring**: Smartwatches and fitness bands capture PPG; integrating learned cardiac representations could improve arrhythmia or stress detection with minimal extra computation.
2. **Hospital decision support**: ECG, PPG, and PCG are routine in clinical settings; a shared representation enables robust multimodal risk stratification even when one or two sensors fail or are unavailable.
3. **Telehealth and remote cardiac screening**: Transfer learning from the foundation model enables accurate cardiac disease detection (murmurs, arrhythmias, heart failure) from single-modality data collected at home.
4. **Cardiac event prediction**: The learned latent states might capture pre-event physiological signatures across modalities, enabling early warning systems.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **Cardiac risk dashboard for primary care**: A web app that ingests patient ECG/PPG/PCG recordings and outputs unified risk scores for common conditions (AFib, murmurs, HF) using frozen CardioState-JEPA embeddings, with explainability via latent state analysis.
2. **Wearable-to-clinic integration platform**: Sync continuous PPG data from consumer wearables with occasional clinical ECG/PCG, unifying predictions across modalities to flag patients who need intervention—charge as SaaS to health systems.
3. **Cardiac AI model-as-a-service**: Offer fine-tuned versions of CardioState-JEPA for specific subpopulations (pediatric, post-surgical, athletes) via API; partner with EHR vendors for embedded integration.
4. **Self-supervised medical imaging labeling tool**: Adapt the delay-aware cross-modal learning to other multi-modality medical data (e.g., ECG + echocardiogram, CT + clinical text) to reduce annotation burden in label-scarce settings.
5. **Smart patch manufacturing**: Develop a multi-sensor wearable patch (ECG + PPG + acoustic sensors) whose data is processed in real-time using the foundation model for continuous, ultra-low-power cardiac monitoring.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Data scarcity for multimodal alignment**: The model still relies on paired (synchronized) recordings to learn the delay alignment; scarcity of such data limits generalization to new populations or sensor combinations.
2. **Temporal offset assumptions**: The delay aligner assumes shifts are static or slowly varying within a recording; dynamic changes in conduction velocity or sensor synchronization drift could break this assumption.
3. **Sensor-specific noise**: Each modality has different noise profiles (60 Hz electrical hum in ECG, motion artifacts in PPG, ambient noise in PCG); the model's robustness to extreme noise or hardware failures is not explored.
4. **Clinical validation gap**: Evaluation is on research benchmarks; real-world deployment would require prospective validation in clinical populations (different age, disease, and demographic groups).
5. **Computational cost in deployment**: No details on inference latency or memory footprint for edge devices; the Transformer encoder may be too large for many wearables.
6. **Interpretability**: Frozen embeddings are powerful but opaque; clinicians may struggle to trust predictions without understanding what cardiac features the latent states encode.

## Open Questions
1. How well does the delay aligner generalize to sensors from different manufacturers or to pathological hearts with abnormal conduction?
2. Can the model learn without paired data by leveraging domain knowledge (e.g., known relationships between QRS and dicrotic notch timing) to initialize alignment?
3. What happens when one modality is noisy or missing at test time—does the representation degrade gracefully?
4. Could uncertainty estimation (Bayesian or ensemble approaches) improve clinical adoption by flagging low-confidence predictions?
5. How do the learned latent cardiac states correlate with specific electrophysiological or hemodynamic parameters that cardiologists care about?
6. Does the model transfer to rare conditions (e.g., specific arrhythmias, congenital heart disease) seen rarely in pretraining?

## Key Takeaway
A shared cardiac foundation model trained jointly on ECG, PPG, and PCG with learned time alignment substantially improves detection across all three modalities.
