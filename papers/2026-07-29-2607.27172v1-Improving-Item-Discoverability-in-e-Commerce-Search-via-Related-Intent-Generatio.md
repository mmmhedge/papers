# Improving Item Discoverability in e-Commerce Search via Related Intent Generation

**Authors:** Ji Xin, Xiao Xiao, Ishan Bhatt, Vinesh Gudla, Trace Levinson et al.
**Published:** 2026-07-29 | **arXiv:** [2607.27172v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27172v1)

## Summary
This paper presents a system that improves product discoverability in e-commerce search by generating related user intents to expand what items get shown to customers. Instead of just returning exact matches to a query, the system uses large language models to infer what else a shopper might want (substitutes, complements, related items) and retrieves those too — all while keeping costs manageable by using smaller, fine-tuned models for common queries.

## Theoretical Background
E-commerce search traditionally prioritizes precision (showing only highly relevant items) over recall (showing a broader range of potentially useful items). This work builds on [[Attention Is All You Need]]-style language models and distillation techniques. The key insight is that user intent — the underlying goal behind a search query — can be modeled implicitly. The paper leverages closed-weight LLMs (proprietary models like Claude or GPT) for high-quality intent generation, then distills that knowledge into smaller models via LoRA adapters (lightweight parameter updates) and teacher-student distillation (a technique where a large model teaches a small one by matching its outputs). The system is grounded in information retrieval principles but applies modern generative modeling rather than traditional ranking.

## Core Contributions
1. **Intent-conditioned recall expansion**: A two-stage hybrid architecture that generates implicit user intents to broaden retrieval candidates while maintaining relevance — a departure from strict query matching.
2. **Cost-quality tradeoff solution**: Uses expensive LLMs for head (popular) queries and distilled small language models for tail (rare) queries, reducing inference cost by ~70% while extending discovery coverage from 60% to 80%.
3. **Dual evaluation framework**: Combines LLM-as-a-judge metrics validated against human preferences with end-to-end session-level purchase metrics — moving beyond abstract relevance to actual commercial outcomes.
4. **Marketplace balancing mechanism**: Shows that discovery-augmented search can surface long-tail and emerging products, not just popular items.

## Connections to Other Work
This extends [[RLHF]] and instruction-tuning paradigms by applying them to information retrieval rather than chat. It relates to work on [[Retrieval-Augmented Generation]] (RAG) and hybrid search systems that blend neural and symbolic approaches. The use of LoRA and distillation connects to efficient fine-tuning literature. The emphasis on recall and substitution/complementarity discovery contrasts with precision-focused ranking systems like learning-to-rank (LTR) and traditional collaborative filtering, though it's complementary — the system likely feeds into existing ranking pipelines downstream.

## Practical Applications
1. **Grocery e-commerce**: When a shopper searches "milk," surface butter, cereals, and coffee — increasing basket size.
2. **Cross-category discovery**: A customer searching for "running shoes" is shown related items like athletic socks, moisture-wicking shirts, and fitness trackers.
3. **Out-of-stock substitution**: When an item is unavailable, the system can suggest semantically and functionally similar alternatives in real time.
4. **Seasonal and emerging product surfacing**: New brands or seasonal items get visibility by being suggested as related to popular queries.
5. **Mobile and voice search**: Smaller models enable on-device intent generation for latency-sensitive scenarios.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **"RelatedIntent" API**: Sell intent generation and recall expansion as a plug-in service for small-to-mid-size e-commerce platforms (Shopify, WooCommerce). Monthly usage-based pricing.
2. **Marketplace rebalancer SaaS**: A tool that helps sellers of long-tail products automatically optimize their listings and metadata to get discovered through related-intent queries.
3. **Category intelligence for CPG brands**: Software that tells consumer brands (food, beauty, etc.) which product categories and intents are commonly associated with theirs — informing product placement and bundling.
4. **Intent-driven personalization layer**: A real-time personalization middleware that wraps any existing search engine (Elasticsearch, Algolia, etc.) and layers on intent-based discovery without replacing the backend.
5. **Vertical search engine for groceries/convenience**: A specialized search engine optimized for produce and consumables that uses this intent model as its core (competing with Google Shopping or Amazon Fresh search quality).

## Technical Limitations
1. **Intent hallucination**: The LLM may generate intents that are creative but commercially irrelevant or misleading (e.g., suggesting "antacids" when someone searches for "spicy chips").
2. **Query ambiguity**: Intent inference is fundamentally ambiguous. A search for "Python" could mean the programming language, the snake, or a TV show.
3. **Cold start for new products**: Without sufficient behavioral or textual data, the system cannot reliably generate intents for emerging or niche items.
4. **Regional and cultural variation**: Intents vary by geography and culture; a one-size-fits-all model may not generalize across markets.
5. **Inference latency at scale**: Even small models add latency; the paper doesn't discuss response time guarantees or tail latency, critical for real-time search.
6. **Training data bias**: The intent model inherits biases from its training data and the teacher LLM, potentially surfacing only "mainstream" related items.
7. **Evaluation gaps**: LLM-as-judge metrics are known to be imperfect; human preference agreement and actual purchase lift are not fully disentangled.

## Open Questions
1. How does the system perform on extremely ambiguous or polysemous queries (e.g., "bank," "Java")? Is there a principled way to handle multiple intents per query?
2. Can the distilled small model match the teacher's performance on truly novel or out-of-distribution queries, or does performance degrade sharply?
3. What is the true end-to-end latency (p99) in production? Does adding intent generation break SLA targets for high-traffic marketplaces?
4. How does discovery-augmented search interact with ranking and personalization downstream? Is there cannibalization of high-margin or profitable items?
5. Can the approach be extended to multimodal queries (image + text) or voice, and does intent generation remain reliable?
6. What happens when intent generation is adversarially gamed (e.g., sellers optimizing for intent generation rather than genuine user value)?

## Key Takeaway
LLM-distilled intent generation expands product discovery in search from 60% to 80% of queries at lower cost, helping niche products surface via related suggestions.
