# PPO-HRAP: Proximal Policy Optimization with a Hybrid Regime-Aware Policy for Risk-Controlled Trading

**Authors:** Duong Hien Chi Kien, Thanh Trung Huynh
**Published:** 2026-10-01 | **arXiv:** [2610.01325v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01325v1)

## Summary
This paper proposes PPO-HRAP, a reinforcement learning method that teaches trading agents to balance profit-seeking with drawdown control by blending learned policies with market regime information. The method achieves strong risk-adjusted returns (0.64 Sharpe ratio) on S&P 500 futures while cutting maximum drawdown nearly in half compared to buy-and-hold, addressing a core challenge in RL for finance: most profit-focused agents either become overly passive or take excessive risks during volatility spikes.

## Theoretical Background
The paper builds on [[Proximal Policy Optimization]] (PPO), a popular reinforcement learning algorithm that updates policies conservatively to avoid training instability. In trading, RL agents learn to map market observations to portfolio actions (e.g., cash allocation), but naive reward functions often fail: maximizing return alone creates procyclical leverage (agents add exposure when markets rise), while heavy risk penalties produce defensive over-hedging. The authors combine PPO with regime-aware priors — using volatility (VIX) and market state to set target portfolio exposures that guide the learned policy. The reward function integrates portfolio log returns, VIX-conditioned drawdown penalties, deviation from target exposure, and turnover costs.

## Core Contributions
1. **Hybrid policy mechanism**: Rather than pure RL or pure rules, PPO-HRAP blends the PPO actor's output with a regime-derived target exposure, allowing learned behavior while anchoring to interpretable volatility-aware targets.
2. **Empirically robust regime conditioning**: The drawdown penalty scales with VIX (volatility index), so the agent adapts risk aversion to market conditions instead of using a fixed penalty weight.
3. **Comprehensive empirical validation**: Demonstrates stability across five random seeds on 2020–2022 SPY data, with detailed risk metrics (Sortino, Calmar ratios, maximum drawdown) rather than return alone.
4. **Cross-asset evidence**: Shows consistent performance on QQQ and DIA, though with limited multi-seed validation.

## Connections to Other Work
This work sits at the intersection of [[reinforcement-learning|RL]] and [[quantitative-finance|quantitative finance]]. It addresses known failure modes of pure RL trading agents documented in prior work (e.g., leverage spirals, regime-blind policies). The regime-aware prior relates to classical risk management (dynamic portfolio insurance, VIX-triggered hedging) but learned jointly via RL. Unlike [[RLHF]] (which aligns language models to human preferences), this approach aligns trading agents to risk objectives via structured reward design and policy blending. The blended action idea is less common in RL but echoes constrained MDPs and hybrid control approaches.

## Practical Applications
1. **Discretionary fund management**: Hedge funds and asset managers could deploy PPO-HRAP as a systematic overlay on passive long exposure, automating tactical de-risking during market stress.
2. **Robo-advisory**: Wealth management platforms could use the method to manage client portfolios with target risk profiles, automatically reducing exposure when VIX spikes.
3. **Pension fund liability matching**: Pension funds could apply PPO-HRAP to manage asset-liability dynamics, controlling drawdowns while pursuing returns.
4. **Systematic CTAs (commodity trading advisors)**: Extend the method to multi-asset futures trading with regime-specific position sizing.
5. **Risk-controlled ETFs**: A fund could mechanically implement PPO-HRAP's logic as a rules-based rebalancing strategy within an ETF wrapper.

## Technical Limitations
1. **High turnover**: The paper acknowledges turnover costs are substantial, suggesting the policy trades too frequently—likely impractical for real deployment with market impact and slippage.
2. **Single-run cross-asset validation**: QQQ and DIA results show only one random seed per asset; multi-seed cross-asset evidence would be stronger.
3. **Regime simplicity**: Relying primarily on VIX ignores other regime signals (yield curve, credit spreads, momentum). The prior may be brittle to novel market regimes.
4. **Backtest-specific optimization**: Reward weights (VIX penalty scaling, turnover cost) appear tuned to 2020–2022; generalization to different market periods untested.
5. **Limited benchmark comparison**: No head-to-head comparison with other RL trading methods or classical risk-parity/momentum strategies.
6. **Single-asset focus**: Despite cross-asset tests, the method is primarily developed and validated on SPY; multi-asset portfolio dynamics (correlations, hedging) are not deeply explored.

## Open Questions
1. **How sensitive is performance to reward weight tuning?** Ablation studies on the VIX penalty scaling, turnover cost, and target-exposure deviation would clarify robustness.
2. **Does the method survive regime shifts?** Test on 2023–2024 data or simulate black-swan events (2008, COVID crash) to assess generalization.
3. **Can higher-order regime features (yield curve, credit spreads, momentum) improve the prior?** A more sophisticated regime model might reduce turnover and improve robustness.
4. **How does transaction cost magnitude affect the learned policy?** The paper includes turnover cost but doesn't vary it systematically; real market impact is nonlinear.
5. **Does the method scale to multi-asset portfolios with non-zero correlations?** Can it learn hedging relationships (e.g., long equities, short bonds), or does it remain single-asset?
6. **What is the computational cost of training PPO-HRAP on longer windows or higher-frequency data?** Scalability to intraday or 1-minute-bar trading is unclear.

## Key Takeaway
Blending learned RL policies with volatility-aware regime priors achieves better risk-adjusted trading returns than pure learning or rules alone.
