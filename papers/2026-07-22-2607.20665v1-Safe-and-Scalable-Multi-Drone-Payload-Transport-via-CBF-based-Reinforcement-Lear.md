# Safe and Scalable Multi-Drone Payload Transport via CBF-based Reinforcement Learning with Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer

**Authors:** Jaeyoun Choi, Oswin So, Songyuan Zhang, Cooper Taylor, Chuchu Fan
**Published:** 2026-07-22 | **arXiv:** [2607.20665v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20665v1)

## Summary
This paper presents a learning-based framework for coordinating multiple drones to safely transport payloads together—a challenging problem because the drones, cables, and load are dynamically coupled. The authors use reinforcement learning with safety constraints (via Control Barrier Functions) and domain randomization to train a single policy that generalizes across different team sizes and real-world conditions without needing to retrain or fine-tune.

## Theoretical Background
The work builds on three key ideas. First, **Control Barrier Functions (CBFs)** are a formal method for ensuring safety constraints are satisfied during learning and control—they define regions of state-space that the system must stay within. Second, **Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)** is a standard reinforcement learning algorithm that updates policies safely by limiting how much the new policy diverges from the old one per step. Third, the paper uses **domain randomization**—training with random physical parameters and environment variations—to make learned behaviors robust to real-world variation without explicit sim-to-real adaptation. The authors combine these into **Discrete Graph PPO (DGPPO)**, which represents multi-agent teams as graphs and enforces safety constraints during learning.

## Core Contributions
1. **Minimal 2D abstraction** that captures the essential drone-payload coupling dynamics while being computationally cheap enough to scale—avoiding the curse of high-dimensional drone modeling.
2. **DGPPO algorithm** that integrates Control Barrier Functions with graph-based distributed PPO, ensuring safety guarantees while learning scalable policies.
3. **Zero-shot sim-to-real transfer** demonstrated on real hardware without fine-tuning, achieved through domain randomization over team size and physical parameters.
4. **Empirical evidence of generalization**: a single trained policy works across different numbers of drones (2, 3, 4 tested) and in dynamic multi-team scenarios with other drone groups moving as obstacles.

## Connections to Other Work
This extends [[Reinforcement Learning]] for robotics by grounding it in formal safety frameworks ([[Control Barrier Functions]]). It relates to [[Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning]] by learning distributed policies (each agent acts locally), but uses graph structure rather than full centralization. The sim-to-real transfer strategy echoes domain randomization approaches in [[Computer Vision]] and robotic manipulation, but applies them to high-dimensional coordination problems. The work sits between pure [[Optimization]]-based control (which is safe but inflexible) and end-to-end learning (which is flexible but unsafe).

## Practical Applications
- **Construction**: Multi-drone lifting and positioning of heavy materials on scaffolding or tall structures.
- **Logistics**: Coordinated transport of large, irregular packages in warehouses or between buildings where single drones are insufficient.
- **Disaster response**: Rapid deployment of emergency supplies or equipment across complex terrain where multiple drones must maintain formation.
- **Infrastructure inspection**: Teams carrying sensor rigs or cameras over power lines or bridges without risk of payload drop.
- **Indoor delivery**: Warehouse or campus autonomous transport of pallets or hazardous materials requiring safety-critical coordination.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **Autonomous Heavy-Lift Delivery Platform**: A commercial service offering multi-drone payload transport for last-mile delivery of construction materials or packages >50kg to urban rooftops or restricted-access sites.
2. **Multi-Drone Logistics-as-a-Service (LaaS)**: SaaS platform where construction companies or warehouses can schedule multi-drone payload runs, with automatic fleet orchestration and real-time safety monitoring.
3. **Disaster Response Drone Swarm Rental**: Pre-trained, deployable multi-drone systems for emergency management agencies—plug-and-play teams that can lift and move debris, supplies, or equipment in post-disaster scenarios.
4. **Autonomous Outdoor Installation & Assembly**: Robotic system for assembling large structures (billboards, solar panel arrays, communication towers) using coordinated drone teams, replacing human teams in hazardous environments.
5. **Multi-Drone Inspection & Mapping Toolkit**: Hardware/software suite for rapid 3D mapping and detailed inspection of infrastructure (mines, tunnels, large industrial plants) where a single drone cannot carry the sensor payload.

## Technical Limitations
- **Payload mass ceiling**: Scalability tested only up to 4 drones; unclear where computational or safety guarantees degrade with 10+ drones.
- **Cable/dynamics idealization**: The 2D abstraction assumes ideal cables (inextensible, massless or near-massless) and may not capture swing dynamics or torsion in real cables under high load.
- **Outdoor wind robustness**: Evaluation appears to be in controlled outdoor conditions; strong wind gusts, turbulence, or GPS-denied environments not addressed.
- **Payload shape constraints**: Assumes point or rigid payloads with known mass; irregular, soft, or deformable loads would violate the abstraction.
- **Real-time computational demands**: No explicit analysis of latency or on-board computation cost for the DGPPO policy; feasibility on resource-constrained embedded drone hardware unclear.
- **Generalization boundaries**: While team-size and parameter randomization are strong, the policy has not been tested on fundamentally different payload types (e.g., swinging vs. rigid) or control objectives.

## Open Questions
- How does policy performance degrade as team size grows beyond 4? Is there a scaling law?
- Can the framework handle dynamic load shedding (dropping payload or changing grip mid-flight)?
- What formal safety guarantees does DGPPO provide, and how tight are they in practice?
- Does the policy learn human-interpretable or robust coordination strategies, or just memorize the domain randomization distribution?
- How sensitive is zero-shot transfer to sim parameters (cable elasticity, air drag model, motor response)? What happens if the simulator is systematically biased?
- Can the same policy or a fine-tuned variant handle mixed payload transport (some heavy, some light, heterogeneous drones)?

## Key Takeaway
Multi-drone teams can learn safe, generalizable payload transport policies without real-world fine-tuning using constraint-based reinforcement learning and domain randomization.
