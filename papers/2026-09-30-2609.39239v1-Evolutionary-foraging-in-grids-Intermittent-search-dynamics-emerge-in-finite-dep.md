# Evolutionary foraging in grids: Intermittent search dynamics emerge in finite, depletable landscapes

**Authors:** Shailendra Bhandari, Alex Szorkovszky, Anis Yazidi, Pedro G. Lind
**Published:** 2026-09-30 | **arXiv:** [2609.39239v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39239v1)

## Summary
This paper studies how search strategies evolve when agents forage in finite environments with limited resources. The researchers simulate evolution of movement traits (step lengths, velocities, turning angles) and find that evolved foraging follows "intermittent search" dynamics — local exploitation with occasional long jumps — rather than the pure power-law "Lévy walk" patterns often assumed in nature. This matters because it reveals how constraints like resource depletion shape optimal search behavior, with implications for understanding animal foraging and designing efficient exploration algorithms.

## Theoretical Background
The paper builds on foraging theory and search dynamics, particularly two competing models. **Lévy walks** are random walks with power-law step-length distributions, proposed as optimal for searching sparse resources because they balance local exploration with occasional long-distance transfers. **Intermittent search** is an alternative where agents alternate between local search (short steps, high turning) and ballistic phases (straight movement). The authors use **evolutionary simulation** — agents with heritable genomes undergo selection based on fitness — to test which dynamics emerge without prescribing them. The fitness function combines energetic gain, movement cost, and spatial coverage, reflecting real constraints.

## Core Contributions
1. **Empirical preference for intermittent dynamics**: By fitting both models to evolved trajectories, the paper shows intermittent search achieves better fits (mean adjusted R² > 0.99) than Lévy walks (R² > 0.9) across multiple environments and conditions.
2. **Evolution without imposition**: Unlike prior work that assumes Lévy distributions, this allows movement traits to evolve freely, then identifies the emergent pattern post-hoc.
3. **Characterization of evolved structure**: Evolved genomes produce short local displacements with a sparse tail of longer relocations — consistent with punctuated exploitation, not scale-free motion.
4. **Robustness across conditions**: The preference for intermittent search holds across grid sizes (tested on 50³ × 50³ grids), resource densities, and both uniform and Lévy-dust distributions.

## Connections to Other Work
This paper engages with classical debates in [[Lévy flight|Lévy walks]], optimal foraging theory, and the emergence of collective behavior in multi-agent systems. It challenges the ubiquity of scale-free search (a common assumption), similar to critiques elsewhere in ecology. The work relates to [[reinforcement-learning]] and agent-based modeling where exploration-exploitation tradeoffs are central — here resolved via evolution rather than learning algorithms. The finite, depletable landscape setting is closer to real ecological niches than infinite uniform environments, making it relevant to practical autonomous systems.

## Practical Applications
1. **Robotic exploration in resource-constrained environments**: Designing search algorithms for drones or rovers with limited energy in partially depleted terrain (disaster response, planetary exploration).
2. **Autonomous search-and-rescue**: Optimizing movement patterns for ground or aerial units searching for targets when resources (fuel, battery) are scarce.
3. **Ant colony or swarm optimization algorithms**: Tuning exploration parameters in metaheuristics used for logistics, facility location, and network design.
4. **Wildlife management**: Predicting and managing animal movement patterns in fragmented habitats with declining resources (poaching prevention, conservation corridors).
5. **Web crawling and data indexing**: Designing crawlers that balance deep local exploration with occasional jumps to distant, underexplored regions under computational budget constraints.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **SearchOS for Autonomous Robots**: A tunable movement-planning library for drones and mobile robots operating under energy/fuel constraints, auto-calibrating intermittent search parameters for terrain type and resource map.
2. **Adaptive Wildlife Tracking SaaS**: Real-time movement prediction and monitoring system for conservation, using evolved search models to forecast where animals will move and alert rangers to poaching hotspots.
3. **Smart Exploration API for Logistics**: Cloud service optimizing delivery routes and warehouse picking paths using intermittent search principles when maps are incomplete or resources (time, fuel) are limited.
4. **EcoSimulation Studio**: Desktop/cloud software for ecologists and conservation planners to simulate agent-based foraging under different landscape and resource scenarios, predicting population dynamics and planning interventions.
5. **Neural Architecture Search Optimizer**: Apply evolved intermittent-search heuristics to hyperparameter tuning and neural architecture search, exploiting local valleys then occasionally jumping to distant regions of the search space.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Toroidal lattice simplification**: Real landscapes have boundaries, obstacles, and non-uniform structure; periodic boundary conditions remove edge effects and navigation complexity.
2. **2D grid constraint**: Natural foraging often includes vertical dimension and 3D heterogeneity; scaling to realistic topography is unclear.
3. **Static resource distributions**: Resources don't regenerate or move; dynamic, patchy, or temporally varying resources may select for different strategies.
4. **Single fitness function**: Assumes fixed trade-off weights between gain, cost, and coverage; real organisms face variable selective pressures.
5. **Limited agent interaction**: No competition, cooperation, or spatial structure among agents; multi-agent feedback and pheromone-like signaling could reshape evolved strategies.
6. **Model fitting ambiguity**: Intermittent and Lévy models are not orthogonal; intermediate models or mixed strategies might fit equally well with more complex parameterizations.

## Open Questions
1. **What happens under regenerating resources?** If resources replenish, does evolution recover Lévy-like exploration, or is depletion-driven intermittence robust across longer timescales?
2. **How do multi-agent interactions change the outcome?** With competition and spatial clustering of agents, do evolved strategies shift toward stronger territorial/local behavior?
3. **Scaling to realistic 3D terrain**: Can the model recover intermittent search in landscapes with obstacles, cliffs, and non-uniform resource availability?
4. **Temporal dynamics**: How do evolved strategies adapt if the resource map changes over evolutionary time? Can agents evolve learning/plasticity alongside movement traits?
5. **Theory-driven characterization**: Can we derive intermittent search from first principles (finite landscape + energy constraint) rather than just observing it?
6. **Transfer and generalization**: Do evolved genomes on one landscape transfer to new environments, or are they overfit?

## Key Takeaway
Agents evolving in resource-limited landscapes discover intermittent search—punctuated exploitation—rather than pure Lévy walks, revealing how constraints shape optimal foraging strategy.
