# GRASP: Generating, Revising, and Assessing for Strategic Planning with Agentic AI

**Authors:** Arunabh Srivastava, Mohammad A.,  Khojastepour, Srimat Chakradhar, Sennur Ulukus
**Published:** 2026-09-24 | **arXiv:** [2609.30147v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30147v1)

## Summary
This paper presents GRASP, a planning framework that breaks down complex task execution into three isolated stages: generating global guidelines, exploring alternative strategies locally, and evaluating outcomes independently. LLMs typically fail on harder tasks, but GRASP's modular approach achieves 12–30% accuracy gains on scheduling and logic puzzles, and critically, maintains performance when juggling multiple tasks simultaneously—something standard LLMs cannot do.

## Theoretical Background
LLMs excel at next-token prediction but struggle with long-horizon planning because errors compound over many steps. Prior work on [[chain-of-thought prompting]] and [[tree-of-thought]] reasoning tried to improve planning by asking models to "think step-by-step" or explore multiple paths. However, these approaches still run into catastrophic interference when handling multiple tasks—the model's attention gets tangled across different problem contexts. GRASP builds on the insight that **context isolation** (keeping separate tasks or sub-problems in separate mental spaces) can reduce interference, combined with **multi-stage decomposition**: separating the generation of high-level rules (macro-guidelines) from local strategy exploration from final validation.

## Core Contributions
1. **Three-stage decoupled pipeline**: GenPlan (pre-compile global guidelines), RevPlan (explore localized strategies in isolated windows), VerPlan (multi-criteria evaluation)—each stage operates independently, reducing cross-contamination.
2. **Multi-task robustness**: Demonstrates that context isolation flattens the performance cliff that normally occurs when LLMs face multiple interleaved tasks (16.7% absolute gain in dual-task settings).
3. **Empirical frontier**: Outperforms GPT-4-level baselines by 14.5% and achieves state-of-the-art on Natural Plan Calendar Scheduling (+12.4%), ZebraLogic (+30.8%), and SciBench Math.
4. **Architectural insight**: Shows that enforcing strict macro-regularization (global policy constraints) combined with local exploration prevents context bleed-through.

## Connections to Other Work
GRASP refines ideas from [[chain-of-thought prompting]], [[tree-of-thought]], and [[self-refinement loops]], but adds a key dimension: **context windowing** and **modular isolation**. Related to [[agentic AI]] and [[planning with language models]], but differs by explicitly preventing multi-task interference rather than just improving single-task reasoning. Connects to work on [[prompt engineering]] and [[in-context learning]] by showing that task separation is as important as prompt design. Also relevant to [[reinforcement learning from human feedback]] (RLHF) literature, since VerPlan acts as a learned evaluator—though here it's task-specific rather than value-aligned.

## Practical Applications
1. **Personal scheduling assistants**: Calendar systems that handle competing meetings, resource constraints, and user preferences without getting confused across overlapping events.
2. **Multi-step customer support**: Routing complex tickets through branching decision trees (refunds → shipping → policy exceptions) without losing context mid-flow.
3. **Workflow automation**: RPA (robotic process automation) for enterprise processes like invoice approval or loan underwriting, where multiple tasks run in parallel without interference.
4. **Scientific reasoning engines**: Planning experimental sequences or mathematical proofs where maintaining global constraints while exploring local variations is essential.
5. **Game-playing and puzzle-solving APIs**: Serving real-time queries on scheduling, logic, or optimization problems (Sudoku variants, timetabling) with consistent quality regardless of request volume.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **Enterprise AI Workflow Optimizer**: Sell GRASP as a backend for RPA platforms (UiPath, Blue Prism competitors). Pricing: per-workflow-execution or per-concurrent-task.
2. **Intelligent Scheduling SaaS**: Vertical SaaS for healthcare (OR scheduling), education (timetabling), or logistics (route planning)—handles complex constraints and multi-stakeholder conflicts.
3. **API for Plan-Generation-as-a-Service**: Expose GRASP as a managed endpoint for developers building planning features into apps (trip planners, project managers, event coordinators).
4. **Context-Aware AI Copilot for Knowledge Workers**: IDE or office plugin that helps users break down and execute multi-step tasks (code design, document structure, legal review) without losing coherence.
5. **Reasoning Model Fine-Tuning Service**: Offer to fine-tune small open-source LLMs using GRASP's modular pipeline as a template, selling to enterprises needing on-prem reasoning without GPT-4 costs.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Scalability of macro-guideline generation**: GenPlan pre-compiles global rules, but for truly novel or open-ended domains, how are these guidelines seeded? Risk of bottlenecking on human annotation.
2. **Context window trade-offs**: RevPlan isolates contexts to reduce interference, but this may artificially prevent useful cross-task transfer. No ablation on whether complete isolation is always optimal.
3. **VerPlan discriminator overhead**: The multi-criteria evaluator adds latency. Unclear if gains justify inference cost, or if cheaper heuristic checks suffice.
4. **Task diversity**: Experiments focus on structured domains (scheduling, logic puzzles, math). Performance on open-ended creative or dialogue tasks unknown.
5. **Comparison fairness**: Paper compares against "direct LLM planners" but doesn't report results with other state-of-the-art modular or tree-search methods at scale.
6. **Assumption: Task independence**: Framework assumes tasks can be cleanly isolated; real-world workflows often have subtle dependencies that isolation might miss.

## Open Questions
1. How does GRASP scale to >10 concurrent tasks? Is there a cliff where isolation breaks down?
2. Can GenPlan automatically learn or refine macro-guidelines via feedback, or does it require manual intervention?
3. How sensitive is the approach to the quality of the macro-guidelines? What happens with adversarial or misleading global rules?
4. Does GRASP generalize to tasks unseen during training, or does it overfit to the three evaluated domains?
5. What is the minimum computational budget (model size, inference steps) needed to preserve GRASP's gains? Can it run on mobile or edge devices?
6. How does performance scale with task interdependency? Can you quantify when isolation helps vs. hurts?
7. Could learned routing (e.g., a meta-agent deciding which sub-module to activate) replace hardcoded stages?

## Key Takeaway
Breaking complex planning into isolated, specialized stages eliminates multi-task confusion and lets LLMs scale to harder problems.
