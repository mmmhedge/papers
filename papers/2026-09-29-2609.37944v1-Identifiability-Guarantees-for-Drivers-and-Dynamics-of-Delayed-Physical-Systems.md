# Identifiability Guarantees for Drivers and Dynamics of Delayed Physical Systems

**Authors:** Julien Boussard, Antoine Débouchage, Théo Saulus
**Published:** 2026-09-29 | **arXiv:** [2609.37944v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37944v1)

## Summary
This paper develops a method to identify the underlying drivers and dynamics of delayed physical systems (stochastic differential equations with time lags) from observed data, with mathematical guarantees. Unlike physics-informed neural networks or symbolic regression approaches that lack identifiability guarantees, this work proves that under reasonable assumptions, you can uniquely recover the true dynamics — a critical requirement for trustworthy scientific modeling.

## Theoretical Background
Physical systems with delays are common (neural networks, mechanical systems, biological processes) but harder to learn than standard differential equations because the state depends on its past values. Prior approaches fall into three camps: (1) physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) — neural networks trained to satisfy physical equations as constraints — which are flexible but offer no proof you've found the true equations; (2) symbolic regression — which searches over predefined mathematical operations to build equations, but requires you to choose which operations to consider upfront; (3) causal discovery — which infers cause-and-effect structure but typically assumes no hidden confounders or other restrictive conditions that real physical systems violate. This paper takes a theory-grounded approach, proving that under permissive assumptions, the structural form and coefficients of stochastic delayed differential equations are *identifiable* — meaning there is only one set of equations consistent with the observed data.

## Core Contributions
1. **Identifiability theory for delayed systems**: Formal proof that the structural drivers (which terms appear) and drift (coefficients) of stochastic delayed differential equations can be uniquely recovered from observations under explicitly stated, permissive assumptions.
2. **Novel method design**: A principled algorithm that leverages this theory to recover both the presence and values of dynamical terms.
3. **Empirical validation**: Outperforms existing baselines (PINNs, symbolic regression, causal discovery) on two benchmarks: one directly evaluating driver recovery accuracy, and one measuring physical consistency of the learned model against ground truth.

## Connections to Other Work
This bridges [[physics-informed neural networks|PINNs]] (powerful but unprincipled) with [[causal discovery]] (principled but restrictive), and relates to broader work on [[mechanistic interpretability]] — understanding what a learned model actually represents. It also connects to symbolic approaches like those used in automated scientific discovery. The identifiability angle is inspired by classical statistics and econometrics, where proving you can recover the true model is a foundational concern often ignored in modern deep learning.

## Practical Applications
- **Systems biology**: Inferring regulatory networks and protein dynamics with measurement delays.
- **Climate modeling**: Recovering ocean–atmosphere coupling equations where feedback lags are physically meaningful.
- **Power grids and control systems**: Learning grid stability equations where physical delays matter for accuracy.
- **Pharmacokinetics**: Identifying drug absorption and clearance rates from delayed concentration measurements.
- **Materials science**: Discovering creep and fatigue models in polymers and alloys from time-lagged stress–strain data.

## Technical Limitations
- **Assumption permissiveness is relative**: While better than causal discovery, the method still requires assumptions (e.g., smoothness, bounded derivatives, limited delay structure) that may not hold for chaotic or highly nonlinear regimes.
- **Computational scaling**: Not discussed how the method scales with system dimension or length of time series; may be intractable for high-dimensional systems.
- **Limited guidance on hyperparameters**: Theory proves identifiability *in principle* but does not provide practical guidance on choosing regularization, kernel bandwidths, or other tuning parameters.
- **Noise robustness**: Identifiability guarantees may degrade sharply as noise increases; finite-sample behavior not characterized.
- **No comparison to ground truth in real data**: Benchmarks use synthetic data where ground truth is known; real experimental data often violates model assumptions.

## Open Questions
- How does identifiability degrade with noise level, and can you quantify the sample complexity required for high-confidence recovery?
- Can the theory extend to **partially observed systems** where you only measure a subset of state variables?
- How do you automatically select the delay structure (which past lags matter) before applying the method?
- Does this approach extend to **stochastic noise models beyond Gaussian** (e.g., heavy-tailed, Lévy processes)?
- Can you combine this with causal discovery to handle **hidden confounders or latent variables**?

## Key Takeaway
Provably recover true equations of delayed physical systems from data, bridging symbolic rigor with neural network flexibility.
