# Policy Synthesis for Finite Populations of MDP Agents under Aggregate Reach-Avoid Chance Constraints

**Authors:** Jie Fu, Anamika Dubey
**Published:** 2026-10-08 | **arXiv:** [2610.12028v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12028v1)

## Summary
This paper solves a control problem for groups of independent agents that must collectively satisfy safety and goal-reaching constraints, even when the group size is finite and randomness is present. Rather than only enforcing constraints *on average* (as standard methods do), this work guarantees constraints hold with high probability for actual, real-sized populations by tracking both the mean behavior and variance of the agent population, then converting these statistical guarantees into concrete control policies.

## Theoretical Background
The paper builds on mean-field control theory — a framework that approximates large populations of identical agents by studying a single "representative" agent and a population density. In practice, populations are finite and exhibit stochastic fluctuations; standard mean-field methods ignore these fluctuations and only enforce constraints in expectation, which is too weak.

The authors use three key mathematical tools:
- **Markov Decision Processes (MDPs)** — models for sequential decision-making under uncertainty, here decoupled (agents transition independently).
- **Empirical density feedback** — agents observe and respond to the fraction of peers in each state.
- **Lyapunov recursions** — a technique to track how system properties (here, variance) evolve over time.
- **Cantelli inequality** — a concentration bound that relates variance to tail probabilities, converting probabilistic guarantees into deterministic constraints on moments.

## Core Contributions
1. **Moment-based surrogate constraints**: Instead of handling stochastic chance constraints directly (which is hard), the authors propagate the second-order moments (mean and variance) of empirical density and use Cantelli's inequality to create deterministic surrogate constraints that guarantee the original probabilistic bounds.

2. **Finite-N certificates**: They derive explicit error bounds showing how the method's guarantees degrade gracefully as population size decreases, enabling rigorous safety certificates for actual (not asymptotic) fleet sizes.

3. **Tractable policy synthesis**: By embedding these moment constraints into a sequential convex approximation algorithm, they transform the problem into a gradient-based optimization that scales better than global approaches.

4. **Empirical validation on realistic domains**: The method is tested on EV-charging aggregation — a practical power-system problem where coordinating thousands of chargers must respect grid safety limits and customer satisfaction targets.

## Connections to Other Work
This work extends [[mean-field control theory]] by handling finite populations rigorously, bridging the gap between idealized asymptotic theory and practical deployment. It relates to [[distributionally robust optimization]] and [[chance-constrained programming]], which also aim to certify finite-sample safety. The aggregate reach-avoid constraint structure is related to [[barrier functions]] and [[control barrier functions]] in safety-critical control, adapted here for density feedback and stochastic settings. The paper also touches on [[multi-agent reinforcement learning]] but takes a more classical control-theoretic perspective rather than learning-based.

## Practical Applications
- **Electric vehicle charging aggregation**: Coordinate thousands of EV chargers to meet grid demand targets while keeping transformer loading below safety limits (motivated example in the paper).
- **Autonomous vehicle fleet coordination**: Ensure a fraction of vehicles reach rendezvous points while keeping unsafe driving patterns below a threshold.
- **Hospital resource allocation**: Manage finite populations of patients in different stages of treatment, ensuring throughput targets and occupancy constraints.
- **Drone swarm deployment**: Guarantee a minimum fraction reach objectives while keeping collision risk below acceptable levels.
- **Manufacturing process control**: Batch of independent machines must collectively maintain quality above a threshold while respecting downtime budgets.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **GridSync Analytics**: A SaaS platform for utilities to design charging coordination policies for distributed EV fleets; inputs are safety/demand constraints, outputs are real-time control signals with finite-population safety certificates.

2. **FleetGuard**: Autonomous vehicle fleet management software that certifies safety properties (e.g., "99% of vehicles keep collision probability <10−5") and synthesizes decentralized policies automatically.

3. **StochasticsOps**: A CI/CD-style tool for manufacturers: upload a finite population of processes, specify aggregate performance targets and constraints, receive formally verified control policies that adapt as processes age or fail.

4. **MicroGridController**: Real-time optimization service for islanded or grid-connected microgrids with heterogeneous distributed energy resources; handles N batteries, solar units, and loads with probabilistic safety guarantees.

5. **Safety-as-a-Service (SafeControl)**: A generic middleware that takes user-defined aggregate constraints and finite-N agent models, automatically synthesizes policies, and provides runtime certificates — targeting robotics, logistics, and industrial control integrators.

## Technical Limitations
- **Decoupling assumption**: Agents must have independent transition dynamics; correlated behavior (e.g., shared environmental shocks) violates the model.
- **Empirical density feedback**: All agents observe the same aggregate state; in practice, observation delays, communication loss, and partial observability are not handled.
- **Linear constraints**: The policy synthesis uses sequential convex approximation, which requires the original constraints to be convex (or affine in policy); nonlinear aggregate constraints are not directly supported.
- **Moment closure**: The method propagates only mean and variance; higher moments (skewness, etc.) are ignored, which can be loose for small N or heavy-tailed dynamics.
- **Asymptotic optimality not claimed**: The paper certifies safety but does not optimize over policies or compare to optimal solutions; there may be large conservatism from Cantelli-based relaxation.
- **Gridworld scalability**: Experiments are on small discrete domains; scalability to high-dimensional continuous state spaces or large N is unclear.

## Open Questions
- How tight are the Cantelli-based moment surrogate constraints in practice? Could tighter concentration inequalities (e.g., large-deviations principles) improve synthesis?
- Can the method handle correlated agent failures or common environmental shocks (e.g., weather, grid faults)?
- How does the conservatism scale with N and the moment bounds — is there a phase transition where finite-N effects dominate?
- Can learning be integrated to estimate unknown transition kernels online while maintaining safety certificates?
- How do constraints on higher moments (variance of variance, etc.) affect the policy, and can they reduce conservatism?
- Can this be extended to time-varying or heterogeneous agent populations?

## Key Takeaway
Finite populations of random agents can be safely controlled by tracking their statistical moments and converting probabilistic constraints into moment-based guarantees.
