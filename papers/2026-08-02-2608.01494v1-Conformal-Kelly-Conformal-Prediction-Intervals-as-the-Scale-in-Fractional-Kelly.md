# Conformal Kelly: Conformal Prediction Intervals as the Scale in Fractional Kelly Position Sizing

**Authors:** Robert Jacob Ryan
**Published:** 2026-08-02 | **arXiv:** [2608.01494v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01494v1)

## Summary
This paper uses conformal prediction—a method for measuring forecast uncertainty—to size trading positions in a portfolio. When prediction intervals are wide (high uncertainty), positions shrink; when narrow (low uncertainty), they grow. The authors show strong returns during development (2016–2021) but find the approach does not generalize to held-out test data (2022 onward), illustrating a hard lesson about overfitting in quantitative trading.

## Theoretical Background
Conformal prediction is a statistical framework that wraps around any predictor to produce prediction intervals with guaranteed coverage rates—meaning the true value falls inside the interval a specified fraction of the time (here, 75%). The Kelly criterion is a portfolio theory result stating that optimal position sizing is proportional to edge (expected return per unit risk) divided by odds; fractional Kelly applies a safety discount to this formula. The paper combines these: use conformal intervals' width as a proxy for prediction reliability, then feed that into Kelly position sizing to dynamically scale bet amounts.

## Core Contributions
1. **Novel pairing**: First application of conformal interval widths as a direct input to fractional Kelly position sizing, rather than using intervals only to describe forecast accuracy.
2. **Counter-intuitive finding on time series**: Against published guidance, slow, unweighted rolling quantiles (the simplest method) outperformed sophisticated adaptive schemes by 0.7–5.3 percentage points annualized—suggesting that stability in position-sizing signal matters more than local sharpness in uncertainty estimates.
3. **Risk control rule**: A dynamic leverage cut triggered when downside misses exceed historical rates, reducing maximum drawdown from 27.7% to 20.3% while raising Sharpe ratio (statistically validated against 40 placebos).
4. **Rigorous experimental discipline**: Sealed test data (2022 onward), pre-registered configurations and benchmarks, and autonomous LLM-agent search over 200 hyperparameters—with honest reporting of failures.

## Connections to Other Work
This work bridges [[conformal prediction]] (primarily a supervised-learning uncertainty quantification tool) with [[Kelly criterion]] and [[fractional Kelly]] betting (classical portfolio optimization). It relates to [[RLHF]] and [[agents]] only tangentially—the LLM is used for hyperparameter search, not as the core algorithm. The paper contrasts with standard approaches like using sample standard deviation for volatility scaling, and implicitly challenges recent literature advocating for adaptive reweighting in conformal prediction on time series.

## Practical Applications
1. **Systematic equity trading**: Dynamically size long/short bets on individual stocks or sector baskets based on model confidence.
2. **Futures or derivatives overlays**: Adjust contract sizes in trend-following or mean-reversion strategies when prediction intervals narrow.
3. **Risk parity portfolios**: Modulate leverage across asset classes or strategies as their forecast uncertainty changes.
4. **Tail-risk hedging**: Use interval widening as an early signal to reduce exposure when market predictability degrades.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **Conformal Portfolio Manager SaaS**: Black-box tool that takes user models (any sklearn-compatible predictor) and outputs position-sizing signals via conformal intervals; white-label for hedge funds.
2. **Uncertainty-aware robo-advisor**: Retail wealth platform that shrinks stock/bond allocations when market prediction confidence drops, reducing drawdowns in crashes.
3. **Adaptive leverage control API**: Middleware for algorithmic traders that dynamically adjusts margin usage and portfolio beta when conformal intervals widen; integrates with existing brokers.
4. **Quantitative strategy incubator**: Boutique that licenses the conformal-Kelly sizing method to trend-following CTAs and systematic managers.
5. **Enterprise risk dashboard**: Real-time visualization of portfolio-wide prediction interval widths (by asset, sector, factor) to inform risk committees and compliance.

## Technical Limitations
1. **No out-of-sample generalization**: Development-window returns (28.5% annualized) collapsed to 7–8.5% in 2022+, and conformal calibration held but growth did not—suggesting the edge came from overfitting, not robust signal.
2. **Dependence on underlying model**: Conformal intervals only transport whatever predictive power the base model has; garbage in, garbage out.
3. **Stale rolling quantiles**: The winning "slow, unweighted" method may lag regime shifts; it is unclear how the approach behaves during market dislocations or structural breaks.
4. **Limited asset scope**: Testing on U.S. equity indices; unclear how well the method scales to illiquid or non-stationary assets (crypto, commodities, emerging markets).
5. **Position sizing alone**: The paper addresses *when* to trade (interval width) but not *what* to trade; it assumes a pre-existing edge in the underlying predictor.
6. **Leverage and liquidation risk**: Assumes leverage is always available at the modeled cost; breaks if margin is recalled or markets gap.

## Open Questions
1. **Why does slow beat fast?** A deeper mechanistic study of why adaptive reweighting harms out-of-sample performance in conformal time-series prediction would be valuable—is it estimation variance, overfitting to calibration data, or regime sensitivity?
2. **Cross-asset generalization**: Does the conformal-Kelly sizing transfer to bonds, commodities, or crypto? Is the finding specific to equity index properties?
3. **Online learning**: How would a continuously updated conformal model (new data daily) perform, and does adaptive retraining help or hurt?
4. **Interval width as a standalone predictor**: Is width genuinely informative for sizing, or is it a proxy for market regime that other simpler signals capture?
5. **Comparison to alternatives**: How does this compare to [[GARCH]]-based or implied-volatility-based dynamic sizing, or to modern Bayesian portfolio methods?
6. **Larger hyperparameter sweep**: The LLM searched 200 configurations; what about a grid or Bayesian optimization over 10,000+ with even stricter pre-registration?

## Key Takeaway
Conformal prediction intervals can size positions better than volatility, but the largest gains vanished in out-of-sample testing, highlighting the difficulty of systematic trading edge.
