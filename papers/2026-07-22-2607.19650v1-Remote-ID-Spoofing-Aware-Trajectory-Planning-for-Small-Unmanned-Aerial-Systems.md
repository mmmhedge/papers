# Remote ID Spoofing-Aware Trajectory Planning for Small Unmanned Aerial Systems

**Authors:** Jeremiah Webb, Bryce Bjorkman, Abel Diaz Gonzalez, Austin Coursey, Noah Dahle et al.
**Published:** 2026-07-22 | **arXiv:** [2607.19650v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19650v1)

## Summary
This paper presents a decentralized trajectory planning system for small unmanned aerial vehicles (drones) that accounts for spoofed Remote Identification broadcasts — fake location signals that adversaries could transmit to cause collisions. Rather than trusting all received location data, the system uses physical signal measurements to detect when broadcasts are false, then plans collision-avoidant paths that remain safe even under uncertainty about true positions. Results show the approach reduces near-collision events in multi-drone delivery scenarios.

## Theoretical Background
The work builds on several foundations: decentralized multi-agent planning (where each agent plans independently without central coordination), chance-constrained optimization (a mathematical framework for ensuring safety when some variables are uncertain), Markov decision processes (MDPs — a standard way to model sequential decision-making under uncertainty), and physical-layer security (detecting attacks using radio signal properties like received signal strength). Remote Identification is a regulatory requirement for drone operations, broadcasting position to ground stations; the paper's innovation is treating this information as potentially adversarial rather than inherently trustworthy.

## Core Contributions
1. **Spoofing-aware formulation**: Explicitly models RID broadcasts as untrusted and derives credibility assessments from received signal strength.
2. **Probabilistic localization**: Uses RSS measurements to estimate both the true position of a spoofing agent and the associated uncertainty.
3. **Chance-constrained integration**: Converts probabilistic uncertainty into a risk-bounded unsafe region that feeds into each drone's MDP-based planner.
4. **Decentralized real-time execution**: Avoids centralized coordination overhead, enabling scalable multi-agent collision avoidance with computational efficiency suitable for onboard drone processors.

## Connections to Other Work
This extends classical [[multi-agent collision avoidance]] (e.g., ORCA, reciprocal collision avoidance) by incorporating adversarial information sources. It relates to broader work in [[risk-aware planning]] and [[robust control under uncertainty]]. The chance-constrained formulation connects to safe [[reinforcement-learning]] approaches that guarantee probabilistic safety constraints. The physical-layer spoofing detection aspect draws from wireless security literature, particularly signal-strength fingerprinting and anomaly detection methods.

## Practical Applications
1. **Urban air mobility (package delivery)**: Multiple drones operating in low-altitude airspace where coordinated spoofing attacks could disrupt operations; this approach maintains safety despite adversarial broadcasts.
2. **Emergency response missions**: Drones deployed in disaster zones with potentially degraded or jammed communications benefit from spoofing-aware coordination.
3. **Military and border surveillance**: Systems operating in contested environments where adversaries actively spoof location signals.
4. **Airport surface operations**: Small drones operating near airports where safety separation is critical and spoofing could cause regulatory violations.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **RID spoofing detection middleware**: A software/firmware module for drone fleets that integrates spoofing-aware planning as a safety layer; license to delivery companies and operators.
2. **Trustworthy drone fleet management platform**: SaaS for multi-drone coordination that explicitly models RID reliability, offering certified-safe operations in adversarial environments.
3. **Adversarial flight testing service**: Offer companies the ability to test their drone systems against realistic spoofing scenarios with validated simulation and real-world trials.
4. **Hardware-software drone security appliance**: A signal-analysis device that mounts on delivery hubs or coordination centers, detects and localizes spoofing agents, and pushes warnings to drone fleets in real time.
5. **Insurance and compliance audit tool**: Help operators and insurers verify that their drone operations remain safe under plausible spoofing attacks; generate automated safety reports for regulators.

## Technical Limitations
1. **RSS-based localization accuracy**: Received signal strength varies with environment, weather, and antenna orientation, limiting precision; the method assumes RSS is harder to spoof than GPS/RID itself, which may not hold for sophisticated attackers.
2. **Computational overhead of uncertainty propagation**: The chance-constrained MDP formulation adds state space; scalability to very large fleets (50+ simultaneous drones) unclear.
3. **False positive rate under normal operation**: Legitimate signal blockage or multipath propagation might trigger spoofing detection, causing conservative (inefficient) maneuvers.
4. **Limited experimental scope**: Evaluation is simulation-only in a package delivery scenario; real-world multi-drone flight tests with actual spoofing attacks not demonstrated.
5. **Assumes adversary broadcasts location at all**: A silent jammer (RID denial, not spoofing) is not addressed; system relies on detectible spoofed signals.

## Open Questions
1. How does the approach perform with **active adversaries that adapt** — e.g., an attacker who observes the drone's responses and refines spoofing strategy in real time?
2. Can the RSS-based spoofing detection be **made robust to environmental variation** (rain, urban canyon, high-altitude wind farms) in outdoor trials?
3. How much does **conservative collision avoidance** (due to uncertainty) reduce mission efficiency (delivery time, energy)? Is there a principled trade-off?
4. Does the decentralized MDP scale efficiently to **50+ concurrent drones**? What is the computational bottleneck?
5. Can the framework extend to **multi-hop or network spoofing**, where an attacker corrupts relayed RID broadcasts from neighboring aircraft?

## Key Takeaway
Drones can safely avoid collisions even when location broadcasts are spoofed, by using radio signal strength to detect and localize attackers.
