# A Distribution Mapping Approach to Counterfactually Fair Reinforcement Learning

**Authors:** Jianhan Zhang, Jitao Wang, John D. Piette, Donglin Zeng, Chengchun Shi et al.
**Published:** 2026-08-09 | **arXiv:** [2608.08743v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08743v1)

## Summary
This paper develops a method to make reinforcement learning (RL) systems fair across different population subgroups in high-stakes settings like healthcare. The key innovation is a "distribution mapping" algorithm that preprocesses training data to remove systematic bias before policy learning, enabling RL agents to make decisions that don't unfairly restrict access to services for certain groups. The method is grounded in causal reasoning and comes with theoretical guarantees.

## Theoretical Background
Reinforcement learning optimizes sequential decision-making to maximize long-term rewards, but can perpetuate or amplify unfairness when trained on biased historical data. Counterfactual fairness (CF) is a causal framework that asks: "What would happen if we changed a sensitive attribute (like race or gender)?" — it treats fairness as a causal reasoning problem rather than just a statistical one. The paper builds on quantile regression methods (techniques for estimating conditional distributions at different probability levels) and extends them to handle sequential dependencies in RL. It relaxes standard additivity assumptions commonly used in causal inference, making the approach more flexible for real-world data.

## Core Contributions
1. **Novel quantile distribution mapping algorithm**: A data preprocessing method that sequentially estimates counterfactual states and rewards without assuming additive causal models — this is more general than prior work.
2. **Theoretical guarantees**: Formal proofs that the approach bounds both per-step counterfactual unfairness and the infinite-horizon suboptimality gap (how much worse the policy is compared to the optimal one).
3. **Integration with policy learning**: Shows how preprocessing can be combined with standard RL algorithms to achieve counterfactual fairness end-to-end.
4. **Real-world validation**: Demonstrates the method on a digital health intervention dataset, not just synthetic experiments.

## Connections to Other Work
This work bridges [[reinforcement-learning]] and fairness — an increasingly important intersection as RL moves into consequential domains. It relates to broader fairness-in-ML literature but uses causal inference (counterfactual reasoning) rather than purely statistical notions of fairness. The approach sits at the intersection of [[alignment]] (ensuring AI systems respect human values) and causal inference for decision-making. It complements work on fairness constraints in optimization and extends ideas from offline RL and causal inference to the fairness setting.

## Practical Applications
- **Clinical trial design and personalized medicine**: RL agents recommending treatment pathways should not systematically deny high-value interventions to certain patient subgroups.
- **Loan and credit decisions**: Sequential RL systems that deny creditworthy individuals access based on demographic history.
- **Hiring and promotion pipelines**: Multi-stage RL systems that allocate training or advancement opportunities fairly across demographic groups.
- **Public health intervention allocation**: Deciding which communities receive preventive health programs, without systematic deprivation.
- **Criminal justice sentencing support systems**: RL systems that recommend sentences should not be biased against particular groups.

## Technical Limitations
- **Data requirements**: The algorithm needs sufficient historical data to estimate counterfactual distributions reliably; sparse or short-horizon data could make estimates unstable.
- **Causal model specification**: The approach still requires specifying a causal graph linking sensitive attributes, states, actions, and rewards — misspecification could propagate bias rather than remove it.
- **Computational complexity**: Quantile regression on high-dimensional state spaces may become expensive; scalability to large state/action spaces is unclear.
- **Definition of fairness**: Counterfactual fairness is one definition; it may not align with all stakeholder values or legal notions of fairness in specific domains (e.g., affirmative action vs. equal treatment).
- **Assumption validity**: The "mild regularity conditions" for theoretical guarantees may not hold in highly non-stationary or distribution-shifted real-world settings.

## Open Questions
- How does the method perform with **misspecified causal models** (i.e., when the assumed causal graph is wrong)?
- Can the approach scale to **high-dimensional state spaces** and large action sets typical in modern RL applications?
- How do results change under **distribution shift** — if the deployment environment differs from training data?
- Can the method be extended to **multi-agent RL** settings where fairness across multiple actors matters?
- What is the **sample complexity** relative to standard RL — how much more data is needed to achieve the same performance with fairness constraints?
- How does this approach compare empirically to other fairness-constrained RL methods on diverse domains beyond healthcare?

## Key Takeaway
A preprocessing algorithm using quantile distribution mapping enables RL to make fair sequential decisions without systematically disadvantaging demographic subgroups.
