# VetClaw: An Edge-Cloud Multimodal Agentic System for Veterinary Disease Screening

**Authors:** Syed Mhamudul Hasan, Anas AlSobeh, Hussein Zangoti, Abdur R. Shahid
**Published:** 2026-07-28 | **arXiv:** [2607.26042v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26042v1)

## Summary
VetClaw is an edge-cloud system that combines a camera on a veterinary device with an AI model running on a server to screen animals for diseases. The key innovation is treating disease screening not as a single prediction but as a multi-step workflow—collecting images, asking about symptoms, checking safety rules, and routing uncertain cases to a veterinarian—which improves accuracy and makes the system safer and more usable.

## Theoretical Background
This work builds on two converging areas. First, **vision-language models** (VLMs)—neural networks that understand both images and text together—have shown promise at zero-shot classification, meaning they can recognize diseases they weren't explicitly trained on. Second, **agentic systems** are frameworks where software agents orchestrate workflows by calling tools (like APIs, models, or sensors) and making decisions based on results; this shifts from static prediction to dynamic reasoning. The paper separates concerns: OpenClaw handles edge-device scheduling and notifications (device-level operations), while LangGraph manages the stateful workflow orchestration (decision logic and tool coordination). This mirrors architectural patterns in autonomous systems where sensing, reasoning, and action are decoupled.

## Core Contributions
1. **Multimodal workflow design**: Rather than treating VLM classification as a black box, VetClaw wraps it in a stateful agent that collects visual evidence *and* symptom descriptions, validates inputs, applies safety checks, and routes uncertain cases.
2. **Edge-cloud separation**: OpenClaw on the device handles real-time user interaction and scheduling; LangGraph on the server manages complex conditional logic and external model calls. This reduces edge compute burden while keeping the UI responsive.
3. **Empirical validation of multimodal input**: The paper shows that image-only predictions are weak, but combining images with symptom descriptions significantly improves zero-shot disease classification accuracy.
4. **Safety-aware orchestration**: Built-in failure handling, deterministic safety rules, and escalation thresholds—moving beyond passive prediction to clinically-aware decision support.

## Connections to Other Work
VetClaw applies [[agents]] and [[generative-models|vision-language models]] in a domain-specific (veterinary) context. It echoes broader trends in:
- **Agentic AI frameworks** like AutoGPT and ReAct, which treat LLMs/VLMs as controllers that call tools and reason over results.
- **Multimodal learning**, where combining image and text modalities outperforms unimodal approaches (echoed in papers on CLIP, GPT-4V, etc.).
- **Edge-cloud ML architectures**, which balance latency/privacy (edge) against compute power (cloud).
- **Medical AI safety**, particularly the need for human-in-the-loop escalation and uncertainty quantification in clinical decision support.

## Practical Applications
1. **Rural/remote veterinary clinics**: Enables screening in areas with limited access to experienced veterinarians; device can flag likely cases for remote consultation.
2. **Livestock farms**: Farmers photograph animals showing signs of illness; system gives immediate triage (urgent vs. monitor vs. safe) before calling a vet.
3. **Animal shelters and rescue organizations**: Rapid health screening of incoming animals to prioritize medical intervention and reduce disease spread.
4. **Pet owner self-triage**: Mobile app for dog/cat owners to photograph symptoms and get confidence-weighted guidance on whether to seek immediate care.
5. **Veterinary training**: Educational tool for veterinary students to practice symptom collection and see real-time feedback on diagnostic reasoning.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **VetTriage Mobile App**: Smartphone app for pet owners; uses device camera to capture pet symptoms, sends to cloud VLM, returns structured guidance ("Monitor at home," "Call vet within 24h," "Urgent—visit ER") with confidence scores and escalation to live vet chat.
2. **Farm Health Monitoring SaaS**: Lightweight edge device for barns/stables that runs daily wellness checks on livestock. Logs historical trends, alerts when animals deviate from normal, reduces antibiotic overuse by enabling targeted treatment.
3. **Shelter Intake Kiosk**: Dedicated hardware kiosk for animal shelters; staff photograph incoming animals, system prioritizes medical quarantine and specialist care, reduces operational costs and liability.
4. **Diagnostic Support Platform for Veterinary Clinics**: Integrated into clinic workflow; vet snaps image of lesion/symptom, gets confidence-ranked differential diagnoses, learns from corrections over time (enables fine-tuning on clinic-specific data).
5. **Veterinary Insurance Underwriting Tool**: Automated preliminary health assessment for insurance quotes; combines symptom questionnaire with photo evidence to speed approval/denial decisions and reduce fraud.

## Technical Limitations
1. **VLM hallucination and bias**: Zero-shot VLMs can confidently predict wrong diagnoses, especially for rare or visually similar conditions. No ground-truth validation in the paper; accuracy thresholds for escalation are not reported.
2. **Image quality dependency**: System assumes reasonable lighting, framing, and animal cooperation. Poorly lit or blurry photos will degrade performance; no image quality checks are mentioned.
3. **Symptom bias**: Reliance on user-provided symptoms introduces selection bias and inconsistency (different people describe symptoms differently). No validation that symptom text improves accuracy *robustly* across diverse user populations.
4. **Cold-start for new diseases**: If a novel pathogen emerges, the VLM has no training data. The system cannot learn or adapt from local case histories without retraining the server model.
5. **Regulatory and liability gaps**: The paper does not address FDA/veterinary board certification, liability for missed diagnoses, or clinical trial standards. Deployment in real clinics requires validation and approval.
6. **Limited scalability analysis**: No discussion of latency, throughput, or cost under real-world load (e.g., 1000 concurrent farmers during morning herd checks).

## Open Questions
1. **What accuracy thresholds trigger escalation to a veterinarian?** How is this threshold validated against ground-truth diagnoses?
2. **Does multimodal performance degrade when symptom text is inconsistent or incomplete?** Robustness to user-provided natural language needs testing.
3. **Can the system learn and improve from vet feedback over time?** Is there a feedback loop to fine-tune the VLM on local case histories?
4. **How does edge-cloud latency affect user experience and clinical decision-making?** What is acceptable latency for a real-time screening workflow?
5. **How does the system perform on rare or emerging diseases?** Zero-shot generalization is weaker on out-of-distribution cases.
6. **What is the cost of operation (cloud API calls, edge hardware) per screening?** Is it economically viable for low-resource settings?
7. **Can the system explain its reasoning?** Interpretability of the VLM's classification is crucial for clinician trust and liability.

## Key Takeaway
Agentic workflows plus multimodal inputs outperform static VLM predictions for veterinary disease screening.
