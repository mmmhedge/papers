# APO: Unsupervised Atomic Policy Optimization for 3D Structure Prediction of Atomic Systems

**Authors:** Shentong Mo, Yatao Bian
**Published:** 2026-07-30 | **arXiv:** [2607.28553v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28553v1)

## Summary
This paper introduces APO (Atomic Policy Optimization), an unsupervised method for predicting 3D structures of proteins and crystals without requiring expensive experimental reference data. The key innovation is using physical principles—thermodynamic stability and structural consistency—as self-supervision signals instead of ground-truth coordinates, which enables the model to learn better structures than supervised methods while dramatically reducing labeling costs.

## Theoretical Background
The work builds on several foundations: (1) flow-matching models (like FlowDPO), which generate 3D structures by learning to reverse a diffusion process; (2) reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) and its recent variant, group-relative policy optimization (GRPO), which aligns model outputs with human preferences; and (3) physical principles in materials science, where thermodynamic stability and low-energy configurations are natural validity criteria. The paper adapts GRPO—a reinforcement-learning algorithm designed for language models—to the continuous, geometric domain of atomic coordinates, treating structure prediction as a policy optimization problem where the "policy" is a generative model.

## Core Contributions
1. **Unsupervised alignment framework**: Eliminates dependence on ground-truth coordinates by using physical signals (thermodynamic stability + structural modes) as reward functions, enabling training in data-scarce regimes.
2. **Dual-reward mechanism**: (i) A structural mode reward via eigen-decomposition of sample similarities that identifies dominant latent configurations; (ii) a thermodynamic stability reward that enforces physical plausibility.
3. **Self-correction through grouping**: The framework groups sampled structures and identifies physically consistent configurations within each group, enabling implicit error correction without explicit supervision.
4. **State-of-the-art results**: Outperforms fully supervised baselines on crystal and antibody structure prediction benchmarks; also straightens probability paths for faster inference.

## Connections to Other Work
APO extends [[GRPO]] and related policy optimization methods from language models into geometric domains, paralleling recent work applying RL alignment to vision and scientific computing. It contrasts with supervised preference learning approaches (FlowDPO, DPO) by replacing noisy coordinate matching with intrinsic physical signals—a shift similar to how [[RLHF]] moved beyond supervised imitation. The thermodynamic stability reward connects to long-standing work in computational chemistry and molecular dynamics, while the geometric sampling strategy echoes techniques in structure sampling and protein folding (e.g., AlphaFold's confidence calibration).

## Practical Applications
1. **De novo drug discovery**: Predict novel protein structures for uncharacterized drug targets without expensive wet-lab crystallography or NMR experiments, accelerating candidate screening.
2. **Materials discovery**: Generate viable crystal structures for novel compounds (batteries, semiconductors, catalysts) without requiring synthetic samples or DFT calculations for every candidate.
3. **Antibody engineering**: Design and predict 3D structures of engineered antibodies for therapeutic development without high-resolution experimental data at early stages.
4. **Protein engineering**: Predict misfolded or engineered protein variants to guide iterative design before costly expression and purification.
5. **Thermodynamic property estimation**: Use discovered structures to estimate phase diagrams, solubility, or stability of materials in silico.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Reward design risk**: The dual-reward mechanism assumes thermodynamic stability and structural mode consistency are sufficient proxies for correctness. This may fail for metastable phases, kinetically trapped states, or biomolecules in non-equilibrium conditions.
2. **Limited generalization**: Trained on specific datasets (crystals, antibodies); unclear how well the method transfers to novel system types (RNA, membrane proteins, amorphous materials) or out-of-distribution chemistries.
3. **Computational cost of eigen-decomposition**: Performing eigen-decomposition on sample similarity matrices scales poorly with group size; practical limits on batch sizes and diversity may constrain scalability.
4. **Lack of active-site fidelity**: The framework optimizes global structure but may not guarantee correct active sites, binding pockets, or functional domains critical for drug binding.
5. **Validation bottleneck**: Without ground truth, the paper relies on match rates to known structures; truly novel structures cannot be validated without expensive experiments, creating a leakage risk.
6. **Group composition sensitivity**: Performance likely depends on group composition and diversity; unclear how to robustly select group size and sampling strategy in practice.

## Open Questions
1. **Scalability to larger systems**: How does APO perform on very large proteins (>1000 residues) or complex multi-component crystals? Does the eigen-decomposition bottleneck become prohibitive?
2. **Transfer learning**: Can an APO-trained model transfer to new chemistries or protein families with minimal fine-tuning, or must it be retrained from scratch?
3. **Active-site prediction**: Can the method be augmented to specifically optimize for functional or binding-site accuracy, not just global structure?
4. **Hybrid supervision**: Does adding small amounts of ground-truth data (semi-supervised APO) further improve performance, and if so, what is the data-efficiency frontier?
5. **Uncertainty quantification**: How can the method output confidence estimates or uncertainty bounds for predicted structures without ground truth?
6. **Kinetic vs. thermodynamic**: Can APO be extended to predict kinetically trapped or metastable phases that are thermodynamically unfavorable but experimentally accessible?
7. **Ablation studies**: Which component of the dual-reward mechanism drives performance—the structural mode reward, the stability reward, or their interplay?

## Key Takeaway
Unsupervised learning of 3D atomic structures using physics as a signal beats supervised methods trained on noisy labels.
