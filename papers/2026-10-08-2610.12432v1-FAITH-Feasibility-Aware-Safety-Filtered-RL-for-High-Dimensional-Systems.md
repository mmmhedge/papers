# FAITH: Feasibility-Aware Safety-Filtered RL for High-Dimensional Systems

**Authors:** Songyuan Zhang, Baljeet Singh, Sarthak Ranjeet Kaingade, Chuchu Fan, Bryan Trinh
**Published:** 2026-10-08 | **arXiv:** [2610.12432v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12432v1)

## Summary

This paper presents FAITH, a framework for safe reinforcement learning in high-dimensional systems that separates safety constraints from task performance using a learned safety filter rather than requiring an explicit safety model. The key innovation is using a neural network to approximate safe actions and apply minimal-intervention filtering at execution time, allowing robots to learn effective behaviors while maintaining safety constraints—demonstrated on real humanoid robots with 29 degrees of freedom.

## Theoretical Background

Safe reinforcement learning traditionally combines safety and task objectives into a single policy loss, which creates competing gradient signals and unstable training. Safety filters decouple these concerns by separating the policy optimization phase from action selection, but prior work typically requires three things: (1) an analytical safety function (e.g., distance to obstacles), (2) knowledge of system dynamics, and (3) the assumption that at least one safe action exists in every state. Classical minimal-intervention filters project unsafe actions to the nearest safe alternative, but they are myopic—they only minimize immediate deviation from the proposed action and ignore whether that "safe" choice ruins long-horizon task success. The paper builds on [[constraint-based reinforcement learning]] and [[safety filters]] literature but removes the requirement for analytic models by learning safety values end-to-end.

## Core Contributions

1. **Feasibility-aware value learning**: FAITH learns an approximate safety value function (state-action safety return) that captures long-horizon safety without requiring system dynamics or an explicit safety function.

2. **Amortized filtering**: Instead of solving an optimization problem at each timestep (expensive for high-dimensional action spaces), the filter is implemented as a feedforward neural network, making it computationally tractable for complex systems like humanoids.

3. **Handling infeasibility gracefully**: When no action satisfies the safety condition, the filter doesn't fail—it instead selects the action with minimum predicted peak harm (most graceful degradation).

4. **Decoupled task learning**: The task policy optimizes purely on task return through the filtered dynamics, eliminating competing safety gradients and recovering the true constrained optimization problem.

5. **Empirical validation on real hardware**: Demonstrated on a real Unitree G1 humanoid robot, achieving 99.95% safety compliance while retaining 97% of unfiltered task performance.

## Connections to Other Work

This work sits at the intersection of several paradigms:

- **Safety filters vs. constrained RL**: Unlike [[constrained MDPs]] (which bake constraints into the Bellman equation), FAITH applies filtering at action execution, avoiding the need for constraint-aware value iteration.
- **Model-based vs. model-free**: Extends safety filtering to model-free settings by learning safety values rather than requiring dynamics models.
- **Safe exploration**: Related to [[safe exploration]] and [[safety shielding]], but with the novelty of learning the shield rather than specifying it analytically.
- **Real-world robotics**: Connects to practical safe RL applications in [[robot learning]] where analytic safety models are difficult to specify.

## Practical Applications

1. **Manufacturing robotics**: Automated assembly tasks where robots must avoid colliding with equipment or humans while meeting production throughput targets.

2. **Healthcare robotics**: Surgical assistance or physical therapy robots that must maintain safety margins around patient anatomy while performing precise manipulation.

3. **Autonomous driving**: Learning lane-keeping and collision-avoidance policies in simulation, then deploying with guarantees that the learned filter prevents harmful actions.

4. **Humanoid assistive robots**: Elderly care or home assistance robots that learn manipulation and navigation tasks while reliably avoiding falls or contact with people.

5. **Inspection drones**: Aerial systems learning to navigate complex environments (e.g., industrial facilities, disaster zones) while keeping safety-critical zones off-limits.

## Product & Startup Ideas

1. **SafeRL-as-a-Service platform**: Cloud service that takes a task policy and learns a safety filter for robotics companies' existing systems, enabling rapid safe deployment without retraining from scratch.

2. **Real-time safety certification tool**: Software that learns safety filters for any RL policy and provides runtime guarantees (e.g., "this policy violates safety constraints <0.05% of the time"), useful for regulatory compliance in healthcare/automotive.

3. **Humanoid robot training suite**: Specialized platform for humanoid manufacturers to safely learn complex behaviors (climbing stairs, manipulation) on physical robots with automatic safety degradation mode.

4. **Sim-to-real safety validator**: Tool that learns safety filters in simulation, then validates they transfer to real hardware by detecting when real-world dynamics violate learned safety assumptions.

5. **Autonomous system risk quantifier**: Enterprise software for logistics/delivery companies to evaluate whether a given learned policy meets insurable risk thresholds using FAITH's harm-prediction mechanism.

## Technical Limitations

1. **Safety value function approximation error**: The learned safety value is an approximation; adversarial examples or distribution shift could cause the filter to miss unsafe states that weren't well-represented in training data.

2. **Assumption of continued feasibility during learning**: The framework assumes enough safe actions exist during policy training to collect meaningful experience; if the task pushes the robot to intrinsically unsafe regions early, the task policy may fail to bootstrap.

3. **Computational overhead during deployment**: Although amortized, the filter network still adds a forward pass at every timestep; for microsecond-critical systems this could be problematic.

4. **Hard constraints on action deviation**: Minimal-intervention filtering only considers distance to the proposed action; in some domains (e.g., balancing), small deviations can cascade into instability.

5. **Limited theoretical guarantees**: No formal proof that the learned filter maintains safety; only empirical evidence. The harm prediction mechanism is heuristic rather than provably optimal.

6. **Sim-to-real transfer of safety values**: Safety is environment-dependent (real friction, contact models differ); the safety filter learned in simulation may not generalize.

## Open Questions

1. **Safety under distribution shift**: How does the filter degrade when the test environment has different dynamics (e.g., robot wearing different payload, working on different surface)? Can we detect such shifts online?

2. **Worst-case safety analysis**: Can we derive formal bounds on the maximum harm the filter allows given training data and function approximator size?

3. **Multi-agent safety**: How does FAITH extend when multiple learned policies interact? Do filters need to coordinate, or do they remain independent?

4. **Sparse safety signal learning**: Current work assumes clear safety signal; what if safety is only defined sparsely (e.g., "don't break the vase")? Can the safety value be learned from rare violations?

5. **Scaling to longer horizons**: Does the learned safety value remain accurate for very long tasks (hours of continuous operation), or does prediction error accumulate?

6. **Adaptive filtering**: Should the safety threshold adapt based on task progress or learned confidence? Could the filter become less conservative as the policy matures?

## Key Takeaway

Learning safety filters with neural networks enables safe robot learning without requiring analytic safety models or system dynamics.
