# Relay, Don't Route: Adaptive Population Handoff for Cost-Efficient LLM-Driven Evolution

**Authors:** Sichun Luo, Yi Huang, Guanzhi Deng, Haibo Wang, Haochen Luo et al.
**Published:** 2026-08-06 | **arXiv:** [2608.05651v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05651v1)

## Summary
Large language model (LLM)-driven evolution has shown promise for program search and algorithm discovery, but relying on strong models throughout long evolutionary runs is costly. A natural alternative is to combine cheap and strong models under a fixed inference budget. However, existing approaches typically allocate models at the level of individual queries or mutation steps, overlooking that evolutionary search is \textit{stateful}: each generated candidate changes the population from which subsequent mutations are produced.

## Theoretical Background
AI agents combine a language model with tools, memory, and an execution loop. Their quality depends not only on individual answers but on decisions made across a complete trajectory, which makes evaluation and debugging harder than ordinary text generation.

The paper is positioned against prior approaches described in its abstract and asks whether a more targeted representation, evaluation method, or optimization procedure can improve the relevant trade-off.

## Core Contributions
1. Large language model (LLM)-driven evolution has shown promise for program search and algorithm discovery, but relying on strong models throughout long evolutionary runs is costly.
2. We empirically analyze LLM-driven evolutionary trajectories and find that search progress is strongly front-loaded, early trajectory performance is informative but noisy, and cheap models recover much of the early progress achieved by strong models at lower cost.
3. Motivated by these findings, we propose \textbf{\model}, a training-free framework that shifts budget allocation from individual calls to evolving populations through adaptive \textit{population handoff}.
