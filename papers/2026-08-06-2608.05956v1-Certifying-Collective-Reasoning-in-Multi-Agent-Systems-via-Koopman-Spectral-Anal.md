# Certifying Collective Reasoning in Multi-Agent Systems via Koopman Spectral Analysis

**Authors:** Nuzhat Khan, Indrakshi Dey
**Published:** 2026-08-06 | **arXiv:** [2608.05956v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05956v1)

## Summary
Orchestrated collectives of large language model (LLM) agents that debate and vote are an emerging form of computational intelligence: the intelligent behaviour resides in the \emph{interaction}, not in any single agent. They improve task accuracy, yet remain black boxes at the system level: there is no principled test of convergence, no bound on the rounds needed, and no faithful account of what drove a decision. This paper develops a novel framework based on Koopman operator theory and validates its theoretical guarantees on multi-agent consensus dynamics.

## Theoretical Background
Multi-agent systems study how autonomous participants coordinate, compete, and form collective outcomes. Their behavior can depend on interaction structure and feedback dynamics rather than the capability of any single agent.

The paper is positioned against prior approaches described in its abstract and asks whether a more targeted representation, evaluation method, or optimization procedure can improve the relevant trade-off.

## Core Contributions
1. This paper develops a novel framework based on Koopman operator theory and validates its theoretical guarantees on multi-agent consensus dynamics.
2. Treating the collective as one nonlinear dynamical system on a communication graph, we read its essential behaviour off the spectrum of its Koopman transfer operator, an exact linear representation of the nonlinear dynamics estimated from interaction traces.
