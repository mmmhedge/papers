# AlphaDiverse: Post-Training Local Quantitative Research Agents for Diverse Exploration in Alpha Factor Mining

**Authors:** Qingzhuo Wang, Zikun Wei, Zhihua Wei, Wen Shen
**Published:** 2026-09-24 | **arXiv:** [2609.29014v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29014v1)

## Summary
This paper proposes AlphaDiverse, a system that uses local language model agents to automatically discover trading strategies (alpha factors) without relying on external APIs. The key innovation is combining diverse exploration strategies with post-training techniques to prevent the system from getting stuck in a few successful but repetitive patterns, enabling both competitive prediction and broader discovery.

## Theoretical Background
The work builds on two main foundations. First, large language models (LLMs) have recently been applied to financial research automation, where agents can reason about economic mechanisms and propose trading factors. Second, multi-agent systems — where multiple AI agents cooperate with different roles — have shown promise for complex problem-solving. The paper addresses a known problem in reinforcement learning called **mode collapse**: when a system converges to a few successful solutions and stops exploring alternatives. The authors also draw on supervised fine-tuning (warm-starting agents with labeled data) and **GRPO** (a reinforcement learning variant that optimizes multiple objectives simultaneously). The financial domain requires agents to reason about **alpha factors** — predictive patterns in stock prices that can generate excess returns.

## Core Contributions
1. **Diverse path collection strategy**: The system generates complementary research portfolios and varies research environments across training loops to ensure agents explore different economic mechanisms rather than converging to the same ones.

2. **Local agent post-training pipeline**: Instead of relying on external APIs, the authors warm-start Planner and Realizer agents using supervised fine-tuning on collected diverse research traces, enabling cost control and privacy.

3. **Joint GRPO optimization**: A novel training method that simultaneously optimizes predictive quality and diversity of discovered factors, preventing research path collapse.

4. **Proper evaluation protocol**: Training feedback uses only inner-period data; final models are frozen and evaluated on outer-period data, avoiding data leakage and test-set tuning.

## Practical Applications
1. **In-house quant research**: Hedge funds and asset managers can deploy local agents to discover proprietary trading factors without sending research data to third-party LLM APIs, protecting trade secrets.

2. **Cost-efficient portfolio research**: Banks and trading firms reduce per-query costs by running fine-tuned local models instead of paying for continuous API access to cloud-based LLMs.

3. **Multi-strategy fund management**: A single system discovers complementary alpha factors across different economic regimes, enabling a fund to maintain diversified strategy portfolios and reduce drawdowns from single-strategy dependence.

4. **Emerging market research**: Firms can quickly adapt agents to new stock universes (as demonstrated on Chinese markets) with minimal retraining, enabling faster alpha mining in less-researched regions.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **AlphaMiner SaaS**: A white-label platform where quant teams upload their own market data and historical research, then deploy fine-tuned local agents that auto-discover factors and output trading signals with full data control.

2. **Diversity-as-a-Service for funds**: A consulting product that audits existing quant strategies for mode collapse, then uses AlphaDiverse-style methods to discover complementary factors, reducing portfolio concentration risk.

3. **Local LLM agent toolkit for finance**: Open-source framework for quantitative researchers to build and fine-tune multi-agent systems for alpha mining, with built-in diversity penalties and proper backtesting protocols.

4. **Continuous factor research automation**: Sell a managed service where agents run nightly on a fund's proprietary data, continuously discovering fresh factors and alerting PMs when new economic mechanisms emerge.

5. **Compliance-first AI research**: A product targeting regulated institutions (banks, pension funds) that offers on-premise or private-cloud agent systems, with audit trails and explainability tools to satisfy compliance officers.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Evaluation on single markets**: Experiments only cover Chinese stock universes; generalization to US, European, or emerging markets with different microstructure is unvalidated.

2. **No comparison to baselines**: The paper lacks ablation studies or head-to-head comparisons with simpler approaches (e.g., standard RL without diversity penalties, or pure supervised learning).

3. **Scalability unclear**: It's unknown how the system scales to very large universes (thousands of stocks) or high-frequency data; computational costs for training multiple diverse agents aren't discussed.

4. **Factor interpretability**: The paper doesn't address whether discovered factors are economically interpretable or statically significant beyond predictive accuracy—they could be spurious patterns.

5. **Data leakage risk**: Even with inner/outer splits, test periods are relatively short; transaction costs, slippage, and regime shifts in live trading could erode discovered factors quickly.

6. **Assumption of stable environment**: The method assumes economic mechanisms repeat across time and stock universes; if market structure changes fundamentally, local agents may need frequent retraining.

## Open Questions
1. How sensitive is performance to the diversity reward weight in GRPO? Is there a principled way to set it?

2. Can the framework discover factors that are robust across different market regimes (bull, bear, crisis)? How does diversity help there?

3. What is the runtime and memory footprint of local agents compared to API-based systems in production?

4. Do discovered factors remain statistically significant in live trading, or do they decay within weeks/months due to mean reversion?

5. How does the method perform on assets with lower liquidity or emerging market stocks where alpha decay is faster?

6. Can the diversity mechanism be extended to multi-objective optimization beyond factor mining (e.g., risk management, execution)?

## Key Takeaway
Local agents with forced diversity can discover multiple profitable trading factors without external APIs, preventing research from converging to the same patterns repeatedly.
