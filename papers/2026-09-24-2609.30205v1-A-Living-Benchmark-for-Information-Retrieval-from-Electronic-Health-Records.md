# A Living Benchmark for Information Retrieval from Electronic Health Records

**Authors:** Jordan L. Cahoon, Chloe O. Stanwyck, Sulaiman Somani, Philip Chung, Kevin R Keet et al.
**Published:** 2026-09-24 | **arXiv:** [2609.30205v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30205v1)

## Summary
This paper introduces BRIE, a scalable framework that automatically generates question-answer pairs from patient medical records to evaluate how well LLMs can retrieve and synthesize clinical information. The work addresses a real problem: existing benchmarks for clinical AI are manually built, expensive to update, and become outdated quickly as models improve—making it hard to safely deploy LLMs in hospitals.

## Theoretical Background
The paper builds on two key ideas: (1) benchmark-driven evaluation of NLP systems, which has long been central to measuring progress in language understanding tasks; and (2) the specific challenges of deploying LLMs in healthcare, where errors can have serious consequences. Clinical information retrieval differs from general web search because it requires synthesizing patient history across multiple visits and documents, and clinicians validate whether answers are medically sound. The authors leverage existing EHR data as a natural source of evaluation material rather than relying on human annotators to manually write test cases.

## Core Contributions
- **Automated benchmark generation pipeline**: A method to extract question-answer pairs directly from longitudinal EHR notes, scaling beyond manual curation.
- **Clinician validation methodology**: Nineteen clinicians validated the generator itself, ensuring the benchmark produces clinically meaningful and diverse questions.
- **Multiple valid answers**: The framework captures variation in how different clinicians reason about the same patient case, enabling more robust evaluation than single-answer benchmarks.
- **Empirical findings**: Evaluation of nine LLMs shows state-of-the-art systems frequently miss clinically important details, especially on synthesis questions spanning multiple encounters.
- **Living benchmark**: A continuously refreshable dataset that guards against benchmark leakage (where models memorize test data) and evolves as models and EHRs change.

## Connections to Other Work
This work sits at the intersection of [[large-language-models]] evaluation and healthcare AI. It relates to broader efforts in [[NLP]] benchmark design (e.g., SQuAD, GLUE) but adapts them to the clinical domain where stakes are higher and manual curation is impractical. It also connects to [[alignment]] concerns—ensuring deployed systems behave safely—and addresses the problem of benchmark obsolescence, similar to concerns in adversarial robustness and model watermarking. The emphasis on clinician validation echoes human-in-the-loop AI evaluation practices.

## Practical Applications
- **Clinical decision support systems**: Hospitals deploying LLM assistants can use BRIE to certify that their systems reliably extract critical patient information before rollout.
- **EHR vendor evaluation**: Healthcare IT companies can benchmark their LLM integrations against a standard, comparable metric across different clinical notes systems.
- **Regulatory compliance**: Healthcare regulators (FDA, etc.) could adopt scalable, continuously updated benchmarks as part of safety certification for AI in patient care.
- **Research quality assurance**: Academic medical centers developing clinical NLP can validate internal systems without manual annotation overhead.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **Clinical AI Auditing SaaS**: A platform that runs BRIE and related benchmarks against a hospital's deployed LLM systems monthly, flagging performance drift and safety regressions.
2. **EHR-native evaluation layer**: Integrate benchmark generation directly into EHR software (Epic, Cerner, etc.) so every healthcare system gets automatic, rolling evaluation of its AI features.
3. **Synthetic clinical QA marketplace**: Sell access to the BRIE generator and validated questions to healthcare AI companies, labs, and startups as a standardized evaluation suite.
4. **Clinician-in-the-loop annotation service**: Offer managed benchmark validation where a network of clinicians continuously curate and validate auto-generated questions for specific medical domains or institutions.
5. **Safety dashboards for hospital IT**: Dashboard product that ingests BRIE benchmark results and alerts hospital admins when LLM performance drops below clinical safety thresholds.

## Technical Limitations
- **Generalization across EHR systems**: The generator is trained on specific EHR formats and vocabularies; transferring to different hospital systems may require retuning.
- **Hallucination in question generation**: The auto-generated questions might themselves be flawed or clinically nonsensical; clinician validation mitigates but doesn't eliminate this.
- **Bias in validation**: Nineteen clinicians provide validation, but their specialty, experience, and practice patterns may not represent all clinical contexts.
- **Incomplete ground truth**: Questions derived from existing EHR notes only capture what was documented; missing or delayed diagnoses in the original records propagate to the benchmark.
- **Temporal leakage**: If the same LLM saw EHR-like data during pretraining, it may have memorized similar cases; continuous refresh helps but doesn't fully solve this.

## Open Questions
- How does performance on BRIE correlate with actual safety incidents or clinician satisfaction in deployed systems?
- Can the framework detect when an LLM is overfitting to specific hospitals' documentation styles?
- How should the benchmark weight rare but high-risk clinical scenarios (e.g., drug interactions) versus common cases?
- Does the generator introduce systematic biases in question difficulty or topic distribution, and how sensitive is this to the choice of clinician validators?
- Can the framework extend to other structured medical domains (radiology reports, pathology, genetic data) or does it require EHR-specific adaptation?

## Key Takeaway
Hospitals can now automatically generate and refresh clinical AI evaluation benchmarks, enabling rigorous safety checks without expensive manual work.
