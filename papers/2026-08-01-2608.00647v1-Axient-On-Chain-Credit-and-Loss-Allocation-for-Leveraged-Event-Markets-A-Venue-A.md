# Axient: On-Chain Credit and Loss Allocation for Leveraged Event Markets: A Venue-Agnostic Protocol for Traders, Credit Providers, Market Makers, and Liquidation Backstops

**Authors:** Maksym Nechepurenko
**Published:** 2026-08-01 | **arXiv:** [2608.00647v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00647v1)

## Summary
This paper designs an on-chain protocol for managing credit and losses in leveraged event markets—where traders borrow capital to amplify their exposure to prediction market outcomes. The work formalizes the interactions between traders, credit providers, market makers, and liquidators, proving that the system maintains balanced accounting and loss priority through deterministic mechanisms. It matters because decentralized leverage has historically been fragile; this protocol provides formal guarantees and empirical validation that capital flows correctly even under stress scenarios like liquidation cascades.

## Theoretical Background
The paper builds on traditional finance concepts of credit provision, collateral management, and loss waterfalls (ordered priority for absorbing losses). It extends these into the blockchain context, where on-chain protocols must be deterministic and transparent rather than relying on trusted intermediaries. The work models multi-agent systems—traders, liquidity providers, market makers, and liquidators—each with incentives to supply or withdraw capital. It employs formal verification (mathematical proofs) and agent-based simulation to validate that protocol rules preserve invariants like debt priority and position ownership across all possible states, including tail-risk scenarios like runs (sudden mass withdrawals) and contagion (losses spreading across venues).

## Core Contributions
1. **Venue-agnostic architecture**: A credit protocol that abstracts away specific market venues, allowing traders to use leverage across different platforms without rebuilding the credit layer each time.
2. **Formal accounting model**: Rigorous proofs that pool shares, debt obligations, and residual ownership are correctly allocated even under partial settlement, liquidation, and withdrawal stress.
3. **Loss waterfall and capital structure**: Explicit formalization of how losses cascade through Senior Credit LPs → Liquidation Backstop Providers, with formal bounds on Senior LP impairment.
4. **Mechanistic contagion analysis**: Empirical demonstration (70+ million invariant checks) of how market-maker capacity constraints and leverage concentration increase systemic shortfall risk.
5. **Deterministic settlement guarantees**: Integer-unit accounting and idempotent settlement rules ensure repeatable, auditable results across transactions.

## Practical Applications
1. **Prediction market leverage**: Traders on Polymarket or similar platforms can use Axient-style credit to take larger positions without centralized brokers.
2. **Decentralized derivatives**: Protocols like Synthetix or dYdX can adopt this credit layer to reduce operational risk in leveraged trading.
3. **Cross-venue aggregation**: Traders holding collateral on one blockchain can borrow against it to trade on another, with transparent loss rules.
4. **Liquidation services**: The formalized loss waterfall enables third-party liquidation bots to operate with known recovery guarantees, reducing adverse selection.
5. **Capital markets on-chain**: Institutions managing leveraged fixed-income or commodity exposure can use this as a transparent, auditable credit infrastructure.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Synthetic behavior, not forecasts**: The agent-based experiments assume author-specified agent behavior (e.g., fixed withdrawal rates, liquidator entry thresholds). Real market behavior under stress may differ.
2. **Venue-agnostic abstraction**: The protocol assumes venues expose consistent liquidity and settlement APIs; fragmented or illiquid venues may break the model's assumptions.
3. **Formal verification scope**: Proofs cover accounting invariants but do not address cryptographic security, smart-contract implementation bugs, or oracle manipulation.
4. **Leverage feedback loops**: The model may underestimate cascading effects if leverage demand spikes sharply (e.g., due to market events or herding).
5. **Collateral quality**: The framework assumes collateral (e.g., stablecoins, event tokens) is stable in value; sudden depeg or venue-specific liquidity collapses are not modeled.
6. **Withdrawal queue design**: Deterministic queues may be perceived as unfair under stress, creating legal or social-contract risk in decentralized governance.

## Open Questions
1. How do endogenous leverage demand and interest rates equilibrate as Senior LP capital becomes scarce or abundant? Are there multiple stable equilibria or bifurcations?
2. What happens to the loss waterfall if collateral undergoes partial recovery after liquidation (e.g., a venue is hacked and funds are partially recovered weeks later)?
3. Can the protocol handle heterogeneous collateral types (e.g., correlated assets like BTC and ETH) without explicit modeling of correlation risk?
4. How sensitive are the contagion bounds to venue-specific liquidity shocks (e.g., if a prediction market oracle goes offline)?
5. Can liquidators be incentivized to operate profitably *and* reliably even when capital is abundant and liquidation opportunities shrink?
6. How would the protocol interact with cross-chain messaging and bridge risks?

## Key Takeaway
Formalized on-chain credit for leveraged prediction markets, with proven loss allocation and empirical stress testing under liquidation and contagion.
