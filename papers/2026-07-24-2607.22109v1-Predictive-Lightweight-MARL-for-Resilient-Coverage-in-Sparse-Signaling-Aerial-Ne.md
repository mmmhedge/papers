# Predictive Lightweight MARL for Resilient Coverage in Sparse-Signaling Aerial Networks

**Authors:** Chuan-Chi Lai, Ang-Hsun Tsai
**Published:** 2026-07-24 | **arXiv:** [2607.22109v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22109v1)

## Summary
This paper presents PL-MARL, a lightweight multi-agent reinforcement learning framework that keeps drone swarms coordinated even when communication is severely restricted. The key innovation is using physics-based trajectory prediction to reconstruct what neighboring drones are doing without constantly exchanging messages, allowing the swarm to maintain coverage and avoid collisions while minimizing bandwidth usage—critical for real-world aerial networks where spectrum is scarce.

## Theoretical Background
Multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) trains multiple autonomous agents to learn cooperative behaviors through trial and error, typically relying on frequent information exchange between agents. However, UAV (unmanned aerial vehicle) swarms face a fundamental constraint: communication bandwidth is limited, and frequent signaling drains batteries and creates interference. Traditional MARL assumes rich, low-latency communication channels; this assumption breaks in sparse-signaling environments. The paper builds on physical modeling concepts—specifically kinematic constraints that govern how objects move—to replace explicit communication with learned predictive models. This trades computation (on-board trajectory inference) for communication (fewer messages), a principle from distributed systems and control theory.

## Core Contributions
1. **Kinematic-Aware Inference Engine**: A lightweight module that reconstructs neighbor drone trajectories using physics priors (velocity, acceleration bounds, motion constraints) rather than waiting for position updates. This decouples coordination from signaling frequency.
2. **Resilience under extreme scarcity**: Demonstrates that coverage and mission continuity are maintained even with signaling reduced to near-silent operation, and with node (drone) failures.
3. **Computation-for-communication trade-off formalization**: Explicitly quantifies how on-board inference replaces bandwidth demand, enabling swarms to reserve spectrum for payload services (sensors, cameras) rather than coordination overhead.
4. **Lightweight architecture**: Framework is designed for resource-constrained embedded systems on small drones, not just simulation.

## Connections to Other Work
This work sits at the intersection of [[multi-agent-systems]], decentralized control, and communication-constrained optimization. It relates to:
- **Decentralized MARL**: Prior work assumes agents share state; this paper addresses the case where agents must be semi-autonomous due to comm blackouts.
- **Cooperative UAV control**: Existing approaches (centralized, or frequent message-passing) don't scale to large swarms or hostile RF environments; PL-MARL enables graceful degradation.
- **Physics-informed learning**: Similar to physics-informed neural networks (PINNs), the use of kinematic priors to constrain inference is a growing trend in embedded AI.
- **Information aging and staleness**: Related to stochastic optimization under delayed or missing observations.

## Practical Applications
1. **Military/surveillance swarms**: Maintaining formation and area coverage over contested terrain where adversaries jam communication or enforce silent protocols to avoid detection.
2. **Disaster response**: After infrastructure collapse (earthquakes, floods), drone swarms need to autonomously map damage and maintain coverage without relying on ground control or wifi.
3. **Remote sensing in rural/arctic regions**: Autonomous multi-drone surveys in areas with no stable comms infrastructure; drones maintain synchronized patterns via local inference.
4. **Spectrum-shared networks**: Drones operating in frequency bands shared with cellular or satellite services; minimizing drone signaling preserves capacity for primary users.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Kinematic model realism**: The framework assumes drones follow smooth, predictable kinematic laws. In reality, wind gusts, motor saturation, and sudden obstacles violate these priors; the paper does not quantify robustness to model mismatch.
2. **Scalability of inference**: As swarm size grows, each drone must infer trajectories for all others; computational cost grows quadratically. The paper does not discuss how to handle swarms beyond ~20 drones.
3. **Training data requirements**: PL-MARL is trained in simulation; sim-to-real transfer for aerial drones is notoriously difficult due to sensor noise and unmodeled dynamics. Not addressed.
4. **Failure mode prediction**: The paper does not specify what happens when a drone's inference diverges from reality (e.g., a neighbor is captured or crashes). No recovery mechanism is described.
5. **Limited to coverage tasks**: Results are specific to area coverage; applicability to other swarm tasks (synchronized manipulation, formation flight under dynamic constraints) is unclear.

## Open Questions
1. How does PL-MARL perform in highly dynamic environments (strong winds, moving obstacles) where kinematic priors are violated?
2. Can the framework scale to 50+ drone swarms? Is there a decentralized communication topology (e.g., multi-hop message passing with inference) that keeps latency low?
3. How is the system initialized and synchronized when drones cannot exchange time-stamped state? (Clock drift is a real problem in aerial swarms.)
4. Can adversaries exploit the predictable inference engine? E.g., by spoofing or jamming selectively, could they cause the swarm to miscoordinate?
5. What is the actual wall-clock latency of the Kinematic-Aware Inference Engine on embedded hardware? (The paper simulates; hardware results are essential for practical adoption.)

## Key Takeaway
Physics-based trajectory prediction lets drone swarms stay coordinated on almost no radio signals, trading bandwidth for on-board computation.
