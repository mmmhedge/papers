# Is Weight Tying Still Beneficial for Decoder-Only LLMs in Private Settings Under DP-SGD?

**Authors:** Razan El Mais, Ali Chehab, Ibrahim Issa, Razane Tajeddine
**Published:** 2026-09-30 | **arXiv:** [2609.40335v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40335v1)

## Summary
This paper examines whether weight tying — a parameter-sharing technique used in many language models to reduce memory — remains beneficial when training LLMs with differential privacy (DP), a mathematical guarantee that protects individual data during training. The authors find that untying the shared weights actually improves both accuracy and memory efficiency under privacy-preserving training, suggesting that standard architectural choices need reconsideration in privacy-constrained settings.

## Theoretical Background
Weight tying is a design choice where input and output embedding layers share the same parameters, reducing model size without hurting performance — a technique adopted in models like GPT-2 for efficiency. Differentially Private Stochastic Gradient Descent (DP-SGD) is a training algorithm that adds carefully calibrated noise to gradients to ensure no single training example can be reverse-engineered from the model, formalized through differential privacy, a rigorous mathematical definition of privacy guarantees. Ghost clipping is a memory optimization technique for DP-SGD that avoids materializing per-sample gradients. The paper investigates how weight tying interacts with these privacy mechanisms, which had not been systematically studied before.

## Core Contributions
1. **First empirical investigation** of weight tying under DP-SGD across multiple decoder-only architectures (GPT-2, DistilGPT-2), showing untied embeddings outperform tied ones by up to 4.74 percentage points on standard NLP benchmarks (SST-2, QNLI, QQP).
2. **Mechanistic insight**: Weight tying creates shared-parameter interactions that break the assumptions underlying ghost clipping, preventing the use of this memory optimization — a constraint that does not exist with untied embeddings.
3. **Practical efficiency gains**: Untied models achieve over 60% lower memory usage while preserving privacy guarantees, flipping conventional wisdom that weight tying is always preferable.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Limited scope**: Only tested on GPT-2 and DistilGPT-2 — unclear if findings generalize to larger models (GPT-3 scale), other architectures (encoder-decoder, fully untied models), or non-English tasks.
2. **Ghost clipping dependency**: The memory gains rely on ghost clipping compatibility; other privacy-preserving optimizations may not exhibit the same pattern.
3. **Privacy budget not fully characterized**: The paper doesn't deeply analyze how privacy budget (epsilon) affects the weight-tying trade-off across different sensitivity regimes.
4. **Missing ablations**: No systematic study of why weight tying specifically hurts under DP — is it gradient structure, noise scaling, or something else?
5. **Hyperparameter sensitivity**: Results may depend on DP-SGD hyperparameters (noise scale, clipping threshold, batch size) in ways not fully explored.

## Open Questions
1. Does the untied-embedding advantage hold for billion-parameter models, or is it an artifact of the smaller models tested?
2. How do other memory optimizations (low-rank adaptation, quantization, pruning) interact with weight tying under DP-SGD?
3. Can we theoretically explain *why* weight tying hurts under privacy — is it a fundamental incompatibility or a limitation of current ghost clipping algorithms?
4. Do vision transformers and other non-NLP domains show similar patterns?
5. Can adaptive privacy budgeting or mixed strategies (tying some layers, not others) recover benefits of both approaches?

## Key Takeaway
Untied embeddings beat weight tying for private LLM training, cutting memory by 60% while boosting accuracy.
