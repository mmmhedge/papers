# From Word Counts to Context: Topic Models for Asset Pricing

**Authors:** Kevin Foley, Jonathan Hartadi, Shivesh Prakash, Swapnil Vatsal
**Published:** 2026-09-28 | **arXiv:** [2609.34169v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34169v1)

## Summary
This paper asks whether modern sentence transformers can extract better risk signals from news text than traditional topic models like LDA for asset pricing. The authors compare these approaches on nearly 400,000 news articles and find that sentence transformers produce more coherent topics and higher Sharpe ratios in portfolio construction, though the evidence remains suggestive rather than conclusive.

## Theoretical Background
The paper sits at the intersection of two areas. First, quantitative finance uses systematic risk factors — patterns that explain stock returns across many assets — to construct portfolios. Second, natural language processing extracts meaning from text; traditional approaches like LDA (Latent Dirichlet Allocation) find latent topics by modeling word frequencies, while newer sentence transformers — neural networks that encode entire sentences into semantic vectors — capture contextual meaning more directly. The core question is whether richer semantic understanding of news articles improves financial factor construction.

## Core Contributions
1. **Direct empirical comparison**: Same 394,661 news articles, same downstream portfolio pipeline, varying only the text extraction method (LDA vs. sentence transformers). This controlled comparison is uncommon in finance papers.
2. **Coherence measurement**: Sentence transformer topics score higher on NPMI (Normalized Pointwise Mutual Information), a linguistic coherence metric, suggesting the extracted topics are semantically cleaner.
3. **Financial performance**: The sentence transformer approach yields higher Sharpe ratios (risk-adjusted returns), with an exploratory multi-horizon model reaching 1.03.
4. **Honest limitations**: The authors acknowledge that statistical tests do not establish outperformance, flagging data-snooping risk and the need for walk-forward validation.

## Connections to Other Work
This work bridges [[Natural Language Processing]] and [[Quantitative Finance]]. It relates to:
- **Factor models** in finance: Fama–French frameworks extract risk factors; this paper explores whether news context can improve factor construction.
- **Neural encoders for finance**: Sentence transformers (e.g., BERT-based models) have appeared in sentiment analysis and anomaly detection for markets, but rarely in systematic factor design.
- **Topic modeling in finance**: Prior work used LDA for earnings calls, earnings surprises, and risk disclosure; this is the first systematic comparison with modern transformers at portfolio scale.
- **Text-to-returns pipelines**: Related to work on news-driven trading signals, but more disciplined about walk-forward testing.

## Practical Applications
1. **Systematic trading desks**: Hedge funds building news-driven factor models could adopt sentence transformers instead of LDA to improve risk signal extraction.
2. **Risk reporting**: Asset managers could use context-aware topics to tag and monitor systemic risks (inflation, supply-chain, geopolitical) for portfolio stress-testing.
3. **News filtering systems**: Trading platforms could rank news by semantic relevance to holdings, reducing noise in real-time decision systems.
4. **Factor licensing**: Quant firms could package context-aware news factors (e.g., "AI sentiment factor") as a service to smaller asset managers.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **News-to-Factors SaaS**: Cloud platform that ingests real-time news, extracts semantic topics via transformers, and outputs daily factor exposures for portfolio managers. Charge per API call or subscription tier.
2. **Risk Dashboard**: Tool for compliance/risk teams that maps news topics to portfolio holdings and alerts on tail-risk topics (e.g., regulatory changes, supply shocks). Sell to asset managers and banks.

## Technical Limitations
1. **No walk-forward validation**: The paper uses the same dataset for training and testing topics. Real-world performance requires testing on hold-out periods.
2. **Single-country data**: Likely US-only news; results may not generalize to other markets or languages.
3. **Frozen embeddings**: The sentence transformer is pre-trained and not fine-tuned on financial vocabulary or market regimes, potentially leaving money on the table.
4. **Statistical power**: Sample size and Sharpe variance are not reported; the observed 1.03 Sharpe could easily disappear with transaction costs or slippage.
5. **Survivorship bias**: Portfolio construction on historical data without accounting for delisted firms or corporate actions.
6. **Causal ambiguity**: It's unclear whether topics drive returns or simply correlate with returns driven by other fundamentals.

## Open Questions
1. **What is the minimal sample size and time span** required to robustly validate outperformance? Can this be tested on rolling windows?
2. **Does fine-tuning the sentence transformer on financial text or market signals** further improve coherence and financial performance?
3. **How do these factors perform in out-of-sample or post-2024 data?** Is there a regime change?
4. **Can you combine sentence-transformer topics with other news signals** (sentiment, event classification) in a learned way (e.g., via [[reinforcement-learning]] portfolio optimization)?
5. **Which sectors or market regimes benefit most** from context-aware news factors (e.g., tech vs. healthcare; bull vs. bear)?
6. **What is the computational cost-benefit trade-off** of sentence transformers vs. LDA for real-time trading systems?

## Key Takeaway
Sentence transformers extract more coherent and profitable news topics than LDA, but rigorous out-of-sample testing is needed before deployment.
