# Hierarchical Spatio-Temporal Transformer for Coherent Emergency Department Forecasting

**Authors:** Filipa Lino, Bárbara Tavares, Carlos Santiago, Cláudia Soares, Manuel Marques
**Published:** 2026-07-29 | **arXiv:** [2607.27106v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27106v1)

## Summary
This paper proposes HierSTT, a Transformer-based forecasting model that predicts emergency department (ED) patient demand at three hierarchical levels — hospitals, regions, and national systems — simultaneously while ensuring the predictions stay logically consistent across levels. The work addresses a real healthcare problem: most forecasting models make independent predictions at each level, leading to incoherent results where hospital forecasts don't sum to regional totals. HierSTT solves this by using a coherence-aware loss function and a spatio-temporal Transformer architecture, cutting prediction error by 32% compared to non-hierarchical baselines.

## Theoretical Background
The paper builds on two main ideas: (1) **Transformers** — neural networks that use attention mechanisms to weight relationships between different time steps and spatial locations, useful for sequence prediction — and (2) **hierarchical forecasting and reconciliation**, a classical statistics approach where predictions at multiple aggregation levels are forced to be mathematically consistent (e.g., hospital forecasts sum to regional totals). Existing work uses post-hoc reconciliation (adjusting independent forecasts after the fact) or classical methods like minimum trace reconciliation. This paper innovates by baking coherence constraints directly into the neural network training process via a specialized loss function, rather than treating it as a post-processing step.

## Core Contributions
1. **HierSTT architecture**: A novel end-to-end Transformer model that jointly forecasts demand at hospital, regional, and national levels in a single pass, with a Temporal Fusion Transformer capturing national dynamics and spatio-temporal encoder-decoder modules modeling lower levels conditioned on higher-level forecasts.
2. **Coherence-aware loss**: A training objective that penalizes cross-level inconsistencies, ensuring hospital-level predictions aggregate correctly to regional and national levels without post-hoc adjustment.
3. **Portuguese ED dataset**: A new nationwide benchmark covering 81 hospitals across 5 regional administrations with heterogeneous covariates at each hierarchical level.
4. **Empirical validation**: Demonstrates 32% WAPE (Weighted Absolute Percentage Error) improvement over non-hierarchical deep learning baselines and outperforms classical hierarchical reconciliation methods.

## Connections to Other Work
This work sits at the intersection of [[Transformers]] for time-series forecasting (building on Temporal Fusion Transformer) and hierarchical forecasting methods from operations research and econometrics. It improves on post-hoc reconciliation approaches (e.g., minimum trace, optimal combination) by embedding coherence constraints into the model itself — akin to how [[Constrained Optimization]] methods embed domain constraints directly into learning. The spatio-temporal modeling is related to graph neural networks and attention-based spatial reasoning, though this paper uses Transformer encoder-decoder blocks rather than explicit graph structures. It also connects to multi-level or multi-task learning, where shared representations at higher levels guide predictions at lower granularities.

## Practical Applications
1. **Hospital staffing and bed management**: Admitting departments can use hospital-level forecasts to schedule nurses, doctors, and allocate ICU beds 1–7 days in advance.
2. **Regional health system coordination**: Health authorities can identify which hospitals are approaching capacity and redistribute urgent cases to neighboring facilities with available beds.
3. **National capacity planning**: Health ministries can use national-level forecasts to allocate emergency funding, approve new facilities, or plan surge capacity during seasonal peaks.
4. **Supply chain and logistics**: Predict demand for ambulances, medical supplies, and laboratory resources at the system level with confidence that predictions remain logically consistent.
5. **Workforce planning**: Multi-year staffing decisions (hiring, training) can be informed by national-level demand trends while remaining grounded in realistic hospital-level estimates.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **ED Forecasting SaaS**: A white-label platform for hospital networks and regional health authorities to upload their own data, generate coherent multi-level forecasts, and receive staffing recommendations via API and dashboard.
2. **Healthcare Capacity Marketplace**: Use hierarchical forecasts to match elective procedures and non-urgent patients to ED slots across a network of hospitals, reducing wait times and ED overcrowding.
3. **Predictive Bed Management Tool**: Real-time forecasts feed an automated bed-allocation system that recommends patient flows between hospitals in a region to balance demand and prevent bottlenecks.
4. **Emergency Response Planning Software**: Integrate ED forecasts with weather, event calendars, and disease surveillance data to pre-position ambulances and mobile medical units at predicted hotspots.
5. **Health System Risk Analytics**: Sell coherent ED forecasts to insurance companies and hospital networks to optimize reimbursement models and identify chronically underfunded regions.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Data requirement**: The model requires clean, aligned time-series data across all hierarchical levels; sparse or missing data at any level could degrade coherence.
2. **Assumption of aggregation linearity**: The coherence loss assumes hospital demand strictly sums to regional demand, which may not hold perfectly if some patients are referred out of region or bypassed to other systems.
3. **Scalability to deeper hierarchies**: The paper tests only three levels (hospital, region, nation). Extending to finer granularities (e.g., ward-level within hospitals) or deeper geographical hierarchies is unclear.
4. **Cold-start problem**: New hospitals or regions with limited historical data may not benefit from the hierarchical sharing of information.
5. **Temporal stationarity**: The model may struggle with sudden, unprecedented shocks (pandemics, major strikes) not represented in training data.
6. **Computational cost**: Training end-to-end Transformer models on large datasets with spatio-temporal dimensions is memory-intensive and may not be practical for very large healthcare networks.

## Open Questions
1. How does HierSTT perform under distribution shift — e.g., after a pandemic, when ED visit patterns change structurally?
2. Can the coherence-aware loss be adapted to allow for some "leakage" (e.g., cross-border referrals) while remaining coherent?
3. Does the hierarchical architecture provide better uncertainty quantification (confidence intervals) compared to independent models, and how should these intervals be reconciled?
4. How sensitive is the model to the choice of hierarchical structure? Would a different tree (e.g., by geography vs. by hospital network) perform differently?
5. Can the framework be extended to multi-horizon forecasts where coherence must hold at each future time step, not just at point forecasts?
6. What is the minimal amount of historical data needed to train HierSTT effectively, and how does it vary by hospital size?

## Key Takeaway
Transformers enforcing logical consistency across hospital, regional, and national ED forecasts cut prediction error 32% while ensuring predictions don't contradict each other.
