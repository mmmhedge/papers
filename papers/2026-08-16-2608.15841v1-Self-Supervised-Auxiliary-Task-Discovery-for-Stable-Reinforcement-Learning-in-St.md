# Self-Supervised Auxiliary Task Discovery for Stable Reinforcement Learning in Stock Trading

**Authors:** Arishi Orra, Himanshu Choudhary, Manoj Thakur
**Published:** 2026-08-16 | **arXiv:** [2608.15841v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15841v1)

## Summary
This paper proposes an automated framework for discovering auxiliary tasks in reinforcement learning agents trained for stock trading. Instead of manually designing auxiliary tasks (which are helper objectives that improve learning), the system learns what tasks would best stabilize training and boost performance across changing market conditions. The approach is evaluated on four major stock indices and shows improved robustness and profitability compared to existing methods.

## Theoretical Background
Reinforcement learning (RL) trains agents to maximize cumulative rewards through interaction with an environment. In trading, the agent learns a policy—a decision rule for buying, selling, or holding stocks—but faces two core problems: non-stationary markets (where conditions change unpredictably) and noisy reward signals (where short-term price movements can mislead learning).

Auxiliary tasks are helper objectives used in representation learning—the process of learning compact, useful encodings of state information. The paper frames these tasks as General Value Functions (GVFs), which predict cumulative discounted returns under arbitrary conditions defined by "cumulants" (reward-like signals) and "discount factors" (time horizons). Meta-learning—learning to learn—is used here to optimize which auxiliary tasks are most useful for the main trading policy, accounting for their long-term impact on performance.

## Core Contributions
1. **Self-supervised auxiliary task discovery**: A secondary network learns to generate task definitions (cumulants and discount factors) automatically, eliminating manual design and regime-specific tuning.
2. **Meta-gradient optimization**: Tasks are updated based on their actual impact on trading performance over time, not just their immediate prediction accuracy.
3. **Empirical validation across regimes**: Demonstrates stability and improved returns on four distinct equity indices, suggesting the approach generalizes across different market structures.

## Connections to Other Work
This bridges [[Reinforcement Learning]] for finance and representation learning. It extends prior work on auxiliary tasks (common in deep RL) by automating their discovery. The use of GVFs connects to temporal abstraction and option discovery in RL. The meta-learning component relates to approaches like [[MAML]] but applied to task design rather than model initialization. It also touches on [[Risk Management]] implicitly through stability improvements.

## Practical Applications
1. **Automated trading systems**: Deploy in live markets where manual task engineering would be prohibitively expensive or slow to adapt to regime shifts.
2. **Risk-adjusted portfolio optimization**: Use discovered auxiliary tasks to predict volatility, drawdowns, or tail risk alongside return maximization.
3. **Cross-asset generalization**: Train once on major indices and adapt with minimal tuning to emerging markets or new asset classes.
4. **Robustness testing**: Use the framework to stress-test trading policies by discovering which auxiliary tasks reveal policy weaknesses.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **AdaptiveTrader SaaS**: Cloud-based platform where retail and institutional traders upload order flow data; the system auto-discovers auxiliary tasks and recommends portfolio actions with confidence intervals on stability.
2. **Market Regime Detection Toolkit**: Sell the auxiliary task discovery mechanism as a standalone API for hedge funds to detect when market regimes shift and retrain policies.
3. **Trading Policy Auditor**: Offer as a compliance tool—regulators and risk officers use discovered auxiliary tasks to understand what a black-box trading policy is "really" optimizing for.
4. **Multi-Strategy Fund Manager**: Spin up a quantitative fund that dynamically creates sub-strategies (each an auxiliary task) and blends them in real-time based on meta-learning signals.
5. **Educational Trading Sim**: Gamified platform where users learn to trade; the system auto-generates hints (auxiliary task predictions) tailored to their performance gaps.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Computational overhead**: Running two networks plus meta-gradient updates adds latency; unclear if this scales to high-frequency trading or many simultaneous assets.
2. **Market regime assumption**: The framework assumes auxiliary tasks remain useful across regime shifts, but extreme market breaks (flash crashes, policy shocks) may violate this.
3. **Reward signal quality**: Still depends on defining the primary trading reward (e.g., Sharpe ratio vs. raw return); noisy or misspecified rewards may lead to discovering adversarial auxiliary tasks.
4. **Limited baseline comparisons**: Paper doesn't compare against other meta-learning or curriculum learning approaches for trading; unclear if task discovery itself is the key benefit or if simpler curriculum designs suffice.
5. **Generalization across assets**: Tested on indices only; unclear how well discovered tasks transfer to individual stocks, crypto, or derivatives with vastly different dynamics.

## Open Questions
1. Can the auxiliary task discovery mechanism uncover interpretable, human-understandable objectives (e.g., "predict volatility spikes")—or are they opaque learned functions?
2. How does performance degrade if the secondary network is frozen after initial training, rather than continuously updated? (i.e., is continual meta-learning necessary?)
3. What happens in truly novel market regimes (e.g., a pandemic, a new asset class)—does the framework catastrophically fail or gracefully degrade?
4. Can discovered auxiliary tasks be visualized or audited for regulatory compliance?
5. How sensitive is the method to hyperparameters controlling meta-learning rate and discount factor ranges?

## Key Takeaway
Automatically discovering auxiliary learning tasks improves RL stability and returns in stock trading without manual task design.
