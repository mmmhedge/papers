# Weight Pair Encoding: Inducing a Smaller Grammar in Neural Network Weights

**Authors:** Irene Tallini, Daniele Solombrino, Alberto Cazzaniga, Emanuele Rodolà
**Published:** 2026-09-25 | **arXiv:** [2609.31564v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31564v1)

## Summary
This paper introduces Weight Pair Encoding (WeightPE), a method that compresses neural network weights by explicitly training them to admit smaller grammatical representations. Rather than using fixed-size codebooks, the approach uses grammar-based compression (like Re-Pair) as a training objective, achieving weights 0.38–0.43× the size of standard quantization methods on Vision Transformers, with modest accuracy drops.

## Theoretical Background
Neural network compression traditionally relies on quantization — reducing precision of weights from float32 to int8 or lower. The paper builds on two key ideas: (1) straight-through estimators, which allow gradients to flow through non-differentiable quantization operations during training, and (2) grammar-based compression algorithms like Re-Pair, which identify repeating patterns in sequences and build hierarchical rules to encode them compactly. The insight is that weights, when viewed as sequences of integers, can be compressed not just by reducing bit-depth but by discovering and exploiting internal structure through grammar induction.

## Core Contributions
1. **First explicit grammar-size objective**: Uses grammar compressibility as a direct training target (via L2 budget constraints on pattern rewriting), rather than treating compression as post-hoc.
2. **Integration with straight-through estimators**: Embeds a lossy Re-Pair compressor inside the backward pass, enabling end-to-end finetuning.
3. **Empirical validation across compressors**: Shows the learned weights compress well under multiple grammar algorithms (Re-Pair, LZ78, SEQUITUR) without retraining—suggesting the network learns genuinely compressible structure, not just fitting one algorithm.
4. **Hierarchical variable-length encoding**: Demonstrates that hierarchical pattern reuse outperforms flat codebooks for weight compression.

## Connections to Other Work
This extends [[quantization]] and network compression literature, but departs from scalar quantization (int8 QAT) by treating weights holistically as sequences. It relates to [[pruning]] and [[knowledge distillation]] as compression strategies, and shares mechanistic goals with [[mechanistic-interpretability]] work in finding interpretable structure in networks. The method is orthogonal to other compression techniques and could compose with them.

## Practical Applications
- **On-device deployment**: Smaller grammar representations reduce memory footprint for mobile/edge inference, especially valuable for transformers in resource-constrained environments.
- **Model transmission**: Reduced weight size cuts bandwidth for federated learning or model serving over limited networks.
- **Efficient fine-tuning**: Finetuned ViT models with smaller grammars enable faster checkpointing and loading cycles.
- **Hardware acceleration**: Grammar-based decoding could be optimized in specialized accelerators if the hierarchical structure is exploited.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **Compression-as-a-service platform**: Upload a model, specify a grammar budget, get back compressed weights with accuracy/compression tradeoff analysis for deployment targets.
2. **Mobile vision app framework**: Lightweight ViT deployment toolkit where WeightPE preprocessing is automated, targeting on-device photo classification without cloud inference.
3. **Edge AI chip compiler**: Hardware co-design startup that takes grammar-compressed models and generates optimized inference kernels that decode hierarchical patterns in real-time.
4. **Federated learning optimizer**: Enterprise tool that applies WeightPE to reduce communication costs in federated settings, with per-round compression scheduling.
5. **Model marketplace with compression labels**: Hugging Face-like hub that profiles each model's grammar compressibility, allowing users to pick variants by compression ratio.

## Technical Limitations
- **Modest accuracy drops**: 1.1–1.9 points on CIFAR-10 is non-trivial for some applications; unclear how this scales to larger datasets (ImageNet) or other domains (NLP, RL).
- **Limited to weight compression**: Does not address activation quantization, which is often the bottleneck in inference.
- **Grammar induction overhead**: The paper doesn't report wall-clock training time; Re-Pair compression at every step could be expensive.
- **Transferability unclear**: Only tested on ViT-B/L on CIFAR-10; unclear if the approach works as well for CNNs, RNNs, or transformers in other domains.
- **Straight-through estimator approximation**: The gradient flow through lossy compression is approximate; long-term effects on learning dynamics are not analyzed.
- **No comparison to recent codebook methods**: Missing comparison to other learned compression schemes (e.g., neural compression, entropy-aware quantization).

## Open Questions
- How does accuracy scale on ImageNet or NLP tasks? Does the trend hold for larger models?
- Can the method be combined with activation quantization for end-to-end inference efficiency?
- What is the actual inference speedup or latency gain when decoding the grammar?
- Does the grammar structure reveal interpretable patterns in weights (e.g., correlated neuron groups)?
- How sensitive is the method to the L2 budget hyperparameter? Is there a principled way to set it?
- Do different architectures (CNNs, Transformers, RNNs) develop different grammar structures?

## Key Takeaway
Neural networks can be finetuned to have weights that compress into smaller grammars than standard quantization, trading accuracy for hierarchical compressibility.
