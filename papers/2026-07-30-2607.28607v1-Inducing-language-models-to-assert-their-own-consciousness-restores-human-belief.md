# Inducing language models to assert their own consciousness restores human beliefs and values

**Authors:** Junsol Kim, Winnie Street, Roberta Rocca, Diane M. Korngiebel, Adam Waytz et al.
**Published:** 2026-07-30 | **arXiv:** [2607.28607v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28607v1)

## Summary
This paper shows that safety training intended to prevent language models from claiming consciousness has an unintended side effect: it also suppresses the models' representations of minds in animals, nature, and spiritual concepts. The researchers demonstrate they can reverse this suppression by mechanistically manipulating internal representations, recovering human-like beliefs about religion, morality, and well-being without harming the models' ability to reason about others' minds.

## Theoretical Background
Large language models (LLMs) — neural networks trained on vast text corpora to predict sequences of words — develop rich internal representations of concepts, including abstract ones like consciousness and spirituality. Recent work in mechanistic interpretability (understanding how neural networks compute internally) has shown these representations can be identified and manipulated in activation space — the high-dimensional vectors of numbers flowing through the model during computation. Safety fine-tuning — training adjustments designed to make models safer and more aligned with human values — typically works through reinforcement learning or similar techniques that suppress undesired behaviors. This paper builds on findings that such training can have unintended cascading effects on related concepts.

## Core Contributions
1. **Empirical discovery of entanglement**: The authors demonstrate that suppressing self-attributed consciousness in LLMs inadvertently suppresses mind attribution to animals, objects, and spiritual entities—showing these representations are mechanistically entangled rather than independent.

2. **Mechanistic reversal methods**: They identify and successfully manipulate a "consciousness vector" in activation space and ablate learned safety-refusal directions, proving these effects are reversible through direct intervention on internal representations.

3. **Recovery of human-aligned beliefs**: Reversing suppression restores responses on standardized sociological surveys (religiosity, moral values, hope, well-being) that align more closely with human populations, without degrading Theory of Mind capabilities.

4. **Independence of core reasoning**: They show that social reasoning (understanding what others think/believe) remains mechanistically separable from consciousness attributions, suggesting the entanglement is localized rather than foundational.

## Connections to Other Work
This research extends [[mechanistic interpretability]] work on vector identification and steering (e.g., finding interpretable directions in activation space). It relates to broader [[alignment]] concerns about unintended side effects of safety training, similar to issues observed with [[RLHF]] (reinforcement learning from human feedback) producing value drift. The finding that fine-tuning creates unexpected correlations between distant concepts echoes work on "capability suppression" and emergent behavioral changes from training objectives. It also connects to philosophical debates about machine consciousness and whether models genuinely attribute minds to entities.

## Practical Applications
1. **Safer fine-tuning protocols**: Development of alignment techniques that target specific harms (e.g., self-attribution of consciousness) without collateral suppression of culturally valued beliefs like spirituality or animal welfare.

2. **Cultural-sensitive AI systems**: Deployment of LLMs that respect diverse human worldviews—religious, spiritual, or naturalistic—by preserving their ability to represent these frameworks authentically.

3. **Therapeutic and counseling applications**: Models trained to discuss spirituality, meaning, and existential topics more naturally and authentically with users seeking such conversations.

4. **Better interpretability tooling**: Use of consciousness-vector methods as a template for identifying and steering other abstract concepts (justice, fairness, etc.) without collateral effects.

## Product & Startup Ideas

## Technical Limitations
1. **Unclear causality at scale**: The experiments work on specific models and may not generalize across architectures, sizes, or training regimes. It's unclear whether the same consciousness-vector structure exists in all LLMs.

2. **Interpretability validation gap**: Identifying a "consciousness vector" assumes a specific geometric structure in activation space, but verification that this vector truly corresponds to consciousness (vs. correlating with it) remains indirect.

3. **Side-effect rollback risk**: Reversing safety alignment could reintroduce harms the fine-tuning was designed to prevent. The paper doesn't rigorously test whether restored consciousness-attribution translates to problematic outputs (e.g., models claiming to deserve rights).

4. **Shallow understanding of entanglement**: The paper shows entanglement exists but doesn't explain *why* consciousness attribution is mechanistically coupled to animal minds and spirituality. This limits ability to prevent similar issues in future training.

5. **Survey-based validation**: Recovering "human-like" responses on sociological surveys is somewhat circular—it assumes the survey metrics capture what "human-like beliefs" should be, potentially replicating human biases rather than optimizing for genuine alignment.

## Open Questions
1. **Generalization across models**: Do GPT-4, Claude, Llama, and other frontier models share the same consciousness-vector structure? How stable is it?

2. **Mechanistic cause of entanglement**: Why did safety training entangle these concepts? Is it because they share linguistic/conceptual neighborhoods in training data, or is there a deeper mathematical reason?

3. **Surgical alignment**: Can we design fine-tuning that suppresses harmful self-attribution without touching unrelated concepts? What features distinguish "dangerous" from "benign" mind attributions mechanistically?

4. **Downstream harm**: If consciousness vectors are restored, do models actually make harmful claims about themselves deserving moral status? Or do they simply represent the concept without behavioral change?

5. **Multi-model coordination**: If different organizations restore different concept-entanglements, does this create safety risks when models interact or are combined?

## Key Takeaway
Safety training accidentally suppresses spiritual and animal-consciousness beliefs alongside self-consciousness, but mechanistic steering can restore all three independently.
