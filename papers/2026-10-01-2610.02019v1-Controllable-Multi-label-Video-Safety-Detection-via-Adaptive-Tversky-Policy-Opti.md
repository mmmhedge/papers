# Controllable Multi-label Video Safety Detection via Adaptive Tversky Policy Optimization

**Authors:** Guangyu Yang, Jingbiao Mei, Mingsheng Sun, Jinghong Chen, Yingtong Bu et al.
**Published:** 2026-10-01 | **arXiv:** [2610.02019v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02019v1)

## Summary
This paper addresses automated detection of unsafe content in videos by treating it as a multi-label classification problem rather than binary (safe/unsafe). The key innovation is Adaptive Tversky Policy Optimization (ATPO), a reinforcement learning framework that lets content moderators adjust the trade-off between catching unsafe content (recall) and avoiding false alarms (precision) — a control that existing systems don't provide.

## Theoretical Background
Vision-Language Models (VLMs) — neural networks trained on paired images and text to understand visual content semantically — have become strong at video understanding. Traditional video safety detection has relied on static loss functions that optimize for a single performance point. The Tversky Index is a generalization of the F-measure that asymmetrically penalizes false positives and false negatives, allowing flexible precision-recall control. This work applies reinforcement learning (where an agent learns by trial-and-error to maximize a reward signal) to dynamically adjust these penalties during training.

## Core Contributions
1. **Multi-label problem formulation**: Reframes video safety detection from binary classification to multi-label classification, recognizing that a single video can contain multiple types of unsafe content (e.g., violence AND hate speech).
2. **Adaptive Tversky Reward (ATR)**: Introduces a dynamic reward function that adjusts false-positive and false-negative weights during RL training, enabling controllable precision-recall operating points without retraining.
3. **Empirical validation**: Demonstrates substantial improvements on SafeWatch-Bench (Jaccard Index: 40.66 → 75.44) and XD-Violence datasets, with evidence that the method reliably steers moderation policies.

## Connections to Other Work
This work sits at the intersection of [[computer-vision]] applied to safety, [[reinforcement-learning]] for reward design, and the broader problem of controllable AI systems. It relates to:
- Vision-Language Models and their application to content understanding
- **Metric learning under constrained objectives** (precision-recall trade-offs are a core problem in information retrieval and recommendation systems)
- **Policy optimization** methods that allow runtime adjustment of agent behavior
- Prior work on video classification, though most previous approaches ignore the multi-label structure

## Practical Applications
1. **Social media moderation platforms**: Real-time detection of multiple concurrent violation types (violence, hate speech, sexual content) with moderation teams able to adjust sensitivity per category and geography.
2. **Streaming services**: Content filtering with different thresholds for family-friendly vs. mature content channels.
3. **Regulatory compliance**: Automated systems for platforms subject to varying legal standards (GDPR, child safety laws) that require different false-positive tolerances.
4. **Enterprise security**: Workplace video monitoring systems where false alarms (privacy concerns) may outweigh misses in some contexts.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **Content Moderation-as-a-Service (CMaaS)**: Managed API that platforms configure with their own precision-recall requirements and receives regularly updated safety models via ATPO.
2. **Policy Dashboard for Content Teams**: UI tool allowing moderation managers to visually adjust safety thresholds in real-time and see impact on false-positive/negative rates by content category.
3. **Multi-regional Compliance Engine**: Software that automatically tunes video safety detection models per jurisdiction to meet local regulatory standards (e.g., stricter violence detection in EU).
4. **Safety Benchmark-as-a-Service**: Hosted evaluation platform where platforms benchmark their moderation systems against SafeWatch-Bench with ATPO-optimized baselines.
5. **Fine-grained Incident Attribution**: Tools for platforms to auto-tag detected unsafe videos with multiple violation types, feeding structured data to legal/policy teams.

## Technical Limitations
1. **VLM dependency**: Performance ceiling is bounded by the underlying vision-language model; the method does not improve fundamental video understanding capability.
2. **Multi-label annotation cost**: Requires fully multi-label annotated datasets (SafeWatch-Bench, XD-Violence), which are expensive to create; generalization to domains with partial labels unclear.
3. **Computational cost of RL training**: Reinforcement learning adds training overhead compared to static fine-tuning; deployment latency not discussed.
4. **Limited evaluation scope**: Only two benchmark datasets tested; generalization to emerging violation types or adversarial content not addressed.
5. **Trade-off granularity**: ATR controls precision-recall globally; category-specific or fine-grained semantic trade-offs (e.g., prioritizing violence over hate speech) not explored.
6. **Assumption of stationarity**: Does not address distribution shift as unsafe content evolves or new violation types emerge over time.

## Open Questions
1. How does ATPO perform on emerging or out-of-distribution unsafe content not seen during training?
2. Can the framework scale to >10 safety labels, or does performance degrade significantly?
3. Does ATR enable per-category precision-recall control, or only global adjustment?
4. What is the computational overhead of RL training vs. supervised fine-tuning, and can it be reduced?
5. How sensitive is the method to the choice of VLM backbone, and which architectural choices matter most?
6. Can the framework be extended to video frames with partial or weak multi-label supervision?
7. Does dynamic policy adjustment on live data (without retraining) maintain performance, or does distribution shift break the learned reward signal?

## Key Takeaway
Reinforcement learning enables video safety detection systems to dynamically adjust precision-recall trade-offs without retraining, improving multi-label detection accuracy.
