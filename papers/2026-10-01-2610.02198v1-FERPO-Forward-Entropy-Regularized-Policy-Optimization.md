# FERPO: Forward Entropy-Regularized Policy Optimization

**Authors:** Sebastian Sanokowski, Alireza Sarmadi, Majid Khadiv
**Published:** 2026-10-01 | **arXiv:** [2610.02198v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02198v1)

## Summary
This paper introduces FERPO, a new reinforcement learning algorithm for continuous control that improves policies without directly differentiating through the critic (value-function estimator). Instead of using action gradients from a learned critic—which can be unreliable—FERPO derives optimal target action distributions using entropy and KL-divergence regularization, then trains the policy to match these targets. The approach shows competitive performance and faster computation than existing methods on robotics benchmarks.

## Theoretical Background
Modern reinforcement learning for continuous control typically uses actor-critic methods, where an actor (policy) and critic (value function) are trained together. The critic estimates expected returns from states and actions; the actor uses gradients of the critic's predictions to improve the policy. However, this assumes that the critic's accuracy in predicting returns translates to accurate action gradients—a questionable assumption. FERPO builds on maximum entropy reinforcement learning frameworks, which add entropy bonuses to encourage exploration, and importance sampling techniques that let us train policies using samples from a different (rollout) distribution. The paper specifically uses forward KL divergence (a statistical divergence measure) instead of reverse KL, which affects how well the learned policy covers the target distribution's modes.

## Core Contributions
1. **Decoupling value prediction from action gradients**: FERPO eliminates critic differentiation entirely, instead using critic values only to define a target distribution via a policy-improvement objective. This sidesteps the unreliability of action gradients from imperfect value estimates.
2. **Forward-KL policy fitting with importance sampling**: Uses forward KL divergence with self-normalized importance sampling (SNIS) to match the actor to the target distribution. Forward KL encourages mode-covering behavior (exploring multiple high-value actions) rather than mode-seeking (collapsing to one mode).
3. **KL-regularized target design**: The target distribution is explicitly regularized to stay close to the rollout policy, which keeps importance weights stable and interpretable—a key practical benefit over reverse-KL methods.
4. **Computational efficiency**: Demonstrates faster actor updates compared to REPPO and other pathwise policy gradients on standard robotics tasks.

## Connections to Other Work
FERPO relates to several RL paradigms:
- **[[Policy Gradient Methods]]**: Builds on actor-critic frameworks but replaces pathwise policy gradients with a distribution-matching approach.
- **[[Maximum Entropy RL]]** (e.g., SAC—Soft Actor-Critic): Shares entropy regularization but differs in how the policy is updated (forward KL + importance sampling vs. reparameterization gradients).
- **[[Trust Region Methods]]** (e.g., PPO, TRPO): The KL regularization is conceptually similar to trust regions but applied differently—as part of the target objective rather than a constraint on the update.
- **[[Offline RL]]**: Importance sampling and KL regularization are common in offline RL to avoid distributional shift; FERPO adapts these ideas to the on-policy setting.
- **[[Relative Entropy Policy Search]]** (REPPO): A direct computational predecessor; FERPO improves efficiency while maintaining or exceeding sample efficiency.

## Practical Applications
1. **Robotic manipulation and control**: MuJoCo and ManiSkill benchmarks (used in the paper) are standard for evaluating robot learning; faster convergence means quicker real-world deployment.
2. **Autonomous navigation**: Continuous control in real-time systems (drones, autonomous vehicles) where sample efficiency and computational speed matter.
3. **Sim-to-real transfer**: Faster, more reliable policy learning in simulation can improve transfer to physical robots.
4. **High-dimensional action spaces**: Systems with many continuous degrees of freedom (humanoid locomotion, complex manipulation) where pathwise gradients are notoriously noisy.
5. **Interactive learning with humans**: Robotics applications requiring frequent policy updates during human oversight or interactive feedback.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **RL middleware for robotics platforms**: Sell FERPO as a plug-and-play policy optimizer for commercial robot arms and autonomous systems, reducing training time and hardware costs.
2. **Sim-to-real optimization SaaS**: Cloud-based service that trains policies using FERPO on customer-provided simulations, then packages them for physical deployment with guaranteed efficiency gains.
3. **AI-driven drone autopilot**: Use FERPO for faster adaptation in aerial vehicles navigating dynamic environments (delivery drones, inspection UAVs).
4. **Adaptive manufacturing control**: Apply FERPO to continuous control in factories (robotic welding, assembly) where sample efficiency translates directly to reduced trial-and-error costs.
5. **Video game and simulation AI**: Use FERPO to train faster, more exploratory NPCs or AI agents in interactive environments where computation is constrained.

## Technical Limitations
1. **On-policy only**: FERPO requires fresh samples from the rollout policy; it cannot leverage offline datasets or experience replay as efficiently as some alternatives, limiting data reuse.
2. **Importance sampling variance**: Even with KL regularization, SNIS can suffer high variance in high-dimensional action spaces or when the target diverges significantly from the rollout policy.
3. **Critic accuracy still required**: While FERPO avoids differentiating the critic, the critic values themselves must still be accurate to define good target distributions. A biased critic directly corrupts the target.
4. **Limited theoretical guarantees**: The paper does not provide convergence proofs or sample complexity bounds; guarantees are empirical.
5. **Scalability unknown**: Experiments are on continuous control with moderate dimensionality; behavior on very high-dimensional or discrete action spaces is unclear.
6. **Hyperparameter sensitivity**: The method introduces additional regularization coefficients (entropy weight, KL weight) that may require tuning per domain.

## Open Questions
1. How does FERPO scale to extremely high-dimensional action spaces (e.g., 100+ DOF) or discrete/mixed action spaces?
2. Can FERPO be extended to off-policy or batch RL settings without losing its computational advantages?
3. What is the theoretical convergence rate, and how does it compare to pathwise policy gradients under different assumptions about critic accuracy?
4. Does FERPO perform well with function approximation (neural networks) in non-stationary environments, or does importance sampling variance grow with policy drift?
5. How sensitive is the method to the choice of KL and entropy regularization weights? Can these be adapted online?
6. Can FERPO be combined with other exploration bonuses (intrinsic motivation, curiosity) to further improve sample efficiency?

## Key Takeaway
FERPO trains robot policies faster by fitting them to entropy-regularized value targets without using noisy critic gradients, improving both speed and exploration.
