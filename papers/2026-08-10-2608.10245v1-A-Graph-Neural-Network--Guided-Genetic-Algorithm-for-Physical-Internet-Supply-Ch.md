# A Graph Neural Network--Guided Genetic Algorithm for Physical Internet Supply Chain Optimization under Cost Uncertainty

**Authors:** Faezeh Ardali, Gerald M. Knapp
**Published:** 2026-08-10 | **arXiv:** [2608.10245v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10245v1)

## Summary
This paper solves supply chain planning in Physical Internet networks — a system where factories, distribution hubs, and retailers collaborate — by combining graph neural networks (GNNs) with genetic algorithms. The key innovation is using a GNN to learn good factory-to-hub assignments, which then guides a genetic algorithm's search through millions of possible configurations. This matters because supply chains face uncertain costs and complex interdependencies; the hybrid approach finds better solutions faster than traditional optimization alone.

## Theoretical Background
Physical Internet supply chains are complex combinatorial problems: you must decide which factories supply which hubs, how much inventory flows where, and which retailers get served — all while costs vary unpredictably. Prior work typically uses either pure mathematical optimization (linear programming, integer programming) or evolutionary algorithms like genetic algorithms (which iteratively improve candidate solutions by mutation and recombination). Graph neural networks — neural networks designed to process relational data as graphs — have recently shown promise at learning patterns in structured problems. This paper bridges these worlds: instead of the GNN solving the problem directly, it learns to initialize and guide the genetic algorithm's search space, combining the GNN's pattern recognition with the GA's ability to navigate discrete assignments.

## Core Contributions
1. **GNN-guided genetic algorithm framework**: Uses GNN predictions of hub-specific factory-selection probabilities to construct the initial GA population (better starting points) and to adapt mutation rates based on prediction uncertainty (entropy-guided mutation).
2. **Formulation of robust supply chain models**: Develops both deterministic and min-max regret optimization models for three-echelon Physical Internet networks under cost uncertainty.
3. **Empirical validation and ablation study**: Demonstrates GNN-GA outperforms standard GA and simulated annealing on 15 instances with matched evaluation budgets; ablation shows learned initialization is the primary source of improvement, while entropy-guided mutation has smaller, instance-dependent gains.
4. **Transfer learning evidence**: Tests the learned GNN model on independently generated unseen instances, showing some generalization.

## Connections to Other Work
This work sits at the intersection of [[Optimization]], [[Graph Neural Networks]], and evolutionary computation. It relates to broader efforts in learning-augmented algorithms — where machine learning guides classical optimization (compare to learning-to-rank in branch-and-bound, or neural combinatorial optimization like [[Attention Is All You Need]] applied to TSP). The min-max regret formulation connects to classical robust optimization under uncertainty. The paper is distinct from end-to-end neural solvers (e.g., GNN-only approaches) because it preserves the GA's flexibility and the LP's exactness for the continuous subproblem.

## Practical Applications
1. **Physical Internet logistics networks**: Optimizing real-time hub-factory-retailer routing and inventory in collaborative delivery systems (e.g., CityLogiX or FESPA projects in Europe).
2. **Multi-echelon inventory management**: Retailers, wholesalers, and manufacturers coordinating orders under volatile input costs (energy, raw materials).
3. **Disaster relief supply chains**: Rapidly re-routing aid through uncertain supply costs and damaged infrastructure.
4. **E-commerce fulfillment**: Dynamic allocation of orders across multiple warehouses and fulfillment centers when shipping costs fluctuate.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Evaluation budget constraint**: On instances 13–15, the evaluation budget is smaller than population size, limiting full multi-generational GA dynamics. Most gains come from initialization, not evolutionary search.
2. **Scalability unclear**: No analysis of how GNN-GA scales to networks with hundreds or thousands of nodes; GNN inference and LP solve time could become prohibitive.
3. **Model training cost excluded**: Per-instance solution times exclude GNN training and one-time setup, masking true computational overhead if models must be retrained frequently.
4. **Limited baseline comparisons**: Only compared to standard GA and simulated annealing; missing comparisons to modern meta-heuristics (ant colony, particle swarm) or learning-based combinatorial solvers.
5. **Uncertainty model simplicity**: Min-max regret assumes cost uncertainty is known/bounded; real-world scenarios may have correlated or non-stationary cost shocks.
6. **Transfer learning is preliminary**: Tests on only three independently generated instances; generalization to real-world topologies with different structural properties is unproven.

## Open Questions
1. **Scalability and computational complexity**: How does GNN-GA perform on networks with 100+ nodes? At what problem size does GNN inference dominate the solution time?
2. **Adaptive model retraining**: Should the GNN be retrained periodically as supply chain conditions change? How often, and what is the retraining cost?
3. **Uncertainty quantification**: Can the GNN's prediction confidence be used to guide robust optimization, e.g., choosing assignment variance thresholds?
4. **Multi-objective extension**: Real supply chains care about cost, resilience, and service level. Does GNN-GA extend to Pareto-front discovery?
5. **Why does entropy-guided mutation underperform?**: The ablation hints mutation-tuning may not help much. Is this fundamental or a tuning/design issue?
6. **Online/dynamic variants**: Can GNN-GA handle real-time order arrivals and reassignments, or only batch planning?

## Key Takeaway
Neural networks can jumpstart genetic algorithms for supply chain optimization by learning good initial solutions, outperforming pure evolutionary search under tight evaluation budgets.
