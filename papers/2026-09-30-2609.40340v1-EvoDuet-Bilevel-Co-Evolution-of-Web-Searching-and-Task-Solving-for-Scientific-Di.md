# EvoDuet: Bilevel Co-Evolution of Web Searching and Task Solving for Scientific Discovery

**Authors:** Young-Jun Lee, Jinheon Baek, Soyeong Jeong, Minki Kang, Seungyeon Jwa et al.
**Published:** 2026-09-30 | **arXiv:** [2609.40340v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40340v1)

## Summary
This paper presents EvoDuet, a method that improves how language models solve complex scientific problems by co-evolving both candidate solutions and web search queries together. The key insight is that a model should dynamically decide when to search for new information, reuse old documents, or proceed without retrieval — rather than blindly fetching pages as solutions change. On 21 optimization tasks, EvoDuet increases discovery gains by 3-21 percentage points compared to baseline approaches.

## Theoretical Background
This work builds on two established paradigms. First, evolutionary search with LLMs — using population-based optimization where language models generate and refine candidate solutions iteratively, similar to genetic algorithms but with neural networks. Second, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) — the practice of grounding LLM outputs with retrieved external documents to reduce hallucination and inject domain knowledge. Prior work like OpenEvolve applies evolution to discovery tasks but treats web search as a static tool, not as a co-evolving component. The paper treats solution-finding and query-refinement as coupled optimization problems, requiring a bilevel optimization framework where the inner loop tunes information-seeking behavior while the outer loop generates solutions.

## Core Contributions
1. **Retrieval gating mechanism**: An LLM-based decision module that assesses knowledge gaps and chooses whether to retrieve, reuse, or skip document lookup at each iteration.
2. **Bilevel co-evolution**: A dual optimization loop where query refinement (inner) is driven by solution quality signals (outer), preventing redundant document re-fetching.
3. **Empirical gains across model scales**: Demonstrates 3-21 percentage point improvements on benchmark tasks with GPT-5.6-Luna and Gemini-3.8-Flash, with evidence of model-dependent benefits.
4. **Scaffold-agnostic design**: Shows the method works with multiple evolutionary search scaffolds (Top-K, EvoX), not just the base OpenEvolve framework.

## Connections to Other Work
EvoDuet sits at the intersection of [[large-language-models]] for task solving, retrieval-augmented generation for knowledge grounding, and [[reinforcement-learning]]-style evolutionary algorithms. It extends the bilevel optimization tradition (common in meta-learning and hyperparameter tuning) into the domain of co-adaptive information retrieval. The retrieval gating mechanism echoes ideas from adaptive computation and "learn to retrieve" paradigms in information retrieval. Unlike [[RLHF]] which optimizes model weights via human feedback, EvoDuet fixes model parameters and instead optimizes the search-solution coupling, making it complementary to weight-based fine-tuning approaches.

## Practical Applications
1. **Scientific hypothesis generation**: Automating discovery in chemistry, biology, or physics where models must iteratively search literature and propose experiments.
2. **Mathematical problem solving**: Competitive programming or theorem proving where external documents (past solutions, lemmas) are needed but must be strategically retrieved.
3. **Engineering design optimization**: Finding optimal configurations for hardware or software systems where retrieval of design patterns, benchmarks, and past solutions accelerates convergence.
4. **Patent and novelty screening**: Evolving novel patent claims while dynamically retrieving prior art and regulatory documents.
5. **Market analysis and financial modeling**: Evolving trading strategies while selectively pulling market data, news, and historical backtests.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **Adaptive Research Assistant SaaS**: A platform for academic labs that auto-evolves experimental hypotheses while intelligently fetching relevant papers and datasets — charge per solved discovery task.
2. **AI-Powered Drug Discovery Accelerator**: A tool that co-evolves molecular structures and ligand designs while retrieving relevant chemical databases and literature on-demand, targeting biotech companies.
3. **Autonomous Code Optimization Engine**: Developer tool that evolves algorithm implementations for performance while selectively pulling code snippets, benchmarks, and library APIs from internal/external knowledge bases.
4. **Smart Patent Generation Suite**: Helps inventors draft novel, defensible patents by co-evolving claim language and technical specifications while fetching prior art and regulatory guidance.
5. **Financial Strategy Optimizer**: Evolves investment portfolios and trading rules while adaptively retrieving market data, historical correlations, and risk metrics — B2B for hedge funds.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Model dependency**: Benefits are inconsistent across model scales (Qwen3.5-9B showed no improvement), suggesting the gating mechanism requires sufficient model capacity; smaller models may not learn when/how to retrieve.
2. **Computational cost unclear**: Paper reports normalized discovery gains but not wall-clock time or API cost — the inner loop query refinement may offset efficiency gains if retrieval is expensive.
3. **Single-candidate-per-iteration constraint**: Experiments use one candidate per iteration; scalability to larger populations is untested.
4. **Limited task diversity**: 21 tasks are mostly synthetic or controlled optimization problems; real-world messy discovery (open-ended research) is unexplored.
5. **Retrieval quality assumption**: Assumes the search backend (web or database) is adequate; if relevant documents are unavailable or noisy, the gating mechanism cannot help.
6. **Fixed parameter regime**: Intentionally doesn't fine-tune the base model, which may leave performance on the table compared to end-to-end learning.

## Open Questions
1. How does EvoDuet perform on truly open-ended discovery tasks (e.g., generating novel proteins or materials) where ground-truth solutions are unknown and evaluation is noisy?
2. Can the retrieval gating mechanism be made interpretable — can we understand *what knowledge gaps* the model is detecting, and does that align with human expert intuition?
3. Does the bilevel co-evolution converge? What are the convergence rates and conditions for different task classes?
4. How sensitive is the method to the quality and size of the retrieval corpus? Is there a sweet spot, or does it scale gracefully?
5. Can the approach be extended to multi-agent scenarios where multiple models collaborate and trade information?
6. How does EvoDuet compare to fine-tuned models or models trained with in-context examples of the target task?

## Key Takeaway
Smart models need smart search: co-evolving queries and solutions beats fixed retrieval for scientific problem-solving.
