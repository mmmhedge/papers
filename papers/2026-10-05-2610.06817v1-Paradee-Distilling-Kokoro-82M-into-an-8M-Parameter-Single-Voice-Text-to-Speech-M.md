# Paradee: Distilling Kokoro-82M into an 8M-Parameter Single-Voice Text-to-Speech Model

**Authors:** Sahil Mahendrakar
**Published:** 2026-10-05 | **arXiv:** [2610.06817v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06817v1)

## Summary
This paper shows how to shrink Kokoro-82M, a multi-voice text-to-speech (TTS) model, down to Paradee: an 8M-parameter single-voice version that runs 25x faster than real time on a CPU and fits in 8.5 MB. The key insight is distilling the teacher model in two separate stages (text-to-features, then features-to-audio) rather than training them jointly, avoiding expensive alignment learning while maintaining quality within 0.11 UTMOS points of the original.

## Theoretical Background
Text-to-speech synthesis traditionally involves two stages: (1) predicting acoustic features (duration, pitch, energy) from phoneme sequences, and (2) converting those features into waveforms. [[Kokoro-82M]] demonstrates that modern TTS can scale to many voices in a single model using shared architecture layers. Knowledge distillation — training a smaller "student" network to mimic a larger frozen "teacher" — is a standard technique for compression, but typically requires careful alignment learning (matching intermediate representations) and joint end-to-end training. This paper bypasses those constraints by distilling each stage independently against the teacher's saved outputs.

## Core Contributions
1. **Two-stage separate distillation**: Rather than joint training, the text-to-feature and feature-to-audio components are trained independently against frozen teacher outputs, eliminating alignment learning entirely.
2. **Phase-locking filter for buzz artifact removal**: A post-hoc signal-processing technique that targets phase coherence in the 2–8 kHz band, fixing a specific artifact without adding trainable parameters.
3. **Extreme compression with maintained quality**: Achieves 10x parameter reduction (82M → 8M) and 25x real-time speedup on CPU while keeping UTMOS score within 0.11 points (4.41 vs. 4.52).
4. **Practical single-voice single-file system**: The entire model quantizes to int8 (8.5 MB) and runs on a laptop with no special hardware.

## Connections to Other Work
This work sits at the intersection of [[knowledge distillation]], [[generative models]] (audio generation), and practical model compression. It relates to broader trends in shrinking large foundation models ([[LLM quantization]], [[model pruning]]) but applies those ideas to speech synthesis rather than language. The two-stage decomposition echoes classical TTS pipelines (e.g., Tacotron, Glow-TTS) but uses modern architectures. The adversarial loss for feature-to-audio mirrors techniques in [[GAN]]-based vocoding and neural vocoders (HiFi-GAN, Glow-TTS). Phase-locking is a novel signal-processing intervention that complements learned approaches to artifact removal.

## Practical Applications
1. **On-device TTS for mobile/embedded systems**: 8.5 MB model + CPU inference enables TTS on smartphones, IoT devices, and edge servers without cloud dependencies.
2. **Real-time dubbing and subtitle synchronization**: 25x real-time speedup makes live or near-live audio generation feasible for video localization.
3. **Accessibility tools**: Lightweight TTS for screen readers on low-power devices (older phones, accessibility readers in developing regions).
4. **Low-latency conversational agents**: Chatbots and voice assistants that need fast, synchronous speech output without network round-trips.
5. **Privacy-preserving voice synthesis**: Single-device inference eliminates sending audio or text to remote servers, appealing for health, legal, or sensitive applications.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **Privacy-first voice assistant SDK**: Package Paradee as a drop-in library for developers building on-device voice UIs (smart home, automotive, wearables) that never phones home.
2. **Lightweight dubbing platform**: Browser-based or desktop app for creators to automatically dub videos in a single voice with instant preview; position against cloud-based competitors on speed and privacy.
3. **Accessibility-as-a-service for governments/nonprofits**: Bundled TTS + reader for documents, websites, and forms targeting public sectors and NGOs in bandwidth-constrained regions.
4. **Voice personalization for IoT**: Allow users to generate single-voice TTS in a few seconds for smart speakers, security systems, and industrial announcements without vendor lock-in.
5. **Real-time translation + TTS combo**: Pair lightweight Paradee with a small MT model to offer offline video subtitle generation and spoken translation on consumer laptops.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Single-voice constraint**: Unlike Kokoro, Paradee speaks only one voice; switching speakers requires retraining or maintaining multiple 8M models, negating the compression benefit for multi-speaker use cases.
2. **Buzziness artifact**: The original model exhibited phase misalignment in the 2–8 kHz band, requiring a post-hoc filter; this suggests the distilled features or decoder do not fully capture the teacher's phase coherence, and the fix is empirical rather than principled.
3. **Limited generalization data**: The paper does not report performance on out-of-distribution text (different domains, rare phoneme combinations, accents); int8 quantization may degrade gracefully or catastrophically depending on input.
4. **No speaker adaptation**: Unlike some TTS systems, there is no fine-tuning or adaptation mechanism; users cannot personalize the voice.
5. **Adversarial training stability**: Relying on a GAN-like loss for feature-to-audio may introduce training instability or mode collapse, which is not discussed.

## Open Questions
1. How does Paradee perform on long-form synthesis (e.g., audiobook chapters) vs. the short utterances likely in the test set? Does accumulated error or drift occur?
2. Can the two-stage distillation approach generalize to multi-voice models, or is single-voice essential to the compression ratio?
3. Why does the phase problem emerge only in distillation, not in the original Kokoro? Is it a fundamental limit of the 8M bottleneck or a training artifact?
4. What is the sensitivity to int8 quantization? Do certain layers degrade more than others, and could mixed-precision quantization improve quality further?
5. Can the phase-locking filter be learned end-to-end, or is it an unavoidable post-processing step for this architecture?

## Key Takeaway
Distilling multi-voice TTS into a tiny, single-voice CPU-runnable model by training stages separately instead of jointly.
