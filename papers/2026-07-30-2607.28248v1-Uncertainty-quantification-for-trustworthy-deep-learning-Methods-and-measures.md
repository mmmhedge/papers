# Uncertainty quantification for trustworthy deep learning: Methods and measures

**Authors:** H. Martin Gillis, Thomas Trappenberg
**Published:** 2026-07-30 | **arXiv:** [2607.28248v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28248v1)

## Summary
This paper surveys methods for uncertainty quantification (UQ) in deep neural networks — techniques that help models estimate how confident they should be in their predictions. It's crucial for safety-critical applications like medical diagnosis or autonomous vehicles, where knowing when a model is uncertain can prevent costly mistakes. The survey organizes dozens of approaches into five families and provides a unified framework for evaluating them.

## Theoretical Background
Deep neural networks are typically trained to output point predictions (single best guesses) without confidence estimates. [[Bayesian methods]] treat neural network weights as probability distributions rather than fixed values, naturally producing uncertainty. Traditional Bayesian inference is computationally expensive for neural networks, so researchers developed approximations: [[Monte Carlo Dropout]] treats dropout regularization as approximate sampling, while [[deep ensembles]] train multiple models and aggregate their predictions. The paper also covers [[conformal prediction]] — a distribution-free approach that guarantees coverage regardless of the model — and calibration methods that adjust model confidence to match true accuracy. Uncertainty measures themselves vary: entropy captures overall uncertainty, while divergence measures quantify disagreement between ensemble members.

## Core Contributions
The paper's key novelty is its architectural separation of *method* (what produces a predictive distribution) from *measure* (how to summarize its uncertainty). It provides depth on efficient ensemble approximations and single-pass methods that were underexplored in prior surveys. The unified evaluation framework enables fair comparison across heterogeneous approaches. The treatment of uncertainty decompositions — contrasting entropy with pairwise divergence — clarifies different notions of uncertainty. Novel scope includes emerging areas: uncertainty in large language models, last-layer diversity theory, and calibration under distribution shift.

## Connections to Other Work
This work synthesizes a fragmented landscape. It relates to [[calibration]], a long-standing problem in machine learning, and [[out-of-distribution detection]], which uses uncertainty to flag unfamiliar inputs. It bridges classical Bayesian statistics and modern deep learning. The coverage of [[conformal prediction]] connects to distribution-free guarantees. Recent work on [[trustworthy AI]] and model transparency overlaps substantially. The paper also acknowledges [[mechanistic interpretability]] as a related direction for understanding model reliability, though from a different angle.

## Practical Applications
- **Medical diagnosis**: Radiologists need confidence estimates to flag uncertain cases for review; uncertainty quantification enables safe human-in-the-loop systems.
- **Autonomous vehicles**: Detecting when perception models are uncertain about pedestrians or road conditions triggers fallback behaviors.
- **Financial modeling**: Banks use uncertainty estimates in credit scoring to set confidence thresholds and reserve capital against model error.
- **Manufacturing quality control**: Robot inspection systems flag parts with ambiguous defects rather than making risky automatic decisions.
- **Content moderation**: Platforms use model uncertainty to escalate borderline violations to human reviewers instead of auto-filtering.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **Uncertainty-as-a-service API**: SaaS wrapper that adds calibrated uncertainty estimates to any pre-trained model via ensemble approximations or conformal prediction, with per-task tuning.
2. **Model audit platform**: Automated system that evaluates a customer's deep learning model's uncertainty quality, flags miscalibration, and recommends fixes (e.g., ensemble size, conformal threshold).
3. **Active learning system for data annotation**: Use uncertainty estimates to identify the highest-value unlabeled examples to annotate, reducing labeling costs by 50%+ versus random sampling.
4. **Trustworthy AI monitoring for regulated industries**: Continuous monitoring dashboard for deployed models, alerting when calibration drifts, epistemic uncertainty rises, or out-of-distribution samples appear.
5. **Conformal prediction toolkit**: Open-source + commercial support for post-hoc conformal wrappers that guarantee coverage guarantees on proprietary model predictions without retraining.

## Technical Limitations
- **Computational cost**: Ensembles and Monte Carlo Dropout require multiple forward passes; single-pass methods (last-layer) are faster but often less accurate.
- **Calibration under shift**: Most methods assume the test distribution matches training; performance degrades significantly under dataset shift or adversarial distribution changes.
- **Scalability to LLMs**: Uncertainty quantification becomes expensive for billion-parameter models; the paper acknowledges this is an open frontier.
- **Measure sensitivity**: Different uncertainty measures (entropy vs. divergence) can rank methods differently; no universal "best" measure exists.
- **Ensemble diversity**: Diversity theory is underdeveloped; it's unclear how to design ensembles that maximize meaningful diversity at reasonable computational cost.
- **Theoretical guarantees**: Most methods lack formal guarantees except conformal prediction, which can be loose in high dimensions.

## Open Questions
- How do you efficiently compute epistemic uncertainty (model uncertainty, not data noise) for classification at scale?
- Can you design ensemble architectures that improve diversity without retraining from scratch?
- How do uncertainty estimates degrade under natural distribution shifts (e.g., seasonal changes, adversarial examples) and how should they be monitored in production?
- What is the relationship between uncertainty and out-of-distribution robustness? Can one objective optimize both?
- How should uncertainty quantification scale to very large language models without multiplying compute costs?
- Can hybrid architectures (e.g., combining conformal prediction with deep ensembles) provide better coverage and efficiency than either alone?

## Key Takeaway
Neural networks need confidence estimates for safety; this survey consolidates five major methods and the measures to evaluate them fairly.
