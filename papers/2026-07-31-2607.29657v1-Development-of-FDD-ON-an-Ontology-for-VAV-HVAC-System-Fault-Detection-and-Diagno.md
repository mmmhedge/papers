# Development of FDD-ON: an Ontology for VAV HVAC System Fault Detection and Diagnostics

**Authors:** Yimin Chen, Brian Fricke, Bo Shen, Jamie Lian, Mingkan Zhang et al.
**Published:** 2026-07-31 | **arXiv:** [2607.29657v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29657v1)

## Summary

This paper introduces FDD-ON, a formal ontology (structured knowledge representation system) designed to standardize how fault detection and diagnostics work in building HVAC systems, specifically variable air volume (VAV) units. The work matters because HVAC faults are poorly documented across different vendors and systems, creating "information silos" that prevent AI-driven maintenance tools and digital twins from working effectively across buildings—FDD-ON solves this by providing a shared vocabulary and explicit mappings between faults, symptoms, and their impacts.

## Theoretical Background

Fault detection and diagnosis (FDD) in HVAC systems has existed for decades, but historically lacked formal representation standards. The paper builds on ontology engineering—a branch of knowledge representation that creates machine-readable formal models of concepts, relations, and constraints in a domain. Prior work in building automation has used various ad-hoc data schemas and proprietary diagnostic frameworks; FDD-ON extends this by explicitly modeling the causal chain: contributing causes → faults → symptoms → impacts. The work also relates to semantic interoperability efforts in the broader IoT and smart buildings community, where heterogeneous sensor data and equipment must be integrated.

## Core Contributions

1. **FDD-ON itself**: A modular, extensible OWL/RDF-based ontology capturing VAV HVAC components, fault types, symptom states, and impact chains with explicit causal relations.

2. **Controlled vocabularies**: Formally defined, reusable terms for fault and symptom attributes, reducing ambiguity across systems and vendors.

3. **Cause-Fault-Symptom-Impact (CFSI) framework**: Explicit representation of how a root cause manifests as observable symptoms and downstream impacts—enabling diagnostic reasoning and knowledge querying.

4. **Validation on public datasets**: Demonstrated interoperability by mapping real VAV FDD outputs to the ontology and testing against real-world HVAC data.

## Practical Applications

1. **Cross-vendor HVAC maintenance platforms**: A SaaS tool that ingests fault reports from different HVAC vendors (Trane, Carrier, Honeywell, etc.) and maps them to a common diagnostic ontology, enabling unified monitoring across a multi-building portfolio.

2. **Predictive maintenance systems**: Using FDD-ON to train ML models that predict which component failures will occur next, enabling proactive maintenance scheduling and spare-parts planning.

3. **Energy auditing**: Structured fault data helps building energy managers quantify how FDD issues degrade efficiency and prioritize fixes by ROI.

4. **Regulatory compliance**: Automated generation of maintenance logs and fault documentation for energy codes or insurance audits.

5. **Building digital twins**: FDD-ON as the semantic backbone for linking real sensor data to a virtual building model for diagnostics and "what-if" scenario analysis.

## Product & Startup Ideas

1. **FDD-ON SaaS Platform**: A cloud service that onboards building owners' HVAC data and diagnostic reports, maps them to FDD-ON, and provides a unified dashboard for fault trends, predictive alerts, and maintenance recommendations across their portfolio.

2. **Ontology-as-a-Service for HVAC vendors**: White-label ontology integration service for HVAC manufacturers (Trane, Lennox, etc.) to embed semantic diagnostics into their systems, enabling interoperability and third-party app ecosystems.

3. **Building Commissioning & Retro-Commissioning Tool**: Automated FDD data capture during commissioning, mapped to FDD-ON, producing machine-readable commissioning reports and identifying design/installation faults early.

4. **AI Maintenance Copilot**: Mobile app for HVAC technicians that uses FDD-ON to guide troubleshooting, suggest parts, estimate repair cost, and log work in standardized format.

5. **Building Carbon Accounting Platform**: Leverage FDD-ON fault data to quantify energy waste from HVAC faults, assign carbon/cost to each issue, and integrate with corporate ESG reporting.

## Technical Limitations

1. **Scope limitation**: FDD-ON focuses on VAV systems; extending to other HVAC architectures (split systems, chiller plants, etc.) requires additional ontology development and may not scale uniformly.

2. **Data heterogeneity**: Real-world HVAC sensors have noise, missing data, and calibration drift—the ontology assumes clean, standardized sensor inputs that rarely exist in practice.

3. **Interpretability of root causes**: The ontology defines cause-symptom links based on expert knowledge, but doesn't validate that these causal models hold across different buildings, climates, or equipment ages.

4. **Integration overhead**: Mapping legacy HVAC systems to FDD-ON requires manual effort and domain expertise; automation of this mapping is not addressed.

5. **Validation scope**: Evaluation uses only publicly available datasets, which are limited in size and diversity compared to real building portfolios; generalization risk is high.

6. **Reasoning complexity**: The paper doesn't thoroughly address how to handle uncertain or conflicting diagnostic signals—real HVAC faults often have multiple overlapping symptoms.

## Open Questions

1. How does FDD-ON performance degrade when sensor data is noisy or missing? What is the minimum data quality required for accurate fault attribution?

2. Can the ontology be semi-automatically extended to other HVAC architectures (ground-source heat pumps, magnetic bearing compressors, etc.) without manual redesign?

3. How well do the cause-fault-symptom relations generalize across climate zones, building types, and equipment vintages?

4. Can FDD-ON be integrated with [[large-language-models]] or [[transformers]] to enable natural-language diagnostic queries ("Why is the economizer not switching?") and automated report generation?

5. What is the business-critical performance threshold: at what fault detection accuracy does a maintenance operator trust the system enough to act on it autonomously?

6. How should FDD-ON handle time-varying symptoms or cascading faults (e.g., a sensor fault masking a real HVAC fault)?

## Key Takeaway

A standardized ontology for HVAC fault data enables interoperable diagnostics and AI maintenance across buildings and vendors.
