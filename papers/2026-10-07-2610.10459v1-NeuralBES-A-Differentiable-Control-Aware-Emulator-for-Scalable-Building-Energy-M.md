# NeuralBES: A Differentiable, Control-Aware Emulator for Scalable Building Energy Modeling

**Authors:** Ting-Yu Dai, Takuya Kurihana, Wing Yee Au, Hon Yung Wong
**Published:** 2026-10-07 | **arXiv:** [2610.10459v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10459v1)

## Summary
NeuralBES is a neural network emulator that predicts residential building energy consumption by combining physics-based thermal models with learned parameters. Rather than choosing between slow, accurate physics simulators or fast but unreliable pure machine learning models, this approach marries both: a classical resistance-capacitance thermal model whose coefficients are predicted by a neural encoder, enabling accurate predictions across millions of heterogeneous buildings while remaining computationally efficient and physically interpretable.

## Theoretical Background
Building energy modeling traditionally relies on **physics-based simulators** (e.g., EnergyPlus) that solve differential equations governing heat transfer but require manual per-building calibration and run sequentially. Recent work has explored **neural sequence models** (transformers, RNNs) that scale to large datasets but treat buildings as black boxes, losing the physical structure that makes predictions trustworthy and generalizable.

NeuralBES builds on **grey-box modeling** — the classical approach of parameterizing simplified thermal dynamics (here, a resistance-capacitance network, a lumped-parameter model of thermal mass and heat loss) — but replaces hand-tuned parameters with a learned neural encoder. The core innovation is solving the linear recurrence relation via **log-space parallel scan**, a differentiable algorithm that allows gradients to flow across an entire year-long prediction horizon efficiently.

## Core Contributions
1. **Hybrid architecture**: A differentiable RC thermal model whose coefficients are predicted by a shared neural encoder from building metadata (floor area, vintage, HVAC type), eliminating per-building calibration.
2. **Physics-aware parameterization**: Static features map directly to physically bounded quantities (capacitances, conductances, equipment coefficients), ensuring predictions respect thermodynamic constraints.
3. **Efficient long-horizon differentiation**: Uses log-space parallel scan to solve the scalar linear recurrence in parallel while preserving gradient flow across full-year rollouts — solving a major computational bottleneck.
4. **Predictor-corrector thermostat closure**: A lightweight loop that captures the nonlinear feedback between thermostat setpoint and indoor temperature without sacrificing differentiability.
5. **Empirical validation**: Trained on ResStock (millions of U.S. buildings), NeuralBES achieves <4 MAPE error while remaining physics-valid, outperforming both black-box transformers/RNNs and simpler RC baselines at comparable parameter counts.

## Connections to Other Work
This work sits at the intersection of **scientific machine learning** (physics-informed neural networks) and **demand-side flexibility** for smart grids. It relates to efforts in [[mechanistic-interpretability]] by design — the learned parameters are directly inspectable and constrained to physical ranges. It contrasts with purely data-driven sequence models ([[transformers]], LSTMs) which sacrifice interpretability for flexibility, and with traditional grey-box approaches that lack the generalization capability of learned encoders.

The problem of **building-agnostic thermal modeling** connects to domain adaptation and meta-learning in deep learning: how can a single model handle extreme heterogeneity (1960s vs. 2010s construction, different climates, HVAC types)?

## Practical Applications
- **Demand response & grid balancing**: Predict which buildings can shift or curtail load (e.g., raise thermostat by 2°C for 4 hours) without comfort violation, enabling utilities to manage peak demand at scale.
- **Retrofit prioritization**: Use predicted energy signatures to identify high-waste buildings and rank retrofit investments (insulation, HVAC replacement).
- **Time-of-use pricing**: Utilities can simulate household energy costs under different dynamic pricing schemes and communicate expected bill impact before enrollment.
- **Building code & climate policy**: Regulators can simulate energy performance of new construction archetypes across climate zones to set realistic efficiency standards.
- **Real-time fault detection**: Compare predicted vs. actual temperature to flag HVAC failures or anomalous occupancy patterns.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **Demand response SaaS for utilities**: White-label platform that uses NeuralBES to identify and pre-qualify buildings for DR programs, then optimize dispatch in real-time. Target: regional utilities (50M+ customers).
2. **Energy audit automation for ESCOs**: Retrofit companies upload building metadata + utility bills; NeuralBES estimates baseline consumption and ROI for common interventions (insulation, heat pump), eliminating expensive on-site audits.
3. **Tenant-facing energy app**: Renters see personalized, physics-grounded predictions of next month's bill under different thermostat behaviors, gamifying conservation with transparent causality.
4. **Carbon accounting for corporates**: Multi-building firms (retail chains, offices) get scope 1-2 emissions with physics-based uncertainty quantification, enabling science-based net-zero commitments.
5. **Grid planning & resilience simulation**: Municipalities/grid operators simulate load under extreme weather (heatwaves, cold snaps) using population-scale thermal models, informing infrastructure investment.

## Technical Limitations
- **Limited to thermal dynamics**: NeuralBES models indoor temperature and HVAC load but does not capture plug/lighting loads, solar gains, or water heating — critical for total building energy.
- **Encoder generalization**: Trained on ResStock (primarily U.S. residential, three climate zones); generalization to commercial buildings, developing nations, or unseen climate extremes is unclear.
- **Metadata availability bottleneck**: Requires floor area, vintage, HVAC type; many buildings lack this data, forcing imputation which may introduce systematic bias.
- **Assumption of linearity in RC model**: The core recurrence assumes linear thermal dynamics; highly nonlinear behaviors (night setback hysteresis, occupant heterogeneity, solar shading) are lumped into a corrector loop whose capacity is limited.
- **Scalability to minute-level control**: Trained on hourly data; deployment for sub-hourly demand response may require retraining or additional latency-aware architecture.

## Open Questions
- **How does performance degrade with scarce or corrupted metadata?** What is the sensitivity to errors in floor area or vintage estimation?
- **Can this architecture handle commercial buildings or industrial thermal loads?** Do the same RC topologies work, or must the encoder be task-specific?
- **What drives physical inconsistency in black-box baselines?** Is it lack of inductive bias, insufficient data, or poor regularization? Could hybrid losses improve RNN/Transformer robustness?
- **Can learned parameters reveal building heterogeneity?** Do inferred capacitances/conductances cluster by vintage or geography, enabling insight into construction practices?
- **How to close occupancy feedback loops?** Current model assumes setpoint is exogenous; integrating actual occupancy (smart thermostat logs) would tighten predictions but require privacy mechanisms.

## Key Takeaway
Physics-aware neural models can predict building energy at scale without per-building calibration, combining speed of machine learning with trustworthiness of physics.
