# The Illusion of Visual Tool-Use: A Causal Audit of Thinking with Images

**Authors:** Zhiheng Wang, Bo Peng, Lai Wei, Chaochao Lu
**Published:** 2026-08-06 | **arXiv:** [2608.06270v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06270v1)

## Summary
The "thinking-with-images" paradigm equips multimodal LLMs with active visual operations such as crop-and-zoom. However, models using these operations often achieve only marginal or negative gains over direct inference at substantially higher token cost. They may also repeatedly crop irrelevant regions and fail on questions that direct inference answers correctly.

## Theoretical Background
AI agents combine models with tools, memory, and execution loops. Reliability therefore depends on decisions across a trajectory, not only the quality of isolated answers.

The paper addresses the trade-off identified in its abstract through a targeted method, representation, or evaluation framework.

## Core Contributions
1. However, models using these operations often achieve only marginal or negative gains over direct inference at substantially higher token cost.
2. To answer this question, we formulate visual tool-use as a causal graph that separates observation-mediated paths from action-induced shortcuts.
3. Across six representative models and five fine-grained perception benchmarks, we uncover policy miscalibration with two failure modes.
