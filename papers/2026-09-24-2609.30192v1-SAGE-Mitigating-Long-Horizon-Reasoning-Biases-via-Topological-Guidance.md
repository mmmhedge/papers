# SAGE: Mitigating Long-Horizon Reasoning Biases via Topological Guidance

**Authors:** Xinyue Zeng, Jiawei Zhang, Yujun Yan, Dawei Zhou
**Published:** 2026-09-24 | **arXiv:** [2609.30192v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30192v1)

## Summary
This paper identifies and addresses two fundamental problems in long-horizon reasoning for large language models: models get stuck exploring locally appealing but structurally flawed reasoning paths, and small mistakes compound across many steps to destroy rare correct solutions. The authors introduce SAGE, a framework that uses algebraic and geometric structural guidance to steer models toward better reasoning paths, achieving up to 8x improvements on challenging problems like the Andrews-Curtis conjecture.

## Theoretical Background
Long-horizon reasoning in LLMs typically faces sparse-reward regimes — situations where correct answers are rare and hard to find through trial and error. The paper builds on existing work in reinforcement learning (using rewards to train decision-making) and reasoning with language models, but identifies a new theoretical perspective: Symbolic Closure Analysis (SCA), which mathematically characterizes how the branching structure of reasoning problems creates two failure modes. The framework draws on algebraic topology (the study of spaces and their structural properties) and hyperbolic geometry (a non-Euclidean space with negative curvature that naturally represents hierarchical structures).

## Core Contributions
1. **Theoretical diagnosis**: Formal characterization of exploration bias (models favor locally plausible but globally wrong branches) and compounding bias (small errors accumulate across depth) through Symbolic Closure Analysis.
2. **Algebraic sparsification**: A technique that projects candidate next steps onto algebraic subspaces indexed by operators, filtering out spurious branching options.
3. **Hyperbolic structural guidance**: Embedding reasoning states into hyperbolic space to provide natural depth-wise distance signals that prevent error accumulation.
4. **Unified framework**: SAGE combines both mechanisms in a single, general approach validated across 12 benchmarks and 7 different model families.

## Connections to Other Work
This work relates to [[reinforcement-learning]] approaches for LLM reasoning (e.g., process reward models, outcome reward supervision) but takes a structural rather than purely reward-based approach. It connects to [[mechanistic-interpretability]] by diagnosing *why* reasoning fails, and to graph-based reasoning methods by using algebraic and topological structure. The problem setting shares DNA with [[agents]] that must plan over long horizons, and the geometric embedding component resonates with work on representation learning and manifold structures in deep learning.

## Practical Applications
1. **Mathematical theorem proving**: Solving open problems in pure mathematics (like the Andrews-Curtis conjecture) where rewards are extremely sparse.
2. **Scientific discovery**: Multi-step reasoning for chemistry, physics, or biology where intermediate steps are hard to evaluate but final results are verifiable.
3. **Program synthesis**: Generating correct code or formal specifications when wrong programs vastly outnumber correct ones.
4. **Multi-hop question answering**: Answering complex questions requiring reasoning across many documents or logical steps.
5. **Planning and control**: Robotics or autonomous systems tasks with long horizons and sparse success signals.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **AI Research Assistant for Mathematics**: A paid SaaS tool that helps mathematicians and students solve competition problems and research conjectures using SAGE-enhanced reasoning, with transparency into the reasoning path.
2. **Formal Verification Engine**: A B2B service for cryptography and financial software companies that uses SAGE to generate and verify formal proofs automatically.
3. **Scientific Literature Analysis Platform**: Software that performs multi-step reasoning over thousands of papers to identify novel hypotheses, sold to biotech/pharma R&D teams.
4. **Coding Interview Prep Tool**: An educational platform that guides users through hard algorithmic problems by showing well-structured reasoning trees (using SAGE guidance), marketed to interview prep companies.
5. **Enterprise Reasoning API**: A cloud API (like OpenAI or Anthropic) that developers can query for tasks requiring long-horizon, sparse-reward reasoning — positioned as "reasoning that doesn't give up."

## Technical Limitations
1. **Scalability**: Algebraic sparsification and hyperbolic embedding add computational overhead; unclear how this scales to extremely long reasoning chains (100+ steps) or massive action spaces.
2. **Problem structure assumptions**: The approach assumes reasoning can be meaningfully represented in algebraic and hyperbolic spaces; some domains (e.g., creative writing, subjective judgment) may not fit this structure.
3. **Sparse reward dependency**: The method still requires defining what constitutes a "correct" solution; it doesn't solve the problem of learning from implicit or human-feedback rewards.
4. **Generalization**: Strong results on specific benchmarks (especially math) don't guarantee transfer to novel reasoning domains without problem-specific tuning.
5. **Interpretability gap**: While the paper diagnoses biases theoretically, it's unclear whether practitioners can easily understand or debug why the structural guidance helps in specific cases.

## Open Questions
1. Can SAGE be combined with learned reward models rather than requiring ground-truth final answers?
2. How does algebraic sparsification perform on problems with multiple valid reasoning paths (where exploration diversity is valuable)?
3. Does hyperbolic guidance help in reasoning domains beyond mathematics and formal logic?
4. What is the sample efficiency compared to vanilla RL baselines — how many trajectories are needed?
5. Can the framework be extended to multi-agent reasoning or settings where intermediate states are partially observable?
6. How sensitive is SAGE to hyperparameters in the algebraic and hyperbolic components?

## Key Takeaway
Geometric structure and algebraic filtering can guide language models through long reasoning chains where sparse rewards would otherwise trap them in local dead ends.
