# Lessons from the hacks

**Source:** Interconnects (Nathan Lambert)
**Published:** 2026-08-09 | **Link:** [https://www.interconnects.ai/p/lessons-from-the-hacks](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/lessons-from-the-hacks)

## Summary
Nathan Lambert reflects on recent model security incidents and what they reveal about alignment and safety in large language models. The post explores the gap between current safety approaches and real-world robustness, offering perspective on priorities for future work.

## Key Points
- Recent "hacks" or jailbreaks expose vulnerabilities in deployed models despite safety training
- Current alignment techniques (RLHF, constitutional AI, etc.) may not address fundamental robustness issues
- Safety requires rethinking beyond post-training fixes to architectural and training-level considerations
- The distinction between alignment (values/intent) and robustness (adversarial resistance) matters practically

## Why It Matters
As models become more capable and widely deployed, understanding what actually prevents misuse is critical. If jailbreaks remain trivial despite alignment work, it signals either that current methods are insufficient or that safety priorities need recalibration. This directly impacts policy discussions, product safety decisions, and research investment.

Relates to [[mechanistic-interpretability]] research seeking to understand model behavior at a granular level, debates around [[reinforcement-learning]] alignment techniques, and broader discussions of adversarial robustness in [[deep-learning]]. Connects to empirical safety work in [[transformers]] and the theory-practice gap in alignment.

## Key Takeaway
Model jailbreaks reveal alignment training alone may not ensure real-world robustness; rethink safety strategy.
