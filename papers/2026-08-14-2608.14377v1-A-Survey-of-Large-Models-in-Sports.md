# A Survey of Large Models in Sports

**Authors:** Yichen Xu, Jianzhe Ma, Chuhan Wang, Zhonghao Cao, Liangyu Chen et al.
**Published:** 2026-08-14 | **arXiv:** [2608.14377v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14377v1)

## Summary
This survey catalogs how large language models and multimodal models are being applied across sports — from game analysis to fan engagement to coaching. It's important because sports is a massive global industry, and LLMs/vision models offer new ways to understand play, predict outcomes, and personalize fan experiences at scale.

## Theoretical Background
Large language models (LLMs) — neural networks trained on vast text data to predict and generate language — have recently been extended to multimodal settings, meaning they can process both text and images or video. In sports, this matters because the domain involves rich, diverse data: game commentary, statistical tables, video footage, and coaching notes. Prior work in [[computer-vision]] and [[NLP]] has tackled isolated sports tasks (e.g., pose estimation, score prediction), but this survey examines how general-purpose [[large-language-models]] and their multimodal variants (MLLMs) unify these tasks and enable new applications.

## Core Contributions
The paper provides: (1) a structured taxonomy of sports tasks and applications across different groups (players, coaches, analysts, fans); (2) an inventory and analysis of existing sports-related datasets and benchmarks; (3) a critical assessment of current limitations and open challenges in applying LLMs/MLLMs to sports. The survey is not proposing a novel model but rather synthesizing the landscape of how large models are being deployed in this domain.

## Connections to Other Work
This builds on broader trends in [[generative-models]] and multimodal AI. It relates to work on video understanding (extending image-based [[computer-vision]] to temporal data), [[transformers]] for sequential data, and the general shift toward foundation models that can be adapted across domains. It also connects to [[reinforcement-learning]] applications in sports (e.g., game strategy optimization), though the survey's emphasis is on language and vision.

## Practical Applications
- Real-time game commentary and highlight generation for broadcast and streaming platforms
- Automated injury risk prediction and player performance analytics for coaching staff
- Personalized fan engagement (chatbots answering sports trivia, generating player stats summaries)
- Scout reports and opponent analysis for professional teams
- Sports journalism automation (game recaps, statistical narratives)
- Referee decision support systems (analyzing controversial calls from multiple angles)

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **AI Sports Commentator Platform**: SaaS tool for smaller sports leagues and esports organizations to auto-generate live play-by-play commentary in multiple languages, reducing production costs.
2. **Injury Prevention & Load Management Dashboard**: Integrate video analysis + LLM reasoning to flag overuse patterns and recommend rest cycles to coaching staff.
3. **Fan-Facing AI Assistant**: Conversational AI that watches games live and answers real-time fan questions about rules, player stats, historical context (white-label for leagues/teams).
4. **Scout Report Automation**: Ingest video of opposing teams and auto-generate detailed tactical breakdowns and player comparisons for coaches.
5. **Sports Content Marketplace**: Platform where LLMs generate premium content (deep-dive player profiles, predictive analysis) for distribution to fans, media, and betting platforms.

## Technical Limitations
- Most datasets are biased toward popular sports (soccer, basketball, American football); niche sports lack labeled data.
- Video understanding is computationally expensive and still struggles with occlusions, crowded scenes, and unusual camera angles common in live sports.
- LLMs can hallucinate statistics or misinterpret nuanced tactical context; they require constant fact-checking against official records.
- Real-time latency is critical but often unmet; training large multimodal models for live inference remains expensive.
- Domain-specific jargon and rule variations across sports make transfer learning difficult.
- Privacy and consent issues arise when analyzing player biometrics or injury data.

## Open Questions
- How can models be trained more efficiently on sports video without requiring massive labeled datasets?
- Can multimodal models learn to reason about counterfactual game scenarios (e.g., "what if this player had been healthy")?
- How do we reliably evaluate whether model predictions actually improve coaching decisions or scouting outcomes in practice?
- Can models capture domain knowledge beyond statistics—e.g., team culture, player motivation, referee tendencies?
- What's the optimal way to combine LLMs (reasoning over text) with vision models (understanding video) for end-to-end sports intelligence?

## Key Takeaway
Large language and vision models are reshaping sports analytics, commentary, and fan engagement, but datasets and real-time performance remain key barriers.
