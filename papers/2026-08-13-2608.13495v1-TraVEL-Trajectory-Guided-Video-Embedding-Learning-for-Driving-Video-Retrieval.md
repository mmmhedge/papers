# TraVEL: Trajectory-Guided Video Embedding Learning for Driving-Video Retrieval

**Authors:** Yi-Chung Chen, Philip Jacobson, Tom Lampo, Yiren Lu, Jin Yao et al.
**Published:** 2026-08-13 | **arXiv:** [2608.13495v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13495v1)

## Summary
This paper addresses efficient retrieval of relevant video clips from large driving datasets by developing TraVEL, a method that fine-tunes multimodal embedding models using trajectory-guided supervision. The approach combines supervised fine-tuning on reasoning traces with reinforcement learning (using ego-trajectory similarity as a reward signal) to create compact video embeddings that capture fine-grained motion events — like turning left versus right — without requiring expert rules or auxiliary perception systems at test time.

## Theoretical Background
The work builds on multimodal embedding models (like Qwen3-VL-Embedding) that map videos and text into a shared vector space, typically trained with contrastive objectives like InfoNCE. Traditional driving-event retrieval relied on hand-crafted rules and multi-stage perception pipelines. General-purpose embedding models exploit shortcut features from static scene context rather than motion dynamics. The paper introduces trajectory similarity as a reward signal for Group Relative Policy Optimization — a reinforcement learning technique that ranks multiple examples based on relative reward differences rather than absolute scores. During training, trajectories act as privileged information (available only at training time) to guide the model toward motion-aware representations; at test time, only single-vector embeddings are needed.

## Core Contributions
1. **Motion-aware embedding adaptation**: Demonstrates that caption-only fine-tuning is insufficient for motion-centric driving events; motion explicitly needs to be supervised.
2. **Trajectory-guided RL framework**: Introduces the use of ego-trajectory similarity as a reward signal within Group Relative Policy Optimization to refine embeddings without requiring trajectories at inference.
3. **Driving-video retrieval benchmark**: Constructs a new benchmark from nuReasoning dataset with motion-focused evaluation metrics (longitudinal and lateral mAP).
4. **Consistent improvements across scales**: Shows gains of 9.8 and 4.7 mAP points at 2B model size (7.2 and 1.5 at 8B), demonstrating the approach scales.

## Connections to Other Work
This work sits at the intersection of [[Computer Vision]] representation learning and [[Reinforcement Learning]]. It relates to broader trends in using privileged information during training (e.g., distillation), adapting general-purpose models to specific domains, and leveraging structured signals (trajectories) as supervision. The use of Group Relative Policy Optimization connects to recent work in preference-based RL for alignment, though here applied to embedding learning. The two-stage approach (SFT then RL refinement) mirrors patterns seen in [[RLHF]] for language models, adapted to the multimodal video domain.

## Practical Applications
- **Autonomous vehicle development**: Accelerate data curation by efficiently finding rare driving scenarios (e.g., specific maneuvers, weather conditions, safety-critical events) from terabytes of fleet data.
- **Safety auditing and compliance**: Quickly retrieve video clips matching specific motion patterns for accident investigation or regulatory reporting without manual review.
- **Simulation and testing**: Source realistic driving scenarios matching particular trajectory profiles for autonomous vehicle testing and validation.
- **Insurance and fleet management**: Analyze driver behavior by retrieving videos of specific maneuvers (aggressive turns, hard braking) for coaching or risk assessment.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **Autonomous vehicle data platform**: SaaS product that lets AV companies upload driving logs and search by motion events ("find all hard left turns under 25 mph") using TraVEL embeddings; charge per query or per GB of indexed video.
2. **Insurance risk analytics**: B2B service that indexes customer dashcam footage and flags risky driving patterns (aggressive acceleration/deceleration, tailgating) via semantic motion search.
3. **Synthetic scenario library**: Generate or filter high-fidelity AV test scenarios by trajectory similarity; sell curated scenario sets to testing/validation teams.
4. **Fleet operations intelligence**: Real-time dashboard for logistics companies to monitor and retrieve unsafe driving events without manual annotation; integrate with driver coaching workflows.
5. **Driving school analytics**: Tool for instructors to automatically retrieve student video clips showing specific mistakes (poor lane alignment, late braking) for personalized feedback.

## Technical Limitations
- **Trajectory dependency at training**: Requires ground-truth or high-quality ego-trajectory labels during fine-tuning; not all driving datasets have reliable trajectory annotations.
- **Generalization beyond driving**: Method is validated only on driving videos; unclear if trajectory-based RL transfer to other motion-heavy domains (sports, robotics, surveillance).
- **Embedding-only retrieval**: Single-vector embeddings may lose fine spatial details; semantic search on motion remains coarser than explicit pose-based systems with multi-stage pipelines.
- **Scale of benchmark**: Built on nuReasoning subset; unclear how performance scales to orders-of-magnitude larger, noisier real-world fleet data with varied camera angles and quality.
- **Computational cost of RL fine-tuning**: Group Relative Policy Optimization requires ranking multiple trajectory-similarity pairs; cost of this RL stage not reported, limiting practical adoption for rapid model updates.

## Open Questions
- How sensitive is TraVEL to trajectory noise or errors in annotation? Does performance degrade gracefully with imperfect privileged supervision?
- Can trajectory similarity be replaced with other motion-grounded signals (optical flow, IMU data, acceleration logs) for generalization to datasets without pose?
- Does the approach scale to orders of magnitude more video and more diverse driving conditions (rain, snow, urban/highway mix)?
- How much labeled data is needed to see improvements? Is there a data efficiency frontier?
- Can the learned embeddings transfer to downstream tasks (e.g., anomaly detection, driver intent prediction) or are they specific to retrieval?

## Key Takeaway
Trajectory-guided reinforcement learning fine-tunes video embeddings to reliably find motion-specific driving events without needing trajectories at test time.
