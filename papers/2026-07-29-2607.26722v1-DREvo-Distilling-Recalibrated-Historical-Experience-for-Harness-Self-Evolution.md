# DREvo: Distilling Recalibrated Historical Experience for Harness Self-Evolution

**Authors:** Hanghui Guo, Weijie Shi, Zhangze Chen, Shengxiang Xu, Yishu Wang et al.
**Published:** 2026-07-29 | **arXiv:** [2607.26722v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26722v1)

## Summary
This paper addresses a practical problem in building AI agents: how to automatically improve "harnesses" (instruction sets or prompts that guide LLM behavior) by learning from past attempts. The authors propose DREvo, a method that intelligently filters historical experience to determine which past insights remain relevant, then distills those insights into concrete directions for improvement. On five benchmarks, DREvo improves agent performance by 14–16% compared to existing approaches while producing more stable, predictable evolution across iterations.

## Theoretical Background
The work builds on recent advances in **harness self-evolution** — the idea that LLM agents can iteratively refine their own instructions using reinforcement learning-style feedback loops. Prior approaches (like those in prompt optimization and in-context learning research) attempted to learn from accumulated trial-and-error data, but they treated all historical evidence equally, ignoring the fact that what worked for an earlier agent configuration may not work now. The paper draws conceptually on curriculum learning and evidence-based search methods, where information must be curated and contextualized to be useful.

## Core Contributions
1. **Problem diagnosis**: Identifies two concrete failure modes in existing harness self-evolution methods — lack of dynamic validity checking and lack of explicit distillation of evidence into search directions.
2. **Function-level evidence anchoring**: A mechanism to ground historical observations in specific, reusable components (functions/capabilities) rather than monolithic past states.
3. **State-dependent evidence recalibration**: Dynamically reassesses whether historical insights remain applicable given the current harness configuration.
4. **Role-conditioned search intent distillation**: Translates valid historical evidence into actionable, interpretable improvements rather than raw signal.
5. **Empirical validation**: Demonstrates 16.2% average improvement on domain reasoning tasks and 14.2% on agentic tasks, with notably smoother convergence trajectories.

## Connections to Other Work
DREvo sits at the intersection of [[reinforcement-learning|RL]]-based prompt optimization, in-context learning, and automated machine learning (AutoML) hyperparameter search. It improves on prior harness self-evolution work by borrowing ideas from curriculum learning (selective experience replay) and from [[mechanistic-interpretability]] (understanding which components of a harness matter). The evidence recalibration step echoes techniques in online learning and non-stationary bandit problems, where the environment changes over time and past rewards may not predict future ones. The distillation component is conceptually similar to [[knowledge distillation]] but applied to search guidance rather than model weights.

## Practical Applications
- **LLM agent platforms**: Tools like AutoGPT, LangChain agents, and enterprise AI assistants could use DREvo to autonomously improve their own system prompts without human intervention.
- **Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems**: Automatically refine retriever and reranker prompts based on query-document matching feedback.
- **Customer support chatbots**: Continuously adapt response templates and decision trees by learning from conversation logs.
- **Agentic workflows in enterprises**: Auto-tune workflow orchestration instructions for tasks like data analysis or document review.
- **Evaluation and testing frameworks**: Automatically improve test harnesses or validation prompts for LLM outputs.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **AutoHarness.ai**: A SaaS platform that takes raw LLM agents and automatically optimizes their system prompts, retrieval instructions, and tool-use policies using DREvo-style evolution — measurable accuracy improvements without manual prompt engineering.
2. **Agent Compliance Tuner**: For regulated industries (finance, healthcare), automatically evolve agent behavior constraints and safety guidelines based on historical edge cases and policy feedback.
3. **Prompt Intelligence Dashboard**: A monitoring tool that continuously evolves agent prompts in production, tracks what changes led to performance gains, and explains recommendations to human operators.
4. **Multi-Agent Orchestra**: Optimize interdependent harnesses in multi-agent systems (e.g., researcher + validator + executor) by learning which instructions improve coordination.
5. **Domain-Specific Agent Marketplace**: Pre-built agents (legal, medical, financial) that self-evolve within sandbox environments and are sold to enterprises with continuous improvement baked in.

## Technical Limitations
- **Harness representation**: The method assumes harnesses can be meaningfully decomposed into functions/components; highly unstructured or narrative prompts may not yield clear evidence anchors.
- **Evaluation cost**: DREvo still requires running agents and evaluating performance at each iteration; it cannot reduce the wall-clock time or compute budget per attempt, only improve the quality of decisions *given* a fixed budget.
- **Domain generalization**: The five benchmarks are likely specialized to reasoning and agentic tasks; applicability to other domains (vision-language, code generation, creative writing) is unclear.
- **Stability in small-data regimes**: With very few historical trials, recalibration may introduce noise rather than reduce it.
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity**: The method introduces recalibration thresholds, distillation weights, and other tuning knobs whose sensitivity is not fully characterized.

## Open Questions
- How does DREvo scale to agents with hundreds of modular components or very large harnesses?
- Can recalibration be made robust in adversarial settings (e.g., when historical data is partly corrupted or biased)?
- Does the method generalize to non-LLM agents (e.g., robotic control, trading systems)?
- Can evidence be transferred across unrelated tasks, or is it always task-specific?
- What is the sample complexity — how many historical trials does DREvo need before it outperforms simpler baselines?

## Key Takeaway
Intelligently filtering historical agent experience enables faster, more stable harness improvement with 14–16% accuracy gains.
