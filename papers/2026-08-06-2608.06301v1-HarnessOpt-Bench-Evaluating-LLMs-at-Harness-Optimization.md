# HarnessOpt-Bench: Evaluating LLMs at Harness Optimization

**Authors:** Varun Ursekar, Apaar Shanker, Yash Maurya, Shehab Yasser, Vijay S. Kalmath et al.
**Published:** 2026-08-06 | **arXiv:** [2608.06301v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06301v1)

## Summary
As LLMs are increasingly deployed within agentic systems, their capabilities depend not only on the model weights but also on the harness: the prompts, tools, control flow, memory, and orchestration code surrounding them. This makes automated harness optimization -- the iterative and evaluation-guided improvement of a harness by an AI system -- both an important route to improving AI systems and a demanding capability for AI systems themselves. Yet the community lacks a common protocol for measuring how well frontier LLMs perform at this task.

## Theoretical Background
AI agents combine a language model with tools, memory, and an execution loop. Their quality depends not only on individual answers but on decisions made across a complete trajectory, which makes evaluation and debugging harder than ordinary text generation.

The paper is positioned against prior approaches described in its abstract and asks whether a more targeted representation, evaluation method, or optimization procedure can improve the relevant trade-off.

## Core Contributions
1. We introduce HarnessOpt-Bench, a benchmark for end-to-end harness optimization under expensive and stochastic evaluation.
2. Experiment results show that optimizer models separate more than the coding harnesses they act through, native harnesses are not consistently superior, and gains vary substantially across tasks and seed regimes.

## Technical Limitations
This note is based on the title, metadata, and abstract rather than a full-paper audit. The abstract cannot establish implementation completeness, statistical robustness, or whether all baselines were tuned comparably. Reported gains may depend on the selected datasets, models, task definitions, and compute environment. Deployment would require independent validation on out-of-distribution inputs, measurement of total runtime and cost, and analysis of failure cases. Any high-stakes use would also need human oversight and domain-specific governance.

## Open Questions
1. Do the results hold across substantially different datasets, model families, and deployment conditions?
2. Which component contributes most to the reported improvement under a controlled ablation?
3. How does the method compare under matched total compute, latency, and engineering effort?
4. What are the dominant failure modes, and can they be detected before they affect downstream decisions?
5. Can the approach scale without weakening its interpretability, guarantees, or data efficiency?
