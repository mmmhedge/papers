# Is Per-Agent Policy Composition Safe? Rethinking Successor-Feature Transfer in Cooperative Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning

**Authors:** Zijian Zhao, Sen Li
**Published:** 2026-08-12 | **arXiv:** [2608.11658v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11658v1)

## Summary
This paper addresses a critical safety problem in multi-agent reinforcement learning: when teams of agents independently recombine learned policies to serve new objectives after deployment, the resulting joint behavior can be worse than any single policy in their library. The authors prove this failure occurs uniquely in multi-agent settings and propose MA-USFA, a hierarchical method that composes agent policies safely while remaining flexible enough to assign different goals to different agents.

## Theoretical Background
The paper builds on successor features with generalized policy improvement (GPPI) — a single-agent framework that allows fast adaptation to new reward functions by decomposing value functions into state features and learned successor feature matrices. In single-agent settings, this approach guarantees that composed policies never underperform the original library. The work extends this to multi-agent contexts where agents must coordinate, adding complexity because each agent's environment depends on the actions of teammates. The failure mode identified is fundamentally multi-agent: when one agent recomposes its policy, it changes the dynamics that other agents experience, breaking the independence assumptions that make single-agent successor features work.

## Core Contributions
1. **Theoretical result**: Proof that independent per-agent policy composition (the naive multi-agent extension) can produce joint behavior strictly worse than every policy in the library—a failure with no single-agent analog.
2. **Characterization of safe rules**: Demonstration that synchronized composition (moving the whole team to one jointly trained policy) is the only unconditionally safe fixed rule, but it sacrifices flexibility for heterogeneous objectives.
3. **MA-USFA algorithm**: A two-layer hierarchical method combining (i) universal successor feature approximators that condition each agent's value estimates on teammates' objectives, and (ii) a cross-agent composer that selects which library entry each agent follows and applies global corrections that individual agents cannot represent.
4. **Deployment efficiency**: The method trains once over an objective distribution and applies at test time without per-task adaptation.

## Practical Applications
- **Fleet management**: Autonomous vehicle fleets that must adapt to changing traffic patterns or delivery priorities without retraining.
- **Traffic signal control**: Multi-intersection coordination systems that shift objectives (e.g., prioritizing throughput vs. emissions) without rebuilding control policies.
- **Warehouse robotics**: Multi-robot systems that dynamically reweight objectives (speed, energy efficiency, collision avoidance) based on operational load.
- **Power grid management**: Distributed agent networks balancing generation, load, and resilience under varying operational goals.
- **Game-playing teams**: Multi-agent games where team strategies must adapt to opponent behavior or rule changes without full retraining.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **Multi-Agent Policy Composition-as-a-Service**: A cloud platform for enterprises (logistics, traffic management, robotics) to upload trained agent libraries and dynamically compose them for new objectives at deployment time, with safety guarantees.
2. **Safe Autonomous Fleet Management Software**: B2B SaaS for delivery and transportation companies that automatically composes driver/vehicle policies to balance speed, cost, and safety as market conditions shift.
3. **Adaptive Multi-Robot Task Allocation**: Middleware that composes pre-trained robot policies for heterogeneous teams, enabling dynamic goal assignment (e.g., some robots optimize for speed, others for precision) without retraining.
4. **Real-Time Traffic Optimization Engine**: Smart city software that composes intersection control policies across a network to balance congestion, emissions, and transit priorities in real-time.
5. **Reinforcement Learning Model Registry & Composer**: Developer-facing platform (similar to model hubs) where teams publish trained multi-agent policies, and other teams safely compose them with plug-in safety validation.

## Technical Limitations
- **Assumption of value decomposability**: The method assumes successor features can be meaningfully conditioned on teammate objectives; this may break if agent rewards are deeply entangled or when teamwork requires implicit coordination.
- **Library coverage**: Safety and performance depend on the objective distribution used during training; deployment objectives far outside this distribution may not be well-served.
- **Scalability**: Two-layer composition may become computationally expensive or unstable as the number of agents or library size grows.
- **Communication overhead**: Conditioning each agent's value estimates on teammates' objectives may require additional communication, which is costly in bandwidth-limited or latency-sensitive systems.
- **Non-stationary teams**: The method assumes a fixed team composition; adding or removing agents mid-deployment is not addressed.
- **Partial observability**: Limited treatment of how method applies under partial observability or when agents cannot directly observe teammates' objectives.

## Open Questions
1. How does MA-USFA scale to teams with 10+ agents or very large policy libraries?
2. Can the method be extended to non-stationary environments where the underlying dynamics change between training and deployment?
3. What is the minimal amount of information agents need to communicate to achieve safe composition? Can purely decentralized variants exist?
4. How does the method perform when the training objective distribution poorly matches deployment objectives?
5. Can successor features be learned more sample-efficiently in multi-agent settings, or does conditioning on teammates' objectives increase sample complexity?
6. How does this approach interact with other multi-agent learning paradigms like independent learners, centralized training with decentralized execution (CTDE), or communication-based protocols?

## Key Takeaway
Independent policy composition fails uniquely in multi-agent systems; MA-USFA achieves safe, flexible adaptation by conditioning agent values on teammates' objectives.
