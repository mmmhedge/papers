# PlotGround: Grounding Plot Digitization in Real Scientific Figures and Their Source Data

**Authors:** Yaohui Zhang, Binxu Li, Haoyi Duan, Jiacheng Miao, Yixin Wang et al.
**Published:** 2026-10-05 | **arXiv:** [2610.06825v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06825v1)

## Summary
This paper introduces PlotGround, a system for automatically creating benchmarks that test how well AI models can extract precise numerical values from real scientific figures. The authors built a dataset of 1,119 questions from actual bioRxiv papers paired with their source data, revealing that current multimodal models (which process both images and text) struggle with precise recovery—achieving 87.5% accuracy at ±5% error but dropping to 64-76% at the tighter ±2% threshold. This matters because many published scientific results exist only as figures in PDFs, and accurate automated digitization could enable better reproducibility and data reuse.

## Theoretical Background
Plot digitization builds on two established areas: optical character recognition and numerical extraction from images. Multimodal models — neural networks that process both visual and textual information, often built on transformer architectures (networks using attention mechanisms to weigh different parts of input) — have recently improved at visual reasoning tasks. The gap this paper addresses is between *approximate visual reading* (can you tell if a line is roughly high or low?) and *precise quantitative recovery* (can you extract the exact plotted value?). Prior work on chart understanding typically relied on synthetic datasets, which don't capture the messy reality of published figures (artifacts, axis labels at odd angles, variable quality).

## Core Contributions
1. **PlotGround pipeline**: An automated method to match scientific figures to their source data tables using cross-document linking, identify which figure panels can be reconstructed from source data, and generate ground-truth quantitative questions.

2. **PlotGround-1k benchmark**: A human-verified dataset of 1,119 questions spanning 1,066 real bioRxiv preprints — substantially larger and more realistic than prior chart benchmarks.

3. **Precision-tolerance tradeoff analysis**: Systematic evaluation showing that models performing acceptably at ±5% error collapse at ±2%, quantifying the gap between approximate and precise reading.

4. **Figure-vs.-source comparison**: A novel paired evaluation structure revealing that providing source tables (as structured data) instead of figures raises accuracy from 90.0% to 97.4% while reducing computational cost by 72%, isolating the visual perception bottleneck.

## Connections to Other Work
This extends prior chart understanding work (which focused on synthetic or limited-scope datasets) into the domain of real scientific communication. It relates to document understanding and information extraction pipelines, and shares methodological DNA with [[RLHF]] (learning from human feedback) by using human verification to validate benchmark quality. The finding that structured data dramatically improves recovery connects to broader themes in [[large-language-models]] research: LLMs excel at reasoning over structured text but struggle with visual grounding. The paper also implicitly speaks to reproducibility challenges in science — a concern raised by initiatives like Open Science — by showing that figure digitization is a bottleneck.

## Practical Applications
- **Automated literature synthesis**: Systematically extract all reported results from a set of papers to build meta-analytic datasets or identify contradictions in published findings.
- **Data rescue for historical papers**: Digitize figures from older publications (where source data was never released) to make them machine-readable for reanalysis.
- **Publisher workflows**: Publishing platforms could use this to automatically generate structured data tables from author-submitted figures as a quality-control or archival step.
- **Regulatory/clinical review**: Automated extraction of reported patient outcomes or drug efficacy metrics from clinical trial papers for compliance verification.
- **Research reproducibility audits**: Flag papers where reported figures don't align with claimed source data.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **PlotGround-as-a-Service**: A web API that takes a PDF figure and returns extracted numerical values with confidence scores; target early adopters: systematic review teams, pharma regulatory affairs, investment research firms.

2. **Scientific Figure Search Engine**: Index all published figures by their quantitative content (e.g., "find all papers showing effect sizes between 0.5 and 1.0 for this outcome"); combine PlotGround extraction with semantic search.

3. **Data Audit & Compliance Tool**: For publishers or grant agencies — automatically cross-check figures against supplementary source files to flag potential transcription errors or fraud before publication.

4. **Legacy Literature Digitizer**: SaaS tool for life sciences companies to bulk-convert scanned historical papers and unpublished internal reports into structured datasets for AI training or meta-analysis.

5. **AI-Powered Lab Notebook Assistant**: Integrate plot digitization into ELN (electronic lab notebook) software; when researchers paste/upload a figure, automatically extract values and compare against their own raw data files for consistency checking.

## Technical Limitations
- **Dataset scope**: Benchmark limited to bioRxiv preprints; generalization to physics, chemistry, or social science figures (different axis conventions, color use, notation) is untested.
- **Panel matching bottleneck**: The pipeline still requires manual or heuristic-based matching between figures and source tables; at scale, this could introduce systematic bias (easier-to-match figures may differ from hard cases).
- **Axis label parsing**: Results depend on legible axis labels and tick marks; heavily annotated or stylized figures could fail upstream.
- **Precision-tolerance tradeoff unsolved**: The paper documents the problem (±2% precision is hard) without proposing solutions; domain-specific priors (e.g., knowing typical effect-size ranges in biology) aren't incorporated.
- **Source data assumptions**: Evaluation assumes source data is available and trustworthy; doesn't address cases where source data itself is corrupted or missing.
- **Multimodal model variance**: Best accuracy (87.5%) comes from specific models; results may not transfer to newer or domain-specific visual models released after publication.

## Open Questions
- How does performance degrade on figures with overlapping lines, error bars, or non-standard plot types (violin plots, heatmaps, network diagrams)?
- Can models be fine-tuned specifically for ±2% precision using PlotGround-1k, and what is the accuracy ceiling?
- Does providing models with the full paper context (methods, figure captions) improve extraction versus isolated figure viewing?
- How transferable are models trained on PlotGround-1k to other scientific domains (physics, chemistry, economics)?
- Can the figure-source matching problem be solved end-to-end without manual intervention, and what accuracy is required downstream?
- Do human extractors show similar ±2% vs. ±5% accuracy collapse, suggesting the limitation is perceptual or computational?

## Key Takeaway
Real scientific figures require ±2% precision for reproducibility, but AI models extract numbers only at ±5% accuracy before collapsing.
