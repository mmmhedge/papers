# Reconcile Once, Write Anytime: A Trust-Tiered Librarian and a Multi-Agent Writer for Drift-Free, Point-in-Time Research

**Authors:** Xing Zhang, Yanwei Cui, Guanghui Wang, Peiyang He
**Published:** 2026-08-13 | **arXiv:** [2608.12984v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12984v1)

## Summary
This paper addresses a critical problem in LLM-generated long-form reports: factual drift, self-contradiction, and loss of provenance (the chain of evidence). The authors build a two-part system—a deterministic "librarian" that maintains a trust-tiered knowledge library with timestamped sources, and a multi-agent "writer" that composes reports grounded in evidence at any historical cutoff without looking ahead. The work matters because it solves reproducibility and verifiability in AI-generated research, moving beyond simple retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) to a structured, auditable knowledge layer.

## Theoretical Background
This work builds on [[Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)]] systems, which inject external knowledge into language models to reduce hallucination. However, standard RAG treats each query independently and cannot maintain consistency across a document or enforce temporal coherence. The paper also draws on knowledge graph construction (organizing facts into structured relationships) and provenance tracking from database and archival systems—the idea that every claim should trace back to its source with a timestamp. The "trust tiering" concept adapts ideas from information science: not all sources are equally reliable (SEC filings > Wikipedia > media reports), and this ranking must be explicit and queryable. The multi-agent aspect leverages recent work in agentic systems where specialized agents (here, reading agents, writing agents, red-team agents) collaborate and feed signals back to improve overall system reliability.

## Core Contributions
1. **Trust-tiered librarian**: A deterministic ingestion system that parses timestamped sources into an ontology with three layers—evidence cards (granular facts with source), a metric ledger (authoritative numeric values), and a claim graph (relationships between statements). Crucially, this is built *once* and reused, not regenerated per query.

2. **Point-in-time replay without look-ahead**: A writing system that respects temporal boundaries—it reads only evidence with `as_of <= T` for cutoff T, preventing future data from leaking into past reports. This enables reproducible historical reports.

3. **Tier-first source selection**: A mechanism that prioritizes sources by trustworthiness (company filings > government stats > media), eliminating cross-sectional contradictions. Evaluated on 22 gold cases where baselines fail.

4. **Red-team feedback loop**: Defects flagged by adversarial review propagate back into the librarian, self-correcting future runs with zero manual intervention.

5. **Model routing by difficulty**: Dynamic selection of LLM capacity (cheaper models for easy tasks, e.g. Claude 3.5 Sonnet, premium models for hard ones, e.g. Opus) yields 3.7x speedup while exceeding all-Opus quality.

## Connections to Other Work
- **[[Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)]]**: This paper moves *beyond* naive RAG (chunk retrieval per query) to a persistent, structured knowledge base—more aligned with semantic caching and knowledge graphs.
- **[[Fact verification and contradiction detection]]**: Related to work on checking LLM outputs against corpora; this paper embeds verification into the source layer itself.
- **[[Temporal reasoning in LLMs]]**: Connects to literature on point-in-time queries and temporal databases; ensures models don't conflate past and present.
- **[[Multi-agent collaboration]]**: Uses patterns similar to [[Chain-of-Thought]] and agent orchestration, but with explicit feedback loops (red-teaming → correction).
- **[[Alignment and truthfulness]]**: Addresses core alignment concerns—ensuring AI systems produce consistent, auditable, evidence-grounded outputs.

## Practical Applications
1. **Financial research platforms**: Automated generation of equity research reports grounded in audited SEC filings, with zero metric contradictions and full provenance links for compliance.
2. **Regulatory and compliance reporting**: Auto-generate historical regulatory narrative reports for audits; replay them at any past cutoff to verify consistency.
3. **Enterprise knowledge synthesis**: Internal research teams use the librarian to curate company data (sales, hiring, costs) and auto-generate board summaries that never contradict themselves.
4. **Academic or policy report generation**: Think tanks or government agencies auto-generate policy briefs grounded in timestamped government statistics and primary sources, with zero look-ahead bias.
5. **News and fact-checking platforms**: Automatically generate and update articles on ongoing events (e.g., crises, earnings seasons) with explicit source tiers and temporal coherence, reducing misinformation.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **FactBase (B2B SaaS)**: A research-report-as-a-service platform for investment banks and asset managers. Upload SEC filings, earnings transcripts, and macro data. Auto-generate point-in-time equity research at any historical cutoff, with contradiction-free metrics and full audit trails. Premium: red-team integration.

2. **ComplianceAI Auditor**: For regulated firms (banks, insurers), auto-audit internal research and reports for factual drift, temporal inconsistencies, and source leaks. Flag when newer lower-tier sources displace authoritative ones.

3. **TemporalGraph (B2B)**: A knowledge-as-a-service platform where enterprises ingest their own data (contracts, employee records, financial statements) into a trust-tiered graph. Expose via API for internal AI agents and humans; guarantee reproducibility and zero look-ahead.

4. **NewsChain (B2C/Media)**: A reader-facing app for long-form journalism. Articles link to their evidence cards, trust tiers are visible, and you can "replay" any article at past cutoffs to see what was known when. Subscription model.

5. **RegulatoryGPT (B2G/Consulting)**: Serve government/policy orgs. Ingest laws, regulations, and guidance as trust-tiered sources. Generate policy briefs, impact assessments, and FAQs that cite legislation precisely and never contradict statutory text.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Ontology design is manual labor**: Building a trust-tiered ontology and defining relationships requires domain expertise. The paper uses SEC/BLS/Wikipedia (well-structured), but messy, unstructured domains (qualitative research, emails, interviews) are harder.

2. **Source ranking assumptions**: The tier system assumes clear hierarchies (SEC > media). Real-world domains may have competing authorities or context-dependent trust (a trade report may outweigh a company statement in some analyses). The paper doesn't address tie-breaking or uncertainty in tier assignment.

3. **Evidence card granularity is a hyperparameter**: Too granular = noise and retrieval overhead; too coarse = loss of nuance. No principled way to choose.

4. **Scalability of the metric ledger**: With millions of entities and metrics, collision detection and merging (identifying when "revenue Q1 2023" across sources is the same metric) becomes a data integration bottleneck. Not clear how the system handles synonymy or slight variations.

5. **Red-team loop assumes error signals are correct**: If the red-team verdict is wrong, it propagates as a false correction. The paper doesn't address human review of red-team outputs or confidence weighting.

6. **No discussion of latency for librarian updates**: Ingesting new sources and recompiling the knowledge layer may take minutes/hours. For real-time domains (high-frequency finance, breaking news), this lag is problematic.

7. **Evaluation is domain-specific**: Tested only on financial/labor/Wikipedia data. Claims about generality to "long-form research" are not validated on other domains (medical, legal, scientific).

## Open Questions
1. **Can the system handle conflicting but credible sources?** (e.g., two reputable economists with opposite forecasts). How does tier-first selection avoid suppressing legitimate debate?

2. **How does the librarian handle concept drift?** If the meaning of "inflation" or "revenue" changes over regulatory time, can the ontology auto-adapt, or does it require manual revision?

3. **What is the human cost of ontology maintenance?** The paper doesn't report person-hours to build/update the trust hierarchy. Is this practical for a startup?

4. **Can you compose *contradictory* reports intentionally?** (e.g., "bull case" vs. "bear case" for the same company at the same cutoff). Does the librarian support multi-thesis querying, or only one canonical truth?

5. **How does the system handle probabilistic claims or confidence intervals?** Most evidence cards are discrete facts; how do you represent "likely," "uncertain," or ranges?
