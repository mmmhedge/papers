# Anatomy Contextualized Adaption of CT Foundation Models

**Authors:** Roshan Kenia, Stephanie L McNamara, William Lotter
**Published:** 2026-07-29 | **arXiv:** [2607.27154v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27154v1)

## Summary
This paper proposes Anatomy Contextualized Adaptation (ACA), a lightweight method to improve CT scan foundation models by aligning fine-grained anatomy features with medical text while preserving global context. Rather than retraining models from scratch, ACA adapts frozen pre-trained models efficiently—in under an hour—and outperforms both standard foundation models and existing fine-grained approaches on medical imaging benchmarks.

## Theoretical Background
CT foundation models are large neural networks pre-trained on whole-volume CT scans to learn general visual representations. Recent work has shown that vision-language models—networks that align images with text descriptions—perform better when both modalities are semantically aligned. However, whole-volume CT models lose fine-grained anatomical detail by compressing entire 3D volumes into single representations. Fine-grained approaches solve this by aligning individual anatomy structures (organs, tissues) to anatomy-specific text, but they typically discard the valuable global context that seeing the whole scan provides. This paper bridges that gap using transformer architectures—attention-based neural networks that model relationships between elements—to maintain cross-anatomy context while working at the anatomy level.

## Core Contributions
1. **ACA framework**: A parameter-efficient adapter that refines anatomy-level embeddings (extracted via TotalSegmentator, a segmentation model) using a transformer to capture cross-anatomy relationships, then aligns them to both anatomy-specific and scan-level text from radiology reports.

2. **Computational efficiency**: Achieves strong performance in <1 hour of training (with cached embeddings), versus training fine-grained models from scratch—making the approach practical for clinical adoption.

3. **Empirical validation**: Demonstrates consistent improvements over frozen foundation baselines and existing fine-grained methods on zero-shot finding classification benchmarks (Merlin, CT-RATE).

4. **Interpretability**: Shows that learned attention weights in the inter-anatomy transformer route contextually plausible cross-anatomy relationships, suggesting the model learns meaningful anatomical dependencies.

## Connections to Other Work
ACA sits at the intersection of several areas: [[vision-language models]] (aligning visual and textual modalities), [[transfer learning]] (adapting pre-trained models rather than retraining), and [[medical image analysis]]. It improves on prior fine-grained vision-language approaches by combining anatomy-level detail with global context—a trade-off that had not been well-solved. The use of TotalSegmentator for decomposition and transformers for cross-anatomy reasoning parallels recent work in [[multi-scale learning]] and [[hierarchical representations]] in medical imaging.

## Practical Applications
1. **Clinical decision support**: Radiologists using ACA-enhanced models to flag findings and abnormalities in CT scans with better accuracy, reducing missed diagnoses.

2. **Automated report generation**: Fine-tuning ACA embeddings to generate natural-language radiology reports from CT images, reducing radiologist workload.

3. **Cross-hospital model adaptation**: Using ACA's lightweight adaptation to quickly tune foundation models to local imaging protocols and patient populations without expensive retraining.

4. **Anatomy-specific risk stratification**: Leveraging anatomy-level embeddings to identify which organs or regions are clinically most relevant for a given patient, improving prioritization in busy clinical settings.

5. **Weakly-supervised learning**: Using ACA-aligned embeddings to label unlabeled CT scans at the anatomy level, enabling downstream tasks like lesion detection with minimal annotation effort.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **Anatomy-Aware Radiology AI Platform**: A SaaS tool that integrates ACA into existing PACS (Picture Archiving and Communication Systems) to provide real-time, anatomy-contextualized AI findings during radiologist review, with minimal retraining per hospital.

2. **Rapid Model Customization Service**: Offer clinical AI vendors a service to adapt their CT foundation models to new anatomies, modalities, or pathologies in hours rather than weeks, using ACA's lightweight approach.

3. **Anatomy Graph Database for Medical AI**: Build a searchable knowledge graph of anatomy relationships learned by ACA's inter-anatomy transformer, enabling researchers and clinicians to query anatomical context for training new models.

4. **Federated Learning for Radiology**: Leverage ACA's efficiency to enable hospitals to adapt shared foundation models on-device (respecting privacy) and collaborate through federated averaging without sharing raw data.

5. **Anatomy-Level Explainability Dashboard**: Create a tool for radiologists to visualize which anatomies the model attended to for a given diagnosis, using ACA's learned attention weights, improving trust and interpretability in clinical workflows.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Dependency on TotalSegmentator**: ACA requires accurate anatomy segmentation upfront; failures in segmentation propagate directly to downstream alignment and performance degrades on poorly-segmented or rare anatomies.

2. **Limited to CT modality**: The paper focuses solely on CT; generalization to other modalities (MRI, X-ray, ultrasound) is unclear and may require retraining the segmentation module.

3. **Radiology report quality**: The method relies on text extracted from radiology reports; noisy, ambiguous, or inconsistently written reports may degrade vision-language alignment.

4. **Scalability of cross-anatomy transformers**: As the number of segmented anatomies grows (hundreds in full-body protocols), transformer complexity scales quadratically; feasibility for extremely granular segmentations is unclear.

5. **Evaluation on limited benchmarks**: Merlin and CT-RATE are relatively small benchmarks; robustness on diverse, larger-scale clinical datasets with varying scanning protocols and pathologies remains unexplored.

6. **No comparison to simpler baselines**: The paper doesn't compare against simpler lightweight adaptation methods (e.g., linear probes, simple concatenation of anatomy embeddings) that might be equally effective.

## Open Questions
1. How does ACA perform on out-of-distribution CT scans (e.g., different scanner types, non-standard protocols, pediatric vs. adult)?

2. Can the cross-anatomy attention weights be validated against radiological domain knowledge? Do they truly reflect clinically meaningful anatomical relationships, or are they artifacts of the training data?

3. Does ACA generalize to other medical imaging modalities (MRI, PET, X-ray)? What architectural changes would be needed?

4. How sensitive is ACA to the quality and quantity of radiology report text? What is the minimum report detail needed for effective alignment?

5. Can ACA be extended to predict anatomy-level clinical outcomes (e.g., which organs are at risk for a specific disease) rather than just global findings?

6. How does ACA perform on rare anatomies or pathological variants that are under-represented in the training data?

## Key Takeaway
Lightweight anatomy-aware adaptation of frozen CT models preserves global context while achieving fine-grained medical image understanding in minimal training time.
