# Explainable Deep Learning for Price-Trade Dynamics: From Black-Box Forecasts to Effective Parametric Models

**Authors:** Manuel Naviglio, Fabrizio Lillo
**Published:** 2026-09-05 | **arXiv:** [2609.06085v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06085v1)

## Summary
This paper uses neural networks as a tool to discover the hidden structure of how prices and trades interact in financial markets, rather than just making better predictions. By applying explainability techniques (specifically Shapley values) to a trained deep network, the authors reverse-engineer an interpretable parametric model that captures nonlinear price-volume dynamics while remaining transparent enough for traders and risk managers to understand and trust.

## Theoretical Background
The paper sits at the intersection of market microstructure — the study of how prices form through the interaction of orders and trades — and modern machine learning interpretability. Market microstructure traditionally relies on linear models like vector autoregressions (VARs), which are easy to interpret but may miss the nonlinear, state-dependent relationships between returns (price changes) and order flow (signed volumes). Deep neural networks can capture these nonlinearities but are opaque "black boxes." The authors leverage Shapley values — a game-theoretic concept that decomposes a model's prediction into contributions from each input — to peer inside the neural network and understand what it has learned. The core insight is that explainability can bridge the gap between predictive power and economic interpretability.

## Core Contributions
1. **Neural networks as discovery tools**: Rather than viewing deep learning solely as a prediction method, the authors demonstrate that explainability techniques can extract economically meaningful relationships from trained networks.
2. **Shapley-based decomposition of price-volume dynamics**: They show that lagged signed volumes produce sign-preserving, saturating effects (consistent with nonlinear price impact), and that lagged returns act as state variables that modulate subsequent price behavior (continuation under low-momentum conditions, reversal under high-momentum).
3. **SHAP-inspired parametric model**: Building on the neural network insights, they propose a parsimonious nonlinear model that achieves performance comparable to the black-box network while remaining fully interpretable and economically grounded.
4. **Empirical validation across stock types**: Results hold for both large-tick and small-tick stocks, suggesting generality.

## Connections to Other Work
This work bridges [[mechanistic-interpretability]] in deep learning with classical [[market-microstructure]] theory. The parametric model it derives echoes structural approaches in microstructure (e.g., VAR extensions, regime-switching models) but is now informed by what a neural network learned from data. ## Practical Applications
1. **High-frequency trading signal design**: Traders can use the discovered nonlinear relationships between volume and price to design more reliable entry/exit rules.
2. **Market impact models for execution**: The quantified price-impact function (how large orders move prices) can improve algorithmic execution algorithms for institutional traders.
3. **Risk management systems**: Banks can use the state-dependent model to forecast short-horizon price moves and adjust risk limits dynamically.
4. **Regulatory compliance and model validation**: Supervisors and risk officers require interpretable models; this framework provides neural-network accuracy without sacrificing explainability.
5. **Synthetic market generation**: The parametric model can be used to simulate realistic price-volume sequences for stress testing or backtesting strategies.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **AI-powered market microstructure analytics platform**: A SaaS tool for hedge funds and banks that automatically trains neural networks on order-book data, extracts interpretable price-volume relationships via SHAP, and surfaces actionable trading signals with full model transparency.
2. **Explainable execution algorithm optimizer**: Software that learns optimal order execution strategies from historical data, decomposes the learned policy into human-understandable rules, and lets traders tweak parameters without retraining.
3. **Regulatory model validation service**: A consultancy or software service that takes clients' black-box trading or risk models, applies SHAP-based explainability, and certifies compliance with regulatory transparency requirements.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Data and venue specificity**: The model is trained on specific stocks and venues; generalization to different assets, time periods, or market regimes is not guaranteed.
2. **Lag selection and memory horizon**: The paper does not deeply explore how to choose the number of lags systematically; the choice may be ad hoc or dataset-dependent.
3. **Causal inference unclear**: The explainability analysis reveals correlations and nonlinear associations, not causal relationships; lagged volume may be a proxy for unobserved order-book imbalances.
4. **Market regime and structural breaks**: The linear VAR and neural network assume a stable joint distribution of returns and volumes; during market stress, crashes, or regulatory changes, the learned relationships may break down.
5. **High-frequency measurement noise**: Returns and volumes at very short (millisecond) horizons are subject to tick-size constraints and rounding; the linear VAR is more robust to this than the neural network.
6. **Scalability to full order-book data**: The paper uses only returns and signed volume; rich order-book features (depth, imbalance, etc.) are not incorporated, limiting the scope of discovered dynamics.

## Open Questions
1. **Can explainability identify causal relationships?** If lagged volume influences price through a mechanism other than direct price impact (e.g., information revelation), how would SHAP decomposition distinguish this?
2. **How does the approach perform out-of-sample and across time?** The paper validates on a single dataset; robustness across market regimes, asset classes, and calendar periods needs investigation.
3. **What is the optimal architecture for structural discovery?** Would deeper networks, recurrent structures (LSTMs, Transformers), or other architectures reveal richer dynamics, or do they overfit?
4. **How do order-book features change the discovered relationships?** Incorporating full limit-order-book data might reveal additional nonlinearities; does the interpretable model extend?
5. **Can this framework identify truly novel trading rules?** The paper confirms theory; could it discover empirically robust but theoretically unexpected relationships?
6. **Computational scalability for real-time deployment**: The parametric model is fast, but can the SHAP-based discovery process scale to continuous retraining as markets evolve?

## Key Takeaway
Neural networks trained on price-volume data reveal nonlinear market dynamics that can be distilled into transparent, economically grounded parametric models via explainability.
