# Traversing the solution space of neural networks with Hessian Null Space Continuation

**Authors:** Ann Huang, Mitchell Ostrow, Zhouyang Lu, William T. Redman, Leo Kozachkov et al.
**Published:** 2026-09-29 | **arXiv:** [2609.38081v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38081v1)

## Summary
This paper introduces Hessian Null Space Continuation (HNC), a method for exploring the weight space of trained neural networks while preserving their output behavior. The key finding is that near any trained solution, there exist many radically different internal representations and computational mechanisms — far more diversity than previously known. This matters because it reveals that neural network solutions are not isolated points but regions full of hidden variation, with implications for understanding how networks compute, merging models, and editing their learned behaviors.

## Theoretical Background
The work sits at the intersection of two established areas. First, **mode connectivity** — the observation that different trained neural networks often lie in low-loss regions connected through weight space, typically studied via path-finding methods like linear interpolation or bezier curves. Second, **representational degeneracy** — the finding that networks can achieve identical task performance with structurally distinct internal computations (different neuron activations, dynamics, feature representations). 

The new ingredient here is **local geometry via the Hessian** — the matrix of second-order derivatives of the loss. The Hessian's null space (directions where the loss doesn't change) defines a local region in weight space where network output is invariant. By navigating this space systematically, the authors can find solutions with different internal structure while maintaining behavior.

## Core Contributions
1. **HNC algorithm**: A scalable, practical method to traverse the Hessian null space of a trained network, revealing internal mechanisms that preserve function.

2. **Empirical unification**: First direct demonstration that mode connectivity and representational degeneracy coexist — many solutions within a mode-connected region have *different* internal computations.

3. **Quantitative geometry**: Methods to measure the local dimensionality and functional sensitivity of solution sets, showing how model size and task complexity shape these properties.

4. **Concrete discoveries across domains**: 
   - RNNs on memory tasks: drastically different learned dynamics within the same mode.
   - Vision Transformers on ImageNet: representations diverging more from the original than independently trained models with different architectures.
   - RL agents: uncovering alternative navigation strategies and exposing reward hacking.

## Connections to Other Work
This naturally extends [[mode connectivity]] research (e.g., work by Garipov et al., Draxler et al.) by moving beyond path-finding to systematic exploration. It complements [[mechanistic interpretability]] efforts by revealing that a single trained model implicitly contains multiple computational strategies. 

The work relates to [[neural network loss landscapes]], particularly recent findings on overparameterization and redundancy. It also connects to model editing and merging literature — HNC provides a principled way to navigate between solutions with specific desired properties, relevant to [[RLHF]] and fine-tuning. The RL safety experiments link to [[alignment]] concerns, particularly reward specification and unintended behaviors.

## Practical Applications
1. **Model debugging and safety**: Use HNC to uncover hidden reward-hacking strategies or unintended behaviors in RL agents or classifiers before deployment.

2. **Efficient model editing**: Rather than retraining, traverse the Hessian null space toward a solution with corrected behavior on specific inputs.

3. **Architecture-agnostic model merging**: Merge models trained with different architectures or data by finding a common low-loss region.

4. **Interpretability audits**: For deployed models, characterize the full set of feasible internal computations to assess robustness and identify failure modes.

5. **Hyperparameter-free fine-tuning**: Steer a pretrained model toward task-specific behavior while preserving learned representations.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **Neural Network Safety Auditor**: SaaS tool that applies HNC to RL agents and classifiers to uncover hidden failure modes, reward hacking, and unintended behaviors before deployment — aimed at robotics and autonomous systems companies.

2. **Model Compression & Merging Platform**: Cloud service that uses HNC to merge multiple fine-tuned models (e.g., domain-specific LLM variants) into one, eliminating redundancy while preserving performance.

3. **Interpretability-as-a-Service**: Provide mechanistic breakdowns of model decision-making by exploring the solution space — useful for regulated industries (finance, healthcare) requiring explainability.

4. **Adversarial Robustness Testing**: Automate discovery of adversarial examples and failure modes by traversing the solution space toward low-loss regions with high sensitivity to perturbations.

5. **One-Shot Model Editing for LLMs**: API that corrects specific model behaviors (e.g., biases, factual errors, safety violations) by navigating the Hessian null space without retraining.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Computational cost**: Computing the Hessian (or an approximation) scales poorly with parameter count. For billion-parameter models, this becomes a bottleneck; the paper doesn't fully address scalability to modern LLMs.

2. **Local vs. global**: HNC explores a *local* region near a trained solution. It's unclear how large these regions are, whether they connect globally, or how findings generalize to other modes.

3. **Discrete vs. continuous**: Solutions found via HNC may not correspond to what standard optimizers discover. It's unclear whether the diversity observed is practically relevant to training dynamics or only a geometric curiosity.

4. **Task specificity**: Results are shown on memory tasks, vision, and relatively simple RL environments. Generalization to complex, high-dimensional tasks (large-scale language modeling) is unexplored.

5. **Steering constraints**: While HNC can be "steered" toward properties, the paper doesn't provide principled ways to specify which properties to target beyond post-hoc measurement.

## Open Questions
1. **Scale**: Does HNC remain tractable and meaningful for 10B+ parameter models? What approximations to the Hessian are sufficient?

2. **Functional relevance**: How much of the diversity found by HNC actually matters for downstream tasks? Are some null-space trajectories more "natural" or easier to find via standard training?

3. **Global structure**: Can you connect different modes via null-space traversal, or do you eventually hit a steep loss gradient? What does the global solution landscape look like?

4. **Optimization implications**: Does understanding null-space geometry improve our ability to design optimizers or training procedures?

5. **Adversarial / robustness link**: Does traversing toward high-sensitivity regions in the null space systematically find adversarial examples or out-of-distribution failure modes?

6. **Fine-tuning theory**: When is null-space steering preferable to standard retraining? Can you theoretically characterize when the null space is "large enough" to contain solutions with desired properties?

## Key Takeaway
Neural networks hide many different internal computations within nearby weight regions; this diversity can be explored and steered without changing output behavior.
