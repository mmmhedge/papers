# Ranking-Aware Prompt Optimization for Multimodal Clinical Diagnosis

**Authors:** Tian Xia, Minghao Liu, Yiqing Liang, Laixi Shi, Jiayun Wang
**Published:** 2026-09-30 | **arXiv:** [2609.40361v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40361v1)

## Summary
This paper addresses a critical flaw in how multimodal language models (MLLMs) are optimized for clinical diagnosis: standard accuracy metrics are useless on heavily imbalanced medical datasets where a trivial predictor can score 90%. The authors introduce Ranking-PE, a prompt optimization method that instead optimizes for AUROC (a ranking-based metric that correctly handles class imbalance), and show it dramatically outperforms accuracy-based approaches on real clinical tasks—gaining +16 points on some models.

## Theoretical Background
Multimodal large language models combine text and vision understanding to process both images and text together. Clinical datasets suffer from severe class imbalance—most patients are healthy, so a model that predicts "negative" for everyone achieves high accuracy while being medically worthless. AUROC (Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic curve) solves this by measuring whether the model ranks positive instances higher than negative ones, regardless of class distribution. The paper builds on prior "reflective" prompt optimization methods like GEPA, which iteratively improve prompts by evaluating candidate prompts on a matrix of per-instance correctness scores. The core insight is to swap what lives in this matrix: instead of binary correctness labels, store pairwise orderings (did this prompt rank the positive case higher than the negative case?), so the column average directly computes AUROC via the Wilcoxon-Mann-Whitney identity.

## Core Contributions
1. **Ranking-PE algorithm**: A three-layer replacement of accuracy-based scoring with pairwise-ranking evaluation in prompt evolution, operating at the Pareto dominance check, reflection feedback, and candidate selection stages—without requiring extra model calls or surrogate losses.
2. **Empirical validation on multimodal clinical data**: Demonstrates that accuracy-based prompt evolution can degrade AUROC on real MIMIC datasets (three diseases), while Ranking-PE recovers and substantially improves it (+5.8–16.2 AUROC points across models).
3. **Analysis of visual backbone requirements**: Shows that prompt search alone cannot compensate for weak visual encoders; medical-grade vision backbones (via vision-encoder-tuned supervised fine-tuning or medical pretraining) are prerequisites.
4. **Extension to multimodal settings**: Generalizes reflective prompt optimization from text-only to clinically realistic image+text diagnosis tasks.

## Connections to Other Work
This work directly extends [[GEPA]] (reflective prompt evolution) and relates to the broader literature on prompt optimization for LLMs. It connects to research on handling class imbalance in machine learning, where AUROC and ranking metrics are standard (e.g., cost-sensitive learning, AUC optimization). The paper also touches on evaluation metric alignment—a core concern in [[alignment]] work: using the wrong objective (accuracy) leads to optimized-but-useless models, a theme parallel to reward model misspecification in [[RLHF]]. The multimodal angle involves vision-language models, similar to recent work on vision-language adaptation (e.g., LLaVA, CLIP fine-tuning).

## Practical Applications
1. **Clinical decision support systems**: Hospitals deploying MLLMs for diagnosis (radiology, pathology) need ranking-aware optimization to ensure models that actually prioritize high-risk patients.
2. **Rare disease detection**: Medical applications where the disease is rare (e.g., <5% prevalence) require AUROC-based metrics; accuracy-optimized systems fail catastrophically.
3. **Triage systems**: Emergency departments and screening programs where false negatives are costly and class imbalance is extreme.
4. **Medical image analysis platforms**: Tools that rank images by risk (e.g., tumor likelihood in radiology screens) benefit from ranking-aware prompts.
5. **Regulatory compliance**: FDA-approved clinical AI systems increasingly require AUROC/ranking metrics in evaluation; prompt optimization aligned with these metrics avoids post-hoc failures.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **Clinical Prompt Optimization Platform**: A SaaS tool that automatically optimizes MLLM prompts for imbalanced medical tasks, allowing hospitals to deploy accurate multimodal diagnostic assistants without retraining models.
2. **Medical MLLM Fine-Tuning Service**: Specialized consultancy that combines Ranking-PE with vision-encoder tuning for client hospitals, offering production-ready diagnostic models with AUROC guarantees.
3. **Imbalanced Data ML Evaluation Toolkit**: Developer library (open-source or commercial) that audits and re-optimizes any LLM prompt optimization pipeline to use ranking metrics instead of accuracy—applicable beyond clinical AI to fraud detection, anomaly detection, etc.
4. **Clinical Trial Screening Assistant**: Use ranking-optimized MLLMs to triage candidate patients for trials, where false negatives (missed eligible patients) are costly and positives are rare.
5. **Risk Stratification Marketplace**: Pre-optimized prompts and model checkpoints for common clinical tasks (sepsis prediction, readmission risk, etc.) sold via marketplace, each optimized for AUROC on representative datasets.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Pair construction overhead**: The method requires forming (positive, negative) pairs; on highly imbalanced datasets with few positive examples, this can limit pair diversity and introduce sampling bias.
2. **Single-disease focus per prompt**: The paper optimizes prompts per disease; a general clinical MLLM handling multiple conditions simultaneously may need different trade-offs, not addressed here.
3. **Vision encoder bottleneck**: The ablations show prompt optimization cannot overcome weak visual backbones—but the paper does not propose solutions to jointly optimize vision and language components during prompt search.
4. **Limited model evaluation**: Only tested on Qwen3-VL-8B and MedGemma-4B; generalization to other recent MLLMs (GPT-4V, Gemini, Claude) and domain-specific medical models (e.g., BioGPT-Large) is unclear.
5. **MIMIC-only validation**: MIMIC is a single (albeit large) dataset; other clinical databases, imaging modalities (CT, MRI), or non-English settings are unexplored.
6. **No cost-sensitive loss weighting**: The method optimizes ranking but does not account for asymmetric costs (false negative vs. false positive in diagnosis), which clinical practice often requires.

## Open Questions
1. **Joint vision-language optimization**: Can Ranking-PE be extended to jointly optimize visual encoder parameters and prompts, rather than treating the vision backbone as fixed?
2. **Multi-task and multi-disease prompts**: How should prompts be optimized when a single model must handle multiple diseases or conditions with different class imbalances?
3. **Calibration and uncertainty**: Does optimizing for AUROC affect model calibration (confidence estimates)? Do clinicians need well-calibrated probabilities alongside rankings?
4. **Computational scaling**: How does Ranking-PE scale to very large prompt spaces or iterative refinement over hundreds of candidates?
5. **Human-in-the-loop integration**: Can doctors provide feedback on ranking errors (e.g., "this prompt incorrectly ranks low-risk over high-risk") to further guide optimization?
6. **Cross-dataset generalization**: Do prompts optimized on MIMIC transfer to other hospital datasets, or do they require re-optimization per institution?
7. **Adversarial robustness**: Are ranking-optimized prompts more or less robust to adversarial inputs (e.g., image perturbations) than accuracy-optimized ones?

## Key Takeaway
Optimizing clinical AI for accuracy on imbalanced data fails; ranking-aware prompt optimization yields diagnostic models that actually work.
