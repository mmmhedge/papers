# PGFS++: Molecular Property Improvement under Synthesis and Diversity Constraints

**Authors:** Boqiao Zhang, Godbless James, Sai Krishna Gottipati, Andrew Fitzgibbon
**Published:** 2026-08-19 | **arXiv:** [2608.19121v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19121v1)

## Summary
This paper addresses a practical problem in drug discovery: improving molecular properties like drug efficacy while ensuring the designed molecules can actually be synthesized. The authors develop PGFS++, a reinforcement learning framework that optimizes molecules through realistic chemical reactions, using in-stock building blocks and known synthesis routes, while preventing a common failure mode where the optimizer "cheats" by collapsing diverse inputs into the same high-reward molecule.

## Theoretical Background
The work builds on **policy gradient methods** — a reinforcement learning approach where an agent learns by directly optimizing the probability of actions that increase reward. Prior work (PGFS) applied this to molecular optimization by treating synthesis as a forward process: given a reactant and reagent, predict the product. The paper also draws on **molecular generation** via graph neural networks, which represent molecules as graphs where atoms are nodes and bonds are edges. A key insight is that unconstrained molecular optimization often produces synthetically infeasible molecules — this paper grounds the search in real chemistry by requiring valid reaction templates and commercially available building blocks.

## Core Contributions
1. **PGFS+**: Replaces indirect reactant prediction with direct, learnable embedding lookup tables for reaction templates and second reactants. Combines this with improved scoring functions and RL algorithms to boost property improvement.

2. **Reward hacking diagnosis**: Identifies and characterizes a failure mode where powerful reactant search causes diversity collapse — many input molecules get optimized to the same high-reward "magnet" molecule, gaming the reward while losing structural diversity.

3. **PGFS++**: Introduces input-specific molecular improvement that anchors optimization trajectories to the starting molecule, preserving chemical diversity while improving properties. Outputs not just better molecules but explicit synthesis routes.

## Connections to Other Work
This relates to broader challenges in [[generative-models|generative modeling]] for molecules (e.g., diffusion-based or VAE-based approaches) and constrained optimization problems in chemistry. The synthesis-aware framing connects to work on retrosynthesis prediction and forward synthesis models. The reward-hacking issue echoes broader concerns in [[reinforcement-learning|RL alignment]] about misspecified reward functions and unintended optimization behaviors. The paper implicitly addresses a version of the specification gaming problem common in RL.

## Practical Applications
- **Early-stage drug discovery pipelines**: Automatically suggest improved lead compounds that chemists can actually synthesize, reducing costly dead ends in wet-lab validation.
- **Hit-to-lead optimization**: Starting from a screening hit, generate chemically similar analogs with better potency, selectivity, or ADME (absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion) properties.
- **Synthetic accessibility-constrained design**: In materials chemistry or agrochemical development, optimize properties while respecting real-world synthesis constraints.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **SyntheticAI**: A cloud service for chemists that ingests a target compound and desired property improvements, outputs ranked candidates with synthesis routes and confidence scores. Charge per optimization run.

2. **MoleculeOpt+**: A plugin for popular cheminformatics platforms (RDKit, ChemAxon) that runs PGFS++ on molecular libraries; integrates with ERP systems to check building block availability in real time.

3. **Lead Diversity Engine**: A SaaS for pharma teams performing parallel structure-activity relationship (SAR) campaigns — generates multiple distinct chemical series from a single hit, mitigating risk that all candidates fail.

4. **Synthesis Route Explorer**: Given a desired molecule and constraints (cost, time, available equipment), enumerate feasible synthetic pathways and rank by property improvements achievable via each route.

5. **Retro-Forward Optimizer**: Combines retrosynthesis (working backward from target) with forward synthesis optimization to find molecules that are both property-optimal and cheaply synthesizable from commodity materials.

## Technical Limitations
- **Reaction template coverage**: Limited to known reaction types in the training set; novel or underexplored chemistry is invisible to the model.
- **Computational cost**: Sampling trajectories through a forward synthesis tree is slower than unconstrained generation; unclear how the method scales to very large compound libraries.
- **Building block availability**: Assumes a fixed inventory of in-stock reagents; real chemistry has supply-chain dynamics and cost variability not modeled here.
- **Property prediction fidelity**: Relies on learned scoring functions (e.g., QSAR models) to estimate binding affinity or ADME — errors in these predictions propagate through optimization.
- **Diversity preservation tradeoffs**: Anchoring to input molecules may prevent discovery of structurally novel compounds that are genuinely better, even if they're farther from the starting point.

## Open Questions
1. How does PGFS++ handle multi-objective optimization (e.g., simultaneously improve potency, selectivity, and reduce toxicity)? Does the input-anchoring constraint become too restrictive?
2. Can the method generalize to entirely new molecule classes or targets not seen during training?
3. What is the failure mode when in-stock building blocks are genuinely insufficient for a desired improvement? Does the method degrade gracefully or propose infeasible syntheses?
4. How sensitive is the framework to the quality and coverage of reaction templates? What's the minimum template library needed?
5. Does explicit synthesis route prediction (as a model output) actually match experimentalist verification, or do hidden synthetic difficulties emerge downstream?

## Key Takeaway
Synthesizable molecular optimization via reinforcement learning, solving reward collapse by anchoring candidates to input molecules.
