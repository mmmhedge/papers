# AI systems and the reproduction of (standard) language ideologies in World Englishes

**Authors:** Kingsley Ugwuanyi
**Published:** 2026-07-30 | **arXiv:** [2607.28528v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28528v1)

## Summary
This paper examines how large language models (LLMs) and AI systems embed and reinforce dominant language ideologies that privilege English spoken in wealthy, historically powerful countries over varieties spoken in the Global South. The author argues that AI reproduces these biases across training data, design choices, evaluation methods, and public discourse—with real consequences for whose English is treated as legitimate—and calls for more inclusive AI design that respects linguistic diversity.

## Theoretical Background
The paper draws on sociolinguistics and World Englishes scholarship, which studies how English varies globally and how power dynamics shape which varieties are considered "correct." A key concept is **language ideology**—the beliefs and assumptions (often unstated) about what counts as proper language use. The paper builds on the recognition that these ideologies are not neutral: they reflect historical colonial and economic hierarchies. **Inner Circle Englishes** (varieties in the UK, US, Australia) have traditionally been treated as standards, while outer and expanding circle varieties face stigmatization. The paper also engages with the **standardization paradox** described by linguist Christian Mair: the tension between homogenization (flattening diversity toward a single standard) and pluralization (increasing awareness of multiple legitimate forms).

## Core Contributions
- Documents empirically how language ideologies are embedded at multiple levels of AI systems: training data composition, annotation protocols, benchmark design, model evaluation, and feedback mechanisms
- Uses the public controversy over the word "delve" to show concretely how speakers from wealthy countries police the language norms of Global South speakers through AI discourse
- Identifies the standardization paradox in action: AI simultaneously homogenizes English by favoring standard forms while potentially pluralizing it through exposure to diverse corpora created by Global South annotators
- Reframes non-dominant Englishes being conflated with AI-generated speech as a symptom of deeper ideological issues, not a technical problem alone
- Argues for inclusive design as a necessary response, not merely a diversity initiative

## Connections to Other Work
This work intersects with [[alignment]] concerns (ensuring AI systems reflect human values) and broader debates about bias in LLMs. It relates to critical work on [[large-language-models]] bias and fairness, but shifts focus from protected categories (gender, race) to linguistic pluralism and ideology. The paper also engages with World Englishes scholarship that has long questioned who has authority over language standards—a question now being answered by AI training practices rather than traditional linguistic institutions. It connects to discussions of whose labor trains models (annotation work by Global South users) and how that labor is valued and credited.

## Practical Applications
- **Content moderation and filtering**: AI systems currently flag or deprioritize non-standard English; understanding ideological bias could improve fair filtering that doesn't penalize legitimate regional varieties
- **Multilingual and cross-regional NLP systems**: Training and evaluation protocols that explicitly account for World Englishes could improve model performance for diverse English speakers
- **Language technology accessibility**: Tools (translation, grammar checking, voice assistants) that currently work poorly for non-standard English could be redesigned to serve Global South users
- **Data annotation and crowdsourcing**: Companies relying on Global South annotators could build more equitable workflows that recognize the legitimacy of annotators' own English varieties
- **Educational technology**: AI tutoring systems and writing assistants should not treat non-dominant English as "errors" needing correction

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **World-Englishes-aware writing assistant**: A browser extension and standalone editor that gives feedback on clarity and impact without policing non-standard grammar, trained on diverse English corpora
2. **Fair AI audit platform**: A SaaS tool for companies to audit their LLM outputs, training data, and evaluation benchmarks for language ideology bias, with specific recommendations for inclusive redesign
3. **Inclusive annotation platform**: A data-labeling marketplace explicitly valuing annotators from the Global South, celebrating linguistic diversity in training data, and ensuring compensation reflects the strategic value of diverse linguistic input
4. **Sociolinguistic NLP research lab**: A consultancy helping AI teams build evaluation benchmarks and test suites that measure model fairness across World Englishes varieties
5. **Open-source multilingual evaluation dataset**: A community-maintained benchmark for testing LLM performance fairly across Inner Circle, Outer Circle, and Expanding Circle Englishes

## Technical Limitations
- The paper is primarily analytical and critical; it does not propose concrete technical methods for detecting or mitigating language ideology in models
- No large-scale quantitative analysis of how prevalent these biases are across different LLMs or training datasets
- The paper does not address the deep tension: if evaluation benchmarks themselves encode language ideology, how do we measure improvement without a "neutral" standard?
- Unclear how to operationalize "inclusive design" at scale without either abandoning all standards (making evaluation meaningless) or creating fragmented, incompatible systems
- Does not deeply engage with the role of base model pretraining in locking in ideologies before fine-tuning or alignment interventions

## Open Questions
- How much of the bias originates in training data distribution (e.g., proportionally more Inner Circle text on the internet) versus annotation and design choices?
- Can we quantify the "harm" of treating non-dominant Englishes as AI-like or illegitimate? What are the downstream effects on speakers?
- What would a truly pluralistic evaluation benchmark look like, and could models be trained to perform well across all varieties simultaneously without loss of coherence?
- How do LLMs trained on diverse corpora actually perform on World Englishes tasks, compared to models trained primarily on Inner Circle data?
- Can annotation protocols or training procedures explicitly encourage models to preserve and celebrate linguistic diversity rather than homogenize it?
- What role do users' own language ideologies play in reinforcing these biases through feedback and prompt engineering?

## Key Takeaway
AI systems inherit and amplify historical power hierarchies that privilege wealthy countries' English, requiring inclusive design that respects linguistic diversity.
