# When Forgetting is not Catastrophic: On the Mechanics of Spurious Forgetting

**Authors:** Vedant Palit, Florent Draye, Nicolas Zucchet, Zhijing Jin, Bernhard Schölkopf
**Published:** 2026-10-06 | **arXiv:** [2610.08718v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08718v1)

## Summary

This paper reveals that language models don't truly "forget" old knowledge during finetuning — instead, they temporarily hide it through shared representational shifts that can be reversed. The authors show that what looks like catastrophic forgetting is actually two distinct processes: a reversible loss of access to old facts and a slow, permanent erosion; understanding which dominates determines whether forgetting is recoverable or not.

## Theoretical Background

The paper builds on research in [[continual learning]] and [[catastrophic forgetting]], which observe that neural networks trained on new tasks often lose performance on previously learned tasks. Prior work treated this as a fundamental limitation of neural network plasticity. The authors draw on associative memory theory — the idea that neural networks store facts as learned associations between input keys and output values — to develop a mechanistic explanation. They connect their findings to [[mechanistic interpretability]] work that traces how information flows through network representations, specifically examining how finetuning perturbs the geometric structure of learned representations in embedding spaces. The paper also relates to recent work on [[superposition]] and [[sparse representations]] in transformers, which describes how networks pack multiple facts into shared representational dimensions.

## Core Contributions

1. **Identification of two forgetting mechanisms**: The paper distinguishes reversible "spurious forgetting" (shared directional shift in representation space) from irreversible "true forgetting" (fact-specific parameter erosion). This is novel — prior work treated forgetting as monolithic.

2. **Minimal associative memory model**: Demonstrates that three simple ingredients (shared key structure, concentrated new values, normalization) reproduce the full empirical forgetting dynamics observed in real transformers, providing a interpretable causal model.

3. **Geometric explanation of finetuning**: Shows that finetuning moves all old representations along a common direction, effectively hiding them without destroying their relative geometry. Normalization then "withdraws" this shift once new facts stabilize.

4. **Practical recovery procedures**: Demonstrates that subtracting the common shift from weights recovers old facts in real pretrained language models, and that removing a single direction per weight update during finetuning prevents collapse entirely.

5. **Predictive principle**: Shows that whether forgetting is catastrophic depends on whether new data move old memories together (reversible) or apart (irreversible).

## Connections to Other Work

This work directly informs the [[continual learning]] problem and challenges assumptions in [[RLHF]] and instruction tuning pipelines. It relates to [[mechanistic interpretability]] efforts like [[lottery ticket hypothesis]] (sparse, preserved subnetworks) and [[superposition]] research (multiple facts in shared dimensions). The paper also connects to work on [[model editing]] and [[knowledge injection]], suggesting these techniques might exploit the reversibility of spurious forgetting. The geometric perspective aligns with recent work on [[representation collapse]] and [[neural interference]] in multitask learning. Understanding these dynamics is relevant to [[alignment]] research concerned with finetuning misalignment into models.

## Practical Applications

1. **Safe continual finetuning**: Organizations can finetune models on new domain-specific facts without permanently losing general knowledge by monitoring and reversing spurious directional shifts.

2. **Knowledge recovery pipelines**: Post-hoc methods to restore forgotten facts in deployed models without full retraining, reducing operational costs of knowledge updates.

3. **Multi-task model training**: Better strategies for training models that must handle multiple domains by understanding which training orderings cause reversible vs. irreversible forgetting.

4. **Model auditing**: Detecting whether performance drops on old tasks reflect true knowledge loss or mere access disruption, informing whether retraining is necessary.

5. **Selective unlearning**: Precisely eroding specific unwanted facts while preserving others, by exploiting fact-specific erosion while protecting shared structure.

## Product & Startup Ideas

1. **Continual Learning as a Service**: A platform that finetunes customer LLMs on proprietary data while automatically preserving general knowledge via directional shift reversal, marketed to enterprises worried about model degradation.

2. **Knowledge Recovery Tool**: A software library that audits finetuned models, identifies what spurious forgetting occurred, and recovers old facts without retraining — sold to companies managing model versions.

3. **Adaptive Model Update Engine**: A deployment tool that batches new facts, monitors forgetting mechanisms in real-time, and rolls back updates if they cause irreversible erosion of critical capabilities.

4. **Selective Unlearning Service**: An API that removes specific facts (e.g., deprecated training data) from models while preserving 99%+ of other knowledge, addressing regulatory and privacy needs.

5. **Model Health Dashboard**: Observability software for LLM teams that visualizes spurious vs. true forgetting in real-time during finetuning, flagging risky training runs before deployment.

## Technical Limitations

1. **Minimal model assumptions**: The associative memory model is deliberately simple and may not capture all dynamics in deep, multi-layer transformers with complex attention patterns and skip connections.

2. **Synthetic data validation**: Core experiments use synthetic tasks (small token vocabularies, controlled structure). Real language exhibits richer interference patterns and polysemy that may violate the "shared key structure" assumption.

3. **Scalability unclear**: It's unknown whether the geometric interpretation holds as model size increases (billions of parameters) or whether the relevant directions become harder to identify or manipulate.

4. **Shallow intervention depth**: Subtracting the common shift is demonstrated only at the output layer; the paper doesn't show whether intervening deeper in the network preserves facts better or whether multi-layer shifts interact.

5. **New data distribution assumption**: The theory assumes new finetuning data are "concentrated" in value space. Heterogeneous or adversarial new data might violate this and produce different forgetting profiles.

6. **Temporal dynamics**: The paper doesn't fully characterize how long spurious forgetting persists or whether the transition from reversible to irreversible forgetting is predictable from early training signals.

## Open Questions

1. **Does the shared-direction mechanism scale to realistic models?** Can practitioners identify and manipulate the relevant directions in billion-parameter models with realistic finetuning, or is the approach limited to smaller models?

2. **What predicts forgetting type?** Can we predict from data properties (overlap with pretraining, semantic similarity, etc.) whether new finetuning will produce reversible or irreversible forgetting?

3. **Can we prevent erosion entirely?** Is there a regularization or training procedure that preserves spurious-forgetting reversibility while still learning new facts efficiently, without knowledge erosion?

4. **How do catastrophic forgetting mechanics interact with [[RLHF]] and reward modeling?** Does spurious forgetting explain why models sometimes lose capabilities after alignment training?

5. **Is there a connection to [[in-context learning]]?** Can in-context information access old facts via similar geometric mechanisms, and would this explain why ICL sometimes recovers "forgotten" knowledge?

6. **How does multi-modal finetuning behave?** Do vision-language models exhibit the same reversible/irreversible split, or do cross-modal interactions change the forgetting profile?

## Key Takeaway

Finetuned language models hide rather than destroy old knowledge through reversible geometric shifts; only slow, fact-specific erosion is truly catastrophic.
