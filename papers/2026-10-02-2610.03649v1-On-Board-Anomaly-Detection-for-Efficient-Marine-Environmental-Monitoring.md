# On-Board Anomaly Detection for Efficient Marine Environmental Monitoring

**Authors:** Thomas Goudemant, Clotilde Szywala, Benjamin Francesconi, Michelle Aubrun, Yves Bobichon et al.
**Published:** 2026-10-02 | **arXiv:** [2610.03649v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03649v1)

## Summary
This paper develops an onboard anomaly detection system for Earth observation satellites to identify marine environmental hazards like oil spills and algal blooms. The key innovation is a self-supervised neural network that compresses satellite imagery into a compact latent space, enabling efficient anomaly detection directly on resource-constrained satellite hardware—eliminating the need to transmit all raw data to ground stations.

## Theoretical Background
The work combines two established ML paradigms: self-supervised learning (training neural networks without hand-labeled data by learning from the structure of unlabeled data itself) and anomaly detection (identifying data points that deviate from normal patterns). Traditional anomaly detection algorithms like Isolation Forest, One-Class SVM, and Local Outlier Factor operate by learning what "normal" looks like, then flagging outliers. The paper applies these classical methods in a modern context—pairing them with a learned representation (the latent space from the self-supervised encoder) to work effectively on satellite imagery with limited onboard compute.

## Core Contributions
1. **Lightweight self-supervised encoder**: A neural network that learns to compress multi- and hyperspectral satellite images into a reduced-dimensional latent space without labeled training data, suitable for onboard execution.
2. **Integrated anomaly detection pipeline**: Systematic comparison of classical anomaly detection algorithms (Isolation Forest, One-Class SVM, Local Outlier Factor) operating in the learned latent space versus raw image space.
3. **Hardware optimization**: The entire pipeline is designed to run on heterogeneous satellite platforms (embedded CPUs and specialized AI accelerators) with hard memory and power constraints.
4. **Real-world validation**: Demonstrated deployment on actual ESA and commercial satellite missions (Phisat-2, IMAGIN-e), proving practical viability.

## Connections to Other Work
This work sits at the intersection of [[computer-vision]] for remote sensing and efficient ML for edge deployment. It relates to broader trends in:
- **On-device inference**: Similar to federated learning and edge AI, the goal is to reduce communication overhead by processing locally.
- **Self-supervised representation learning**: Parallels approaches like contrastive learning that don't require labeled data.
- **Anomaly detection in time-series/imagery**: Common in industrial monitoring and intrusion detection; the novelty here is the satellite-specific constraints and integration with actual operational systems.

## Practical Applications
1. **Oil spill early warning**: Real-time detection of spills enables rapid response vessel dispatch, reducing environmental damage.
2. **Algal bloom monitoring**: Coastal ecosystems can issue public health alerts (toxicity warnings) with shorter latency.
3. **Illegal fishing detection**: Anomalous vessel behavior or unauthorized activity in protected marine zones.
4. **Sediment flood tracking**: Monitoring riverine outflows and coastal erosion patterns for infrastructure planning.
5. **Port congestion and traffic monitoring**: Identifying unusual vessel clustering for maritime traffic optimization.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **SatWatch Marine**: A subscription service offering real-time marine anomaly alerts to fishing companies, environmental agencies, and shipping insurers—monetized per alert or by jurisdiction.
2. **Coastal Insurance Risk Platform**: Use satellite anomaly detections to dynamically update marine insurance premiums and environmental liability assessments for oil/gas infrastructure.
3. **NGO Analytics Dashboard**: White-label the detection pipeline for environmental nonprofits (e.g., Ocean Conservancy, Greenpeace) to monitor protected areas and publish transparent monitoring reports.
4. **Autonomous Satellite Swarm Manager**: Coordinate multiple low-cost satellites to collectively detect and track marine events, with onboard processing reducing backend infrastructure needs.
5. **Government Environmental Compliance**: Sell to maritime authorities (Coast Guard, NOAA) as a continuous monitoring tool for permit verification and illegal activity detection.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Weather and cloud cover**: Satellite sensors cannot see through thick clouds, limiting applicability in cloudy seasons/regions.
2. **Temporal resolution**: Most satellites revisit a location every few days; fast-moving hazards (small spills, brief blooms) may be missed.
3. **Spectral limitations**: Hyperspectral data is rare and expensive; many operational satellites have limited bands, reducing detection sensitivity.
4. **False positive tuning**: Anomaly detection models are sensitive to the threshold for "abnormal"; setting it too low floods with false alerts, too high misses real events.
5. **Training data bias**: The self-supervised encoder is trained on historical imagery—unusual but benign phenomena (e.g., seasonal plankton, sun glint) may be flagged as anomalies.
6. **Generalization across sensors**: A model trained on one satellite's sensors may not transfer to another's without retraining.

## Open Questions
1. How does performance scale with different geographic regions (tropical vs. polar, open ocean vs. coastal shelf) where normal patterns vary?
2. Can the pipeline detect cascading or secondary events (e.g., an anomaly that triggers another)?
3. What is the latency from detection onboard to ground station alert? Is this latency competitive with existing near-real-time satellite data systems?
4. How does the self-supervised encoder perform on emergent anomalies not present in training data?
5. Can the system be adapted for multimodal inputs (e.g., combining visible, infrared, and radar imagery from multiple satellites)?

## Key Takeaway
Lightweight neural networks enable satellites to detect ocean hazards onboard, reducing communication and speeding up environmental alerts from space.
