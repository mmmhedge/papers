# LeWAM: A JEPA World Action Model with Diffusion-Steering-Based MPC

**Authors:** Shashank Hegde, Alexander Popov, Elie Aljalbout, Nikolai Smolyanskiy
**Published:** 2026-10-08 | **arXiv:** [2610.12407v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12407v1)

## Summary
This paper introduces LeWAM, a bidirectional transformer model that predicts both actions and future observations for robotic control. Unlike traditional world models that reconstruct images (adding noise and redundancy), LeWAM works in a cleaner latent space and handles four prediction tasks—forward dynamics, backward dynamics, inverse dynamics, and policy prediction—end-to-end. The key innovation is using this unified model for both understanding robot state and planning with model predictive control, while a clever trick (planning in the policy's noise space rather than raw actions) improves real-world performance.

## Theoretical Background
This work builds on several foundational ideas. **JEPA** (Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture) is a framework that learns latent representations by predicting future latents rather than reconstructing images—avoiding the computational waste of predicting high-dimensional pixel values. **World models** in robotics predict how the environment evolves given an action, enabling offline planning and control. **Model predictive control (MPC)** repeatedly predicts future states using a dynamics model and optimizes action sequences. **Transformers**—neural networks using attention mechanisms to process sequences—enable flexible multi-task learning when trained on diverse prediction objectives. The paper contrasts **decoder-free** approaches (which avoid reconstructing observations) against **reconstruction-based** methods that explicitly predict raw sensory input.

## Core Contributions
1. **Bidirectional JEPA architecture**: A single transformer trained jointly on forward dynamics (state→next state), backward dynamics (next state→state), inverse dynamics (state + next state→action), and policy prediction—all in latent space without image reconstruction.

2. **Improved state alignment**: Linear probes trained on LeWAM's latents recover robot and object state more accurately than prior JEPA approaches, while still filtering visual distractors effectively.

3. **Policy-noise MPC planning**: Demonstrates that planning by sampling in the latent noise space of the policy head (rather than sampling raw actions) improves closed-loop performance in downstream robotic tasks, even when the dynamics model is imperfect.

4. **Unified model for perception and control**: Shows that a single LeWAM checkpoint can serve as both a world model for planning and an implicit policy network, matching the performance of flow-matching policies at equivalent model sizes.

## Connections to Other Work
LeWAM extends [[JEPA]]-style representation learning by adding inverse and backward dynamics prediction alongside forward prediction. It relates to prior **world models** work (e.g., PlaNet, Dreamer) but avoids expensive pixel reconstruction. The bidirectional transformer design parallels masked prediction in [[BERT]], but applied to spatiotemporal robotics. The insight about planning in noise space connects to recent work on diffusion-based planning and [[RLHF]]-style policy distillation, where acting in a learned policy's output distribution (rather than raw action space) can be more tractable. It also touches on **mechanistic interpretability** via the finding that linear probes recover state—suggesting LeWAM's latents encode disentangled, interpretable factors.

## Practical Applications
1. **Robotic manipulation**: Real-world arm control (grasping, assembly) using a learned world model for closed-loop MPC without explicit state sensors.

2. **Offline reinforcement learning**: Pre-training LeWAM on a large corpus of unlabeled robot trajectories, then fine-tuning the policy head for downstream tasks without online exploration.

3. **Sim-to-real transfer**: Training LeWAM in simulation and deploying it on real robots; the latent representation and backward dynamics may be more robust to visual domain shifts than pixel-level models.

4. **Planning under uncertainty**: Video prediction and trajectory forecasting for autonomous systems (vehicles, drones), where the model naturally captures stochastic futures.

5. **Interactive AI assistants**: Learning dynamics of user interactions or multi-agent environments for proactive suggestion or adaptive interfaces.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **Robot Learning-as-a-Service**: Cloud platform where customers upload robot videos; LeWAM automatically trains a control model for their specific tasks (grasping, assembly, navigation), avoiding manual reward engineering.

2. **Plug-and-play sim-to-real solution**: SaaS tool that ingests physics simulations (Gazebo, MuJoCo) and real robot data, fine-tunes a pre-trained LeWAM, and exports a control policy—target: manufacturing and logistics companies automating pick-and-place.

3. **Autonomous vehicle trajectory planner**: Offline MPC using learned world models for safer path planning; sell as middleware to autonomous driving or delivery robot companies.

4. **Video forecasting API**: Competitive intelligence or supply-chain visibility—predict future states of factory floors, warehouses, or ports from video to enable proactive logistics.

5. **Interactive game/VR engine**: Real-time environment simulation using a lightweight LeWAM latent model for physics-based gameplay or interactive storytelling with minimal compute.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Scalability to high-dimensional action spaces**: Paper focuses on robotic tasks; unclear how well bidirectional prediction scales to continuous control with many DoF (degrees of freedom) or discrete action spaces with thousands of options.

2. **Latent collapse and representation quality**: JEPA latents can suffer from representation collapse (all examples mapping to similar latents). Paper shows linear probes work well, but semantic disentanglement is not guaranteed and may degrade with visual diversity.

3. **Dynamics model errors accumulate**: MPC relies on rollouts of the learned dynamics model; multi-step errors compound, especially in long-horizon tasks. The noise-space planning trick helps but doesn't solve fundamental uncertainty.

4. **Limited evaluation scope**: Experiments appear focused on relatively constrained robotic domains. Generalization to diverse visual environments, varying lighting, or new object types is not thoroughly tested.

5. **Computational cost of MPC**: Real-time planning with a transformer MPC still requires iterative optimization; latency may be prohibitive for fast-moving robots or safety-critical closed-loop control.

6. **Assumption of Markovian dynamics**: Model assumes future state depends only on current latent + action; doesn't explicitly model partial observability or long-term correlations that might matter in complex scenes.

## Open Questions
1. **How does LeWAM scale to visual complexity?** What happens when training on diverse, naturalistic video rather than controlled robot settings? Does the latent remain interpretable?

2. **Can backward dynamics improve exploration?** In RL, could predicting state→previous-state help uncertainty estimation or curiosity-driven exploration?

3. **What is the minimal data regime?** How much robot video is needed to match the performance of a small policy network? Can LeWAM pre-train effectively on unlabeled internet video?

4. **Does the noise-space planning insight generalize?** Would other diffusion or normalizing-flow-based models also benefit from planning in latent noise rather than action space? Is this task- or architecture-agnostic?

5. **Disentanglement via auxiliary losses?** Could adding auxiliary losses (e.g., contrastive learning on state factors) improve the interpretability that linear probes currently achieve?

6. **Multi-agent scenarios:** How does LeWAM handle competitive or cooperative multi-agent dynamics? Can it learn partial observability of other agents?

## Key Takeaway
LeWAM combines world modeling and policy learning in a single latent transformer, improving robotic control through cleaner representations and smarter MPC planning in noise space.
