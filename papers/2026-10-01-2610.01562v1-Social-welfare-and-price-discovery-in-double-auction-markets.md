# Social welfare and price discovery in double auction markets

**Authors:** Teemu Pennanen
**Published:** 2026-10-01 | **arXiv:** [2610.01562v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01562v1)

## Summary
This paper explains why double auctions (a common trading mechanism where buyers and sellers simultaneously submit bids and asks) tend to drive prices toward competitive equilibrium — a phenomenon observed in lab experiments but never rigorously proven. The author shows mathematically that when agents bid based on their true preferences, repeated double auctions converge to Walras equilibria (prices where supply equals demand), proving the mechanism works as economic theory predicts.

## Theoretical Background
Double auctions are trading mechanisms where multiple buyers and sellers submit simultaneous bids; trades occur at prices determined by matching supply and demand. This paper builds on two classical traditions: (1) laboratory experiments (by economists like Smith) showing double auctions converge to competitive equilibrium empirically, and (2) Walras equilibrium theory — a foundational concept in microeconomics stating that markets "clear" (supply equals demand) at specific prices. The paper frames the problem in a pure exchange economy (agents trade existing goods rather than produce new ones) and uses fixed-point theory — a mathematical tool for proving that dynamical systems settle into stable states.

## Core Contributions
1. **Theoretical bridge**: Proves that Walras equilibria are precisely the fixed points (stable outcomes) of the double auction mechanism — connecting experimental observation to rigorous mathematics.
2. **Convergence result**: Demonstrates that *sequences* of repeated double auctions generate bounded price and allocation paths whose limit points are Walras equilibria.
3. **Endogenous bidding model**: Assumes agents bid their indifference prices (the prices at which they are indifferent between trading or not), derived directly from current holdings and preferences — a behaviorally grounded assumption rather than arbitrary.

## Connections to Other Work
This work addresses a gap between experimental economics and general equilibrium theory. It relates to classical [[Walras equilibrium]] literature and builds implicitly on [[market microstructure]] research that examines how trading mechanisms affect price discovery. The paper contrasts with algorithmic trading models that assume agents use complex strategies; here, rationality is simpler and more transparent. It also echoes debates in mechanism design about which auction formats maximize social welfare.

## Practical Applications
- **Exchange design**: Regulatory bodies (SEC, exchanges) designing or validating trading mechanisms can use this to justify or improve double auction structures.
- **Cryptocurrency protocols**: Decentralized exchanges using order-matching algorithms benefit from knowing convergence guarantees improve user welfare.
- **Commodity markets**: Agricultural and energy markets relying on periodic batch auctions (a variant of double auctions) gain theoretical assurance they reach fair prices.
- **Market simulation**: Risk managers and regulators can use convergence results to stress-test whether market mechanisms remain stable under stress.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **Market mechanism validation software**: A tool that simulates proposed trading mechanisms on historical data, certifying they satisfy convergence properties and won't lock in prices away from equilibrium.
2. **Blockchain-based fair exchange protocol**: A decentralized trading platform that provably converges to fair prices by implementing double auction logic on-chain, marketed to institutions wary of centralized exchanges.
3. **Corporate swap matching engine**: A SaaS service for large financial institutions to execute inter-company barter or swap exchanges using double auctions; guaranteed price discovery reduces negotiation friction.
4. **Robo-advisor auction optimizer**: An advisory tool for brokerages that recommends when to use double auction mechanisms for institutional trades, predicting convergence time and welfare gains.
5. **Market transparency dashboard**: Real-time visualization of price discovery in double auctions (e.g., for OTC bonds or derivatives), showing convergence to equilibrium to reduce information asymmetry.

## Technical Limitations
- **Assumes rational bidding**: The model requires agents to bid their true indifference prices; in reality, traders often strategically misrepresent to gain advantage, which the theory does not address.
- **Pure exchange only**: The framework excludes production, entry/exit of agents, or endogenous supply/demand shocks — real markets are dynamic and non-stationary.
- **No market frictions**: Ignores transaction costs, information delays, or bid-ask spreads, all of which slow convergence in practice.
- **Bounded vs. asymptotic convergence**: The paper shows allocations remain bounded and cluster at equilibrium, but doesn't guarantee *finite-time* convergence, so repeated trading might take arbitrarily long.
- **Fixed preferences**: Assumes preferences and endowments don't change; doesn't handle learning or preference evolution.

## Open Questions
1. **Strategic bidding**: What happens if agents deviate from truthful bidding? Does the mechanism still converge, or can manipulation push prices away from equilibrium?
2. **Convergence rate**: How many rounds of double auctions are needed to get close to equilibrium? Can this be characterized in terms of market size and structure?
3. **Extension to production**: Can similar convergence results hold in economies where agents can produce goods, not just exchange?
4. **Empirical calibration**: How well do real laboratory or market data match the model's predictions? Where do deviations occur?
5. **Heterogeneous bidding rules**: If different agents use different bidding strategies (some truthful, some strategic), does convergence still hold?

## Key Takeaway
Double auctions provably converge to fair market prices because traders' rational bids align equilibrium with price-discovery dynamics.
