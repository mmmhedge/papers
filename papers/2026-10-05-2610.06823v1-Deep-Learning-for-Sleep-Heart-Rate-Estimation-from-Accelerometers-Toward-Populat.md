# Deep Learning for Sleep Heart Rate Estimation from Accelerometers: Toward Population-Scale Cardiac Insight Without Optical Sensors

**Authors:** Tanbin Islam Rohan, Pranjol Sen Gupta, Tanusree Debi, Nazmus Sakib
**Published:** 2026-10-05 | **arXiv:** [2610.06823v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06823v1)

## Summary
This paper presents SeqSmoother, a transformer-based neural network that estimates heart rate during sleep using only wrist accelerometer data—without optical sensors. The key motivation is that many large health studies collect motion data but lack dedicated heart-rate sensors; recovering cardiac information from this existing data could unlock population-scale insights into nocturnal cardiovascular health at minimal cost.

## Theoretical Background
The work builds on two foundations: (1) **temporal sequence modeling** using transformers—neural networks that use attention mechanisms to learn dependencies across time—and (2) **signal processing for physiological estimation** from wearable accelerometers. Prior work (Nightbeat) extracted heart rate from accelerometry using hand-crafted spectral features and quality filters, but with limited coverage of available data. The authors combine learned temporal patterns with physics-informed spectral features, grounding the model in the biomechanics of how body motion couples to cardiac oscillations.

## Core Contributions
1. **SeqSmoother architecture**: A transformer-based temporal corrector that improves heart-rate estimates by modeling sequential dependencies in accelerometer signals over time windows.
2. **Physics-informed features**: Introduction of a sub-harmonic ratio feature designed to detect "harmonic lock-on" (when accelerometer-detected frequencies align with cardiac harmonics), achieving 0.972 AUROC for identifying these events.
3. **Accuracy-availability trade-off characterization**: Empirical demonstration that learned models improve coverage (estimate availability) but sacrifice point-wise accuracy compared to quality-gated signal processing, quantifying this trade-off explicitly.
4. **Cross-participant validation**: Rigorous evaluation using participant-disjoint held-out folds, preventing overfitting to individual physiological signatures.

## Connections to Other Work
This work directly compares against and builds upon [[Nightbeat]], the prior reference standard for accelerometer-based sleep HR estimation. It relates to broader efforts in **wearable biomarker recovery** and **weakly-supervised learning** (using ECG only for labels and quality weights, not during inference). The physics-informed feature design echoes approaches in mechanistic signal processing, though the paper prioritizes learned models. It complements work on [[deep-learning|deep learning]] for physiological time-series forecasting and aligns with the trend toward extracting maximum value from multi-modal sensor datasets.

## Practical Applications
1. **Retrospective sleep-health studies**: Researchers analyzing existing accelerometer datasets (e.g., large cohort studies like UK Biobank) can now extract nocturnal heart-rate variability without re-collecting data.
2. **Population-level cardiovascular screening**: Identifying sleep-stage-dependent arrhythmias or abnormal heart-rate patterns in large populations for epidemiological research.
3. **Wearable device software upgrades**: Consumer smartwatches and fitness trackers equipped with accelerometers can estimate HR during sleep even without optical sensors, expanding sensor fusion strategies.
4. **Sleep apnea and cardiac event detection**: Temporal modeling of HR fluctuations during sleep can flag episodes of oxygen desaturation or irregular rhythms for clinical follow-up.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **Retrofit HR Analytics Platform**: A SaaS tool that processes historical accelerometer datasets from existing health studies or wearable clouds to retroactively compute sleep HR metrics without recollection costs.
2. **Smart-Ring or Wristband Firmware**: License SeqSmoother to wearable makers as a software module enabling HR estimation on accelerometer-only devices in sleep mode, reducing power consumption vs. optical sensing.
3. **Telemedicine Sleep Screening**: An AI service for sleep clinics that ingests patient wearable data and flags nocturnal HR anomalies for physician review, automating triage before polysomnography.
4. **Corporate Wellness Integration**: White-label sleep-health dashboards for enterprise wellness platforms that surface sleep HR variability as a stress/recovery biomarker without new hardware.
5. **Research Data Harmonization Tool**: A pipeline that standardizes HR extraction across legacy wearable datasets (mismatched sensors, sampling rates) using physics-informed learning, enabling meta-analyses.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Accuracy vs. coverage trade-off**: SeqSmoother achieves 1.60 bpm MAE across all eligible data, but Nightbeat's quality-gated approach reaches 0.615 bpm on overlapping windows—a 2.6× gap suggests learned models may overfit noise or miss subtle signal artifacts.
2. **Generalization across populations**: Trained on 13 participant-disjoint folds; unclear how well the model transfers to different age groups, BMI ranges, fitness levels, or sleep disorders (e.g., severe apnea).
3. **ECG dependency for training**: The model requires high-quality simultaneous ECG reference labels to train, limiting applicability to studies lacking such data.
4. **Inference window assumptions**: The 60-s window + 15-s step protocol is arbitrary; performance under real-time or variable-window constraints is unexplored.
5. **Sleep-stage heterogeneity**: No analysis of whether HR estimation accuracy degrades in specific sleep stages (REM vs. deep sleep), which have distinct cardiac dynamics.

## Open Questions
1. **Cross-device transfer**: Does SeqSmoother trained on one accelerometer type generalize to different sensors with different sampling rates or noise profiles?
2. **Pathological validation**: How does performance degrade in patients with atrial fibrillation, sleep apnea, or other cardiac/respiratory disorders?
3. **Continual learning**: Can the model adapt to individual users over time without retraining, improving as more home data accumulates?
4. **Uncertainty quantification**: What calibrated confidence intervals can the model provide, and how do they correlate with actual error?
5. **Physics-guided architectures**: Could explicit domain constraints (e.g., HR ranges, spectral priors) be baked into the transformer architecture to improve both accuracy and interpretability?
6. **Real-time feasibility**: What is the computational cost of SeqSmoother inference on low-power wearable chips vs. cloud?

## Key Takeaway
Transformers can recover sleep heart rate from wrist motion alone, trading per-beat accuracy for broader data coverage in accelerometer-only cohorts.
