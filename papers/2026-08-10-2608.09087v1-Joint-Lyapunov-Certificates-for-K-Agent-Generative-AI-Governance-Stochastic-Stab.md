# Joint Lyapunov Certificates for K-Agent Generative AI Governance: Stochastic Stability, Emergent Ensemble Risk, and Zero-Knowledge Governance Attestation

**Authors:** Sriram Nagaraj
**Published:** 2026-08-10 | **arXiv:** [2608.09087v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09087v1)

## Summary
This paper addresses a critical blind spot in AI governance: when multiple generative AI models interact through shared learning, each model can individually appear stable while the combined system drifts dangerously. The authors develop a mathematical framework using "Joint Lyapunov Certificates" to detect and prove stability of multi-agent AI systems without exposing proprietary model weights, using cryptographic attestation protocols.

## Theoretical Background
The paper builds on Model Risk Management (MRM) — a regulatory framework requiring AI systems to prove their stability and safety. Traditional MRM uses Lyapunov analysis, a classical control-theory tool that proves a system won't diverge by constructing an "energy function" that monotonically decreases. However, Lyapunov proofs typically work on single agents. The novelty here is extending this to K-agent systems where models share a meta-learning coupling (they learn from each other through an interaction matrix). The framework combines stochastic stability theory with zero-knowledge proofs (SNARKs — Succinct Non-Interactive Arguments of Knowledge, a cryptographic primitive that proves a statement is true without revealing the underlying data).

## Core Contributions
1. **Joint Lyapunov Proof (JLP)**: A protocol that proves the aggregate K-agent system satisfies MRM stability bounds without revealing individual model weights.
2. **Formal characterization** of the infinitesimal generator of the joint quadratic Lyapunov function — the mathematical machinery governing multi-agent stability.
3. **Critical coupling threshold theorem**: Exact quantification of how much inter-agent learning can occur before the system loses mean-square stability, even if individual agents remain stable.
4. **Noise-Floor Theorem**: Identifies the minimum variance floor below which zero-knowledge attestation becomes meaningless.
5. **Per-epoch SNARK implementation**: A practical cryptographic protocol running at each validation epoch on live model weights.

## Connections to Other Work
This work sits at the intersection of three domains:
- **Control theory & stability**: Extends classical Lyapunov methods (rooted in dynamical systems) to multi-agent settings with stochastic coupling.
- **AI governance & alignment**: Related to broader [[alignment]] challenges — how to certify that AI systems behave as intended — but focuses on *emergent* risks from interaction rather than individual misbehavior.
- **Cryptographic attestation**: Uses [[zero-knowledge proofs]] frameworks similar to those used in blockchain and confidential computing, but applied to AI model verification.
- **Multi-agent learning**: Connects to reinforcement learning multi-agent literature, but with emphasis on stability certification rather than optimal control.

The paper's core insight — that agent-level stability ≠ system-level stability — parallels systemic risk problems in quantitative finance ([[market-microstructure]], contagion) and complex systems theory.

## Practical Applications
1. **Regulatory compliance for large AI systems**: Financial institutions and healthcare providers deploying multiple models (e.g., fraud detection + transaction scoring) can now prove joint stability to regulators without exposing model internals.
2. **Foundation model governance**: When organizations fine-tune shared base models, this framework certifies that the ensemble doesn't drift into adversarial or unsafe regimes.
3. **AI model marketplaces**: Third-party model aggregators can attest to customers that deployed model combinations are stable, without accessing proprietary weights from different vendors.
4. **Real-time monitoring dashboards**: Per-epoch SNARK protocols enable continuous, cryptographically-verified stability monitoring in production AI systems.
5. **Federated learning governance**: Multi-organization collaborative AI training can validate joint stability while maintaining data/model privacy.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **AI Stability-as-a-Service**: SaaS platform that runs JLP checks on customer multi-model deployments, generating regulatory-grade attestation reports without touching model weights.
2. **Cryptographic Model Audit Protocol**: Toolkit for enterprises to auto-generate per-epoch zero-knowledge proofs of stability for compliance dashboards (SOC 2, regulatory filing).
3. **Multi-Agent Model Broker**: Marketplace that combines models from different vendors and certifies their joint stability via JLP before deployment, reducing buyer risk.
4. **Emergent Risk Detection Engine**: Real-time monitoring tool for financial/healthcare firms that detects when coupled models cross the critical coupling threshold (early warning system).
5. **AI Model Insurance Underwriting**: Insurance product pricing multi-model deployment risk using the critical coupling theorem to quantify exposure — JLP protocols provide underwriters with cryptographic proof of stability claims.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Quadratic Lyapunov assumption**: The framework assumes quadratic (degree-2) Lyapunov functions. Complex, highly nonlinear multi-agent dynamics may require higher-degree or exotic Lyapunov candidates.
2. **Mean-square stability only**: The Noise-Floor Theorem constrains what can be attested; extremely low-noise regimes may become unverifiable.
3. **Interaction matrix assumption**: Assumes models couple through a fixed or slowly-varying interaction matrix. Rapidly time-varying or sparse couplings may violate assumptions.
4. **Computational cost of SNARKs**: Zero-knowledge proofs are expensive; per-epoch execution may become bottleneck for large models or high-frequency validation.
5. **Limited to 5 experimental validations**: Validation on "multi-agent softmax system" — unclear how well this generalizes to transformers, CNNs, or other modern architectures.
6. **Adversarial model coupling**: If models are adversarially coupled (not just learning from each other benignly), the framework may miss intentional drift.

## Open Questions
1. **Scalability**: How does SNARK proof generation scale with K (number of agents) and model weight count? Can it run efficiently for 100+ coupled models?
2. **Non-quadratic Lyapunov functions**: Can the JLP framework extend to higher-degree or hybrid Lyapunov candidates?
3. **Emergent behaviors beyond drift**: Does the framework detect other emergent risks (e.g., mode collapse, reward hacking in multi-agent RL settings)?
4. **Adaptive coupling matrices**: If the interaction matrix itself is learned/adaptive, how would the critical threshold theorem change?
5. **Empirical validation on real architectures**: How does JLP perform on actual multi-LLM or multi-vision-model systems deployed in production?
6. **Privacy-utility trade-off**: What information do SNARKs leak about model behavior despite being "zero-knowledge"? How tight is the attestation?

## Key Takeaway
Multi-agent AI systems can drift as a whole even when each model individually looks stable—this paper proves joint stability without revealing model secrets.
