# Faynt: Scaling and Optimizing Policies for Competitive Melee

**Authors:** Ali Janati, Nikita Kuzmin, Rohit Swamy, Charles Niu
**Published:** 2026-10-01 | **arXiv:** [2610.02144v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02144v1)

## Summary
This paper presents Faynt, a family of Transformer-based policies that play Super Smash Bros. Melee at competitive levels, with a single model controlling all 26 characters. The 10M-parameter version wins 98.4% of same-character matches against existing bots and achieves 100% win rate against a zero-delay baseline, demonstrating that large-scale pretraining on human replays combined with specialized post-training can produce superhuman performance in complex, real-time competitive games.

## Theoretical Background
This work builds on several foundational areas:

**Reinforcement Learning (RL)** — training agents by rewarding desired behaviors — is combined with **supervised pretraining** on human demonstrations. The paper leverages transformer architectures — neural networks that use attention mechanisms to process sequential information — which have become standard for policy learning in sequential decision-making tasks.

The approach follows the paradigm of **behavioral cloning** (learning from human replays) followed by RL fine-tuning, similar to methods used in games like Dota 2 and StarCraft. The use of **curriculum learning** (training on progressively harder tasks) and **knowledge distillation** (training a smaller model to match a larger one) are established techniques for improving policy quality and efficiency.

The paper also engages with **scaling laws** — the empirical observation that larger models often perform better — and hyperparameter transfer, which explores whether optimal settings for one model size generalize to others.

## Core Contributions
- **Unified multi-character policy**: A single 10M-parameter checkpoint controls all 26 Melee characters with 98.4% win rate in same-character matchups, versus prior work requiring specialist models per character.
- **Scaling and architecture analysis**: Systematic study of how model size, architecture choices, and pretraining data quantity affect performance in this domain.
- **Post-training pipeline**: Combination of rank-based and outcome-based curricula, distillation from 75M to 10M, and RL restricted to specific mirror matchups that outperforms the larger pretrained model.
- **Inference efficiency**: 5.2 ms decision latency on T4 GPU for the 10M model — fast enough for real-time play without artificial action delays.
- **Evaluation methodology and artifacts**: Open-source weights, two benchmark suites, and an automated tournament platform for reproducible competitive evaluation.

## Connections to Other Work
This work relates to:

- **Game-playing agents** — extends the tradition of [[AlphaGo]], [[AlphaStar]], and other RL-based game-playing systems to a real-time, pixel-based, high-action-space domain.
- **Behavioral cloning + RL** — follows the [[RLHF]]-like pattern of using human data to warm-start, then RL to surpass human performance.
- **Multi-task learning** — controlling all characters in one model is a form of transfer learning across related tasks.
- **Scaling laws in RL** — contributes empirical data on how policy performance scales with model size in competitive gaming.
- **Curriculum learning** — uses ranked and outcome-based curricula, similar to self-play curricula in [[AlphaStar]].

## Practical Applications
- **Competitive gaming AI**: Developing opponents for esports training, content creation, and automated tournament systems.
- **Game balancing**: Using trained policies to evaluate character matchup win rates and identify balance issues in game design.
- **Speedrunning optimization**: Applying learned policies to find optimal strategies and movement sequences.
- **Accessibility tools**: AI opponents at tunable difficulty levels for casual and learning players.
- **Real-time decision-making systems**: The inference speed and architecture choices could transfer to other latency-sensitive control tasks.

## Technical Limitations
- **Action delay discrepancy**: Faynt uses no artificial delay while some baselines have 21–24 frame delays; the paper does not isolate how much of the performance gap is due to this advantage.
- **Evaluation scope**: Only tested on Super Smash Bros. Melee; generalization to other fighting games or real-time domains is unclear.
- **Computational overhead at scale**: Inference is optimized for T4, but deployment on consumer hardware (e.g., emulation clients) may be slower.
- **Black-box policy**: As with most deep RL agents, the decision-making process is not interpretable — hard to debug or explain why the model makes specific reads.
- **Human replay dataset dependency**: Performance is capped by the quality and diversity of 840,000 human replays; rare strategies or emerging metagames may not be well-represented.
- **Distillation trade-off**: The 10M model outperforms the 75M on win rate but only after distillation; it's unclear whether this generalizes to unseen opponents or novel situations.

## Open Questions
- How does performance degrade against zero-delay opponents that use strategies from the post-2020 metagame if training replays are older?
- Can the approach generalize to other fighting games with minimal retraining, and what makes transfer easier or harder?
- What are the learned representations in Faynt? Can we extract interpretable decision rules or scouting heuristics from the model?
- How much of the win rate is due to the absence of input delay versus superior strategy, and can the baselines match performance if given the same latency?
- Does Faynt learn robust counter-strategies, or does it exploit deterministic patterns in opponent AI? How does it perform against adaptive opponents that learn from it?
- Can smaller models (sub-1M parameters) achieve comparable performance with better quantization or architecture choices for edge deployment?

## Key Takeaway
Single Transformer policy learns to play all 26 Melee characters at 98% win rate through human replay pretraining and specialized post-training curricula.
