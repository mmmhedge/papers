# AtumAI: A Principled Framework for Agentic Generation of Datacenter Control-Plane Policies

**Authors:** Qiushi Lin, Chaojie Zhang, Íñigo Goiri, Aditya Akella, Ricardo Bianchini et al.
**Published:** 2026-08-03 | **arXiv:** [2608.02569v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02569v1)

## Summary
This paper presents AtumAI, a framework that uses AI agents to automatically design datacenter control policies (the software that manages how resources are allocated and managed in data centers). Rather than relying on humans to manually engineer these policies over months, AtumAI takes a plain-language goal, formally specifies the problem, and then uses a combination of language models, evolutionary algorithms, and machine learning to propose and test candidate policies until one works well. The key insight is that you can structure the agent's search space formally and combine multiple AI techniques to explore more of the design space than an LLM alone could.

## Theoretical Background
This work builds on three conceptual pillars. First, **agentic AI** — systems that autonomously perceive, reason, and act to achieve goals — has emerged as a way to automate complex engineering tasks. Second, **formal specification and constraint satisfaction** is a classical computer science approach where you translate informal requirements into machine-checkable logical statements, enabling systematic search. Third, **generative and evolutionary methods** — diffusion models for generating diverse candidates, evolutionary algorithms for iterative refinement, and surrogate models (learned approximations of expensive evaluations) for efficiently exploring large design spaces — have proven effective for design automation in other domains.

The paper recognizes that off-the-shelf agentic LLM systems lack formality (no structured problem statement), transferability (solutions don't generalize across tasks), and systematicity (LLM-only search is narrow and gets stuck in local optima).

## Core Contributions
1. **Datacenter Task Compiler**: A novel component that automatically translates a plain-language datacenter goal into a formal specification capturing objectives, hard/soft constraints, decision variables, and evaluation metrics. This formalizes the search problem in a machine-checkable way.

2. **Evolutionary Design Discovery Loop**: A structured search algorithm that goes beyond the LLM by combining:
   - A diffusion model to generate diverse policy candidates
   - An evolutionary algorithm to breed and refine promising candidates
   - A surrogate model to predict policy performance without expensive simulation
   
3. **Empirical validation** on three distinct datacenter tasks (workload placement, resource scaling, power management) showing that AtumAI-generated policies consistently outperform expert-engineered baselines.

4. **Reduction in engineering overhead**: Onboarding a new control-plane task drops from months of manual engineering to writing a task description in natural language.

## Connections to Other Work
This paper intersects several research threads:
- **[[Agents]]** and agentic AI: Similar to work on autonomous agents for code generation or scientific discovery, but applied to systems optimization.
- **[[Reinforcement-learning]]** and policy search: The iterative refinement mirrors RL policy gradient methods, though AtumAI uses evolution + learned models rather than RL.
- **[[Optimization]]** and design automation: Echoes surrogate-assisted optimization (Bayesian optimization, evolutionary strategies with neural surrogate models) used in hyperparameter tuning and AutoML.
- **Formal methods and program synthesis**: The Task Compiler borrows from formal specification languages and synthesis-based approaches that bridge natural language to executable code/policies.
- **Neural architecture search (NAS)**: Similar multi-objective, constrained search over large design spaces.

AtumAI's novelty is in *combining* these techniques systematically for a concrete systems domain and showing that formal + evolutionary + generative approaches beat LLM-only baselines.

## Practical Applications
1. **Datacenter operations**: Reducing the time and expertise needed to deploy new control policies as hardware/workloads evolve (e.g., new GPU types, federated training, serverless platforms).
2. **Multi-cloud resource allocation**: Automatically designing policies for distributing workloads across heterogeneous cloud providers under cost and latency constraints.
3. **Edge computing**: Rapidly generating edge-specific policies for resource-constrained environments (IoT clusters, regional edge nodes).
4. **Energy efficiency compliance**: Automatically designing power-management policies that meet sustainability targets while maintaining SLA.
5. **Hardware-software co-design**: Using the framework to optimize policies when new hardware accelerators or network topologies are introduced.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **PolicyAsAService**: A SaaS platform where operators describe their datacenter goals in natural language, AtumAI generates and simulates candidate policies, and the platform handles deployment and continuous refinement as workloads change. Target: mid-size cloud providers, large enterprises with private datacenters.

2. **AutoOps for Edge/IoT**: Specialized version of AtumAI for resource-constrained environments (edge nodes, on-premises clusters), automatically generating lightweight control policies. Target: industrial IoT companies, telco edge networks.

3. **Policy Marketplace**: A platform where AtumAI-generated policies are validated, ranked, and shared (with privacy-preserving abstractions). Customers can discover and adapt proven policies for similar hardware/workload profiles. Target: open-source datacenter operators, smaller cloud providers.

4. **Simulation-in-the-loop Testing**: A tool for chip designers and cloud architects to rapidly prototype how new hardware (CPUs, accelerators, network) changes optimal control policies before tape-out or deployment. Target: semiconductor companies, hyperscalers' hardware teams.

5. **Multi-objective Policy Optimizer**: Enterprise tool to automatically trade off competing goals (cost, latency, power, fairness) in clusters, with continuous A/B testing and rollback. Target: large tech companies, financial institutions with compute clusters.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Simulation fidelity**: The evaluation loop relies on simulation; if the simulator is inaccurate (e.g., doesn't capture tail latency, network congestion, or thermal effects), the learned policies may fail in production.
2. **Scalability of formal specification**: The Task Compiler must be extended for each new class of problem; it's unclear how well it generalizes to radically different domains (e.g., networking, storage).
3. **Surrogate model brittleness**: The learned surrogate may be unreliable in unexplored regions of the design space, leading to poor candidate proposals.
4. **Diversity vs. exploitation trade-off**: Evolutionary algorithms can get stuck in local optima or lose diversity; the paper doesn't deeply analyze how the diffusion model + evolution balance exploration.
5. **Computational cost**: Training surrogate models and running evolution requires significant compute; the break-even point (when automation saves more engineering time than it costs in compute) is not quantified.
6. **Hard constraints may not transfer**: Policies optimized for one datacenter (with specific SLAs, hardware, workload mix) may not work on another; transferability claims need more cross-domain validation.

## Open Questions
1. **Transferability across domains**: Can policies (or learned models) transfer between datacenters with different hardware, workloads, or cost structures? How much retraining is needed?
2. **Robustness and adversarial validation**: How do generated policies perform under distributional shift (e.g., novel workloads, cascading failures, adversarial inputs)? Is there a systematic way to validate policies before deployment?
3. **Interpretability of evolved policies**: What makes an evolved policy good? Can we extract human-readable insights or design principles from successful policies to accelerate future designs?
4. **Formal verification**: Can the framework prove that generated policies satisfy safety or fairness constraints, not just that they were evaluated positively in simulation?
5. **Multi-objective Pareto frontiers**: How does AtumAI handle tasks with multiple conflicting objectives (e.g., latency, cost, power)? Can it efficiently map the Pareto frontier?
6. **Human-in-the-loop refinement**: How can human operators guide or correct the evolution loop based on domain knowledge? What are the interaction patterns?
7. **Scaling to real-time policies**: Current work focuses on batch scheduling; can this approach apply to millisecond-scale control loops (e.g., congestion control, load balancing)?

## Key Takeaway
Formal specifications plus evolutionary search let AI automatically design datacenter policies better than expert engineering, slashing months of work to hours.
