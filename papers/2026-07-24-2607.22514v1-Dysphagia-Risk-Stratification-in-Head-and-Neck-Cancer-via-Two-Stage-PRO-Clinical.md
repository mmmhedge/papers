# Dysphagia Risk Stratification in Head and Neck Cancer via Two-Stage PRO-Clinical Stacking

**Authors:** Siyuan Zhao, Eric Ababio Anyimadu, Zachary G. Brumm, Yue Ma, Clifton David Fuller et al.
**Published:** 2026-07-24 | **arXiv:** [2607.22514v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22514v1)

## Summary
This paper develops a machine learning model to identify head and neck cancer patients at risk of dysphagia (swallowing difficulty) using patient-reported symptoms and clinical data, avoiding the need for expensive imaging. The method stacks two prediction stages to create an interpretable risk assessment tool that could help clinicians decide which patients need specialized swallowing evaluation during survivorship care.

## Theoretical Background
The paper builds on clinical risk stratification methods and ensemble machine learning (specifically stacking — combining multiple models to improve predictions). Dysphagia is assessed clinically using the Dynamic Imaging Grade of Swallowing Toxicity (CTCAE-DIGEST), a validated standard that requires videofluoroscopic imaging (video X-ray of swallowing). Patient-reported outcomes (PROs) are validated self-reported symptom measures — cheap and scalable alternatives to imaging. The MDADI (M.D. Anderson Dysphagia Inventory) is a standard PRO questionnaire for swallowing symptoms. The core challenge is translating subjective patient responses into objective clinical decisions about escalation of care. Two-stage stacking uses a base learner and meta-learner architecture to combine predictions, allowing interpretable feature importance analysis.

## Core Contributions
1. **Framework design**: Formulates a single-visit, imaging-free prediction approach integrating PRO responses with structured clinical variables for dysphagia risk.
2. **Interpretable stacking model**: Develops a two-stage stacking architecture specifically designed for clinical interpretability, moving beyond black-box prediction.
3. **Granular PRO analysis**: Demonstrates that individual MDADI items contain independent predictive signal beyond composite summary scores — actionable for personalized symptom monitoring.
4. **Clinical validation scope**: Validates the approach on real HNC survivorship data, establishing proof-of-concept for PRO-clinical integration in practice.

## Connections to Other Work
This work relates to broader effort in [[machine-learning-for-clinical-prediction]], [[interpretable-ml-in-healthcare]], and ensemble methods. ## Practical Applications
1. **Survivorship clinic triage**: Automated decision support for oncology nurses deciding which post-HNC patients need specialist swallowing evaluation at routine follow-up visits.
2. **Telehealth risk screening**: Remote baseline PRO collection for HNC patients, with automated risk flagging before in-person visits.
3. **Resource allocation**: Hospital quality improvement — predict which patients can safely defer imaging, reducing wait times and equipment burden.
4. **Clinical trial stratification**: Prospectively enroll high-risk patients into dysphagia intervention studies.
5. **Patient engagement**: Provide patients with personalized risk scores to motivate early symptom reporting and self-advocacy.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **HNC Survivorship AI Dashboard**: SaaS platform for cancer centers integrating PRO collection, automated risk scoring, and clinical decision alerts for oncology teams.
2. **Wearable Swallowing Monitoring**: Combine PRO data with passive sensor data (voice, accelerometers) to continuously monitor dysphagia risk in real time post-treatment.
3. **Telehealth Triage Bot**: Automated intake system for HNC follow-up that collects PROs, runs risk model, and recommends appointment type (routine vs. swallowing specialist).
4. **Clinical Decision Support API**: White-label model integration for EHR systems (Epic, Cerner) to surface dysphagia risk at the point of care.
5. **Patient Portal Engagement**: Mobile app where HNC survivors log symptoms and receive personalized feedback on their dysphagia risk trajectory over time.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Data scope**: Single-visit snapshot — does not capture symptom trajectory or temporal patterns; may miss progressive decline.
2. **Imaging gold standard**: Model is validated against CTCAE-DIGEST, which itself has limitations (requires trained raters, not universally available); performance ceiling depends on imaging reliability.
3. **Confounding**: PROs capture subjective perception; anxiety, depression, or other late effects may confound symptom reporting and weaken PRO signal.
4. **Generalization**: HNC is heterogeneous (oropharyngeal, laryngeal, etc.); different tumor sites and treatment regimens may have different PRO-swallowing relationships not controlled for.
5. **Missing mechanistic insight**: Model predicts risk but does not identify why a given patient is at risk (physiologic cause vs. perception). Stacking improves interpretability but is not fully mechanistic.
6. **Class imbalance**: Dysphagia prevalence in survivorship is typically low; risk of model bias toward negative cases or inflated specificity.

## Open Questions
1. **Longitudinal validation**: Does PRO-clinical risk score predict future swallowing decline when tracked over months/years?
2. **Intervention response**: Can model predict which high-risk patients will benefit from swallowing therapy vs. those with irreversible damage?
3. **Subgroup heterogeneity**: Which tumor sites, treatment modalities (surgery vs. radiation vs. combined), or patient demographics show strongest PRO-imaging correlation?
4. **Threshold optimization**: What cutoff score minimizes false positives (unnecessary imaging) while maintaining sensitivity? How does this trade-off vary across clinical settings?
5. **Item importance stability**: Are the key MDADI predictors stable across different HNC populations, or do they vary by site and treatment?
6. **Real-world implementation**: What happens to model performance when deployed in routine clinic workflows with variable patient compliance on PRO completion?

## Key Takeaway
Machine learning can identify dysphagia risk in cancer survivors using symptom surveys alone, reducing need for specialized swallowing imaging and improving clinical triage.
