# Spatially explicit feature importance for building height estimation using research-access high-resolution SAR and optical sensors

**Authors:** Guilherme Iablonovski, Pierre-Louis Frison, Tatiana Silva da Silva
**Published:** 2026-08-18 | **arXiv:** [2608.17822v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17822v1)

## Summary
This paper estimates building heights in cities using freely available satellite data (radar and optical imagery) combined with machine learning, achieving 5-meter accuracy without expensive LiDAR. The key innovation is showing that different satellite sensors matter in different urban contexts — tall buildings rely on shadows, while low-rise buildings depend on footprint shape — which helps practitioners choose the right data for their region.

## Theoretical Background
Building height estimation traditionally relied on airborne LiDAR (light detection and ranging), which is expensive and unavailable in most developing regions. Recent work has explored using Sentinel satellites (freely available through Copernicus), but their 10–20 meter resolution limits accuracy for individual buildings. This paper builds on two complementary remote sensing traditions: optical imaging (capturing visible light reflection, including building shadows) and synthetic aperture radar or SAR (which penetrates clouds and uses radio waves to measure surface geometry). The core statistical method is **geographically weighted random forests** — a variant of random forests (ensemble models that combine many decision trees) that accounts for spatial autocorrelation, meaning nearby buildings tend to be similar and violate standard independence assumptions. TerraSAR-X StripMap and PlanetScope are higher-resolution variants of these standard sources, accessible under research licenses.

## Core Contributions
1. **Demonstrates feasibility of research-access satellite data** for fine-grained building mapping in the Global South, avoiding the cost barrier of commercial very high-resolution imagery.
2. **Spatially explicit feature importance analysis**: quantifies which predictors (footprint geometry, shadow height, spectral reflectance, radar backscatter) dominate in different intra-urban zones — low-rise vs. isolated tall buildings vs. dense urban cores. This is novel because global ML models hide this heterogeneity.
3. **Validates complementary roles of multiple sensors** (Sentinel-1 backscatter and InSAR occupy different spatial niches), showing no single sensor is universally best.
4. **Provides practical optioneering guidance** for practitioners to choose sensors and models based on local urban morphology, rather than applying one-size-fits-all algorithms.

## Connections to Other Work
This work sits between [[remote-sensing for urban mapping]], [[machine-learning for Earth observation]], and [[interpretable-ML]]. It contrasts with global deep learning or neural network approaches (e.g., CNN-based height regression) by prioritizing local explainability over black-box accuracy. Related to [[Sentinel data applications]] and [[material stock accounting]] literature in sustainability science. The geographically weighted framework is an alternative to standard transfer learning, which often fails when source and target regions differ in building morphology.

## Practical Applications
- **Material stock accounting**: City planners and circular economy researchers need building volumes for recycling and demolition planning; this enables cheap, scalable estimates in regions with no LiDAR.
- **Post-disaster damage assessment**: After earthquakes or floods, rapid building height mapping (correlated with structural damage risk) aids emergency response in developing regions.
- **Urban energy modeling**: Building height feeds into solar potential, wind exposure, and heating/cooling load estimates.
- **Informal settlement mapping**: In Global South cities, accurate per-building geometry helps target infrastructure investment and formalization programs.
- **Climate adaptation planning**: Height-based flood risk and heat island effect modeling for low-income urban areas.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **SatHeight-as-a-Service**: SaaS platform for cities in Africa, South Asia, and Latin America to upload study areas and receive building height maps + local feature importance dashboards, enabling transparent data-driven planning without LiDAR surveys.
2. **Insurance underwriting tool**: Automated building inventory for emerging-market insurers using satellite-derived height + damage risk models, reducing underwriting costs and expanding coverage.
3. **Climate resilience consultancy**: Combine building heights with terrain and rainfall data to map urban flood vulnerability; sell reports and mitigation plans to municipal governments.
4. **Developer's geospatial API**: Package the geographically weighted model as a REST API; real estate firms and NGOs query building attributes at scale for site selection and impact measurement.
5. **Disaster response toolkit**: Embed this pipeline into emergency response software; humanitarian orgs rapidly map building stock post-disaster to allocate aid and prioritize rescue operations.

## Technical Limitations
- **5.34 m RMSE is still coarse** for individual-building stock accounting if buildings are irregularly shaped or have complex rooflines; flat-roof assumption may not hold globally.
- **Spatial autocorrelation modeling**: While geographically weighted random forests mitigate autocorrelation bias, they require careful tuning of bandwidth and can be computationally expensive for very large cities.
- **Research-access data limitations**: TerraSAR-X and high-resolution PlanetScope are not freely available to non-researchers or commercial users; reproducibility and deployment in production systems is constrained.
- **Shadow-derived heights** (for tall buildings) fail in low-sun-angle regions (high latitudes, winter) or cloudy climates; optical sensors are weather-dependent.
- **Generalization uncertainty**: Model trained on Brazilian city; transferability to different climate zones, building styles, or dense informal settlements untested. Feature importance patterns may not replicate.
- **No uncertainty quantification**: Paper reports point estimates; no confidence intervals per building or per zone, limiting use for high-stakes decisions (e.g., insurance claims).

## Open Questions
- How does model performance degrade in regions with very different building morphology (e.g., dense informal settlements, or high-latitude sparse developments)?
- Can the spatially explicit feature importance be used to *automatically* select optimal sensor combinations for new cities, or must local tuning always occur?
- Do neural network approaches (e.g., CNNs or graph neural networks) recover the same spatial heterogeneity in predictor importance, or do they genuinely obscure it?
- How sensitive are results to the LiDAR reference dataset's accuracy and temporal mismatch with satellite acquisitions?
- Can this framework extend to other urban attributes (building footprint refinement, roof material, solar potential) in a multi-task learning setup?

## Key Takeaway
Freely available satellites can estimate building heights accurately in developing cities if you account for how different sensors matter in different neighborhoods.
