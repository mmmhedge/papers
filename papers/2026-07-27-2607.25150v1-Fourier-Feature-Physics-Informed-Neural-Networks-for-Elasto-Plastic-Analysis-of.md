# Fourier Feature Physics-Informed Neural Networks for Elasto-Plastic Analysis of Geomaterials with a Non-Associative Mohr-Coulomb Model

**Authors:** Apisit Robjanghvad, Sompote Youwai
**Published:** 2026-07-27 | **arXiv:** [2607.25150v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25150v1)

## Summary
This paper addresses a fundamental limitation of Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) — a machine learning approach for solving differential equations — when applied to geotechnical problems involving plastic deformation in soil. The authors propose Fourier Feature PINNs (FF-PINNs), which use random Fourier feature mapping to better capture sharp stress gradients at failure zones. The work matters because it demonstrates a mesh-free alternative to expensive finite element simulations while maintaining physics consistency, potentially accelerating design and analysis cycles in civil and geotechnical engineering.

## Theoretical Background
Physics-Informed Neural Networks embed physical laws (differential equations, boundary conditions) directly into neural network loss functions, rather than learning purely from data. This approach avoids the need for costly mesh generation required by Finite Element Method (FEM), the conventional workhorse of structural analysis.

However, PINNs suffer from spectral bias — neural networks naturally learn low-frequency patterns first and struggle with high-frequency features like sharp gradients. In geomaterials undergoing plastic deformation, stress concentrations at yield boundaries create exactly these sharp transitions.

The paper addresses this using Random Fourier Features — a technique that maps inputs to a higher-dimensional space of sinusoidal functions before feeding them to the network, helping it learn oscillatory patterns more easily. The underlying constitutive model is the non-associative Mohr-Coulomb criterion, a pressure-dependent plasticity rule used in soil mechanics where strength increases with confining stress and the direction of plastic strain differs from the yield surface normal (non-associative flow).

## Core Contributions
1. **Fourier feature embedding for geomechanics**: First application of random Fourier feature mapping to resolve spectral bias in elasto-plastic problems, achieving 66% error reduction in displacement predictions versus standard PINNs.

2. **Handling non-associative plasticity**: Demonstrates that FF-PINNs can accurately capture the narrower plastic zones and steeper gradients generated by pressure-dependent yield criteria — a harder problem than pressure-independent models.

3. **Strain-adaptive sampling strategy**: Proposes a collocation point sampling scheme that concentrates points where plastic strain is active, improving efficiency.

4. **Multi-objective loss formulation**: Enforces equilibrium, constitutive relations, and Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) complementarity conditions simultaneously against FEM reference solutions, ensuring physics consistency.

5. **Computational efficiency gains**: Achieves 50% reduction in wall-clock training time while improving accuracy, making the method practical for engineering workflows.

## Connections to Other Work
This work sits at the intersection of [[Physics-Informed Neural Networks]], [[Deep Learning for PDEs]], and computational geomechanics. It parallels efforts to overcome spectral bias in neural operators (e.g., [[Fourier Neural Operators]]), though applied to a constrained optimization setting rather than pure function approximation.

The strain-adaptive sampling echoes importance sampling and active learning strategies used in surrogate modeling. The multi-objective loss design is similar to penalty method formulations in constrained [[optimization]], enforcing inequality constraints (KKT conditions) via soft constraints in the loss function.

The work also connects to broader efforts replacing expensive simulators with learned surrogates in engineering, though most prior work focused on linear or simpler nonlinear problems.

## Practical Applications
1. **Real-time slope stability assessment**: Rapid prediction of failure mechanisms and plastic zones in embankments or natural slopes during design or monitoring, replacing hours of FEM simulation.

2. **Foundation design optimization**: Fast stress field prediction during iterative design of deep foundations (piles, caissons) under complex loading, accelerating parametric studies.

3. **Tunnel and underground structure analysis**: Quick assessment of ground response and plastic deformation around tunnels during construction planning or emergency response scenarios.

4. **Liquefaction and cyclic loading analysis**: Predicting accumulation of plastic strain and pore pressure rise in saturated soils under earthquakes or ocean wave loading.

5. **In-situ monitoring integration**: Embedding trained FF-PINNs in sensor networks or edge devices to infer subsurface stress states from surface displacement measurements in real time.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Training data dependency**: FF-PINN still requires high-fidelity FEM solutions for loss computation and validation. The method trades FEM runtime for training time; if FEM is unavailable or expensive to generate, the approach loses its advantage.

2. **Generalization across soil properties**: Model is trained on specific soil parameters (friction angle, cohesion, Young's modulus). Extrapolating to significantly different materials or depth-dependent properties requires retraining; no evidence of transfer learning is presented.

3. **Complex boundary conditions**: Tests use relatively simple geometries and boundary conditions. Behavior under mixed boundary conditions (part Dirichlet, part Neumann) or time-dependent loading is not explored.

4. **Limitation to 2D**: All benchmarks are 2D; 3D problems may require prohibitive computational cost and memory for Fourier feature expansion.

5. **KKT enforcement via soft constraints**: Inequality constraints (plasticity conditions) are enforced through loss terms, not hard constraints. Severe violations at test time are possible; no explicit bounds on constraint violation are guaranteed.

6. **Noise sensitivity capped at 2%**: Robustness analysis shows sensitivity analysis only up to 2% noise. Real sensor or measurement noise can exceed this, and robustness beyond this threshold is unexplored.

7. **No adaptive architecture**: Fixed network architecture and Fourier feature dimension; no guidance on how to choose these for new problem classes.

## Open Questions
1. **Transfer learning**: Can a FF-PINN trained on one soil type or geometry be fine-tuned efficiently for similar problems with different properties, reducing training cost?

2. **3D scalability and efficiency**: What is the computational cost in 3D? Do Fourier features remain beneficial, or do alternative encodings (e.g., positional embeddings from [[NeRF]]) scale better?

3. **Time-dependent plasticity**: Can the framework handle cyclic or time-varying loads, path-dependent plasticity, and hardening rules? How would strain history be encoded?

4. **Uncertainty quantification**: How can ensemble or Bayesian variants provide prediction intervals and principled uncertainty bounds for design-critical decisions?

5. **Hard constraint enforcement**: Is it possible to use augmented Lagrangian or other penalty-free methods to guarantee KKT conditions exactly rather than approximately?

6. **Comparison with other surrogate methods**: How does FF-PINN compare to Gaussian Process surrogates, polynomial chaos expansions, or reduced-order models on the same problems in terms of accuracy per unit cost?

7. **Interpretability of learned stress fields**: Can the learned network be post-hoc analyzed to extract dominant failure mechanisms or stress flow patterns, providing engineering insight beyond point predictions?

## Key Takeaway
Neural networks with Fourier features can solve complex soil failure problems faster and more accurately than traditional methods, eliminating expensive mesh generation.
