# Hybrid Joint-Selective Optimization: Reduced-Space Levenberg-Marquardt Refinement of Low-Dimensional Parameters of Interest

**Authors:** Muhammad Luthfi Shahab, Gabriella Alfa Indahsari, Imam Mukhlash, Hadi Susanto
**Published:** 2026-09-29 | **arXiv:** [2609.37308v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37308v1)

## Summary
This paper proposes a hybrid optimization strategy (HJSO) that combines first-order gradient descent on all parameters with targeted second-order refinement on a small subset of "parameters of interest." It's useful when you have a huge neural network but only care deeply about getting a few specific outputs right — like option prices in financial models or specific eigenvalues in matrix problems. The method trades off computational cost for accuracy in the dimensions that matter most.

## Theoretical Background
The paper builds on classical numerical optimization, particularly the Levenberg-Marquardt (LM) algorithm — a second-order method that interpolates between gradient descent and Newton's method to handle ill-conditioning. Standard LM is expensive for high-dimensional problems because it requires computing and storing Hessian information (second derivatives). The core insight is that in many applied problems, you don't need global second-order accuracy; you only need to refine performance on a low-dimensional slice of the parameter space. This combines ideas from constrained optimization (partitioning parameters by importance) with modern deep learning (using first-order methods for large-scale training). The paper also engages with physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) — neural networks trained to solve differential equations — and deep backward stochastic differential equation (DeepBSDE) solvers for option pricing.

## Core Contributions
1. **The HJSO framework**: A practical two-stage algorithm that first performs joint optimization on all parameters (standard gradient descent), then freezes non-critical variables and applies reduced-space LM to the parameters of interest.
2. **Reduced-space LM formulation**: Derives how to efficiently apply second-order refinement when you've already optimized the larger parameter block, avoiding full Hessian computation.
3. **Empirical demonstration across three domains**: Shows the approach works on matrix eigenvalue problems, inverse PDE problems (Bratu equation solved via PINN), and option pricing (100D Black-Scholes with DeepBSDE), proving it's not domain-specific.
4. **Convergence acceleration**: Demonstrates faster error reduction on parameters of interest compared to pure first-order baselines, without the cost of full-space second-order methods.

## Connections to Other Work
This connects to broader themes in [[deep-learning]] optimization: the tension between efficiency (first-order methods scale to billions of parameters) and accuracy (second-order methods converge faster locally). It relates to recent work on [[PINN]] training, where convergence can be slow and practitioners often care about accuracy only on specific physical quantities. The paper also touches on [[derivatives-pricing]] via the Black-Scholes application, where certain outputs (option values) are economically critical while the neural network has many latent degrees of freedom. The reduced-space idea echoes trust-region methods and quasi-Newton approaches that exploit problem structure.

## Practical Applications
1. **Inverse problem solving**: When calibrating a physics simulation (e.g., material properties, boundary conditions), you measure a few outputs directly but have thousands of hidden parameters. HJSO refines only the measured quantities.
2. **Option pricing and financial engineering**: The Black-Scholes example is concrete — you want accurate option prices (the POIs) but train on a large neural network. This could accelerate calibration of real trading models.
3. **Eigenvalue computation for spectral methods**: In solving PDEs via spectral discretization, you care about a few critical eigenvalues; HJSO can refine those while the rest of the spectrum remains coarse.
4. **Sensor-based neural network training**: In robotics or sensor networks, you have a large latent model but only a few sensor outputs matter; refine those outputs while keeping the rest of the model stable.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **Financial calibration SaaS**: A cloud service that fits neural networks to multiasset option prices and rates, using HJSO to guarantee accuracy on observed market quotes while fast-training on thousands of latent factors. Target: quant hedge funds and banks doing exotic derivatives pricing.
2. **Physics-informed ML platform**: A plug-and-play tool for engineers solving inverse problems (material science, geophysics, structural optimization) where you measure a few physical quantities and need fast, accurate refinement. Sell to R&D teams in manufacturing and energy.
3. **Automated model calibration for risk management**: A system that calibrates large stochastic volatility or term-structure models to market data, ensuring regulatory risk metrics (VaR, CVA) are exact while optimizing other parameters efficiently.
4. **Scientific computing library**: An open-source or commercial package (competing with JAX/PyTorch for scientific ML) that handles inverse problems and PDE solving, with HJSO as a built-in optimizer for mixed-precision accuracy requirements.
5. **Reinforcement learning fine-tuning**: For large pretrained models in [[deep-learning]], apply HJSO to refine a small set of task-critical output neurons or policy parameters while keeping the bulk of the model frozen — useful in transfer learning scenarios.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Requires prior knowledge of POIs**: You must know in advance which parameters matter. This isn't always obvious, and bad choices lead to wasted second-order effort.
2. **Limited to weakly-coupled parameter blocks**: If the POIs are strongly coupled to the remaining parameters, freezing the latter after first-order training may trap you in a local minimum. The paper doesn't thoroughly analyze this coupling.
3. **Second-order cost grows with POI dimension**: The reduced-space LM still requires Hessian-vector products or approximations. At 10–100D in POIs it's fine; at 1000D it becomes expensive.
4. **No convergence guarantees**: The paper is empirical. Theoretical convergence rates (local vs. global) for the hybrid scheme are not analyzed.
5. **Hessian approximation quality**: The paper doesn't discuss what Hessian approximations are used in practice (full Hessian, BFGS, Gauss-Newton). Approximation error could undermine the second-order benefit.
6. **Hyperparameter tuning**: When to switch from first-order to second-order, how long to run each phase, and LM damping parameters are not thoroughly studied.

## Open Questions
1. **Coupling analysis**: Under what conditions (measured quantitatively) does freezing the remaining parameters hurt convergence? Can you bound the optimality gap?
2. **Adaptive POI selection**: Can the method automatically identify which parameters should be treated as POIs using sensitivity analysis or information-theoretic measures?
3. **Higher-dimensional POIs**: How does HJSO scale when POIs are 100–1000D? Does it beat alternatives like natural gradient or Riemannian optimization?
4. **Theoretical rates**: What are the local convergence rates for the hybrid scheme? Can you prove superlinear convergence?
5. **Hessian approximations**: How do different Hessian approximations (full, BFGS, Gauss-Newton) affect the POI-error reduction rate?
6. **Distributed / federated training**: Can HJSO be parallelized for distributed training, where different machines compute first-order updates and one aggregates for second-order refinement?

## Key Takeaway
Hybrid optimization that does cheap gradient descent on all parameters then refines a small critical subset with second-order methods: faster and more accurate than first-order alone, without the cost of full-space Newton.
