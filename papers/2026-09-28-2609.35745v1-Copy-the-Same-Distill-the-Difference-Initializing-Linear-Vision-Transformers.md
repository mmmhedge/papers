# Copy the Same, Distill the Difference: Initializing Linear Vision Transformers

**Authors:** Huaiyuan Qin, Muli Yang, Gabriel James Goenawan, Shiqi Huang, Min Kass Chong et al.
**Published:** 2026-09-28 | **arXiv:** [2609.35745v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35745v1)

## Summary
This paper addresses how to efficiently initialize linear Vision Transformers (models that replace expensive attention mechanisms with faster linear-complexity alternatives) by reusing weights from pre-trained standard Vision Transformers. The key finding is counterintuitive: attention weights cannot be simply copied between the two architectures, but MLP (feedforward) weights can be, and attention's behavior can be recovered through knowledge distillation. This enables linear ViTs to match or exceed the performance of their slower Softmax-based counterparts while remaining computationally efficient.

## Theoretical Background
Vision Transformers (ViTs) are neural networks that process images as sequences of patches using attention mechanisms — neural operations that let models weight which parts of an image matter most for each prediction. Softmax attention is the standard approach but has quadratic computational cost with sequence length, making it expensive for high-resolution images. Linear attention operators reduce this to linear complexity by approximating the full attention mechanism. Knowledge distillation is a transfer learning technique where a smaller or simpler model (student) learns from a larger, pre-trained model (teacher) by matching its intermediate representations or outputs. The paper's core insight is that different components of ViTs (attention vs. MLPs) have different transfer properties across architectures.

## Core Contributions
1. **Empirical discovery that attention is operator-specific**: Directly copying attention weights from Softmax to linear ViTs hurts or barely helps performance, contradicting prior work on attention transfer between Softmax ViTs.
2. **MLP weights are operator-agnostic**: Simple weight copying of feedforward layers transfers effectively across attention types, carrying most pre-training benefits.
3. **Distillation strategy for attention recovery**: A tailored loss design recovers the token routing behavior of Softmax attention in linear ViTs without weight copying.
4. **Consistent validation**: Results hold across multiple linear ViT variants, model sizes, and datasets, suggesting the findings generalize.

## Connections to Other Work
This work directly engages with [[Attention Transfer]] literature, which showed attention is transferable between Softmax ViTs, but reveals this assumption breaks at the Softmax-to-linear boundary. It relates to broader transfer learning and domain adaptation research in vision, and to recent work on efficient ViTs (like kernel attention and other linear approximations). The distillation approach connects to [[knowledge distillation]] methods and representation learning theory. It also touches on mechanistic understanding of what makes ViT components reusable — an implicit interpretability question.

## Practical Applications
1. **Efficient deployment of large vision models**: Organizations can initialize linear ViTs from existing Softmax ViT checkpoints, reducing pre-training compute while maintaining accuracy for tasks like image classification, object detection, and segmentation.
2. **Mobile and edge AI**: Linear ViTs are faster and lower-memory; this initialization method makes them practical for resource-constrained devices (phones, IoT cameras, embedded systems).
3. **Real-time video processing**: Linear attention scales better with sequence length; initialized models enable real-time multi-frame video understanding without the latency of Softmax attention.
4. **Transfer learning for custom domains**: Teams can fine-tune initialized linear ViTs on domain-specific visual data (medical imaging, satellite imagery) faster than training from scratch.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Distillation overhead**: While attention is distilled, the process still requires a pre-trained Softmax ViT teacher at initialization time; this doesn't eliminate the dependency on existing models.
2. **Limited to initialization**: The method optimizes the *starting point* for linear ViT training but doesn't fundamentally change their optimization landscape; downstream fine-tuning may still be necessary.
3. **Task and architecture specificity**: Results are shown on image classification and some vision tasks; generalization to other modalities (video, 3D, multimodal) is untested.
4. **Linear attention quality ceiling**: Even with optimal initialization, linear ViTs approximate attention and may not match Softmax's expressiveness on all problems; the paper shows closing the gap, not proving equivalence.
5. **Computational overhead of distillation**: The distillation process itself requires forward passes through the teacher; deployment savings don't apply during the initialization phase.

## Open Questions
1. How does the Softmax-to-linear transfer principle extend to other architectures (CNNs, RNNs, hybrid models)?
2. Can the distillation strategy be combined with other efficient attention approximations (sparse, kernel-based) for further improvements?
3. Why exactly are MLPs operator-agnostic while attention is not? Is this a fundamental property of the learned representations or artifact of ViT training?
4. Does the approach work in reverse (linear-to-Softmax) or across other attention variants (multi-head, flash attention, etc.)?
5. What is the minimal data/compute required for effective distillation? Can it work with synthetic or limited labeled data?
6. How do initialized linear ViTs perform on downstream tasks (detection, segmentation, CLIP-style vision-language learning)?

## Key Takeaway
Copy feedforward weights directly, distill attention behavior instead—this simple strategy lets efficient linear vision models match pre-trained standard ones without full retraining.
