# Continuous surrogates versus threshold Boolean networks for modeling Arabidopsis ISR gene regulation

**Authors:** Gonzalo A. Ruz
**Published:** 2026-07-25 | **arXiv:** [2607.23289v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23289v1)

## Summary
This paper compares two modeling approaches for gene regulation in plants: continuous machine learning models (Random Forest and neural networks) versus discrete Boolean networks. The key finding is that these methods excel at different things—continuous models predict numerical values accurately, while Boolean networks better capture the true dynamic behavior of gene systems over time.

## Theoretical Background
Gene regulatory networks (GRNs) model how genes influence each other's expression levels. Traditional approaches use threshold Boolean networks—discrete models where genes are either "on" or "off" and switch states based on logical rules from other genes. Modern machine learning offers continuous surrogates that predict actual expression values. The paper builds on two distinct modeling traditions: symbolic approaches (Boolean logic, mechanistic models) and statistical learning (regression, neural networks). The trade-off between interpretability (understanding the rules) and accuracy (fitting the data well) is central to systems biology.

## Core Contributions
1. Direct empirical comparison of continuous vs. discrete approaches on the same biological dataset using matched evaluation metrics—this hadn't been systematically done before.
2. Discovery that one-step prediction accuracy and multi-step trajectory fidelity are misaligned: RF had best point-wise accuracy but worst long-term dynamics; TBN had poorer single-step predictions but reproduced observed temporal patterns exactly.
3. Demonstration that binarization (converting continuous measurements to on/off) changes which model wins—Boolean networks outperform continuous models in the discrete domain.
4. Evidence that these approaches should be viewed as complementary rather than competing.

## Connections to Other Work
This work sits at the intersection of [[mechanistic interpretability]] (favoring transparent, rule-based models) and modern machine learning (favoring flexible statistical learners). It relates to broader questions in systems biology about whether biological systems are fundamentally discrete or continuous, and echoes debates in interpretable machine learning about accuracy-interpretability trade-offs. The Boolean network approach connects to symbolic AI and knowledge representation, while the continuous surrogates use standard [[deep-learning]] and [[optimization]] methods.

## Practical Applications
- **Synthetic biology design**: Boolean models help engineers design genetic circuits with predictable on/off behavior; continuous models validate performance against real noisy measurements.
- **Crop improvement**: Understanding plant immune response (ISR) can guide breeding for disease resistance.
- **Drug target discovery**: Gene regulatory models identify which genes to perturb to achieve desired cellular outcomes in therapeutic contexts.
- **Systems diagnostics**: Combining both model types could detect when a biological system deviates from expected dynamics.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **GeneMatch**: SaaS tool for synthetic biologists that automatically fits both Boolean and continuous models to experimental data, recommending which to use for design vs. simulation.
2. **PlantShield**: Precision agriculture biotech using ISR-informed breeding pipelines to develop naturally disease-resistant crop lines with predicted shelf-life.
3. **RegulatoryDigest**: Consulting service that reverse-engineers regulatory networks from patient gene expression data, extracting Boolean rules for oncologists to target.
4. **BioCompass**: Cloud platform comparing discrete vs. continuous models on any uploaded time-series gene expression data, explaining trade-offs visually.
5. **ImmuneOptim**: Drug discovery platform optimizing immune response pathways by selecting which regulatory nodes to modulate using Boolean-guided search + continuous validation.

## Technical Limitations
- **Dataset scale**: Only 8 genes and 9 time points—too small to test scalability or overfitting risk. Real GRNs have hundreds/thousands of genes.
- **Binarization loss**: Converting continuous data to on/off is information-destructive; the paper doesn't explore intermediate thresholds or soft binarization.
- **Model selection bias**: RF and MLP chosen somewhat arbitrarily; other continuous architectures (GPs, graph neural networks) might change conclusions.
- **Prediction horizon**: Short 9-point timescale may not reveal long-term divergence. Boolean networks could fail on longer rollouts; continuous models might stabilize differently.
- **Biological interpretation**: Boolean rules aren't inferred—they're assumed or fit post-hoc. Causal validation is missing (e.g., knockout experiments).
- **Stochasticity ignored**: Real gene expression is noisy; deterministic models (both types) don't capture cell-to-cell variation.

## Open Questions
1. Does the one-step vs. multi-step accuracy gap persist with larger networks (100+ genes)? At what scale do continuous models' flexibility win out?
2. Can hybrid models—continuous base layers with Boolean decision layers—combine the best of both worlds?
3. How sensitive is the Boolean network to binarization threshold choice? A robustness analysis across threshold ranges is needed.
4. Can you infer Boolean rules directly from data rather than assume them, and does that change the comparison?
5. How do these methods scale to irregular sampling rates or missing data—common in real experiments?
6. Do the models transfer across plant species or mutant backgrounds, or are they dataset-specific?

## Key Takeaway
Continuous models and Boolean networks solve different problems: numerical prediction versus capturing true system dynamics.
