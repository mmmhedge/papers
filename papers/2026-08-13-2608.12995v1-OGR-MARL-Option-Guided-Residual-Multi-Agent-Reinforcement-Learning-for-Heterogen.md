# OGR-MARL: Option-Guided Residual Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning for Heterogeneous USV Cooperative Pursuit in Constrained Port Waterways

**Authors:** Mao Jiayang, Wang Lanfeng, Peng Zhao-Han
**Published:** 2026-08-13 | **arXiv:** [2608.12995v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12995v1)

## Summary
This paper presents OGR-MARL, a framework for controlling multiple heterogeneous unmanned surface vehicles (USVs) working together to chase a target in constrained port waterways. The key innovation is combining rule-based navigation constraints with learned corrective behaviors, allowing agents to satisfy safety and traffic rules while still optimizing for mission success — achieving a 75% capture rate in realistic port scenarios.

## Theoretical Background
Multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) — where multiple independent agents learn policies simultaneously — has traditionally struggled in constrained environments like harbors where safety rules and traffic patterns matter. Prior work in MARL (MADDPG, MAPPO, MATD3, MASAC) learns policies from scratch, which can be sample-inefficient and unsafe in real-world settings. Options framework — a hierarchical RL approach that decomposes policies into high-level action primitives — provides a way to structure learning around discrete roles or goals. The paper combines these: using options to guide multi-agent exploration and residual policy learning (where agents learn corrections on top of existing rule-based behaviors rather than learning everything from scratch) to balance constraint satisfaction with task optimization.

## Core Contributions
1. **OGR-MARL framework**: Algorithm-agnostic architecture that decouples from specific MARL backbones; instantiated with four different continuous-control MARL algorithms (MADDPG, MATD3, MAPPO, MASAC).
2. **Role-conditioned option targets**: Agents are guided by shared beliefs about evader location and role-specific option policies, reducing exploration in constrained spaces.
3. **Adaptive rule penalties**: Dynamic constraint enforcement that tightens or relaxes penalties based on mission state, balancing safety compliance with capture objectives.
4. **Residual policy learning**: Agents learn corrective actions on top of pre-programmed rule-guided behaviors, dramatically reducing sample complexity and ensuring baseline safety.
5. **Zero-shot transfer**: Policies trained on abstract port scenarios transfer to realistic QGIS/AIS-informed maps without retraining.

## Connections to Other Work
This work bridges [[hierarchical reinforcement learning]] (options framework) with [[MARL]] algorithms. It relates to curriculum learning and constrained RL by gradually exposing agents to environment complexity. The residual learning approach echoes ideas from [[imitation learning]] (learning corrections to expert policies) and [[behavioral cloning]]. The port-specific application connects to real-world robotics constraints emphasized in [[sim-to-real transfer]] literature. Unlike pure learning approaches, OGR-MARL acknowledges domain expertise (navigation rules, traffic laws) as a starting point rather than something to discover.

## Practical Applications
1. **Autonomous harbor security**: Real-world pursuit of unauthorized vessels in restricted port zones with guaranteed traffic rule compliance.
2. **Maritime search and rescue**: Coordinated multi-boat operations to intercept distressed vessels while respecting shipping lanes and collision avoidance.
3. **Port congestion management**: Autonomous USVs guide or intercept commercial vessels to optimize harbor traffic flow.
4. **Environmental monitoring**: Multi-boat teams pursuing pollution sources or invasive species in constrained waterways while respecting ecological zones.
5. **Military/coast guard operations**: Coordinated interception of illegal vessels in territorial waters with explicit compliance to engagement rules.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **Port Authority Control Platform**: SaaS offering port operators rule-compliant autonomous USV swarms for security, traffic management, and emergency response; licensed per-vessel-per-year with real-time telemetry dashboards.
2. **Maritime Autonomy-as-a-Service**: Retrofit existing USVs with OGR-MARL stack as a managed service, providing pursuit and coordination capabilities without custom development.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Scalability unclear**: Experiments show 2–4 heterogeneous USVs; behavior under 10+ agents or dynamic agent addition/removal is unstudied.
2. **Rule formalization burden**: Requires explicit encoding of port-specific navigation rules as penalty functions; no automatic rule extraction from data or natural language.
3. **Evader modeling**: Assumes evader behavior is partially observable; performance degrades if evader acts adversarially or unpredictably beyond training distribution.
4. **Computational cost**: Multi-agent policy training still likely requires significant compute; real-time inference latency on edge USV hardware not reported.
5. **Generalization limits**: Zero-shot transfer tested only between similar port geometries (Xiazhimen variants); transfer to fundamentally different harbors or shallow/narrow waterways untested.
6. **Communication assumptions**: Likely assumes reliable inter-agent communication for shared beliefs; performance under communication delays or dropouts not addressed.

## Open Questions
1. How does OGR-MARL scale to 10+ heterogeneous agents? Does coordination quality degrade?
2. Can rule penalties be learned automatically from demonstrations or regulatory documents rather than hand-crafted?
3. What happens if the evader actively models and exploits the pursuers' constraints?
4. How sensitive is the framework to errors in the shared evader belief estimate? What if sensors are noisy?
5. Can the approach handle dynamic rules (e.g., traffic patterns that change with time of day)?
6. How does performance compare to centralized planning (if communication/computation allowed) as a theoretical upper bound?
7. Can this framework transfer to fundamentally different domains (e.g., drone swarms in urban airspace, robot teams in factories)?

## Key Takeaway
Combining learned corrections with rule-based navigation lets autonomous boat teams safely chase targets in busy ports without learning from scratch.
