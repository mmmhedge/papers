# When Is Accuracy Evidence? A Unified Theory of Generalisation, Validation, and Information Fusion

**Authors:** JM Gorriz
**Published:** 2026-10-02 | **arXiv:** [2610.03465v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03465v1)

## Summary
This paper develops a unified statistical framework for understanding when model accuracy constitutes valid evidence of real-world performance. The core insight: K-fold cross-validation doesn't always provide stronger evidence as you increase K — when folds are correlated (as they often are in small, heterogeneous datasets), you get an "effective" number of folds much smaller than K, making validation less reliable. The paper replaces point-wise accuracy scores with principled upper bounds on true risk, showing when simple resubstitution with corrections can outperform K-fold CV.

## Theoretical Background
The paper builds on classical statistical learning theory — specifically [[PAC learning]] (Probably Approximately Correct bounds), [[Hoeffding inequality|Hoeffding bounds]], and [[Bernstein bounds]], which give probabilistic guarantees on how well a model trained on finite data will perform on unseen examples. K-fold cross-validation is the standard practical tool for estimating this generalization gap, but it treats fold predictions as independent, which breaks when data is heterogeneous or correlated. Recent work (CUBV — Cross Upper-Bound Validation) moved from point estimates to conservative upper bounds. This paper generalizes that via moment-generating functions and cumulant envelopes — mathematical tools that control tail behavior of random variables. The key mathematical object is a joint sub-Gaussian proxy matrix that models dependence between fold-wise gaps without requiring independence assumptions.

## Core Contributions
1. **Unified exponential framework (gamma-CUBV)**: Derives a single family of risk bounds covering Hoeffding, Bernstein, dependency-aware, PAC-Bayesian, and heterogeneous data-fusion bounds as special cases — previously treated separately.

2. **Effective fold count formula**: Shows Keff = K/[1+(K-1)ρ] where ρ is fold correlation, proving that increasing K provides diminishing statistical evidence when folds depend on each other. This is a concrete, actionable insight for practitioners.

3. **Small-sample regime characterization**: Demonstrates empirically that in low-dimensional, small-sample settings (e.g., neuroimaging data with rare disease classes), K-fold can *increase* uncertainty because minority modes are under-represented in individual folds, while corrected resubstitution remains valid and tighter.

4. **Posterior and multi-source extensions**: Generalizes to Bayesian posterior distributions over predictors and principled weighted fusion of heterogeneous data sources — weight selection via risk bound minimization, not empirical error.

## Connections to Other Work
Related to [[generalization bounds]], [[model selection]], and [[regularization]] in learning theory. Overlaps with [[domain adaptation]] and [[transfer learning]] literature on multi-source fusion, though from a validation angle rather than algorithmic. Complements [[confidence intervals]] and [[uncertainty quantification]] work, offering a decision-theoretic route from empirical CV scores to actionable risk statements. Addresses long-standing practical tension between resubstitution (biased but tight) and K-fold CV (unbiased but noisy) — previously heuristic choices.

## Practical Applications
- **Neuroimaging & rare disease diagnosis**: High-dimensional, small n (100s of patients), heterogeneous scanners/sites. Standard 5-fold CV fails; corrected resubstitution provides tighter, valid bounds.
- **Multi-site medical trials**: Fusing data from 10+ hospitals with different protocols. Weighted fusion with risk-minimized weights beats uniform averaging.
- **Imbalanced classification**: When minority class is rare (e.g., fraud, equipment failure), stratified K-fold still under-represents rare modes in each fold; explicit bounds help practitioners detect and correct.
- **Meta-learning & few-shot learning**: Episodic validation where "folds" are tasks; high correlation between task-wise gaps.
- **Regulatory ML validation**: FDA/EMA submission of clinical AI models — explicit separation of observed performance, uncertainty, dependence, and confidence helps auditors assess evidence rigor.

## Technical Limitations
- **Cumulant envelope specification**: The framework requires defining γ(λ) (the moment-generating function envelope); for complex, real-world data, learning this from data itself is an open problem. Choices may be loose or data-dependent.
- **Sub-Gaussianity assumption**: Joint proxy matrix assumes sub-Gaussian tails for fold-wise gaps. Heavy-tailed losses or outliers violate this; bounds may be vacuous.
- **Scalability to high dimensions**: Experiments use linear classifiers on Gaussian mixtures. Unclear how tight bounds become for deep nets, very high-dimensional data, or highly nonlinear decision boundaries.
- **Dependence structure learning**: Estimating fold correlation ρ requires sufficient cross-fold signal; with extremely small K or tiny datasets, ρ itself is uncertain.
- **No algorithmic guidance**: Framework validates, it doesn't improve models. If resubstitution + correction is tighter than CV, practitioners must still manually refit on full data and interpret results.
- **Computational cost**: Computing bounds for many weight/source combinations in fusion may be expensive; no complexity analysis provided.

## Open Questions
- How does gamma-CUBV perform on modern deep neural networks and vision transformers? Are bounds still tight?
- Can γ(λ) be learned or estimated from data automatically, and how much does misspecification degrade bound validity?
- How to extend beyond equicorrelation assumption (current theory) to general, realistic fold-dependence structures?
- When is resubstitution + correction provably better than K-fold CV in terms of both bias *and* variance? Exact sample-size thresholds?
- Can this framework guide active learning or sample acquisition — i.e., which additional samples reduce the gap between Keff and K most efficiently?
- How to incorporate domain-specific priors (e.g., known batch effects in neuroimaging) into the bound construction?

## Key Takeaway
K-fold validation gives weaker evidence than you think when folds are correlated; effective folds = K/(1 + (K−1)ρ).
