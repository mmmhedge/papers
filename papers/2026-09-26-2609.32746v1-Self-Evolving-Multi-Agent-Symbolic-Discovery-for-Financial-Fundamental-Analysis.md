# Self-Evolving Multi-Agent Symbolic Discovery for Financial Fundamental Analysis

**Authors:** Kelvin J. L. Koa, Filip Orestav, Shengqiong Wu, Michael J. Wooldridge, Ke-Wei Huang
**Published:** 2026-09-26 | **arXiv:** [2609.32746v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32746v1)

## Summary
This paper presents MUFASA, a multi-agent framework that discovers interpretable financial valuation equations by treating the problem as symbolic discovery with multiple competing perspectives. Unlike symbolic regression in physics (which has one "correct" answer), financial valuation is inherently ambiguous—different models can be valid simultaneously, markets change constantly, and signals are noisy. MUFASA solves this by deploying specialized agents for different valuation viewpoints, coordinating them hierarchically, and using performance memory to learn robustly despite noise.

## Theoretical Background
Symbolic regression (SR) automatically discovers mathematical equations from data—useful in physics for rediscovering known laws. However, finance presents a different challenge: there is no ground truth equation. The paper builds on multi-agent systems, which decompose complex problems across specialized agents, and adapts SR to handle non-stationary environments (markets that change over time) and multiple valid solutions. The framework incorporates ideas from meta-learning (learning how to learn) and reinforcement learning principles around learning from imperfect feedback signals.

## Core Contributions
1. **Disentangled equation discovery**: Rather than one global SR model, MUFASA deploys multiple specialized agents, each discovering equations from a different valuation perspective (e.g., earnings-based vs. cash-flow-based models). This mirrors how real financial analysts use complementary frameworks.

2. **Hierarchical meta-coordination**: A coordinator agent reasons over market context (economic regimes, volatility, etc.) to weight and blend the specialized agents' outputs dynamically—it doesn't just average them.

3. **Memory-guided learning under noise**: Instead of raw price signals, the system tracks statistical performance summaries (accuracy, stability, tail risk) across evolution iterations, enabling more robust learning when feedback is weak or noisy.

4. **Empirical validation across geographies**: Demonstrated state-of-the-art performance on valuation tasks across multiple countries versus classical methods, financial LLMs, and baseline SR approaches.

## Connections to Other Work
This work sits at the intersection of [[symbolic regression]], [[multi-agent reinforcement learning]], and quantitative finance. It differs from traditional SR by acknowledging non-stationarity and multiple valid solutions—challenges also central to [[market-microstructure]] research. The hierarchical coordination mechanism relates to hierarchical reinforcement learning concepts. Unlike purely neural approaches (e.g., [[transformers]] for financial prediction), MUFASA prioritizes human interpretability through symbolic equations—addressing a key pain point in black-box financial AI.

## Practical Applications
1. **Equity research automation**: Generate interpretable valuation models that analysts can audit and modify, reducing time spent on manual model building.
2. **Multi-strategy portfolio construction**: Deploy different valuation perspectives as separate portfolio signals, blending them via market-regime detection.
3. **Risk framework discovery**: Use MUFASA to uncover novel risk factor equations that adapt to changing market conditions.
4. **Regulatory compliance**: Produce human-readable equations that satisfy explainability requirements (e.g., GDPR, MiFID II) better than neural black boxes.
5. **Asset pricing model research**: Discover new fundamental relationships in corporate finance or macroeconomics that inform investment theses.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **FinEquation OS**: White-label SaaS platform where asset managers upload their own price/fundamental data and MUFASA discovers custom valuation models. Sold as a research tool competing with Bloomberg terminal fundamental analysis modules.

2. **Multi-Perspective Valuation Engine**: Institutional service that runs MUFASA across asset classes (equities, credit, commodities) and distributes the discovered equations and context-weighted recommendations to paying subscribers.

3. **Regulatory Explainability Middleware**: Tool that converts legacy neural-network-based valuation systems into transparent MUFASA-discovered equations for compliance teams and auditors.

5. **Academic/Enterprise Data Licensing**: Monetize the distilled learnings (evolution trajectories, context-dependent strategy weights) as structured datasets for quantitative research firms and universities.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Computational cost**: Multi-agent symbolic discovery with hierarchical coordination is likely expensive; scalability to very large feature sets or high-frequency data is unclear.

2. **Equation interpretability at scale**: As discovered equations become complex (many terms, deep hierarchies), they may lose practical interpretability despite being formally symbolic.

3. **Non-stationarity bounds**: The paper doesn't clarify how quickly the framework can adapt to regime shifts or how performance degrades during genuine structural market breaks (e.g., regulatory changes, technological disruption).

4. **Out-of-sample generalization**: Testing on multiple countries is good, but results within each country are not isolated—cross-country temporal validation would strengthen claims.

5. **Noise robustness assumptions**: The memory mechanism assumes statistical summaries (accuracy, stability, tail risk) are informative; this may fail if noise is adversarial or correlated with market regimes.

6. **Causality**: Discovered equations may reflect spurious correlations; no causal inference mechanisms are discussed.

## Open Questions
1. **Adaptability speed**: How many iterations does MUFASA need to recalibrate when entering a new market regime? Can it detect regime shifts in real time?

2. **Feature engineering burden**: How sensitive is performance to the choice of raw features fed to the agents? Does the system discover its own features, or is that a manual step?

3. **Generalization to unseen assets**: If trained on large-cap equities, can MUFASA's discovered equations transfer to small-cap or emerging-market stocks?

4. **Comparison to causal discovery**: How does MUFASA compare to modern causal inference methods (e.g., causal graphs, instrumental variables) for discovering structural relationships?

5. **Theoretical convergence**: Under what conditions is the hierarchical coordinator guaranteed to find good weightings? Is there a convergence rate?

6. **Combination with LLMs**: The paper compares against financial LLMs but doesn't integrate them—could LLMs provide useful priors or feature suggestions to the symbolic agents?

## Key Takeaway
Multi-agent symbolic discovery finds interpretable financial valuation equations by accepting multiple valid perspectives and learning robustly from noisy market feedback.
