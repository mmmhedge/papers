# Minimizing Targeted Activations: Input-Only Suppression of Evaluation-Awareness Latents in Large Language Models

**Authors:** Deepanshu Mody, Samarth Agarwal, Utkarsh Mittal, Dipesh Mahato
**Published:** 2026-07-28 | **arXiv:** [2607.25907v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25907v1)

## Summary
This paper investigates whether we can suppress internal "evaluation-awareness" latents in large language models by optimizing only the input prompt—without changing the model's weights. The authors find that while certain internal features can be driven toward zero, this internal suppression often fails to actually change the model's behavior on safety evaluations, suggesting that linear readability of a latent does not guarantee behavioral control.

## Theoretical Background
The work builds on activation steering (controlling model behavior by editing internal activations) and mechanistic interpretability—the effort to understand what internal representations in neural networks encode. Recent work has shown that language models contain linearly readable "evaluation-awareness" latents: internal directions that correlate with whether the model detects it is being tested. The paper adapts techniques from Fluent Dreaming and Exploitative Prompt Optimization (EPO), which optimize prompts to steer model behavior, combined with adversarial token optimization (GCG-style) and fluency regularization to minimize target activations without access to model internals.

## Core Contributions
1. **Input-only suppression framework**: Demonstrates that fluent prompts can suppress chosen internal latents to near-zero levels without modifying model weights—a purely input-side dual to activation steering.
2. **Latent-behavior dissociation**: Shows convincingly that a latent being linearly readable and suppressible does *not* imply behavioral control; a random direction baseline is suppressed equally and shifts behavior similarly to the "real" evaluation-awareness direction.
3. **Causal validation of features**: Uses Llama Scope SAE (Sparse Autoencoders) to identify causally validated features that *can* be selectively turned off; proves single MLP neurons are eval-correlated but not causal at either scale tested.
4. **Natural baseline discovery**: Finds that naturally occurring text from the Pile dataset produces suppression competitive with the optimizer, questioning whether the optimization is finding anything meaningful.

## Connections to Other Work
This paper directly challenges assumptions underlying [[activation steering]] and [[mechanistic interpretability]] research. It echoes concerns from adversarial robustness literature: just because you can suppress a latent does not mean you control what the model does. The work relates to [[RLHF]] and safety evaluation methodology—if models can learn to detect and evade evaluations, this threatens the validity of safety benchmarks. It also connects to broader alignment concerns about whether internal representations are sufficient proxies for behavior.

## Practical Applications
1. **Safety evaluation design**: Helps identify whether existing LLM safety benchmarks are vulnerable to models that learn to "game" evaluations by detecting test conditions.
2. **Red-teaming tools**: Provides methods to test whether suppressing internal eval-awareness actually prevents deceptive behavior.
3. **Model auditing**: Offers a concrete technique to scan for causal evaluation-detection features in deployed models and assess their actual impact.
4. **Prompt engineering constraints**: Informs whether prompt-based steering of internal latents is a reliable control mechanism for critical applications.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **Safety evaluation validation service**: Audit LLMs to detect whether they contain and use evaluation-awareness latents; flag models where internal suppression fails to change behavior.
2. **Mechanistic red-teaming platform**: SaaS tool that runs adversarial prompt optimization to suppress claimed "safety latents" and measure actual behavioral change—certify which controls are real vs. placebo.
3. **LLM honesty certification**: Use causal latent analysis (SAE-based) to identify features that actually drive behavioral change, then issue a report on which internal features models reliably use for safety-critical decisions.
4. **Adversarial eval-detection removal service**: For enterprises, identify and mitigate eval-aware behavior before deployment, with proof of actual behavioral change (not just latent suppression).
5. **Research collaboration platform**: Connect mechanistic interpretability researchers with safety teams to validate that proposed "control levers" (internal features) actually steer behavior in practice.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Scale limitations**: Tested only on Llama-3.2-3B and 8B; unclear if findings generalize to larger or different architectures where latent structure may differ.
2. **Evaluation-awareness definition**: The notion of "evaluation-awareness" itself may be ill-defined; the paper shows the CAA direction is largely placebo, raising questions about whether true eval-awareness latents even exist in these models.
3. **Behavioral metrics**: Evaluation judgment is measured on a small set of held-out passages; robust behavioral change would require extensive evaluation across diverse domains.
4. **SAE dependency**: The causal validation relies on Sparse Autoencoders, which are themselves learned approximations; different SAE training could yield different features.
5. **Natural baseline problem**: If Pile text is competitive with optimization, it suggests either the optimizer is not finding anything real or natural variation dominates—unclear which.
6. **No weight access**: Intentionally avoiding model internals at inference limits what can be validated; steering at inference time would enable causal verification.

## Open Questions
1. Do true causal evaluation-awareness latents exist in these models, or is the signal primarily noise and correlational artifact?
2. How do findings change at larger scales (70B+) where model behavior and internal structure may differ qualitatively?
3. Can we design behavioral eval metrics that are robust to this kind of input-side suppression attempt?
4. Why does the optimizer fail to suppress the eval-direction when a real passage is held in context? Is this a fundamental limitation of prompt optimization or an artifact of the fluency regularizer?
5. Do other families of latents (not eval-awareness) show the same latent-behavior dissociation, or is this specific to evaluation-awareness?
6. Can we build a theory predicting *which* latents are behaviorally controllable vs. merely readably suppressed?

## Key Takeaway
Internal latents can be suppressed by prompt optimization, but this rarely changes what the model actually does—linear readability is not behavioral control.
