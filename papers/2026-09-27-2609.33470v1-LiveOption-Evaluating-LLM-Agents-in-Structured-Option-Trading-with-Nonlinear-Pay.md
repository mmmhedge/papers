# LiveOption: Evaluating LLM Agents in Structured Option Trading with Nonlinear Payoffs

**Authors:** Haochen Luo, Yifan Li, Binh Minh An, Xiaolong Luo, Zhengzhao Lai et al.
**Published:** 2026-09-27 | **arXiv:** [2609.33470v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33470v1)

## Summary
This paper introduces LiveOption, an evaluation framework designed to test how well large language models (LLMs) perform at option trading—a financial task far more complex than simply predicting stock price direction. Unlike previous benchmarks that focus on equity trading, LiveOption measures whether LLM agents can construct multi-leg strategies, respect capital constraints, and manage the nonlinear risk profiles inherent in derivatives markets. The work reveals that current LLM agents struggle to compete with traditional baselines on realistic option trading tasks.

## Theoretical Background
Option trading differs fundamentally from equity trading because options have nonlinear payoff structures—their value and risk don't scale proportionally with underlying price moves. Traditional financial decision-making frameworks treat this as a constrained optimization problem: select actions that maximize expected returns while respecting portfolio limits and execution costs. Recent work has explored using LLMs and multi-agent systems (groups of AI agents coordinating to solve problems together) for financial tasks, but most prior evaluations have been limited to directional prediction on stocks—answering "will the price go up or down?"—which sidesteps the structural complexity of derivatives. The paper builds on earlier benchmarks in LLM financial reasoning but extends them into the richer domain of structured decision-making under realistic market constraints.

## Core Contributions
1. **LiveOption framework**: A standardized evaluation environment with reproducible execution, realistic capital and margin constraints, and standardized protocols for LLM agents to interact with the trading system.

2. **Three task suites**: Portfolio overlays (hedging existing positions), event-driven earnings trading (trading around earnings announcements), and 0DTE intraday trading (zero days to expiration—options expiring the same day).

3. **Hierarchical metric suite**: Beyond simple profit/loss, the framework evaluates action validity (did the agent propose legal trades?), decision quality (did it construct sensible strategies?), risk characteristics (how volatile and drawdown-prone was the portfolio?), and outcome-level performance (absolute returns).

4. **Empirical finding**: Demonstrates that current LLM agents fail to achieve competitive risk-adjusted returns in most scenarios, establishing a meaningful performance gap to close.

## Practical Applications
1. **Hedge fund agent development**: Firms building automated trading systems can use LiveOption to benchmark new LLM-agent architectures before deploying capital.

2. **Retail options trading platforms**: Robo-advisors offering options strategies to retail clients could test LLM agents' competence on portfolio hedging or income strategies (covered calls, collars).

3. **Risk management dashboards**: Options desks at investment banks could adopt the hierarchical metric suite to continuously evaluate whether their decision-support AI systems are staying within acceptable risk bounds during earnings season or volatile events.

4. **Compliance and model validation**: Regulatory frameworks (e.g., model risk management in banking) require rigorous validation of algorithmic systems; LiveOption provides a structured, reproducible audit trail for LLM-driven trading agents.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **LiveOption-as-a-Service**: A SaaS platform where quant teams and hedge funds submit their LLM agents and receive standardized backtest results, benchmarks against peers, and detailed breakdowns of failure modes (e.g., "your agent violates margin constraints 12% of the time").

2. **FinanceAgent IDE**: An integrated development environment for building and debugging LLM trading agents, with LiveOption's task suites as built-in test scenarios and real-time simulation feedback.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Backward-looking backtests**: LiveOption uses historical data; agents may overfit to past market regimes and fail in novel conditions (e.g., a volatility regime shift).

2. **Simplified execution model**: Real options markets have complex microstructure (queue position, time priority, partial fills). The framework may not capture how agents behave under realistic order-book dynamics.

3. **LLM reasoning opacity**: The paper measures *what* agents decide but not *why*. Failure modes (e.g., "agent ignored margin constraint because it misunderstood the prompt") are hard to diagnose without interpretability tools.

4. **Capital and leverage assumptions**: The framework assumes agents respect stated constraints, but LLMs can hallucinate or misparse numerical limits, especially under time pressure or in high-dimensional state spaces.

5. **No real execution data**: Backtesting assumes perfect market impact models. Real-world execution on illiquid options (especially 0DTE) can incur severe slippage not modeled here.

6. **Task suite coverage**: Three task suites may not represent the full spectrum of options strategies (volatility arbitrage, calendar spreads, exotic structures) or market regimes (crashes, earnings volatility spikes).

## Open Questions
1. **Can fine-tuning or specialized prompting improve performance?** Does providing agents with domain-specific instructions (e.g., "always check the Greeks—Delta, Gamma, Vega") measurably boost decision quality?

2. **How do different LLM architectures and sizes compare?** Do larger models or specialized financial models (if they exist) outperform general-purpose LLMs?

3. **What is the root cause of failures?** Is it poor strategic reasoning, inability to parse state/constraints, or lack of financial domain knowledge? Mechanistic interpretability analysis could help.

4. **Can agents learn from feedback within a session?** If an agent makes a mistake and receives corrective feedback (e.g., "that violates margin"), can it adapt in subsequent decisions?

5. **How do agents handle unprecedented market conditions?** LiveOption should test out-of-distribution scenarios (e.g., volatility 3x the historical average) to measure robustness.

6. **Is outcome-based evaluation sufficient, or do we need interpretable plans?** Should the framework require agents to *explain* their strategy in human terms before allowing execution?

## Key Takeaway
LLM agents struggle with real options trading because it demands precise handling of nonlinear risk and multi-leg strategies, not just directional guessing.
