# From Knowledge Access to Source Learning: Developing Source-Specific Competence

**Authors:** Lucheng Fu, Kejing Xia, Yiyang Wang, Yiqiao Jin, Jinjin He et al.
**Published:** 2026-10-01 | **arXiv:** [2610.02150v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02150v1)

## Summary
This paper addresses how LLM-based agents can improve their understanding of external knowledge sources through repeated interaction. Instead of treating each source access as independent, the authors propose SourceLearn—a system that progressively builds a "source model" capturing how knowledge is structured and should be applied. The method combines two learning strategies and demonstrates substantial performance gains (up to 22.6 points) across multiple benchmarks, making it highly relevant for practical agent systems that rely on persistent information sources.

## Theoretical Background
The work builds on two existing paradigms: **retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)** — techniques that let language models query external documents to ground their responses — and **agent-memory systems** that preserve knowledge from past interactions. Prior work has focused on *how* to access sources (better retrieval, organization) or *what* to remember from interactions, but hasn't treated repeated source engagement as a learning opportunity. The paper introduces the idea of a persistent **source model** — a learned representation that captures the internal structure and conventions of a particular knowledge source, similar to how humans develop domain expertise by studying a single authoritative reference.

## Core Contributions
1. **Conceptual framing**: Reframes repeated source access as an opportunity for *source learning* rather than mere repeated retrieval.
2. **Self-Directed Source Learning**: An adaptive mechanism that identifies incompletely understood aspects of a source and prioritizes revisiting those areas.
3. **Task-Guided Source Learning**: Uses failures on downstream tasks to reveal gaps in how source knowledge is organized or applied.
4. **Empirical validation**: Demonstrates consistent improvements (13/15 settings) across five benchmarks and three LLM backends, with the persistent source model outperforming static representations and memory-only baselines.

## Connections to Other Work
SourceLearn extends [[Retrieval-Augmented Generation]] by moving beyond one-shot retrieval to iterative refinement. It relates to work on [[agent memory systems]] and [[in-context learning]], but differs by building structured, reusable source-specific representations rather than just appending experiences. The adaptive revisiting aspect connects to [[active learning]] and curriculum design. The combination of task signals and self-directed signals parallels ideas in [[reinforcement learning]] where agents balance exploration and exploitation.

## Practical Applications
- **Enterprise Q&A systems**: Companies can deploy agents that improve their understanding of internal documentation, policies, or technical specs over time without manual annotation.
- **Customer support**: Support bots that learn the structure of a company's FAQ or knowledge base, improving answer quality and consistency.
- **Research assistants**: Tools that become progressively more effective at mining domain-specific literature (medical papers, legal databases) by building source models.
- **Multi-document reasoning**: Systems that synthesize information across persistent sources (e.g., government databases, scientific repositories) with increasing coherence.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **Enterprise Knowledge Agent**: SaaS platform where teams upload private documents (internal wikis, policies, historical emails) and deploy an agent that learns and improves its understanding automatically—with audit logs showing what it learned.
2. **Domain-Specific AI Consultant**: Specialized agents for legal, medical, or financial firms that continuously refine understanding of regulation databases, case law, or market data feeds—sold as managed services.
3. **API for Progressive Source Learning**: Provide SourceLearn as a microservice that companies integrate into their RAG pipelines to avoid paying for repeated document re-indexing and retrieval.
4. **Personalized Learning Tutor**: Educational platform where a tutor agent learns the structure of a textbook or curriculum over multiple student interactions, adapting explanations based on recurring confusion points.
5. **Data Warehouse Intelligence**: Tool for data analysts where an AI assistant learns the schema, relationships, and query patterns of a company's database, improving query suggestion and data exploration.

## Technical Limitations
- **Dependency on authoritative source**: The method assumes a single, stable, ground-truth source; it breaks down if the source is inconsistent, frequently updated, or of varying quality.
- **Computational overhead**: Building and updating persistent source models incurs storage and inference costs that may not be justified for small or one-off sources.
- **Generalization across domains**: The paper doesn't clearly address whether a source model learned on one task transfers to unrelated tasks using the same source, limiting modularity.
- **Source quality assumption**: If the source contains errors or gaps, the agent's model will encode those limitations; there's no mechanism for error correction.
- **Limited to text**: The approach is demonstrated only on text-based sources; applicability to structured databases, APIs, or multimodal sources is unexplored.

## Open Questions
- How does SourceLearn perform when the source is partially updated or inconsistent?
- Can source models transfer across related but distinct downstream tasks without retraining?
- What is the trade-off between source model sophistication and computational cost? Is there a sweet spot?
- How does this approach scale to very large sources (e.g., the entire web or billion-token corpora)?
- Can the framework be extended to learn from *multiple* sources with different conventions, or handling conflicting information?

## Key Takeaway
LLM agents can progressively learn a source's structure through repeated interaction, improving task performance without manual annotation.
