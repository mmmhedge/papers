# OrderFusion+: Probabilistic Buy--Sell Price Trajectory Forecasting in Intraday Electricity Markets

**Authors:** Runyao Yu, Derek W. Bunn
**Published:** 2026-09-20 | **arXiv:** [2609.23598v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23598v1)

## Summary
This paper introduces OrderFusion+, a deep learning model that forecasts probabilistic buy and sell price trajectories in intraday electricity markets by combining order book data from multiple delivery products. It improves upon the prior OrderFusion model by incorporating neighboring-product information and handling price trajectories rather than single-product snapshots, advancing the practical accuracy of trading decisions in real-time electricity markets.

## Theoretical Background
Intraday electricity markets operate very close to physical delivery, allowing traders to hedge positions or adjust schedules with minimal lead time. Price forecasting in these markets has evolved from using macro-level features (renewable generation, load demand) to micro-level features—specifically, the order book structure showing bids and asks at different price levels. The OrderFusion model, a prior deep learning architecture, explicitly models the interaction between buy and sell orders to predict prices. Time-series foundation models (pretrained deep networks) have recently shown promise in financial and energy forecasting by learning general temporal patterns that transfer across datasets. This paper extends OrderFusion by leveraging cross-product correlations and trajectory modeling, while also benchmarking against foundation models adapted to order book data.

## Core Contributions
1. **OrderFusion+**: Extends the original OrderFusion model to incorporate historical orders from neighboring delivery products (e.g., combining ID3, ID2, and ID1 contracts), improving signal by capturing cross-product dependencies.
2. **Probabilistic trajectory forecasting**: Generates full buy-sell price trajectories rather than point estimates, enabling risk-aware trading decisions.
3. **Dynamic masking mechanism**: Introduces a technique to probe market efficiency under different conditions, revealing how price dynamics shift with market stress or liquidity changes.
4. **Foundation model benchmarking**: Systematically evaluates pretrained time-series models on intraday order book data—a previously under-explored question with significant practical value.
5. **Open-source implementation and datasets**: Provides reproducible code and forecasts for the German ID products (liquid, standardized intraday contracts).

## Connections to Other Work
OrderFusion+ builds directly on [[OrderFusion]], which pioneered micro-level order book modeling for electricity price forecasting. It relates to broader work on [[Attention Is All You Need]]-inspired architectures (transformers) adapted to financial time series, as well as recent advances in pretrained foundation models for temporal data (e.g., TimeGPT, PatchTST). The dynamic masking mechanism echoes interpretability techniques from [[mechanistic-interpretability]] applied to understand when and why models succeed. The cross-product fusion aspect connects to multi-task learning and the use of side information in forecasting (e.g., auxiliary features improving energy price models).

## Practical Applications
1. **Algorithmic trading**: Real-time buy/sell decisions in intraday electricity auctions, hedging positions as grid conditions evolve.
2. **Flexible resource scheduling**: Data centers, battery storage, and demand-response operators use probabilistic forecasts to schedule load or generation with confidence bands.
3. **Risk management**: Utilities and traders quantify downside price risk and adjust hedge ratios using full trajectory distributions rather than point forecasts.
4. **Market surveillance**: Regulators monitor order book dynamics and efficiency metrics (via the dynamic masking insights) to detect manipulation or system stress.
5. **Consumer demand response**: Aggregators pricing dynamic electricity tariffs to incentivize load shifting can use these forecasts to optimize customer notifications.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Single market focus**: The paper benchmarks on German ID products; generalization to other intraday markets (US, UK, other EU zones) with different liquidity and order book structures is unexplored.
2. **Historical dependence**: Assumes order book microstructure patterns persist; may break during systemic shocks (e.g., extreme weather, geopolitical events) when market regimes shift rapidly.
3. **Computational overhead**: Multi-product order book ingestion and trajectory generation incur latency; unclear if model inference fits sub-second trading constraints in highly competitive venues.
4. **Forecast horizon unclear**: The paper does not specify lead times (seconds to minutes?) over which probabilistic forecasts remain reliable; practical traders need this clarity.
5. **Foundation model overhead**: Pretrained models may overfit to recent data or require frequent retraining; the paper doesn't clarify update cadence or drift mitigation.
6. **Missing ablations**: No clear breakdown of how much improvement comes from neighboring-product information vs. trajectory modeling vs. dynamic masking—makes it hard to understand which components practitioners should prioritize.

## Open Questions
1. How do probabilistic forecasts degrade across longer time horizons (seconds → minutes → hours)? Where does trajectory accuracy become too poor for trading?
2. Do the dynamic masking insights transfer to other electricity markets, or are efficiency patterns market-specific?
3. Can OrderFusion+ adapt to regime changes (e.g., post-outage supply shocks)? What retraining frequency is needed?
4. How sensitive is the model to order book data quality, partial fills, and latency jitter in real trading systems?
5. Would reinforcement learning agents trained on OrderFusion+ forecasts outperform rule-based or traditional optimal execution strategies?
6. Can the model be extended to longer-horizon (day-ahead) markets, or does its strength lie only in ultra-short-term micro-structure?

## Key Takeaway
Deep learning on order book microstructure and neighboring products improves electricity price forecasting for real-time trading and resource scheduling.
