# FactorBench: A Portfolio-Aware Benchmark for Automated Factor Mining

**Authors:** Zhuohan Wang, Carmine Ventre
**Published:** 2026-10-03 | **arXiv:** [2610.06947v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06947v1)

## Summary
FactorBench is a standardized benchmark for comparing automated factor mining methods across five equity markets, evaluating roughly five thousand factors discovered by nine different algorithms (including genetic programming, reinforcement learning, generative models, and large language models). The paper shows that no single paradigm consistently produces better trading signals or portfolio returns, revealing that recent machine learning advances haven't clearly translated into economically useful financial discoveries.

## Theoretical Background
Factor mining builds on decades of empirical asset pricing research, which seeks to identify predictive signals (factors) that explain future stock returns beyond market risk. Traditional approaches like Fama–French factors rely on economic intuition; modern automated approaches use machine learning to discover factors without prior assumptions. Recent work has applied genetic programming (evolving symbolic expressions), reinforcement learning (learning discovery policies), generative models (sampling new signal structures), and large language model agents (using natural language to guide search). The core tension is whether algorithmic sophistication actually improves real-world trading performance or merely fits historical data.

## Core Contributions
1. **Unified benchmark infrastructure**: A portfolio-aware evaluation framework with standardized data contracts supporting both symbolic expressions and executable Python factors, bridging disparate discovery paradigms.
2. **Systematic multi-level assessment**: Traces factor quality across three dimensions—validity (statistical significance and temporal stability), distinctness (novelty relative to other factors and benchmark Alpha101), and economic utility (portfolio returns after transaction costs).
3. **Empirical finding on paradigm equivalence**: Demonstrates that genetic programming, reinforcement learning, generative models, and LLM agents produce comparable signal quality and portfolio performance—no clear winner despite methodological diversity.
4. **Large-scale comparative dataset**: 5,000+ mined factors across 5 markets from 9 methods, enabling future research on factor discovery.

## Practical Applications
1. **Factor selection for asset managers**: Practitioners can use FactorBench to select discovery methods matching their risk tolerance and infrastructure constraints, rather than assuming cutting-edge algorithms are superior.
2. **Portfolio construction optimization**: Banks and hedge funds can benchmark their internal factor pipelines against FactorBench to identify performance gaps.
3. **Retail algorithmic trading platforms**: Fintech apps offering AI-driven factor discovery can validate that their method produces economically meaningful signals.
4. **Regulatory stress testing**: Financial regulators can use the benchmark to assess systemic risks from correlated AI-generated factors across institutions.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **Factor-as-a-Service API**: A cloud platform offering multiple automated factor mining methods (genetic programming, RL, LLM agents) with FactorBench-style evaluation, letting quants rent compute rather than build discovery systems.
2. **Factor marketplace and certification**: A platform where quants publish discovered factors, ranked by FactorBench metrics and certified for out-of-sample performance, creating a reusable signal library.
3. **Automated factor audit tool**: Enterprise software that compares a fund's internal factors against FactorBench to flag overcorrelation, style drift, or known factors (reducing redundancy).
4. **Hybrid factor discovery engine**: A SaaS combining all nine methods into an ensemble, leveraging FactorBench's finding that no single paradigm dominates by pooling diversity.
5. **Factor sustainability and backtesting as code**: Developer-friendly CLI/SDK allowing quants to submit factor definitions (Python or symbolic) and get FactorBench-style validation across markets and time periods.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Historical data only**: All evaluation is on past returns; no guarantee discovered factors persist as market regimes shift or become crowded with copycats.
2. **Survivorship bias**: Factors evaluated only on surviving stocks; delisted firms excluded, inflating apparent returns.
3. **Transaction cost simplification**: Assumes fixed bid-ask spreads and linear market impact; real large-scale deployment faces nonlinear slippage and market-impact costs.
4. **Limited factor universe**: Benchmark restricted to equities in five markets; generalization to bonds, FX, or crypto unclear.
5. **Hyperparameter fairness**: Different mining algorithms may have been tuned to different intensities; a method might underperform due to suboptimal search configuration rather than inherent weakness.
6. **Economic vs. statistical significance**: Factors may pass statistical tests but have return differences too small to exploit after taxes and fees.

## Open Questions
1. **Why does paradigm diversity not translate to dominance?** Are all methods converging on the same optimal factors, or are discovered factors simply noisy and unstable?
2. **Out-of-sample generalization**: How do these factors perform on truly held-out future data or new markets not in FactorBench?
3. **Ensemble methods**: Could combining factors from multiple paradigms (via FactorBench's pool) outperform any single method?
4. **Interpretability and explainability**: Are LLM-discovered factors more explainable to risk committees, or do they suffer the same black-box problem as neural network factors?
5. **Computational efficiency**: Which method achieves best signal quality per unit of compute, enabling cost-aware factor mining?
6. **Dynamic factor decay**: Do discovered factors have predictable half-lives? Can mining methods explicitly optimize for longevity?

## Key Takeaway
No automated factor-mining paradigm consistently beats others—genetic algorithms, RL, and LLMs produce similarly useful trading signals.
