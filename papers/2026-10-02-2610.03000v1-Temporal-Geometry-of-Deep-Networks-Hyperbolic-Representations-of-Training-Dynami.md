# Temporal Geometry of Deep Networks: Hyperbolic Representations of Training Dynamics for Intrinsic Explainability

**Authors:** Ambarish Moharil
**Published:** 2026-10-02 | **arXiv:** [2610.03000v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03000v1)

## Summary
This paper proposes a novel approach to understanding neural network behavior by representing training dynamics in hyperbolic geometry — a curved, non-Euclidean space where distances expand exponentially. Instead of analyzing networks at single snapshots, the authors track how network weights evolve over the entire training trajectory and embed these temporal "parameter graphs" into the Poincaré ball (a hyperbolic model). This reveals how networks self-organize geometrically during learning, offering intrinsic explainability without post-hoc interpretation methods.

## Theoretical Background
The paper builds on several foundational ideas. **Hyperbolic geometry** has emerged as a natural space for representing hierarchical and tree-like structures because distances grow exponentially — making it efficient for embedding graphs with power-law degree distributions. Recent work shows many real-world networks (social networks, knowledge graphs) embed naturally in hyperbolic spaces.

The explainability challenge addressed here recognizes that traditional **mechanistic interpretability** methods often examine networks at fixed checkpoints (e.g., "what does this neuron do?"), missing the dynamic process of learning. The authors argue that information is encoded not only in weights but in the optimization *trajectory* itself — a temporal, geometric perspective.

The framework draws on **graph neural networks** and meta-learning ideas: they design a learnable model that reads temporal parameter graphs and produces hyperbolic embeddings while maintaining permutation equivariance (the output shouldn't change if neurons are reordered within a snapshot) and invariance to the ordering of past snapshots.

## Core Contributions
1. **Temporal parameter graphs**: A novel representation of neural networks as sequences of snapshots across training time, not isolated checkpoints.
2. **Hyperbolic embedding framework**: A method to embed these temporal parameter graphs into the Poincaré ball, leveraging hyperbolic geometry's natural fit for hierarchical structures.
3. **Equivariance-preserving metalearning**: A graph-based meta-network that learns from temporal parameter graphs while respecting neuron permutations within snapshots and snapshot order invariance, preserving functional equivalence.
4. **Empirical validation**: Demonstrations on regression and classification tasks showing the approach recovers interpretable geometric structure reflecting how networks self-organize during training.

## Connections to Other Work
This work sits at the intersection of several research threads:
- **[[Mechanistic interpretability]]**: Complements existing approaches (probing, activation analysis) by offering a geometric, dynamic lens instead of static feature importance.
- **[[Graph neural networks]]**: Uses GNN-like architectures to process temporal parameter graphs with symmetry constraints.
- **Hyperbolic embeddings**: Builds on recent work (e.g., Poincaré embeddings for hierarchies) applied here to *neural network structure* rather than external data.
- **Neural network geometry**: Relates to loss landscape visualization and mode connectivity studies, but focuses on parameter space structure over time.
- **Meta-learning**: Frames understanding network training as a meta-learning problem — learning to represent training dynamics.

## Practical Applications
1. **Network compression and pruning**: Hyperbolic representations of training geometry could identify which neurons/connections emerge early vs. late, guiding targeted pruning.
2. **Transfer learning**: Understanding how geometry evolves during pretraining could inform which training stages transfer best to downstream tasks.
3. **Early stopping and curriculum design**: Temporal geometry might reveal optimal training phases, enabling adaptive curricula that exploit natural geometric transitions.
4. **Adversarial robustness analysis**: Temporal geometric structure could reveal vulnerability emergence during training — attacking when/where networks become brittle.
5. **Model distillation**: Capturing the essential geometric trajectory could enable efficient student model training by replaying key geometric phases.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Scalability**: Storing and processing temporal parameter graphs for large networks (LLMs, vision models) is computationally expensive — unclear how method scales beyond small MLPs.
2. **Hyperbolic geometry assumption**: The paper doesn't rigorously justify *why* training dynamics should follow hyperbolic geometry. This may be a good empirical fit for some tasks but not universal.
3. **Equivariance constraints**: While permutation equivariance is theoretically clean, it may discard information (e.g., which neurons correspond across layers). The invariance to snapshot order also may lose temporal ordering information critical to understanding learning.
4. **Limited task diversity**: Experiments shown only on regression/classification tasks; unclear whether insights transfer to modern settings (vision transformers, large language models, reinforcement learning).
5. **Interpretability claims**: "Explainability" here means recovering geometric structure, not necessarily human interpretability. It's unclear whether hyperbolic representations actually make networks more understandable to practitioners.
6. **Comparison baseline**: No quantitative comparison to existing explainability methods (LIME, SHAP, activation analysis, etc.), making it hard to assess practical value.

## Open Questions
1. **Why hyperbolic?** Rigorous theoretical or empirical analysis: which task/architecture properties necessitate hyperbolic vs. Euclidean embeddings? When does the assumption break?
2. **Scaling**: Can the framework handle modern large networks (transformers, ResNets)? What are computational bottlenecks?
3. **Downstream utility**: Can temporal geometric insights improve real tasks (better pruning, faster training, better transfer)? Quantitative benchmarks needed.
4. **Generalization**: Do temporal geometries transfer across tasks? Can a network trained on one task's geometry transfer to another?
5. **Dynamics of different architectures**: How do CNNs, RNNs, Transformers differ in their temporal geometric evolution? Does architecture determine geometry?
6. **Connection to loss landscape**: How does temporal hyperbolic geometry relate to loss landscape structure, mode connectivity, and generalization bounds?

## Key Takeaway
Networks encode learning behavior geometrically over time; hyperbolic embeddings of training trajectories reveal this structure without post-hoc interpretation.
