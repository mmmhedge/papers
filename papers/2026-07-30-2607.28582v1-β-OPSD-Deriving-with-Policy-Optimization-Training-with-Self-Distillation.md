# $β$-OPSD: Deriving with Policy Optimization, Training with Self-Distillation

**Authors:** Jiawei Xu, Minghui Liu, Juzheng Zhang, Tom Goldstein, Furong Huang
**Published:** 2026-07-30 | **arXiv:** [2607.28582v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28582v1)

## Summary
This paper introduces β-OPSD, a method that unifies self-distillation and policy optimization for training reasoning language models. The key insight is that standard on-policy self-distillation (OPSD) is a special case of a broader family controlled by a regularization parameter β, which can be tuned to balance fidelity to a reference policy against learning from a teacher model. The authors show how to implement this family efficiently via logit mixing while maintaining the computational simplicity that makes distillation practical.

## Theoretical Background
On-policy self-distillation (OPSD) is a technique for improving language models by having them learn from their own generations; it's related to approaches like [[RLHF]] (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback) but avoids expensive RL by treating teacher outputs as supervised targets. The paper builds on the classical insight that KL divergence penalties in machine learning act as regularizers anchoring learning to a reference distribution. The authors connect this to policy optimization — the framework underlying reinforcement learning where an agent learns a probability distribution (policy) over actions to maximize cumulative reward. Their contribution is recognizing that OPSD with a KL penalty is mathematically equivalent to a policy optimization problem with a specific choice of regularization weight, and that varying this weight traces a geometric path through policy space.

## Core Contributions
1. **Structural unification**: Derives that vanilla OPSD (β=1) is a member of a broader policy-optimization family parameterized by β, converting an implicit hyperparameter into an explicit regularization control.
2. **Closed-form solution**: Shows the optimal policy for any β is a geometric interpolation between the reference policy and privileged teacher, given by mixing their token-level logits.
3. **Efficient implementation**: Rather than solving the RL objective directly (expensive and high-variance), treats the closed-form solution as a distillation target, enabling efficient training via logit mixing.
4. **Credit assignment refinement**: Incorporates return-to-go (a sequence-level signal) to better align token-level updates with the full reasoning objective while preserving computational simplicity.
5. **Empirical validation**: Demonstrates consistent improvements over vanilla OPSD on mathematical reasoning benchmarks, with better stability and performance.

## Connections to Other Work
This work sits at the intersection of [[Self-Distillation]], [[Policy Optimization]], and [[KL-Regularized RL]]. It relates to recent work on improving reasoning in language models (e.g., process reward models, outcome reward optimization) and connects to broader themes in [[Alignment]] around training models to behave in desired ways. The logit-mixing approach echoes ensemble and interpolation methods in deep learning, while the geometric interpretation of policy space connects to optimal transport and Wasserstein geometry. The use of return-to-go credit assignment is inspired by recent work in RL on better credit assignment for long-horizon tasks.

## Practical Applications
1. **Mathematical reasoning systems**: Training models that reliably solve competition-level math problems, where reasoning steps matter more than final answers.
2. **Code generation and synthesis**: Improving code quality by self-distillation from better-reasoning variants without expensive RL fine-tuning.
3. **Automated theorem proving**: Strengthening proof-search models that need to balance fidelity to base training with guidance from stronger oracles.
4. **Question answering with chain-of-thought**: Enhancing reasoning-heavy QA systems that benefit from balancing memorization against step-level guidance.
5. **Tuning production models**: A practical middle ground between supervised fine-tuning and full policy optimization for improving deployed models.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **β-tuning-as-a-service**: A SaaS offering that automatically tunes β for clients' language models on their own data, treating β selection as a hyperparameter optimization problem with benchmarks on math/code tasks.
2. **Reasoning model marketplace**: A platform selling pre-trained reasoning models (math, code, logic) trained via β-OPSD, with variable β versions available (conservative to aggressive).
3. **Distillation middleware**: A tool that sits between large teacher models and smaller student models, automatically configuring β to trade off deployment cost against quality for specific use cases.
4. **Alignment verification toolkit**: Software that uses β-OPSD to generate multiple interpolation points along the reference-to-teacher path, helping companies audit model behavior across safety-performance tradeoffs.
5. **Enterprise reasoning RL platform**: An end-to-end system for companies to distill internal domain experts' reasoning into smaller models via β-OPSD without requiring RL expertise.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Limited to reasoning tasks**: Evaluation focuses on mathematical reasoning; generalization to other domains (NLU, generation, dialogue) is unclear.
2. **Logit mixing assumes alignment**: The approach assumes teacher and reference model logits are meaningfully comparable; may struggle if their vocabularies or training objectives differ significantly.
3. **β as a free parameter**: While β is now explicit, the paper doesn't provide principled guidelines for choosing it; practitioners may still need manual tuning or cross-validation.
4. **Teacher quality dependency**: The method still depends on having a strong privileged teacher; performance ceilings are inherited from teacher quality.
5. **Scalability to longer sequences**: No analysis of how logit mixing scales with very long contexts or whether credit assignment via return-to-go remains effective at extreme scales.
6. **Closed-form assumption**: The derivation assumes the closed-form solution is well-defined; robustness to cases where interpolation is less natural is unexplored.

## Open Questions
1. **How to select β principally?**: Can we derive an objective or heuristic to automatically choose β based on reference-teacher distance or task properties, rather than manual tuning?
2. **Does β-OPSD work beyond reasoning?**: How does the method perform on NLU, summarization, dialogue, or other non-reasoning tasks where step-level credit may be less meaningful?
3. **Multi-teacher interpolation**: Can the geometric interpolation idea extend to multiple teachers, and would a mixture of teachers along different dimensions of the β path be beneficial?
4. **Token vs. sequence credit assignment trade-off**: How do different return-to-go designs (or other credit assignment schemes) interact with β? Is there a synergy or a tension?
5. **Theoretical convergence**: What convergence guarantees exist for β-OPSD? Does the logit-mixing approximation incur a cost in the limit, and can we quantify it?
6. **Connection to optimal transport**: Is there a deeper connection to optimal transport or Wasserstein metrics that could inform the geometry of the reference-to-teacher interpolation?

## Key Takeaway
β-OPSD unifies self-distillation and policy optimization, enabling practical control over the reference-to-teacher trade-off via efficient logit mixing.
