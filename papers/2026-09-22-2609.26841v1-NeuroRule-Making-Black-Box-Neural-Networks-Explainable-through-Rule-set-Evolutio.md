# NeuroRule: Making Black-Box Neural Networks Explainable through Rule-set Evolution

**Authors:** Tapaswini Kodavanti, Hormoz Shahrzad, Risto Miikkulainen
**Published:** 2026-09-22 | **arXiv:** [2609.26841v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26841v1)

## Summary
This paper proposes NeuroRule, a method to convert opaque neural networks into human-readable rule-sets using evolutionary algorithms. The key insight: you can distill a black-box model's decision logic into simple logical rules without needing the original training data, making neural networks trustworthy enough for high-stakes decisions like healthcare or finance.

## Theoretical Background
The paper builds on two main threads. First, **knowledge distillation** — the practice of training a simpler "student" model to mimic a complex "teacher" model — has long been used to compress neural networks for efficiency. Second, **rule extraction** or **symbolic AI** seeks to recover human-interpretable logical rules from neural networks, a problem that dates back decades but remains unsolved in a practical sense. NeuroRule adapts EVOTER, an existing evolutionary algorithm framework for generating rule-sets, to work backwards: instead of evolving rules to fit data, it evolves rules to match a neural network's predictions. The paper frames this as a black-box optimization problem where the fitness function is simply "how well do these rules agree with the neural network?"

## Core Contributions
1. **An evolutionary distillation pipeline**: A concrete algorithm that treats neural network outputs as a target function for evolution, producing rule-sets without access to training data or network internals.
2. **Conciseness-aware evolution**: A multi-objective approach that doesn't just maximize accuracy but also minimizes rule complexity, trading off fidelity for human readability.
3. **Data-free distillation**: Empirical validation that rule extraction works even when the original training data is unavailable — a significant practical advantage in privacy-sensitive domains.

## Connections to Other Work
This bridges [[mechanistic-interpretability]] (understanding how neural networks work) and [[knowledge distillation]] (compressing models). Related work includes LIME and SHAP (local post-hoc explanations), concept-bottleneck models, and neural-symbolic integration efforts. Unlike attention visualization or gradient-based saliency methods, NeuroRule produces *globally applicable* rules rather than instance-specific explanations. It complements [[RLHF]] and other alignment techniques by offering a path to interpretability rather than just reward modeling.

## Practical Applications
- **Healthcare diagnostics**: Convert a trained cancer detection model into a rule-set doctors can verify and audit ("if PSA > X and biopsy shows Y, predict malignancy").
- **Loan approval systems**: Regulatory bodies (e.g., Fair Lending Act) demand explainability; NeuroRule lets banks deploy neural networks while providing rule-based justifications.
- **Autonomous vehicle safety**: Convert learned driving policies into explicit decision rules for regulatory approval.
- **Fraud detection**: Banks can maintain neural network performance while producing rules auditors can validate.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **Regulatory Compliance Platform**: SaaS tool that automatically converts proprietary ML models to explainable rule-sets for financial services, healthcare, and insurance — targeting auditors and compliance officers.
2. **Model Transparency API**: White-label service where enterprises upload black-box models and receive downloadable rule-sets, marketed to enterprises facing regulatory pressure (GDPR, SEC, FDA).
3. **Risk-Aware ML Debugger**: IDE plugin or dashboard that lets ML teams evolve rule-sets from models to stress-test decision logic and find edge cases (competitor to integrated model cards).
4. **Trustworthy AI Certification Service**: Consultancy that distills models to rules and certifies them as "human-verifiable," targeting procurement teams in regulated industries.
5. **Hybrid Decision Engine**: Commercial tool that runs both the neural network and its evolved rule-set in parallel, alerting when they diverge — useful for drift detection and model monitoring.

## Technical Limitations
- **Fidelity vs. simplicity trade-off**: Concise rule-sets may miss nuanced decision boundaries, especially in high-dimensional spaces where the neural network's implicit features don't map cleanly to interpretable logic.
- **Scalability**: Evolutionary algorithms are computationally expensive; unclear how NeuroRule scales to very large networks or high-dimensional input spaces.
- **Rule quality depends on representation**: Propositional logic has limited expressiveness; rules may be inadequate for data where decisions require spatial or temporal reasoning.
- **No guarantee of global optimality**: Evolutionary search can get stuck in local optima; the resulting rules may not truly capture the neural network's logic.
- **Assumes neural network is "correct"**: If the original model is biased or makes poor decisions, the distilled rule-set inherits those flaws.

## Open Questions
- How does rule fidelity degrade with network size and input dimensionality? Is there a fundamental limit?
- Can the method extend beyond propositional logic to more expressive rule languages (e.g., first-order logic, decision trees)?
- How sensitive is the evolved rule-set to hyperparameters of the evolution process?
- Can you evolve rules that not only match the network but *improve* upon it (e.g., by removing adversarial vulnerabilities)?
- Does the method work equally well for different architectures (RNNs, vision transformers, etc.)?

## Key Takeaway
Neural networks can be converted into simple, auditable rule-sets without original training data, enabling trustworthy AI in regulated industries.
