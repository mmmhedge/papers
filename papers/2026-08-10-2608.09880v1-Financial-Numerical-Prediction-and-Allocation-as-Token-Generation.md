# Financial Numerical Prediction and Allocation as Token Generation

**Authors:** Xu Ouyang, Moontae Lee
**Published:** 2026-08-10 | **arXiv:** [2608.09880v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09880v1)

## Summary
This paper shows that a causal language model can directly generate financial forecasts and investment decisions as constrained tokens, rather than using separate task-specific prediction heads. FinATOM unifies stock-return forecasting and ETF portfolio allocation through a single generative interface, achieving better risk-adjusted returns (Sharpe ratio improvements of 7–10%) than traditional approaches on 2023–2025 test data.

## Theoretical Background
The work builds on two main ideas: (1) causal language models that predict sequences token-by-token using self-attention — the same architecture underlying LLMs like GPT; and (2) recent findings that these models can be adapted for numerical tasks beyond text. In finance, portfolio optimization traditionally uses mean–variance frameworks (Markowitz theory) and policy-gradient reinforcement learning to learn trading decisions. The paper combines these: it treats financial outputs as a constrained token generation problem and uses supervised fine-tuning plus reinforcement learning (specifically GRPO — group relative policy optimization) to align model outputs with realized returns.

## Core Contributions
- **Unified token interface**: A single generative model handles both forecasting (predicting returns) and allocation (choosing portfolio weights) without separate regression or classification heads.
- **Volatility-standardized tokens**: Returns are discretized into tokens weighted by volatility, making the generation task more stable.
- **Two-stage training**: Ordinal/ranking supervision on historical returns, followed by one-epoch reinforcement learning directly optimizing 21-day Sharpe ratio.
- **Multimodal fusion for allocation**: The allocation model accepts both time-series and text-based market features, achieving higher risk-adjusted returns (Sharpe 1.540) than unimodal versions.
- **Empirical improvements**: Gross Sharpe rises from 1.428 to 1.529; net Sharpe (accounting for 5-basis-point transaction costs) rises from 1.394 to 1.494 on live 2023–2025 ETF data.

## Connections to Other Work
This research sits at the intersection of [[large-language-models|LLM]] generalization, [[reinforcement-learning|policy optimization]], and quantitative finance. It extends the recent trend of using transformers for numerical prediction (e.g., TimeGPT, Chronos) and relates to [[RLHF|reinforcement learning from human feedback]] in spirit—though here the "reward" is financial (Sharpe ratio) rather than human preference. The allocation problem mirrors portfolio optimization in [[risk-management]] and connects to [[algorithmic-trading]] systems. The ordinal loss and ranking supervision echo ideas from learning-to-rank, common in information retrieval.

## Practical Applications
- **Systematic hedge funds**: Automated portfolio rebalancing with real-time return forecasts and dynamic weight allocation, reducing latency and improving consistency.
- **Robo-advisors**: Direct integration into retail wealth-management platforms to generate client portfolio recommendations that adapt to market regimes.
- **Index tracking and smart-beta funds**: Dynamic ETF weighting that outperforms passive allocation while remaining interpretable to regulators and compliance teams.
- **Market-neutral strategies**: Pair trading or statistical arbitrage where the model generates relative forecasts for long–short positioning.
- **Risk monitoring**: Volatility-standardized token emission enables early detection of regime shifts and tail-risk scenarios.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **FinLLM Studio**: A SaaS platform letting quant teams train and deploy their own financial language models on proprietary data, with built-in backtesting and live-paper trading.
2. **Allocation-as-a-Service (AaaS)**: A B2B API that accepts market data and returns optimal ETF/asset weights updated daily, licensed to wealth managers and robo-advisors.
3. **Multimodal Market Intelligence**: A platform fusing price/volatility tokens with news sentiment and economic text to generate enhanced allocation signals for institutional clients.
4. **Retail Trading Copilot**: A mobile app that generates stock/ETF forecasts and personalized portfolio rebalancing advice via a fine-tuned financial LLM, competing with traditional robo-advisors on UX.
5. **Enterprise Risk Dashboard**: Real-time Sharpe optimization and volatility forecasting for corporate treasury and pension funds, deployed on-premise with explainable token-level decision logs.

## Technical Limitations
- **Small asset universe**: Tests limited to five ETFs; scalability to thousands of stocks or global asset classes is unproven.
- **Historical regime bias**: The model is trained and tested on 2020–2025 data (mostly low-rate, high-liquidity environment). Performance in rate-shock or liquidity-crunch regimes is unknown.
- **Transaction costs**: 5-bp cost model is simplistic; slippage, market impact, and execution risk are not explicitly modeled.
- **Causality and lookahead bias**: The paper does not deeply explore whether learned patterns reflect genuine predictive features or artifacts of train–test splits. Cross-validation and out-of-sample stability are not emphasized.
- **Interpretability**: Token generation is inherently opaque; understanding *why* the model forecasts a given return or chooses a weight is difficult, limiting regulatory and client acceptance.
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity**: The Sharpe improvements are modest (7–10%); sensitivity to learning rates, token discretization, and GRPO parameters is not thoroughly ablated.

## Open Questions
- How does the model degrade on out-of-distribution market regimes (e.g., 2008-style crisis, 1970s stagflation) not seen in training?
- Can the token interface scale to hundreds or thousands of assets without exponential token-space growth?
- What is the causal structure of the learned forecasts? Do ablations reveal which input features drive predictions?
- How much of the Sharpe gain comes from the model's flexibility vs. overfitting to the specific 2023–2025 test window?
- Can the approach handle intraday trading frequencies, or is it inherently limited to daily/weekly rebalancing?
- How stable are the learned weights across different random seeds and modest changes to hyperparameters?

## Key Takeaway
Language models can generate financial forecasts and portfolio weights as tokens, achieving better risk-adjusted returns than traditional methods without task-specific heads.
