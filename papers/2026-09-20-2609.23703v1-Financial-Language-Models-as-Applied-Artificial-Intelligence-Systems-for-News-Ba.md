# Financial Language Models as Applied Artificial Intelligence Systems for News-Based Trading under Market Frictions

**Authors:** Kemal Kirtac
**Published:** 2026-09-20 | **arXiv:** [2609.23703v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23703v1)

## Summary
This paper presents MFAST, a framework that uses language models to turn financial news into trading signals while accounting for real-world constraints like transaction costs and liquidity. It's the first integrated system to rigorously test whether NLP-based sentiment extraction actually works for profitable trading when you include market frictions — the delays, costs, and constraints that exist in real trading.

## Theoretical Background
Financial trading relies on predicting price movements from available information. News — both in price action and text — is a traditional alpha source. Prior work in machine learning has tackled time-series forecasting, text classification, and multimodal prediction separately, but the financial domain has specific demands: trades must respect event timing (you can only trade on news you've observed), transaction costs reduce profits, and liquidity constraints limit trade size. Decoder-only language models (like GPT-style transformers) have shown advantages in few-shot learning and instruction following. The paper builds on sentiment analysis traditions and combines them with practical trading considerations that computer science research typically ignores.

## Core Contributions
1. **MFAST framework**: A domain-specific evaluation protocol for financial language models that jointly validates decision outputs against event-time observability, probability calibration, execution timing, transaction costs, liquidity, capacity, and statistical inference — not just accuracy.
2. **Rigorous temporal discipline**: Out-of-sample testing restricted to news released after language model training cutoffs, preventing look-ahead bias.
3. **Reproducible financial NLP**: Integration pipeline linking news (Refinitiv) to security identifiers (CRSP) with a public replication arm using open data.
4. **Operational diagnostics**: Empirical trade-offs quantified between model accuracy, latency, memory, throughput, and inference cost — showing that best accuracy ≠ best deployment choice.
5. **Comparative validation**: Decoder-only LLMs outperform encoder baselines and dictionary sentiment across classification, calibration, return prediction, and net portfolio returns.

## Practical Applications
1. **Systematic news-based hedge funds**: Automated strategies that ingest firm-specific news and execute trades within microseconds, respecting capacity and liquidity limits.
2. **Risk management dashboards**: Real-time sentiment monitoring for portfolio managers to flag emerging risks from news events.
3. **Index fund rebalancing**: Using NLP-based confidence scores to improve execution timing and reduce transaction costs in large rebalances.
4. **Earnings season trading**: Automated parsing and position-sizing for earnings announcements, calibrated to actual execution feasibility.
5. **Market surveillance**: Detecting manipulation or anomalous sentiment patterns in news feeds for regulatory compliance.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **FinNLP-as-a-Service**: White-label API providing calibrated sentiment scores and trade signals directly integrated with brokers (Bloomberg, FactSet) — charge per signal or % of AUM managed via signals.
2. **Trading Operations SaaS**: Platform that bundles news ingestion, model inference, execution simulation, and post-trade analytics specifically for quant teams — reduce operational overhead for systematic traders.
3. **News Alpha Monitor**: Institutional tool that ranks news-based alpha decay in real time; help portfolio managers know when NLP-based signals have degraded and need retraining.
4. **Execution Optimizer for News Events**: Specialized smart-order-routing that timestamps news, predicts optimal execution windows, and minimizes market impact for large positions around earnings/macro releases.
5. **Backtesting Audit Service**: Third-party validation of financial NLP models, ensuring temporal discipline, transaction cost realism, and calibration — de-risk adoption for conservative institutions.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Data freshness bias**: Even with training cutoff discipline, models may implicitly learn seasonal patterns or macro regimes that don't persist; future market regime shifts can invalidate signals.
2. **Execution timing assumption**: Framework assumes news can be parsed and acted upon in milliseconds; real brokers introduce latency, and by the time a retail signal executes, institutional players have moved price.
3. **Liquidity constraints underspecified**: Model doesn't adapt position size dynamically to realized liquidity; in stress events, constraints could tighten much faster than historical data suggests.
4. **Limited causality**: Sentiment → return relationship is correlational. Confounding variables (analyst coverage, earnings surprises, sector rotation) could drive both news tone and returns without causal link.
5. **Generalization risk**: Trained on specific news sources (Refinitiv) and equities; untested on emerging markets, small-cap stocks, or novel news formats (social media, alternative data).
6. **Single-stock focus**: Doesn't model portfolio interactions or hedge effects; cross-asset arbitrage and correlation breakdowns ignored.

## Open Questions
1. **Does model confidence correlate with forward returns?** Probability calibration is tested, but do high-confidence predictions actually deliver higher Sharpe ratios out-of-sample?
2. **How quickly does the alpha decay after publication?** Is the signal strongest in first 5 minutes, first hour, or longer? Where is the practical execution frontier?
3. **Can the model adapt to regime shifts?** What happens in bear markets, volatility spikes, or earnings blackout periods? Should re-training schedules be dynamic?
4. **How does multimodal information (news + price momentum + options implied volatility) improve calibration vs. text alone?** Is text additive or redundant?
5. **Can smaller, distilled models match decoder performance with 10× lower latency?** Trade-off frontier between model size and usefulness in practice?
6. **Does the framework generalize to non-English news or emerging markets?** What additional friction arises in less-liquid geographies?

## Key Takeaway
Language models can extract profitable trading signals from news, but only if you rigorously test them with real transaction costs and execution timing included in evaluation.
