# Latent Inference-Time Guidance of Time Series Foundation Models

**Authors:** Chloé Hashimoto-Cullen, Amaury Durand, Laurent Bozzi, Benjamin Guedj, Yannig Goude et al.
**Published:** 2026-09-29 | **arXiv:** [2609.38058v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38058v1)

## Summary
This paper presents a method to improve time series forecasts by intelligently combining predictions from multiple foundation models (large pre-trained neural networks) at prediction time. Rather than picking a single "best" model configuration, the approach uses a latent variable model to learn how to weight different forecasts together, adapting across time — and it does this without modifying the foundation models themselves, keeping them as plug-and-play tools.

## Theoretical Background
Time Series Foundation Models (TSFMs) are large neural networks pre-trained on diverse time series data that can be applied to new forecasting tasks with minimal adjustment — similar to how large language models transfer across NLP tasks. The paper builds on the observation that in-context learning (where a model adapts based on recent historical data shown to it) in TSFMs is sensitive to design choices like how much past data to use (lookback window), which additional variables to include (covariates), and prediction horizon length.

The core theoretical contribution uses latent variable models with independent components — a framework where unobserved factors explain correlations in observed data — combined with identifiability theory, which guarantees that learned latent factors have a unique, interpretable structure. This contrasts with ad-hoc ensemble methods that simply average predictions without principled reasoning about why different models succeed or fail.

## Core Contributions
1. **Adaptive ensemble framework**: A learnable, time-dependent weighting scheme for combining multiple TSFM forecasts through a latent space with independent components (disentangled factors).
2. **Theoretical guarantees**: Identifiability and reconstruction guarantees — proving that the latent factors can be uniquely recovered and faithfully reconstruct forecast quality.
3. **Off-the-shelf compatibility**: The method works with any pre-trained foundation model without retraining, preserving their generalist capability.
4. **Empirical validation**: Demonstration across multi-frequency, multi-domain datasets showing competitive or superior performance to traditional ensemble baselines.

## Connections to Other Work
This relates to [[ensemble learning]] and [[mixture of experts]] paradigms, which combine multiple models to reduce variance and improve robustness. It extends beyond simple averaging (e.g., stacking) by using latent structure — similar in spirit to how variational autoencoders (VAEs) learn disentangled representations, though applied to the ensemble weighting problem rather than data generation.

The work also connects to recent trends in foundation model adaptation — like [[in-context learning]] and [[prompt engineering]] — by recognizing that single "best" configurations don't exist and proposing a learnable, principled alternative to manual hyperparameter selection.

## Practical Applications
1. **Demand forecasting in retail/supply chain**: Automatically combines forecasts from models trained on different historical windows to reduce forecast variance and improve inventory planning.
2. **Energy load forecasting**: Utilities can apply multiple pre-trained models with different configurations and let the method weight them adaptively, improving grid planning without retraining models for each region or season.
3. **Financial time series prediction**: Combines models trained on different look-back periods (short-term vs. long-term trends) to capture multiple timescale dependencies without manual selection.
4. **Weather and climate modeling**: Ensembles forecasts from models trained on different geographic regions or historical periods, adapting weights as seasonal patterns shift.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **Time Series Forecasting SaaS**: A platform offering pre-trained TSFMs with automatic ensemble weighting — users upload data, select context options, and the system automatically optimizes forecast accuracy without manual tuning.
2. **Demand Planning Plugin**: B2B software for supply chain teams that hooks into existing ERP systems, replacing manual forecast selection with adaptive ensemble logic for SKU-level demand.
3. **Energy Analytics Dashboard**: Enterprise software for utilities and manufacturing plants that visualizes multi-model forecasts and learns optimal weighting per facility, improving operational efficiency and reducing stranded inventory/power costs.
4. **Financial Forecasting API**: RESTful service for hedge funds and quantitative traders that ingests ticker data, automatically combines multiple pre-trained foundation models, and returns weighted ensemble forecasts with uncertainty bands.
5. **Autonomous Forecast Optimizer**: Consulting/MLOps service that audits a client's existing ensemble forecasting pipeline, deploys this latent guidance method, and guarantees variance reduction with zero retraining.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Computational overhead**: Requires training a latent variable model on top of multiple foundation model outputs — adds inference-time cost compared to a single model, though smaller than retraining.
2. **Limited theoretical analysis of when latent decomposition helps**: The paper doesn't deeply characterize *which* types of forecast disagreement (data distribution shift, different effective horizons, etc.) the latent factors can capture — some failure modes may remain opaque.
3. **Assumption of independent latent components**: Real forecast errors may have interdependencies that the independence assumption misses.
4. **Scalability with large forecast pools**: As the number of candidate models grows, the latent model's capacity must grow too; unclear how performance scales to hundreds of pre-trained variants.
5. **Domain shift in new data**: The latent weighting learns from a training set; if the test time series distribution drifts significantly, learned weights may not adapt online.

## Open Questions
1. **When does adaptive weighting beat static averaging?** A formal characterization of data and forecast diversity conditions under which the latent guidance approach significantly outperforms simple averaging would strengthen the work.
2. **Can the latent factors be interpreted?** Do learned independent components correspond to identifiable phenomena (e.g., "trend capture," "seasonal robustness")? Interpretability studies would improve debugging and trust.
3. **Online/continual learning**: Can the latent model be updated in real time as new forecasts arrive, rather than fixed at training time?
4. **Scalability to very large model pools**: How does the approach perform with 50+ candidate models? Does a hierarchical or sparse weighting scheme help?
5. **Theoretical finite-sample bounds**: What guarantees hold for reconstruction error and identifiability in finite samples, and how do they scale with dimensionality and sample size?

## Key Takeaway
Combine multiple foundation model forecasts intelligently by learning adaptive weights in a latent space, beating manual selection without retraining.
