# A Theoretical Framework for Parallel Lifelong MAPF Using Group Decentralized Planning

**Authors:** Alex DeWeese, Jiaoyang Li, Guannan Qu
**Published:** 2026-08-18 | **arXiv:** [2608.17928v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17928v1)

## Summary
This paper solves a major computational bottleneck in lifelong multi-agent path finding — the problem where many robots or agents must repeatedly move between destinations without crashing into each other or obstacles. The authors prove that a popular but slow algorithm (RHCR) is near-optimal, then use that insight to design a faster parallel version (GD-RHCR) that partitions agents into groups and plans for each group in parallel, maintaining the same quality guarantees while scaling to more agents.

## Theoretical Background
The work builds on the **Rolling-Horizon Collision Resolution (RHCR)** framework, a state-of-the-art approach for Lifelong Multi-Agent Path Finding (L-MAPF) that produces high-quality solutions but at high computational cost. The authors ground their analysis in **Locally Interdependent Multi-Agent MDP** theory — a framework for modeling multi-agent systems where agents' decisions affect only nearby neighbors. They formulate L-MAPF as a discounted Markov Decision Process (MDP), allowing them to apply dynamic programming and optimality guarantees. The key insight is recognizing that RHCR's time-based horizon restriction (solving only a limited future window) has an equivalent effect to spatial partitioning in a decentralized setting.

## Core Contributions
1. **Theoretical proof** that RHCR achieves near-optimality (exponentially close to optimal) under a discounted MDP formulation of L-MAPF — prior work lacked this formal guarantee.
2. **GD-RHCR algorithm**: A decentralized variant that partitions agents via transitive communication graphs and plans each partition in parallel, maintaining the same theoretical optimality bounds as RHCR.
3. **Duality result**: Formal equivalence between RHCR's time-based horizon restrictions and GD-RHCR's space-based agent partitioning, showing two fundamentally different ways to achieve the same guarantees.
4. **Empirical validation** demonstrating that GD-RHCR scales to higher agent counts with significantly lower per-plan cost while maintaining throughput.

## Connections to Other Work
This work extends classical **multi-agent path finding** research by tackling the lifelong variant (infinite horizons). It relates to broader [[RLHF|reinforcement learning]] and MDP theory through its use of discounted MDPs. The decentralization strategy connects to work on **independent learners** and communication-constrained multi-agent systems. The paper's use of transitive communication graphs echoes ideas from **distributed constraint satisfaction** and **local coordination protocols**. The theoretical framework bridges gap between planning-based (RHCR) and learning-based approaches in multi-agent settings.

## Practical Applications
- **Warehouse automation**: Robot fleets in fulfillment centers (Amazon, DHL) picking and moving items continuously without collisions, now feasible at larger scales.
- **Autonomous traffic control**: Coordinating delivery drones or autonomous vehicles in constrained urban airspace or congested intersections.
- **Smart manufacturing**: AGVs (autonomous guided vehicles) in factories moving materials between stations in real time.
- **Search and rescue**: Coordinating multiple robots in disaster zones that must navigate repeatedly between areas of interest.
- **Hospital logistics**: Autonomous carts delivering supplies and specimens throughout large medical complexes during extended operations.

## Technical Limitations
- **Transitive communication graphs** assumption may not hold in practice — real wireless networks have asymmetric or lossy links.
- **Decentralized planning requires local coordination overhead** — the paper doesn't measure communication bandwidth or latency costs of maintaining group structure.
- **Scalability claims are tested on standard benchmarks** (grids), not real-world maps with complex topology or dynamic obstacles.
- **Discounted MDP formulation** uses a discount factor that may not match actual deployment horizons; sensitivity analysis is absent.
- **Partitioning strategy is static** — agents are partitioned once; no adaptive repartitioning if communication or congestion patterns change.
- **No comparison to other decentralized methods** (e.g., independent learners, consensus-based planning) beyond RHCR.

## Open Questions
1. How does GD-RHCR perform under **communication delays or packet loss** in the transitive communication graph?
2. Can the partition structure be **learned or adapted online** rather than fixed a priori?
3. How do results generalize to **non-grid environments** (real maps, 3D spaces, continuous motion)?
4. What is the **communication overhead** (messages, bandwidth) for maintaining decentralized coordination versus centralized RHCR?
5. How does performance degrade if the **discount factor assumption breaks** or is mis-specified?
6. Can similar theoretical duality be shown for other decentralization strategies (e.g., hierarchical, temporal windows)?

## Key Takeaway
Decentralized group planning can match centralized path-finding quality while enabling parallel computation for large robot fleets.
