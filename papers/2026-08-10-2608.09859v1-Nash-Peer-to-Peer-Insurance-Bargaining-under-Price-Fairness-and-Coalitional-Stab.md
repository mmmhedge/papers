# Nash Peer-to-Peer Insurance Bargaining under Price Fairness and Coalitional Stability

**Authors:** Tim J. Boonen, Wing Fung Chong, Kenneth Tsz Hin Ng, Tak Wa Ng
**Published:** 2026-08-10 | **arXiv:** [2608.09859v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09859v1)

## Summary
This paper studies how peer-to-peer insurance groups can fairly distribute risk and premiums when members bargain with a reinsurer. The authors use Nash bargaining — a game-theoretic framework where parties negotiate from disagreement points — to design contracts that are both individually rational (each member prefers the contract to no deal) and coalitionally stable (no subgroup wants to defect). The work matters because P2P insurance is growing, and understanding how to price and allocate risk fairly while preventing group collapse is a practical problem.

## Theoretical Background
The paper builds on two established literatures. First, Nash bargaining solution theory — a foundational concept in cooperative game theory that formalizes how rational agents divide gains from cooperation under symmetric conditions. Second, insurance economics, particularly the concept of the expected-value premium principle, which ties premium loading to actuarial risk. The paper extends these by combining asymmetric Nash bargaining (where agents have different bargaining power) with coalitional games — settings where subgroups can form side agreements and potentially break apart from the larger pool. A key constraint is the "price fairness" condition: all peers pay a common proportional markup on their expected losses, ensuring no peer is overcharged relative to their risk.

## Core Contributions
1. **Axiomatic justification for Nash bargaining in P2P insurance**: The authors show that the Nash bargaining solution satisfies axioms (like Pareto efficiency and independence of irrelevant alternatives) that are natural for voluntary, small-scale insurance pools, distinguishing it from ad-hoc weighted-sum optimization.
2. **Full characterization under price fairness**: They derive closed-form or first-order conditions for optimal contracts across three regimes (full reinsurance, partial, and none), all while respecting the price-fairness constraint.
3. **Coalitional stability conditions**: They provide computationally tractable sufficient conditions to verify that no subgroup can profitably deviate — a key requirement for contract stability.
4. **Empirical insights on pool dynamics**: Numerical experiments reveal that larger pools don't automatically improve welfare; the interplay between diversification gains and bargaining-power dilution matters.

## Practical Applications
1. **P2P insurance platform design**: Companies like Lemonade or Instrat could use these models to price group policies and distribute losses fairly, reducing attrition from perceived unfairness.
2. **Microinsurance in emerging markets**: Small village-based or community insurance pools can use these bargaining frameworks to set premiums that feel legitimate and encourage participation.
3. **Parametric and index-based reinsurance**: Reinsurers can apply these methods to negotiate multi-member risk-sharing contracts with stable payoff structures.
4. **Credit union or mutual aid networks**: Any voluntary pooling of financial or operational risk can adopt these frameworks to ensure stability and fair pricing.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **P2P Insurance Smart Contract Optimizer**: A SaaS tool that computes Nash-bargaining optimal contracts for decentralized or community insurance pools, visualizing fairness trade-offs and showing each member's welfare gain.
2. **Coalition-Stability Auditor**: A compliance platform that verifies proposed insurance or risk-sharing contracts satisfy core/stability conditions, preventing member defections and regulator pressure.
3. **Dynamic P2P Reinsurance Marketplace**: An automated exchange matching peer pools with reinsurers, using bargaining algorithms to price deals in real time while respecting fairness constraints.
4. **Fairness-Aware Premium Calculator for Fractional Insurance**: White-label pricing engine for emerging-market microinsurance platforms, enabling fair-loading pricing that adapts to pool size and member risk profiles.
5. **Blockchain-based Mutual Insurance DAO**: A decentralized autonomous organization using these bargaining models to distribute claims and set premiums transparently, proving fairness on-chain to build trust.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Asymmetric information**: The model assumes full information about loss distributions and bargaining power. In reality, peers may misrepresent risk, and reinsurer power is often opaque.
2. **Computational scalability**: While the authors provide sufficient conditions for stability, checking all possible coalitions becomes intractable for large pools (exponential in group size), limiting practical application to small or special-structure pools.
3. **Static analysis**: The framework is one-shot; real P2P insurance involves dynamic membership (entry/exit), evolving risk, and repeated interaction — none of which the paper captures.
4. **Fairness definition**: Price fairness (common loading on expected loss) is imposed exogenously; whether peers actually perceive this as fair, or prefer other fairness notions (e.g., equal premiums, proportional to initial wealth), is not empirically tested.
5. **Default and moral hazard**: The model does not address what happens if a peer or reinsurer defaults, or how fairness interacts with incentives to take excessive risk.

## Open Questions
1. How do these bargaining contracts perform under misspecification — e.g., if true loss distributions differ from assumed ones?
2. Can the framework be extended to dynamic/repeated settings, where fairness and stability might matter differently across time?
3. What happens if coalitions can re-negotiate over time, or if entry/exit is continuous?
4. Do empirical P2P insurance users actually demand Nash-bargaining style fairness, or do simpler rules (equal charge, actuarial pricing) suffice?
5. How sensitive are stability conditions to small errors in estimating member risk profiles, and can robust variants be derived?
6. Can machine learning be combined with these bargaining models to infer fairness preferences from data?

## Key Takeaway
Nash bargaining with price fairness ensures peer-to-peer insurance is both individually rational and stable against coalitional defection.
