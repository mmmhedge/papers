# Socially Grounded Agentic AI: Coordinating Plural Perspectives through Social Theory

**Authors:** Matt Ratto, Abhishek Moturu, Daniel Silver
**Published:** 2026-08-04 | **arXiv:** [2608.03910v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03910v1)

## Summary
As AI systems are deployed across increasingly diverse social contexts, alignment can no longer be framed as the optimization of a single, unified set of values. Instead, systems must be able to recognize, represent, and respond to multiple legitimate perspectives. This has led to growing interest in pluralistic alignment, which seeks to move beyond one-size-fits-all models of appropriate behaviour.

## Theoretical Background
AI agents combine models with tools, memory, and execution loops. Reliability therefore depends on decisions across a trajectory, not only the quality of isolated answers.

The paper addresses the trade-off identified in its abstract through a targeted method, representation, or evaluation framework.

## Core Contributions
1. Instead, systems must be able to recognize, represent, and respond to multiple legitimate perspectives.
2. In this paper, we argue that social theory provides essential conceptual and design resources for addressing these challenges.
3. Drawing on established traditions in sociology, we show how perspectives can be understood as structured by roles, shaped through interaction, and distributed across fields of power and expertise.
