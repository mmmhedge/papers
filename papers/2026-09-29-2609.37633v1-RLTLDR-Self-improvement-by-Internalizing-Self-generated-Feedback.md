# RLTL;DR: Self-improvement by Internalizing Self-generated Feedback

**Authors:** Michael Kirchhof, Eleonora Gualdoni, Andrew Szot, Khashayar Gatmiry, Aryo Lotfi et al.
**Published:** 2026-09-29 | **arXiv:** [2609.37633v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37633v1)

## Summary
This paper tackles a hard problem in self-improvement: teaching agents to solve tasks where success is extremely rare or impossible initially. The authors introduce RLTL;DR, which lets a policy generate its own textual insights after each failure, then conditions future attempts on these insights. The key finding is that by "internalizing" the task-to-insight mapping through backpropagation, the model learns to solve hard problems without needing the insights at test time—suggesting a new training paradigm for difficult generalization.

## Theoretical Background
The paper builds on [[Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards]] (RLVR), a framework where agents attempt tasks repeatedly and optimize towards successes. Standard approaches like [[GRPO]] (Group Relative Policy Optimization) work well when agents have a reasonable chance of solving tasks early on, but fail when success is rare or absent initially. This relates to broader challenges in [[few-shot learning]] and [[in-context learning]] with language models—the ability of transformers (neural networks using attention mechanisms) to adapt to new instructions dynamically. The core mechanism draws inspiration from [[chain-of-thought prompting]], where intermediate reasoning steps improve performance, and extends it to self-generated feedback that's then internalized into model weights through training.

## Core Contributions
1. **RLTL;DR mechanism**: After failed attempts, the verifier's output is shown to the policy, which writes a "TL;DR" (compact insight). Subsequent rollouts are conditioned on accumulated insights until success.
2. **Task-to-insight internalization**: Crucially, the paper shows that backpropagating through these insights allows the model to learn a direct mapping from task to insight, eliminating the need for in-context insights at test time.
3. **Compacted training paradigm (SFTL;DR)**: A striking finding that training on just 4k (task, insight) tuples—without any rollouts—recovers ~90% of RLTL;DR's performance, suggesting a much more data-efficient training mode.
4. **Empirical breakthrough**: On filtered tool-calling and coding datasets (where standard GRPO achieves 0–1% Pass@1), RLTL;DR reaches 12–13% Pass@1 even without in-context insights at eval time.

## Connections to Other Work
This work sits at the intersection of several research threads:
- **[[In-context learning]]** and **[[Prompt engineering]]**: Uses dynamic conditioning on previous attempts, but goes further by internalizing the pattern.
- **[[RLHF]] and [[Reward modeling]]**: Similar to how RLHF uses human feedback to shape model behavior, RLTL;DR uses self-generated feedback, but the model learns to generate relevant insights rather than just following them.
- **[[Curriculum learning]]**: Each new rollout conditions on accumulated insights, creating a soft curriculum where the model has more information with each attempt.
- **[[Chain-of-thought reasoning]]** and **[[Interpretability]]**: The TL;DR insights are interpretable intermediate steps, touching on mechanistic understanding of how models reason.
- **[[Meta-learning]]**: Learning a task-to-insight mapping is implicitly learning a meta-pattern about how to approach hard problems.

## Practical Applications
1. **Autonomous code generation & debugging**: Tools that iteratively solve hard coding challenges by learning their own debugging strategies without human intervention.
2. **Tool-use agents**: Autonomous systems (e.g., API callers, data processors) that learn to plan complex multi-step tasks and internalize strategies for failure recovery.
3. **Scientific reasoning engines**: Systems that tackle novel research problems by generating and refining hypotheses based on failed experiments.
4. **Educational AI tutors**: Adaptive systems that learn what conceptual insights help students overcome specific hard problems, personalizing explanations without retraining.
5. **Robotic task learning**: Robots learning to solve complex manipulation tasks by generating interpretable "lessons learned" that generalize to new variations.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Scalability to truly open-ended tasks**: Experiments focus on narrowly-scoped tool-calling and coding tasks; unclear how this scales to domains with very diverse problem structure.
2. **Insight quality dependency**: The method assumes the verifier output is meaningful. If verifier feedback is noisy or misleading, the insights may be poor, potentially hurting generalization.
3. **Limited to language-based feedback**: The approach requires the policy to articulate insights in text; doesn't apply to purely continuous-control domains (e.g., robotics without language).
4. **Generalization gap at eval**: While the paper shows 12–13% Pass@1 without in-context insights, this is still a significant drop from in-context performance (14–31%), suggesting imperfect internalization.
5. **Computational cost not analyzed**: Requires multiple rollouts per task during training; total training cost vs. baseline GRPO not clearly quantified.
6. **Small-scale models**: Main experiments on Qwen 3.5 9B; unclear if findings hold for larger models or across different architectures.

## Open Questions
1. **How do insights evolve over training?** An analysis of what the model learns to generate (and how it changes) would illuminate the internalization process.
2. **Can this scale to truly unseen task distributions?** Does SFTL;DR generalize to tasks structurally different from the training set?
3. **What is the theoretical limit of insight-based learning?** Is there a fundamental gap between in-context and internalized performance, or can it close with better inductive biases?
4. **Can insights be shared across agents or tasks?** If insights are task-type-specific, can we build reusable insight libraries?
5. **How does this compare to other self-improvement methods** (e.g., [[search-based decoding]], [[test-time scaling]], [[process reward models]])? A systematic comparison would clarify when RLTL;DR is preferable.
6. **Interpretability of internalized insights**: Once internalized, can we decode what representations the model learned? Are they semantically similar to the text-based TL;DRs?

## Key Takeaway
Models can learn to solve hard tasks by generating and internalizing their own textual insights, bypassing the need for human feedback or successful rollouts.
