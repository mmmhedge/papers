# A Unifying Relational Perspective on Expressive Lottery Tickets

**Authors:** Lorenz Kummer, Samir Moustafa, Anatol Ehrlich, Franka Bause, Marco Nennstiel et al.
**Published:** 2026-08-19 | **arXiv:** [2608.18819v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18819v1)

## Summary
This paper proves that sparse graph neural networks can retain their expressive power—the ability to distinguish different graph structures—even after aggressive pruning. The authors generalize prior lottery ticket results to multi-relational and temporal graphs, showing that random pruning has a mathematically quantifiable probability of preserving expressivity. This matters because it bridges neural network compression (making models faster and smaller) with theoretical guarantees about what those smaller models can still represent.

## Theoretical Background
Graph neural networks (GNNs) process relational data by passing messages between connected nodes. The **Weisfeiler-Leman (WL) test** is a classical algorithmic procedure that checks graph isomorphism; its expressivity level is used as a theoretical ceiling for what GNNs can distinguish. The **Strong Expressive Lottery Ticket Hypothesis (SELTH)** claims that sufficiently overparameterized networks contain sparse subnetworks matching the full network's expressivity. This paper extends SELTH to relational WL (RWL) — a variant for multi-relational graphs — and temporal graphs. The core mathematical tool is probabilistic analysis of random pruning's success rate.

## Core Contributions
1. **Generalized SELTH to relational domains**: Proves that relational GNNs (RGNNs) and temporal GNNs (TGNNs) contain sparse subnetworks preserving 1-RWL expressivity, not just on static graphs.
2. **Probability bounds**: Derives a lower bound on the probability that random pruning yields an expressive sparse subnetwork, moving beyond existence results to quantitative predictions.
3. **Unified reformulation framework**: Shows that common TGNNs and cross-graph message passing schemes can be reframed as RGNN variants, unifying multiple architectures under one theoretical umbrella.
4. **Optimization-expressivity connection**: Links sparse RGNN expressivity to actual training dynamics and downstream task performance, bridging theory to practice.

## Connections to Other Work
This work builds directly on [[Lottery Ticket Hypothesis]] literature, extending it from vision and language domains to structured (graph) data. It relates to [[Weisfeiler-Leman]] expressivity frameworks—a key lens for understanding GNN limitations. The probabilistic analysis echoes recent work on sparse neural networks and implicit bias, while the temporal extension connects to broader efforts in [[Temporal Graph Neural Networks]]. The unification of TGNNs under RGNN reformulations suggests connections to categorical and relational learning frameworks.

## Practical Applications
- **Model compression**: Deploy GNNs on edge devices (IoT, mobile) or embedded systems with guaranteed expressivity preservation.
- **Molecular property prediction**: Pruned RGNNs could reduce inference cost for drug discovery pipelines while maintaining chemical discrimination power.
- **Temporal knowledge graphs**: Compress models for real-time reasoning systems (e.g., fraud detection, traffic prediction) where both speed and expressivity matter.
- **Scalable link prediction**: Reduce memory footprint for industrial social networks or recommendation systems without losing ranking discriminative power.

## Technical Limitations
- **Probabilistic bounds are loose**: The lower bound on random pruning success may not be tight; actual pruning success could be far higher or lower in practice.
- **Initialization and training dependence**: The theory assumes sufficient overparameterization and doesn't fully account for how initialization, optimization algorithms, or data distribution shift affect sparse subnetwork discovery.
- **1-RWL vs. higher expressivity**: The guarantee holds for 1-RWL; extending to higher-order WL expressivity is left open, and many tasks may require richer expressivity.
- **Scalability of proofs**: The probabilistic argument relies on existence proofs; constructively finding these sparse subnetworks (versus random search) remains unclear at scale.
- **Temporal dynamics underspecified**: Real temporal graphs have nonstationary structure; the paper's temporal reformulation may not capture all nuances of evolving edge patterns.

## Open Questions
- Can you **constructively find** the expressive sparse subnetworks (beyond random pruning) in polynomial time?
- Do the empirical pruning probabilities **scale predictably** as graphs grow larger, or do constants in the bound become prohibitive?
- How do **adversarial or noisy edge perturbations** affect the maintained expressivity of pruned RGNNs?
- Can tighter, problem-specific bounds be derived for common GNN tasks (e.g., node classification, link prediction) rather than worst-case WL expressivity?
- Does **structured pruning** (removing entire layers or attention heads) offer better guarantees or practical speed-ups than unstructured sparsity?

## Key Takeaway
Sparse graph neural networks can preserve expressive power with quantifiable probability, unifying theory across relational and temporal domains.
