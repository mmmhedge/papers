# Evidence Attribution in Visual Document Understanding without Coordinates or Region Labels

**Authors:** Zhuchenyang Liu, Yao Zhang, Yu Xiao
**Published:** 2026-07-27 | **arXiv:** [2607.24651v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24651v1)

## Summary
This paper tackles a critical failure in document understanding systems: models answer questions correctly but cite the wrong evidence regions (called "attribution hallucination"). The authors show that replacing coordinate-based evidence selection with a text-quoting approach — where models quote relevant passages and a layout parser locates them — dramatically improves both evidence recall and accuracy. They further introduce a training method that learns to produce better evidence quotes without expensive region-level labels.

## Theoretical Background
Visual document understanding combines vision-language models (systems that process both text and images) with document analysis. Traditionally, systems justify answers by outputting bounding box coordinates that mark evidence regions — a coordinate interface. This builds on recent work in visual question answering (VQA) and document intelligence. The paper also draws on reinforcement learning methods, specifically group relative policy optimization (GRPO) — a technique that trains models using reward signals rather than direct supervision. Attribution, in this context, means explicitly showing which parts of a document support a given answer.

## Core Contributions
1. **Empirical diagnosis of attribution hallucination**: Demonstrates that the coordinate interface itself constrains what models can express; switching to a quote-based interface (text output + layout-parser-driven retrieval) improves evidence recall from ≤8 points to 26–47 points across six vision-language models.

2. **Quote-and-retrieve pipeline**: Proposes a practical alternative to coordinates where models output text quotes verbatim, and a layout parser locates these passages, handling both text and figures/tables through captions.

3. **GRPO training without region labels**: Introduces a training scaffold that learns to produce better evidence quotes using only gold answers and retrieved region crops as reward signals, eliminating the need for expensive region-level supervision. On an 8B model, this raises strict attributed accuracy from 22.4% to 33.8%.

## Connections to Other Work
This work relates to [[RLHF]] (reinforcement learning from human feedback) and broader attribution methods in question-answering systems. It addresses failures documented in recent vision-language model benchmarks and connects to document layout analysis pipelines. The quote-based approach echoes retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) patterns in NLP, where models cite retrieved passages rather than generate freely. The paper also touches on mechanistic issues: why models hallucinate in coordinate space even when they "know" the right answer.

## Practical Applications
- **Legal document review**: Automating contract analysis with auditable evidence trails for compliance.
- **Financial report analysis**: Extracting key metrics (earnings, guidance) with explicit source citations for regulatory compliance.
- **Academic paper parsing**: Extracting claims with cited evidence for literature review automation.
- **Medical records processing**: Identifying diagnoses or recommendations with provenance for clinical accountability.
- **Insurance claim processing**: Justifying decisions by explicitly citing policy terms and applicant statements.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **Compliance Auditing SaaS**: A tool for enterprises that auto-analyzes long documents (contracts, policies, filings) and generates answers with verifiable evidence chains, marketed to legal and finance teams.

2. **Research Paper Mining API**: A service that extracts facts from academic papers with in-text quotes and location metadata, enabling researchers to build verifiable literature databases.

3. **Document Intelligence for Supply Chain**: Analyze invoices, shipping manifests, and contracts; output decisions (payment approval, delivery routing) with cited evidence for audit trails.

4. **Accessible Document Q&A**: A consumer tool (browser extension or mobile app) that lets users ask questions about PDFs and get answers with highlighted evidence passages — useful for students and remote workers.

5. **Attribution-First Enterprise Search**: Internal knowledge search that returns answers with explicit document excerpts and locations, differentiating on verifiability rather than speed.

## Technical Limitations
- **Layout parser dependency**: The method relies on a layout parser to locate quoted text; poor OCR, handwriting, or non-standard formatting breaks the pipeline.
- **Quote alignment assumptions**: Assumes models can output exact verbatim quotes; in practice, paraphrasing or minor misquotation may cause retrieval to fail.
- **Scalability to very long documents**: As documents grow, the retrieval problem becomes harder; the paper doesn't discuss latency or resource costs.
- **Language and domain generalization**: Tested on bilingual CiteVQA; unclear how well the approach generalizes to non-English documents, domain-specific jargon, or degraded document images.
- **Reward signal design**: The GRPO training relies on a "judge's reading" of correctness, which may not capture all valid evidence or nuanced partial correctness.
- **Figure and table handling**: The current approach quotes figures/tables via captions or notes, which may lose fine-grained data integrity.

## Open Questions
1. How does performance degrade with OCR errors, scanned documents, or handwritten text?
2. Can the quote-and-retrieve pipeline handle cross-document reasoning (e.g., synthesizing information from multiple pages)?
3. What reward design improvements would further boost GRPO training efficiency and accuracy?
4. How does the method scale to thousand-page documents or real-time inference constraints?
5. Can this approach extend to multimodal evidence (e.g., tables, charts) without relying on captions?
6. Does the quote interface improve human interpretability and trust in model decisions in user studies?

## Key Takeaway
Asking models to quote evidence text rather than output coordinates reduces hallucination and improves attribution without costly region labels.
