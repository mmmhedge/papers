# Earth observation embeddings are effective sub-grid descriptors for probabilistic weather downscaling

**Authors:** Pedro Sousa, Will Tebbutt, Sadiq Jaffer, Robin Young, Anil Madhavapeddy et al.
**Published:** 2026-08-12 | **arXiv:** [2608.12271v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12271v1)

## Summary
This paper shows that Earth observation embeddings—learned visual representations of land surface conditions from satellite imagery—can improve weather prediction at specific locations by capturing terrain and surface properties that coarse global weather models miss. By combining satellite-derived surface descriptors with a neural downscaling model, the authors achieve 11.5% better temperature predictions and 6.2% better wind predictions across diverse regions, even generalizing to new locations with no training history.

## Theoretical Background
Weather forecasting typically operates on coarse grids (~25 km resolution in this case) because finer global computation is prohibitive. Real-world applications—like solar farms or wind turbines—need predictions at arbitrary locations where local terrain (mountains, forests, water) and land-surface properties (elevation, vegetation, albedo) dramatically affect conditions. Traditional downscaling uses hand-crafted topographic features; this work instead leverages **foundation models**—large pre-trained neural networks trained on diverse data that learn general-purpose representations. TESSERA is an Earth observation foundation model trained on satellite imagery to encode landscape properties. A **convolutional conditional neural process** is a neural architecture that learns to make probabilistic predictions conditioned on nearby observations, naturally suited to spatial prediction tasks.

## Core Contributions
1. **First demonstration** that long-timescale Earth observation embeddings (trained on annual satellite data) can improve short-timescale weather downscaling (instantaneous 2m temperature and 10m wind speed).
2. **Transfer learning evidence**: embeddings trained for general surface characterization directly benefit weather prediction without task-specific fine-tuning.
3. **Generalization proof**: improvements persist when switching from ERA5 reanalysis to Aurora AI forecasts as input, and when predicting at newly deployed stations with no regional history—demonstrating the method doesn't overfit to specific locations or data sources.
4. **Variable-specific insights**: topography dominates temperature structure, while satellite embeddings provide complementary information for wind (which depends more on surface roughness and vegetation).

## Connections to Other Work
This bridges [[computer vision]] (satellite imagery + foundation models) and atmospheric science (probabilistic weather downscaling). Related to broader trends in **transfer learning**—using pre-trained models from one domain (Earth observation) to improve performance in another (meteorology). Conceptually similar to how [[Attention Is All You Need]]-style foundation models transfer across NLP tasks, but applied to geospatial data. Connects to the emerging field of **neural weather prediction** (e.g., Aurora, GraphCast) which replaces traditional physics-based models with learned components.

## Practical Applications
- **Renewable energy forecasting**: accurate local wind/solar irradiance for wind farms and solar arrays.
- **Agricultural planning**: site-specific temperature and precipitation for crop yield prediction.
- **Climate adaptation**: downscaling global climate projections to specific communities for localized impact assessment.
- **Infrastructure resilience**: precise local extreme weather risk for power grids, water systems, and transportation networks.
- **Insurance and parametric contracts**: location-specific weather indices for weather derivatives and crop insurance payouts.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **Hyper-local weather APIs**: SaaS product providing AI-downscaled forecasts at 100m resolution for any GPS coordinate, targeting renewable energy developers and insurers.
2. **Precision agriculture platform**: combine downscaled weather with soil + crop models to optimize irrigation and pesticide timing at field scale.
3. **Climate risk analytics for commercial real estate**: integrate satellite-based downscaling into property valuation and climate-resilience scoring for institutional investors.
4. **Weather-derivative trading platform**: use improved downscaling to price and hedge wind/temperature contracts with better local accuracy than competitors.
5. **Municipal climate adaptation tool**: package downscaled climate projections into interactive planning software for cities to simulate local flood/heat risk under different emissions scenarios.

## Technical Limitations
- **Computational cost**: compressing TESSERA embeddings and running neural processes for each location adds inference latency; not clear if real-time deployment (sub-minute) is feasible.
- **Satellite imagery gaps**: cloud cover and seasonal data quality variations may degrade embedding quality in persistently cloudy regions.
- **Limited evaluation scope**: tested on five regions; generalization to poles, deserts, or rapidly urbanizing areas unknown.
- **Static embeddings**: TESSERA embeddings capture annual-mean surface properties; cannot capture seasonal changes (snow cover, vegetation phenology, crop cycles) or long-term changes (land-use conversion).
- **Coarse-grid bias**: improvements depend on the quality of the input coarse field (ERA5 or Aurora); cannot fix systematic biases in the parent model.
- **Sparse validation data**: improvements shown for weather stations; sparse station networks may hide biases over sparsely observed regions.

## Open Questions
- How do embeddings degrade with time? Do they need retraining as landscapes change (deforestation, urbanization, climate-driven vegetation shifts)?
- Can the method work in data-scarce regions where recent satellite imagery is unavailable?
- How much of the improvement comes from topography vs. vegetation vs. other surface properties? Ablation studies could isolate this.
- Does the method work for precipitation, or is it limited to temperature and wind?
- Can embeddings from *different* Earth observation models (e.g., Sentinel-2 vs. Landsat) be combined to improve robustness?
- What is the minimum spatial resolution of satellite imagery required? Can coarser, more frequent satellite data substitute for higher-resolution annual mosaics?

## Key Takeaway
Satellite-learned surface embeddings improve weather forecasting at specific locations by encoding persistent terrain properties that coarse global models miss.
