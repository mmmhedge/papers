# CENDRe: Concept Extraction with Natural Domain Representations

**Authors:** Antonia Holzapfel, Andres Felipe Posada Moreno, Sebastian Trimpe
**Published:** 2026-07-31 | **arXiv:** [2607.29621v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29621v1)

## Summary
This paper presents CENDRe, a method for extracting interpretable concepts from CNNs trained on time-series data. Unlike existing approaches that work only in the time domain, CENDRe automatically discovers the right number of concepts, localizes them precisely using gradient-based methods, and reveals both temporal and frequency-domain patterns the model relies on—making neural networks more transparent and trustworthy in critical applications like machinery fault diagnosis.

## Theoretical Background
Concept extraction builds on the broader field of neural network interpretability, which seeks to understand what features and patterns drive model predictions. Time-series classification with CNNs is a standard approach, where the network learns hierarchical representations in its latent (hidden) layers. The paper builds on prior concept extraction methods that work by clustering learned representations and then localizing them in the input space. However, existing methods typically operate only in the time domain and require manual specification of the number of concepts. CENDRe extends this by incorporating frequency-domain analysis (using tools like the Fourier transform — a mathematical technique that decomposes signals into their frequency components) and automating concept count selection via silhouette analysis (a clustering validation metric).

## Core Contributions
1. **Dual-domain concept extraction**: Discovers concepts in latent space and automatically localizes them in both time and frequency domains by propagating gradients through an invertible transformation (e.g., Fourier transform).
2. **Automatic concept counting**: Uses silhouette-guided aggregation to determine the optimal number of concepts without manual hyperparameter tuning, addressing a key limitation of prior work.
3. **Improved localization alignment**: Employs gradient-based presence scores that contrast latent representations with learned prototypes, yielding masks that better align with regions the model actually uses for prediction.
4. **Relevance scoring**: Quantifies each concept's contribution to individual class predictions, enabling principled concept importance ranking.
5. **Empirical validation**: Demonstrates on synthetic benchmarks and real bearing-fault diagnosis data that CENDRe achieves higher "importance correctness" (whether highlighted regions truly drive predictions) than existing time-domain CE methods.

## Connections to Other Work
CENDRe extends the concept extraction literature, which includes methods like TCAV (Testing with Concept Activation Vectors, used in image models) and related prototypical network approaches. It relates to broader [[mechanistic-interpretability]] efforts aimed at understanding neural networks' internal decision-making. The use of invertible transformations to move between domains connects to work on disentangled representations and frequency-domain analysis in neural networks. The automatic concept selection via silhouette analysis borrows from unsupervised clustering practices and addresses limitations in prior time-series CE work that either require manual concept counts or produce misaligned localizations.

## Practical Applications
1. **Predictive maintenance**: Automatically identify frequency bands and temporal patterns in sensor data (vibration, acoustic) that indicate machinery faults, enabling engineers to validate and refine diagnostic models before deployment.
2. **Clinical diagnosis from vital signs**: Extract temporal and spectral biomarkers from ECG or EEG data that CNNs use to predict arrhythmias or seizures, aiding clinicians in understanding model decisions.
3. **Quality control in manufacturing**: Isolate the specific signal features (temporal or spectral anomalies) that trigger defect detection in production lines, reducing false positives and increasing operator confidence.
4. **Seismic event detection**: Identify frequency bands and time windows in seismic time-series that the model uses to classify earthquakes versus noise, improving scientific understanding and model transparency.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **Model Validation & Debugging SaaS**: A cloud platform where engineers upload trained time-series CNN models and sensor datasets; CENDRe automatically extracts and visualizes concepts in dual domains, generating human-readable reports on what the model learned (useful for regulatory compliance in medical devices or autonomous systems).
2. **Fault Diagnosis Explainer**: A tools-as-a-service product that integrates CENDRe into existing predictive maintenance pipelines, converting opaque CNN predictions into interpretable frequency-domain and time-domain diagnostic insights for field engineers.
3. **Sensor Data Analytics Assistant**: An IDE/plugin for industrial IoT platforms that automatically applies CENDRe to stream data, letting users interactively query which frequency bands and time windows drive model outputs without coding.
4. **Regulatory Compliance for Neural Networks**: A consultancy or software service helping companies in safety-critical domains (automotive, medical, energy) document and explain their neural network behavior using CENDRe-style concept extraction—meeting transparency requirements.
5. **Time-Series Model Zoo & Benchmark**: An open-source repository of pre-trained time-series CNNs (fault diagnosis, anomaly detection, forecasting) with CENDRe concept extraction results built in, accelerating domain experts' ability to choose and validate models.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Invertibility requirement**: The method requires a differentiable invertible mapping (e.g., Fourier transform) to move between domains; not all transformations are invertible, and the choice of transformation can influence results.
2. **Clustering assumptions**: Silhouette-guided aggregation may fail if the true concept structure doesn't form tight, well-separated clusters in latent space, or if the number of concepts varies with input context.
3. **Gradient reliability**: Localization via gradient-based presence scores assumes gradients reliably indicate input importance; in networks with pathological gradient flow or adversarial robustness, this may break down.
4. **Prototype-based design**: The method assumes concepts can be represented as prototypes (single reference points in latent space); more complex, multimodal concepts may be poorly captured.
5. **Limited to CNNs on time-series**: The paper focuses on CNNs; applicability to RNNs, Transformers, or other architectures for time-series is unclear and would require adaptation.
6. **Scalability**: Clustering per-timestep representations for long sequences could be computationally expensive; performance on very high-dimensional latent spaces or extremely long sequences is not discussed.

## Open Questions
1. **Concept stability**: How stable are extracted concepts across different random seeds, training runs, or architectural variations? Do they generalize to unseen data domains?
2. **Comparison to other interpretability methods**: How does CENDRe compare to attention mechanisms, saliency maps, or layer-wise relevance propagation (LRP) in terms of both computational cost and human interpretability?
3. **Applicability beyond CNNs**: Can the core ideas (dual-domain extraction, silhouette-guided counting) extend to Transformers or RNN-based time-series models, and do they still work well?
4. **Human evaluation**: Do domain experts (e.g., mechanical engineers, physicians) find CENDRe's explanations more trustworthy or actionable than existing methods? User studies would strengthen claims.
5. **Concept entanglement**: How do concepts interact or overlap? Does the method identify redundant or correlated concepts, and how should practitioners handle those?
6. **Adversarial robustness**: If the model is adversarially perturbed, do the extracted concepts change substantially? Can CENDRe detect when a model's decision-making has been compromised?

## Key Takeaway
CENDRe automatically extracts interpretable time-series patterns from neural networks in both time and frequency domains, making fault diagnosis and other critical applications more transparent and trustworthy.
