# BAM! Bayesian Anything Model: a foundation model for generative computational imaging

**Authors:** Alessio Spagnoletti, Charlesquin Kemajou Mbakam, Jonathan Spence, Andrés Almansa, Marcelo Pereyra
**Published:** 2026-09-30 | **arXiv:** [2609.39660v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39660v1)

## Summary
This paper introduces BAM (Bayesian Anything Model), a lightweight foundation model that performs physics-aware image reconstruction in a few steps without requiring likelihood approximation or task-specific training. The key innovation is making the model condition on instrument physics at inference time rather than during training, allowing a single 36M-parameter network to handle diverse computational imaging tasks like deblurring, inpainting, and super-resolution across different datasets and camera systems.

## Theoretical Background
This work sits at the intersection of generative modeling and computational imaging, two fields with separate trajectories that are now converging. Computational imaging uses inverse problem theory to reconstruct images from degraded or incomplete measurements—mathematically, you observe *y = Ax + noise* where *A* is a forward operator (e.g., blur kernel, mask) and want to recover *x*. Traditionally, Bayesian approaches solved this using hand-crafted priors or physics models. Recently, generative models—neural networks trained to sample from image distributions—have become powerful priors because they learn rich natural-image statistics from large corpora. The paper builds on the Reconstruct Anything Model (RAM), a recent operator-conditioned architecture that takes both an image and an operator as input. BAM upgrades RAM by framing it as a conditional flow map (a type of generative model that learns to transform noise into samples via learned transformations), enabling true posterior sampling without approximation.

## Core Contributions
1. **Operator conditioning at inference time**: Instead of training separate models for each physics task, BAM accepts the forward operator *A* as a conditioning input during inference, enabling zero-shot generalization to new instruments and degradation types.
2. **Lightweight, unified architecture**: A single 36M-parameter flow-based network replaces task-specific or dataset-specific models, reducing training cost and enabling practical deployment.
3. **Exact posterior sampling in few steps**: BAM samples from the true Bayesian posterior without likelihood approximation or guidance weights, avoiding the bias and tuning overhead of zero-shot prior methods.
4. **Joint pre-training on operators and images**: The model is pre-trained on diverse forward-operator libraries alongside large image corpora (FFHQ, AFHQ, LSUN, DIV2K), enabling robust few-shot adaptation.
5. **Empirical efficiency**: Outperforms specialized models and leading zero-shot methods in 3 inference steps, with significantly lower computational cost.

## Connections to Other Work
BAM addresses a fundamental tension in the field. Large foundation models like DALL-E or Stable Diffusion excel at image generation but treat computational imaging as an ill-posed optimization problem—they use approximate likelihood guidance and require task-specific tuning, introducing bias. Physics-aware generative models (variational autoencoders, score-based diffusion models trained on specific tasks) are statistically principled but require retraining for each new instrument or degradation. BAM bridges these worlds by combining the generality of foundation models with the principled physics-awareness of Bayesian methods. The work parallels recent advances in [[conditional diffusion models]] and [[operator learning]] (e.g., neural operators, FNOs), but differs by focusing on few-step posterior sampling rather than deterministic regression. It also relates to [[RLHF]] and guidance methods in that it avoids external guidance entirely—the physics is baked into the network weights via conditioning.

## Practical Applications
1. **Medical imaging**: Single model handles X-ray reconstruction, MRI undersampling artifacts, CT noise reduction, and ultrasound enhancement without retraining—critical for hospitals using multiple instruments.
2. **Smartphone computational photography**: Real-time on-device deblurring, super-resolution, and low-light enhancement using the 36M-parameter footprint, enabling privacy-preserving post-processing.
3. **Satellite and remote sensing**: Rapid adaptation to new sensors and atmospheric conditions (dust, cloud cover, haze) without expensive per-mission fine-tuning.
4. **Scientific imaging**: Microscopy, telescopy, and lab-scale imaging where physics models are well-characterized but datasets are small—BAM can be lightly fine-tuned per instrument.
5. **Video stabilization and motion deblurring**: Camera-shake removal using the Kohler benchmark; generalizes across camera models and blur types.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **Cloud API for on-demand image reconstruction**: SaaS platform where researchers/companies upload degraded images and specify instrument physics (blur kernel, sampling mask, noise model); BAM returns posterior samples in milliseconds, undercutting specialized software licenses.
2. **Lightweight edge-AI toolkit for embedded systems**: SDK packaging BAM for phones, drones, and IoT cameras; real-time enhancement without cloud connectivity, competing with Adobe/Apple's on-device ML.
3. **Medical imaging software add-on**: Standalone plug-in for DICOM workstations (GE, Siemens, Philips) that improves reconstruction across vendors' modalities; pay-per-study licensing.
4. **Autonomous inspection platform**: Drones + BAM for infrastructure (bridges, power lines, solar arrays); removes motion blur and sensor noise in real-time to improve defect detection.
5. **Content restoration studio**: Web-based tool for archivists, photographers, and artists to restore old/damaged photos, film frames, and documents by specifying degradation physics—simpler and faster than current manual restoration or specialized model training.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Operator specification at inference**: The model assumes the forward operator *A* is *known*—blind inverse problems (where *A* is unknown) remain unsolved. Real-world blur and noise are often not precisely parameterizable.
2. **Limited to small-to-medium resolution**: Pre-training on standard benchmarks (FFHQ 1024×1024, DIV2K 2048×2048); scaling to 4K/8K imagery and high-dimensional tasks (volumetric MRI) is unexplored.
3. **Few-step sampling trade-off**: 3-step inference is fast but may sacrifice sample diversity or fine detail compared to longer sampling chains; exact number of steps needed per task not thoroughly characterized.
4. **Generalization to extreme corruptions**: Pre-training on natural images may fail on highly stylized, scientific, or adversarial corruptions far outside the training distribution.
5. **No theoretical guarantees**: Unlike classical Bayesian methods, no formal posterior convergence proof or uncertainty quantification; user must trust empirical results.
6. **Computational imaging assumptions**: Assumes linear forward models; non-linear physics (e.g., scattering, phase retrieval) requires different architectures.

## Open Questions
1. **Blind inverse problems**: Can BAM or a variant learn to jointly infer both the image *x* and unknown operator *A* from *y* alone? This would require architectural or pre-training innovations.
2. **Uncertainty quantification**: How can BAM output not just posterior samples but also pixel-level uncertainty estimates to inform downstream decisions in medical imaging?
3. **Non-linear physics**: Does the operator-conditioning framework extend naturally to non-linear forward models (e.g., Poisson imaging, phase retrieval, scattering)?
4. **Scaling laws**: What is the sample complexity (pre-training data size, operator diversity) needed to achieve current performance? Is there a "sweet spot" for model size vs. generalization?
5. **Real-world robustness**: How does BAM perform when the specified operator *A* is misspecified or when real-world noise deviates from the training distribution?
6. **Few-shot adaptation**: What is the minimal fine-tuning cost (data + compute) to specialize BAM to a new sensor or domain, compared to training from scratch?

## Key Takeaway
A single lightweight generative model solves diverse image reconstruction tasks by conditioning on physics at inference time, eliminating per-task retraining.
