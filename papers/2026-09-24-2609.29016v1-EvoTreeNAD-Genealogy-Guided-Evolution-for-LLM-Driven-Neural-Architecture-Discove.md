# EvoTreeNAD: Genealogy-Guided Evolution for LLM-Driven Neural Architecture Discovery

**Authors:** Lishan Yu, Derek Jiu, Qizhen Lan, Xiaoqian Jiang
**Published:** 2026-09-24 | **arXiv:** [2609.29016v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29016v1)

## Summary
This paper presents EvoTreeNAD, a system that uses LLM agents to automatically discover neural network architectures by growing a family tree of designs and selecting promising lineages based on performance. Rather than searching a predefined space, the method starts from scratch and lets an evolutionary process guided by historical context find better architectures — achieving state-of-the-art results on image classification benchmarks without requiring manual specification of what to search over.

## Theoretical Background
Neural architecture search (NAS) and neural architecture discovery (NAD) are methods to automatically find good network designs instead of hand-crafting them. Traditional NAS restricts the search to a predefined space of building blocks; NAD removes this constraint by generating architectures more freely. The paper builds on evolutionary algorithms — optimization methods inspired by natural selection that iteratively refine a population of candidate solutions. It combines this with recent advances in LLM agents (language models that can reason and generate code), which can propose architecture modifications and implement them. The key insight is that instead of treating each architecture evaluation as independent, the method maintains a genealogy — a persistent record of ancestry — so that successful design patterns can be inherited and refined across generations.

## Core Contributions
1. **Genealogy-guided selection mechanism**: Rather than random mutation or simple fitness ranking, the algorithm selects which lineages to continue by computing top-percentile performance values across entire family branches. This biases exploration toward proven design directions.
2. **LLM-based proposal and implementation**: An "Idea Agent" (LLM) proposes architectural variants in natural language; a "Code Agent" converts these into executable implementations — bridging human-like reasoning with executable code.
3. **Open-ended discovery without seed or search space**: The method starts from an empty architecture and grows candidates without requiring hand-specified primitives or operation sets, increasing generality.
4. **Theoretical analysis of stationary regimes**: The paper proves that under certain variation assumptions, sustained top-percentile family values emerge as the genealogy grows, providing probabilistic guarantees on the likelihood of generating high-reward architectures.
5. **Empirical validation**: Discovered architectures outperform prior NAS/NAD baselines on CIFAR-10/100 and all six MedMNIST-v2 medical imaging tasks.

## Connections to Other Work
This work sits at the intersection of [[evolutionary algorithms]], [[large language models as agents]], and automated machine learning. It extends classical evolutionary strategies by adding genealogy tracking (similar to lineage-based selection in genetic algorithms) and leverages recent breakthroughs in LLM reasoning. It differs from prior NAS work (e.g., DARTS, ENAS) which typically assume a fixed search space, and from simpler LLM-based generation approaches that lack cumulative feedback. The multi-agent structure (Idea Agent + Code Agent) echoes [[multi-agent-systems]] for AI-driven discovery, and the top-percentile selection relates to quality-diversity optimization and parent selection strategies in evolutionary computation.

## Practical Applications
1. **Automated computer vision model design**: Enterprises can discover task-specific neural architectures for medical imaging, satellite imagery, or autonomous vehicles without ML expertise in NAS.
2. **Fast prototyping in resource-constrained settings**: Startups or research labs with limited compute can use LLM-guided discovery to find efficient architectures before scaling up to expensive hyperparameter tuning.
3. **Domain-specific architecture search**: Financial institutions could adapt the method to discover architectures for time-series forecasting or anomaly detection tailored to market data distributions.
4. **Federated or edge AI**: Discovering lightweight architectures that perform well on edge devices or under communication constraints (e.g., mobile, IoT) where manual design is inefficient.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **AutoML SaaS platform** — Cloud service that takes task description, data samples, and budget constraints; returns optimized neural architectures and code without users needing to specify a search space or primitive operations.
2. **Specialized NAS for medical imaging** — Vertically integrated tool for hospitals and biotech firms to discover domain-adapted architectures for diagnostic imaging (MedMNIST validated this).
3. **LLM-guided architecture marketplace** — Catalog of discovered architectures versioned by lineage; users browse, fine-tune, or re-evolve for their tasks; genealogy data feeds community insights.
4. **Interactive AI design studio** — IDE-like interface where engineers propose high-level architectural ideas in English; system expands via evolution and shows top candidates with convergence plots and lineage trees for interpretability.
5. **Hardware-aware architecture discovery** — Extend genealogy selection to jointly optimize for accuracy and deployment cost (latency, memory, energy); target edge and IoT manufacturers.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Evaluation cost remains high**: Although genealogy reduces redundant exploration, each new architecture still requires training and evaluation. The paper does not eliminate the fundamental bottleneck of expensive experiments; it only makes the search smarter.
2. **LLM proposal quality dependency**: The Idea Agent's ability to generate useful architectural variants depends on prompt engineering and LLM capability; poor proposals waste evaluations. No ablation on LLM size or fine-tuning.
3. **Limited generalization evidence**: Strong results on CIFAR-10/100 and MedMNIST (small images, relatively simple domains), but no evaluation on large-scale ImageNet, video, or other modalities where architectural constraints differ.
4. **Scalability of genealogy tree**: As the family tree grows large, memory and retrieval of lineage statistics could become bottlenecks; paper does not discuss pruning or tree management strategies.
5. **Theoretical assumptions may not hold**: The stationary regime analysis assumes specific variation distributions; if LLM proposals deviate significantly or evaluation noise is high, theoretical guarantees weaken.
6. **Reproducibility and code complexity**: Multi-agent system with LLM calls introduces randomness; exact reproduction may be difficult without careful seed management and LLM versioning.

## Open Questions
1. How does performance scale to significantly larger architecture spaces (e.g., multi-modal models, transformers) where evaluation cost is orders of magnitude higher?
2. Can the genealogy-guided strategy transfer to other open-ended design problems (e.g., circuit design, molecular structures) or is it specific to neural architectures?
3. What is the optimal genealogy update frequency and tree pruning strategy? At what point do old branches become dead weight?
4. How sensitive is the method to LLM quality, prompt design, and Code Agent correctness? Would fine-tuning a domain-specific LLM improve discovery?
5. Can the approach be extended to multi-objective optimization (accuracy *and* latency, fairness, or energy efficiency) while maintaining genealogy-guided selection?
6. Does the discovered architecture structure exhibit interpretable patterns (e.g., modular designs, attention-like operations) that humans could learn from, or are they opaque?

## Key Takeaway
LLM agents guided by evolutionary genealogy trees discover better neural architectures without predefined search spaces, achieving state-of-the-art image classification results.
