# Evolving Self-Organising Agents Without Fitness: Three Falsifiable Experiments from Constraint-Driven Selection to Developmental Encoding

**Authors:** Anushka Sharma
**Published:** 2026-07-18 | **arXiv:** [2607.21630v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21630v1)

## Summary
This paper asks whether evolution can produce increasingly complex organisms without a designer-specified fitness function—only physical constraints. The authors built Genesis, a simulation where agents evolve in a chemical substrate, and ran three controlled experiments with falsifiable predictions. They found that constraints alone drive some evolution, but only when agents use an indirect genome encoding (CPPN) do they achieve genuine structural complexity growth.

## Theoretical Background
The paper builds on open-ended evolution—the study of systems that generate novelty indefinitely without explicit fitness targets. It draws from developmental biology (how organisms grow from simple rules) and evolutionary computation, particularly neuroevolution (evolving neural networks). The work engages with Gray-Scott reaction-diffusion models, which simulate chemical pattern formation in nature. It also uses NEAT (NeuroEvolution of Augmenting Topologies), a technique that evolves both neural network weights and structure while protecting diversity through speciation. The key conceptual tension is whether fitness functions are necessary for complex evolution, or whether constraints alone suffice.

## Core Contributions
1. **Genesis platform**: A testbed combining reaction-diffusion chemistry with embodied agent evolution under pure constraint-driven selection (no designer fitness).
2. **Three falsifiable hypotheses tested systematically**: Rather than claiming success, the authors treat null results as informative data points, establishing a reusable diagnostic protocol.
3. **Evidence that indirect encoding matters**: Only when agents use CPPN genomes (which encode development rules rather than direct morphology) does progressive complexity emerge—a mechanistic finding absent in prior work.
4. **Sham-control methodology**: A rigorous framework for evaluating open-ended evolution claims, applicable beyond this work.

## Practical Applications
1. **Robotics design**: Automated morphology discovery for robots that must adapt to unknown environments—constraints (material limits, energy) replace hand-coded objectives.
2. **Drug discovery**: Simulating molecular systems evolving under biochemical constraints rather than researcher-specified fitness, finding novel stable structures.
3. **Game AI**: Non-player character evolution driven by world physics rather than explicit scoring, generating more naturalistic agent behavior.
4. **Synthetic biology**: Predicting how biological systems evolve under environmental chemical gradients without external selection pressure.

## Product & Startup Ideas

2. **Morpho-AI**: Generative design tool for mechanical/soft robotics that evolves morphologies under physical constraints (material cost, weight, space) without explicit CAD objectives.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Scalability unclear**: Experiments run on small, controlled substrates—unclear if principles hold in higher-dimensional, realistic chemical/physical systems.
2. **CPPN advantage not fully mechanistic**: The paper shows indirect encoding works but doesn't deeply explain *why*—the causal mechanism remains somewhat black-box.
3. **Limited agent morphology**: Agents in Genesis are relatively simple; unclear if complexity ceiling persists with richer body plans or sensory modalities.
4. **Niche construction undershooting**: The paper shows niche construction (via chemical secretion) is "real but causally insufficient"—the boundary conditions for when it becomes *sufficient* remain unclear.
5. **Generalization risk**: Results are specific to Gray-Scott chemistry; whether findings transfer to other constraint systems (gravity, friction, etc.) is untested.
6. **Computational cost**: CPPN+NEAT systems are computationally expensive; practical scalability for real-world applications unclear.

## Open Questions
1. **Mechanism of CPPN advantage**: What properties of indirect encoding unlock complexity in constraint-driven systems? Is it evolvability, search-space structure, or developmental flexibility?
2. **Niche construction conditions**: Under what substrate/constraint configurations does niche construction become *sufficient* to drive complexity?
3. **Transferability**: Do these dynamics hold in other physical systems (gravity, magnetic fields, thermodynamics)?
4. **Sensorimotor coupling**: What happens when agents have rich sensors and actuators? Does the fitness-free paradigm still hold?
5. **Comparative benchmarks**: How does complexity growth in Genesis compare quantitatively to fitness-based evolution or biological development?
6. **Scaling laws**: Is there a phase transition—a substrate size or agent population threshold above which constraint-driven selection breaks down?

## Key Takeaway
Evolution produces growing complexity without fitness functions when agents use indirect developmental encodings under physical constraints.
