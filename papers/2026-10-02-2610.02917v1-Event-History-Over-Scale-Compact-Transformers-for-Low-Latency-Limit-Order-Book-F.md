# Event History Over Scale: Compact Transformers for Low-Latency Limit Order Book Forecasting

**Authors:** David Schaurecker, Lasse B. Strand, Kevin O'Sullivan, Robert Jakob
**Published:** 2026-10-02 | **arXiv:** [2610.02917v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02917v1)

## Summary
This paper introduces MBOFormer and MBOFusion, tiny transformer models (7–14 KB) designed to predict short-term price movements in equity and electricity markets using fine-grained order book event data. The key innovation is achieving high accuracy predictions while maintaining sub-millisecond inference latency and extreme model compactness—critical for real-time trading systems where speed and deployment constraints matter as much as accuracy.

## Theoretical Background
The work builds on two traditions. First, [[transformers]] — neural networks using attention mechanisms to process sequences — have become standard for time-series forecasting, though most prior applications use much larger models. Second, market-microstructure research (the study of how trades occur and how order books function) has shown that fine-grained event sequences—individual order submissions, cancellations, and executions—contain predictive signal for prices. Previous work typically either used coarse "level-2" order book snapshots (aggregated prices and volumes at each price level) or relied on large, slow models. This paper's insight is that causal transformers can extract signal efficiently from raw event histories without bloating model size.

## Core Contributions
1. **Extreme model compression for transformers**: MBOFormer achieves 7,203 parameters (and MBOFusion 14,371) while maintaining competitive or superior predictive performance—5–8× smaller than typical baselines with comparable accuracy.
2. **Event-level processing advantage**: Processing market-by-order (level-3) data—raw order submissions, cancellations, executions—outperforms baselines that sample coarse snapshots, showing that fine granularity enables better feature extraction in compact models.
3. **Production-grade latency**: Both models achieve sub-millisecond median inference on a single CPU thread (Apple M2), demonstrating viability for real-time deployment without specialized hardware.
4. **Consistent generalization**: Highest F1 scores in 11 of 12 test configurations across three real markets and four prediction horizons, validating the approach beyond a single market regime.

## Connections to Other Work
This paper sits at the intersection of [[deep learning]] for finance and efficient neural architecture design. It implicitly challenges the "bigger is better" paradigm that dominates LLM research (see [[large-language-models]]) by showing that domain-specific inductive biases (causal masking, event-based input design) can replace scale. The work relates to [[algorithmic-trading]] systems that require sub-millisecond decisions, and to broader [[optimization]] of neural networks for edge and embedded deployment. It also echoes recent interest in mechanistic interpretability: smaller models are often easier to understand. The "slow temporal-context branch" in MBOFusion resembles multi-scale or hierarchical attention patterns seen in other efficient transformer variants.

## Practical Applications
1. **High-frequency equity trading**: Proprietary trading firms can deploy these models on commodity hardware (laptops, edge servers) to forecast minute-to-second-scale price moves without expensive GPU/FPGA infrastructure.
2. **Intraday electricity market forecasting**: Utilities and energy traders use the model to predict real-time electricity prices for better bidding and hedging decisions in day-ahead and intraday markets.
3. **Retail trading platforms**: Brokerages can embed these models in mobile/desktop apps to provide clients with sub-second price-trend signals without server round-trips.
4. **Market surveillance**: Regulators and exchanges can run these models on historical/live order book feeds to detect anomalies or manipulative trading patterns in real time.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **Order-book analytics SaaS**: Package MBOFormer as a cloud API that ingests level-3 market data and returns price-direction predictions; target prop trading firms and HFT shops avoiding GPU CapEx.
2. **Edge trading intelligence**: Lightweight Python/C++ library for deployment on traders' own infrastructure—sell to mid-market quant funds that want to avoid cloud latency and data-residency concerns.
3. **Electricity market forecast service**: Real-time forecasting platform for power traders; combine MBOFormer with grid data to sell predictions to energy retailers, balance-sheet managers, and grid operators.
4. **Mobile trading assistant app**: Embed these models in a retail trading app (iOS/Android) to show micro-timescale price-trend alerts directly on the user's phone—no server latency, privacy-preserving.
5. **Market microstructure analytics**: B2B platform for regulators and exchanges to monitor order book health and detect market stress; visualize event-level predictions and flag anomalies in real time.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Short-horizon only**: Models predict intraday/sub-minute trends; useless for longer-term investment or portfolio decisions. Market regimes that shift over hours or days will break the learned patterns.
2. **Market-specific training**: Each market (equities, electricity, others) requires retraining; no evidence these models transfer zero-shot across asset classes or market conditions.
3. **Event ordering assumption**: Assumes order book events are causally ordered and sufficiently granular; may fail in markets with batch auctions, circuit breakers, or extreme volatility where event streams become irregular.
4. **Survivorship/data bias**: Backtest results depend on the quality and completeness of historical order book feeds; real-time data corruption or exchange feeds delays could degrade performance unpredictably.
5. **No transaction costs**: Predictions are raw price-direction forecasts; profitability depends on slippage, commissions, and spread, which the paper does not model. Sub-millisecond latency doesn't guarantee profitable trades.
6. **Causality leakage risk**: Causal masking in training is not foolproof; if test scenarios include partial fills or latency artifacts not in training data, the model may "look ahead" inadvertently.

## Open Questions
1. **Out-of-distribution robustness**: How do these models perform during market crashes, flash crashes, or regime shifts (e.g., regulatory changes, mergers)? Do they need continual retraining?
2. **Cross-market transfer**: Can a model trained on liquid equities generalize to illiquid assets or different markets (crypto, FX) without full retraining?
3. **Interpretability**: Which order-book features (cancellations, large orders, execution patterns) matter most for predictions? Can we extract actionable trading signals or rules?
4. **Ensemble and risk**: How do ensembles of these models perform? Can we quantify prediction uncertainty or calibrate confidence scores for position sizing?
5. **Adversarial robustness**: If traders know these models exist, can they strategically manipulate the order book to fool them? How stable are predictions under small perturbations to event sequences?
6. **Multi-horizon prediction**: Current work focuses on single horizons; can a single model predict multiple time scales (1s, 10s, 100s ms) jointly without losing accuracy?

## Key Takeaway
Tiny transformers reading raw order events beat large models at predicting price moves in milliseconds, enabling real-time trading on any computer.
