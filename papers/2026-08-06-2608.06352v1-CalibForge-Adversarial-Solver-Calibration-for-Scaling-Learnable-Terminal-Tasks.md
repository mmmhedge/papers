# CalibForge: Adversarial Solver Calibration for Scaling Learnable Terminal Tasks

**Authors:** Fanzhe Meng, Guoxin Chen, Jiale Zhao, Shuang Sun, Zhiyu Lin et al.
**Published:** 2026-08-06 | **arXiv:** [2608.06352v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06352v1)

## Summary
Training terminal agents requires executable and verifiable tasks that are not merely solvable, but appropriately challenging for learning. Executable validation establishes feasibility, yet does not reveal how a task behaves relative to a given solver setting. In this paper, we present CalibForge, an autonomous terminal-task synthesis system that uses verified solver behavior to revise candidate tasks through adversarial solver calibration.

## Theoretical Background
AI agents combine a language model with tools, memory, and an execution loop. Their quality depends not only on individual answers but on decisions made across a complete trajectory, which makes evaluation and debugging harder than ordinary text generation.

The paper is positioned against prior approaches described in its abstract and asks whether a more targeted representation, evaluation method, or optimization procedure can improve the relevant trade-off.

## Core Contributions
1. In this paper, we present CalibForge, an autonomous terminal-task synthesis system that uses verified solver behavior to revise candidate tasks through adversarial solver calibration.
2. Multi-solver calibration targets disagreement within a heterogeneous solver pool, whereas contrastive solver calibration targets a designated strong-pass/weak-fail relation; both operationalize a solver-relative learnable zone anchored in demonstrated solvability.
3. Our ablations show that both strategies yield more effective supervision than authoring and validation alone or ordinary single-solver feedback.
