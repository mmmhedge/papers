# Handling Missing Data in Probabilistic Regression Trees

**Authors:** Taiane Schaedler Prass, Alisson Silva Neimaier, Guilherme Pumi
**Published:** 2026-08-06 | **arXiv:** [2608.06195v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06195v1)

## Summary
Probabilistic Regression Trees (PRTrees) are a smooth and consistent alternative to classical regression trees, producing continuous predictions through probabilistic split assignments. This paper extends the PRTree framework to accommodate missing predictor values directly during tree construction, eliminating the need for prior imputation. Three strategies are proposed, each exploiting the available information differently: a uniform-probability approach, a partial-observation approach, and a dimension-reduced smoothing approach.

## Theoretical Background
Neural networks learn distributed representations from data. Their practical value depends on how well those representations preserve task-relevant information, generalize to new inputs, and can be computed efficiently.

## Core Contributions
1. Three strategies are proposed, each exploiting the available information differently: a uniform-probability approach, a partial-observation approach, and a dimension-reduced smoothing approach.
2. The proposed methods are evaluated on several real-world datasets exhibiting different levels of missingness and are compared with classical regression trees.
3. The results show that the effectiveness of probabilistic tree construction depends strongly on the treatment of missing observations.
