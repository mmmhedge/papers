# Taming the Greeks: Option Portfolios with Inductive Biases

**Authors:** Wee Ling Tan, Stephen Roberts, Stefan Zohren
**Published:** 2026-09-27 | **arXiv:** [2609.33767v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33767v1)

## Summary
This paper presents a deep learning framework for options trading that enforces specific risk constraints during training by penalizing unwanted portfolio sensitivities. Unlike standard neural networks that optimize returns blindly or reinforcement learning methods that simulate markets, this approach directly embeds hedging behavior—ensuring portfolios stay neutral to chosen risk factors while improving returns—and demonstrates better real-world performance on equity options data.

## Theoretical Background
Options trading relies on understanding "Greeks"—mathematical sensitivities that measure how an option's value changes with underlying price movements (delta), volatility (vega), time decay (theta), and curvature (gamma). Traditional approaches use rules-based hedging: traders manually construct portfolios to achieve delta-neutrality or other risk targets. Neural networks can learn to optimize risk-adjusted returns (like Sharpe ratio) from historical data, but they typically don't enforce constraints on these Greeks. Reinforcement learning methods approximate optimal hedging by simulating market dynamics and learning policies through trial-and-error, but require strong assumptions about market behavior. This paper sits at the intersection: using deep learning to directly optimize constrained objectives on historical data, without RL's reliance on simulation.

## Core Contributions
1. **Inductive bias framework**: Explicitly embeds hedging constraints into the neural network loss function via differentiable Greek-penalty terms, forcing the learned portfolio to respect risk neutrality objectives.
2. **Two penalty variants**: Proposes exposure-normalized penalty (controls raw directional exposure) and Greek-ratio drift penalty (controls how exposures evolve), offering flexibility for different risk management goals.
3. **Historical-data optimization**: Operates entirely on real market data without needing market simulators, making it more grounded than RL approaches and easier to implement.
4. **Empirical validation**: Demonstrates that regularized networks achieve better out-of-sample Sharpe ratios *and* lower realized directional exposure simultaneously on Nasdaq 100 options—solving the tension between return-chasing and risk control.

## Connections to Other Work
This builds on recent work showing neural networks can beat traditional trading strategies, but addresses a key criticism: learned portfolios lack interpretability and risk discipline. ## Practical Applications
1. **Systematic options desks**: Hedge funds and prop trading firms can use this to automate straddle portfolio management, reducing manual monitoring while maintaining risk limits.
2. **Retail options platforms**: Embedded in trading apps to automatically construct and rebalance hedged options positions without requiring users to understand Greeks.
3. **Risk management overlays**: Banks use the framework to monitor institutional options books and flag portfolios drifting from delta-neutrality targets.
4. **Volatility arbitrage**: Market makers can systematically exploit options mispricing while staying hedged against directional moves.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **"Greeks Guardian"**: A SaaS platform that ingests a user's options portfolio and continuously recommends rebalancing trades to maintain delta-neutrality and other risk targets, marketed to wealth advisors.
2. **Algorithmic options market-maker**: A trading bot that uses this framework to post tight bid-ask spreads on equity options while automatically hedging directional exposure in real-time.
3. **Risk audit tool**: Enterprise software for options desks (banks, hedge funds) that trains the model on each desk's historical trades and predicts whether new strategies will exceed risk thresholds.
4. **Retail options education + execution**: An app teaching retail traders options Greeks, then executing their strategies using the constrained neural network as a "safety layer" to prevent over-leverage.
5. **Institutional portfolio insurance**: Offer structured products where the issuer uses this framework to dynamically hedge client portfolios within specified volatility/drawdown bands.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Static straddle focus**: Evaluated only on delta-neutral straddles; unclear if the approach scales to more complex multi-leg strategies or dynamic rebalancing schedules.
2. **Historical-data assumption**: Requires sufficient clean historical options data; performance degrades in new markets or during regime shifts (e.g., crisis periods with structural breaks).
3. **Greek blindness to higher-order effects**: Delta-neutrality controls first-order risk but doesn't directly address gamma (convexity), vega (volatility), or vanna (cross-Greeks), which matter in turbulent markets.
4. **Computational cost**: Training on large option surfaces (many strikes, maturities) is computationally expensive; unclear if the approach scales to full equity index option books.
5. **Causality assumption**: Optimizes on historical correlations; if underlying market microstructure or option-pricing dynamics change, learned hedges may break.

## Open Questions
1. **Multi-Greek objectives**: Can the framework simultaneously enforce neutrality across delta, gamma, and vega without trading off performance? Does a single shared network or ensemble of specialized networks work better?
2. **Regime adaptation**: How does the model perform across market regimes (bull, bear, high-volatility)? Would online learning or periodic retraining help?
3. **Transaction costs**: Real trading involves bid-ask spreads and fees; how much of the performance gain remains after accounting for rebalancing costs?
4. **Longer horizons**: Results are likely short-term; what happens over weeks or months? Does drift accumulate despite the penalty?
5. **Interpretability**: Can we extract human-readable rules from the learned hedging policy, or is it a black box? Would attention mechanisms help?

## Key Takeaway
Deep learning can embed explicit risk constraints into options trading strategies, achieving both better returns and tighter risk control on real data without simulating markets.
