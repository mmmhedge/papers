# From Siloed Algorithms to Compliance-First Agentic Platforms: A Multi-Layered Architecture for Hospital AI Systems

**Authors:** Manideep Dhar, Ritwik Singh, Sharat Chandra Kumar Manikonda
**Published:** 2026-08-06 | **arXiv:** [2608.06112v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06112v1)

## Summary
Hospitals are rapidly adopting artificial intelligence for triage, imaging, scheduling etc., yet most deployments remain isolated point solutions locked inside departmental silos, resulting in duplicated effort, hidden risks, and unrealized enterprise value. Despite explosive growth of AI in healthcare market and accelerating investment, an estimated 70-80% of healthcare AI pilots fail to scale, largely due to governance gaps, fragmented data, and missing integration blueprints. This research proposes a hospital-specific, compliance-first, Agentic AI architecture with multiple interoperable layers, extending existing hospital AI platform models with: (i) an Agent Orchestration Layer for multi-agent workflows across clinical, operational, and financial domains, (ii) a Compliance and Policy Layer that centralizes policy-as-code for HIPAA, GDPR, the EU AI Act, DISHA Act, India's DPDP Act, and ISO/IEC security and safety standards, and (iii) a Privacy-Preserving Data Fabric that plugs federated learning, differential privacy, and secure enclaves into real-world Hospital Information Management System (HIMS) flows.

## Theoretical Background
High-stakes predictive systems require traceable inputs, calibrated uncertainty, and evidence that their outputs generalize beyond the development data. Operational use also demands clear failure modes and human oversight.

The paper is positioned against prior approaches described in its abstract and asks whether a more targeted representation, evaluation method, or optimization procedure can improve the relevant trade-off.

## Core Contributions
1. This research proposes a hospital-specific, compliance-first, Agentic AI architecture with multiple interoperable layers, extending existing hospital AI platform models with: (i) an Agent Orchestration Layer for multi-agent workflows across clinical, operational, and financial domains, (ii) a Compliance and Policy Layer that centralizes policy-as-code for HIPAA, GDPR, the EU AI Act, DISHA Act, India's DPDP Act, and ISO/IEC security and safety standards, and (iii) a Privacy-Preserving Data Fabric that plugs federated learning, differential privacy, and secure enclaves into real-world Hospital Information Management System (HIMS) flows.

## Technical Limitations
This note is based on the title, metadata, and abstract rather than a full-paper audit. The abstract cannot establish implementation completeness, statistical robustness, or whether all baselines were tuned comparably. Reported gains may depend on the selected datasets, models, task definitions, and compute environment. Deployment would require independent validation on out-of-distribution inputs, measurement of total runtime and cost, and analysis of failure cases. Any high-stakes use would also need human oversight and domain-specific governance.
