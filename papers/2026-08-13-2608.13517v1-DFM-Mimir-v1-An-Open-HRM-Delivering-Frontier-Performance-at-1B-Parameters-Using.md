# DFM Mimir v1: An Open HRM Delivering Frontier Performance at 1B Parameters Using Only Permissible Post-Training Data

**Authors:** Peter Schneider-Kamp, Jacob Nielsen, Gianluca Barmina, Kenneth Enevoldsen, Lukas Galke Poech
**Published:** 2026-08-13 | **arXiv:** [2608.13517v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13517v1)

## Summary
Mimir v1 is a 1-billion-parameter language model trained entirely on permissible (legally and ethically licensed) datasets that matches the performance of much larger models. It achieves state-of-the-art results for Danish and competitive English performance, showing that you don't need scraped web data or restricted datasets to build frontier-quality models — a significant finding for researchers and organizations committed to open-source and ethical AI development.

## Theoretical Background
This work builds on the Hierarchical Reasoning Model (HRM) architecture, a transformer-based design that uses attention mechanisms to process language. The key prior insight is that most current large language models like GPT-4 or Claude rely on massive unlicensed web scrapes and proprietary datasets, creating both legal and ethical concerns. Mimir v1 instead uses a curated mixture of 161 openly licensed datasets (books, academic papers, permissively licensed code, etc.), applying standard techniques like next-token prediction during training. The authors compare against models like Qwen 3.5 4B and Gemma 4 E2B — larger competitors — to establish competitive positioning.

## Core Contributions
1. **First permissible-data frontier model at 1B scale**: Demonstrates that state-of-the-art performance is achievable without relying on legally ambiguous or non-consensual data sources.
2. **Danish language breakthrough**: Sets new SOTA on Danish benchmarks, directly addressing underrepresented languages in open-source ML.
3. **Dataset curation methodology**: Documents a 161-dataset mixture strategy and shows it can yield 4B-equivalent performance at 1B parameters through careful selection and balancing.
4. **Reproducible baselines**: Full model release on Hugging Face enables validation and downstream research without licensing friction.

## Connections to Other Work
Mimir v1 extends the original [[HRM-Text 1B]] with better post-training data. The permissible-data constraint parallels concerns raised in [[RLHF]] and [[alignment]] literature about training data provenance. It also connects to work on efficient scaling — showing that architecture + data quality can matter more than raw parameter count, relevant to [[optimization]] in neural networks.

## Practical Applications
- **Enterprise compliance**: Organizations in regulated industries (finance, healthcare, law) can now deploy capable models without legal risk from unlicensed training data.
- **Non-English markets**: Danish companies and institutions get a genuinely competitive open model, reducing reliance on English-centric closed services.
- **Academic research**: Researchers can iterate on language models without institutional IP constraints or data licensing overhead.
- **Open-source ecosystems**: Downstream fine-tuning projects (chatbots, coding assistants, domain adapters) gain a permissible base.
- **Government/NGO deployment**: Public institutions that require audit trails and ethical sourcing can adopt Mimir without controversy.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **Permissible Model Marketplace**: A curated platform selling fine-tuned Mimir variants (legal-domain, medical, code, Danish-specific) — monetizing the ethical model base for enterprises that need compliance.
2. **Regional Language Models SaaS**: Offer Mimir-scale models for 10–20 underrepresented languages (Scandinavian, Balkan, regional EU languages), capturing markets neglected by frontier labs.
3. **Enterprise Compliance Audit Tool**: Software that certifies whether an LLM's training data is permissible; use Mimir as flagship reference and audit competitors for clients.
4. **Open-Source Model Optimization Service**: Offer distillation, quantization, and fine-tuning services on Mimir to help startups ship ethical AI products faster than training from scratch.
5. **Responsible AI Consulting**: Position Mimir as proof-of-concept for ethical AI; sell consulting to enterprises transitioning from closed models, bundling training, governance, and deployment.

## Technical Limitations
- **Scale ceiling**: 1B parameters is competitive but not frontier-grade for highly complex reasoning, math, or code tasks — still outmatched by 70B+ models on hard benchmarks.
- **Permissible data scarcity**: The 161-dataset mixture is effective but finite; gains may plateau as the reusable permissible corpus is exhausted, unlike proprietary models with unlimited scrape access.
- **Language coverage**: Strong for English and Danish, but generalization to other languages is unclear; multilingual permissible data is sparse outside major European languages.
- **Benchmark inflation**: Competitive on current benchmarks, but these may have overlapped with permissible training data; performance on truly held-out tasks unknown.
- **Post-training details**: Paper does not extensively detail RLHF, instruction tuning, or safety procedures — practical robustness and misuse resistance not thoroughly evaluated.
- **Long-context limitations**: No mention of context window extension or long-sequence capabilities, a growing requirement for modern applications.

## Open Questions
- How does Mimir v1 performance degrade on domain shifts (e.g., specialized code, biomedical text) compared to closed-source models trained on web-scale data?
- Can the permissible-data strategy scale beyond 1B? What would a 10B or 100B permissible model look like, and what performance ceiling would it hit?
- How vulnerable is Mimir to adversarial inputs, jailbreaks, or harmful outputs compared to models with more aggressive RLHF safety training?
- Which of the 161 datasets contribute most to performance? Can the mixture be pruned or prioritized to reduce training cost further?
- Does Mimir generalize to languages beyond English and Danish using the same architecture, or do language-specific tuning steps become necessary?
- How do downstream fine-tuning outcomes compare (e.g., legal, medical, code) when using Mimir vs. proprietary bases?

## Key Takeaway
Permissible data and careful curation can yield state-of-the-art language models at 1B scale, opening ethical AI development to researchers without massive proprietary datasets.
