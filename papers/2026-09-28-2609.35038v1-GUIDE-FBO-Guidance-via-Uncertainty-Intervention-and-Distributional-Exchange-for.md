# GUIDE-FBO: Guidance via Uncertainty Intervention and Distributional Exchange for Federated Bayesian Optimization

**Authors:** Jintao Wei, Chenxi Li, Songhao Wang
**Published:** 2026-09-28 | **arXiv:** [2609.35038v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35038v1)

## Summary
This paper presents GUIDE-FBO, a federated Bayesian optimization framework where multiple distributed agents collaborate to optimize expensive black-box functions by exchanging compact probability distributions rather than raw data. The key innovation is a "Federated Interventional GP" that lets each agent use shared distributional information while maintaining local privacy and adapting to task differences, with theoretical guarantees that the method preserves standard optimization efficiency rates.

## Theoretical Background
Bayesian optimization uses Gaussian processes (GPs) — probabilistic models that learn surrogate functions from expensive evaluations — to guide exploration of black-box objectives efficiently. Federated learning distributes computation across agents without centralizing raw data. This paper bridges these by having agents share posterior beliefs (represented as distributions) rather than observations. The core theoretical tool is the upper confidence bound (UCB) strategy, which balances exploring uncertain regions against exploiting known good regions. The paper leverages regret analysis — a standard framework measuring optimization suboptimality — to prove that their uncertainty rescaling preserves convergence rates.

## Core Contributions
1. **Distributional message passing**: Agents exchange compact distributions over optima locations (inferred from local GP posteriors) instead of raw observations, query points, or model parameters — reducing communication overhead while preserving information.
2. **Federated Interventional GP (FI-GP)**: A novel local surrogate that preserves the posterior mean but spatially rescales covariance, enabling principled incorporation of federated signals while maintaining decision-making autonomy.
3. **Regret-preserving uncertainty intervention**: Proves that bounded covariance rescaling does not degrade the leading-order cumulative regret rate of standard GP-UCB, providing theoretical safety guarantees.
4. **Heterogeneity robustness**: Demonstrates effectiveness across homogeneous and severely heterogeneous task settings, with ablations isolating the importance of spatially localized uncertainty interventions.

## Connections to Other Work
This work extends classical [[Gaussian Process]] optimization into the federated regime, related to [[federated learning]] frameworks that prioritize privacy and communication efficiency. It complements [[RLHF]] and multi-agent learning by enabling distributed collaboration without model sharing. The regret analysis methodology aligns with bandit optimization theory. Unlike centralized hyperparameter tuning or distributed [[reinforcement-learning]] approaches, GUIDE-FBO preserves local autonomy while leveraging global signals — a middle ground between full centralization and isolated learning.

## Technical Limitations
- **GP scalability**: Gaussian processes scale poorly to high-dimensional spaces or very large datasets; GUIDE-FBO inherits this bottleneck and is best suited for small-to-moderate dimensional problems.
- **Distributional exchangeability assumption**: The method assumes agents' posterior distributions are meaningfully comparable (same parameter space); severely misaligned tasks may violate this.
- **Limited heterogeneity analysis**: While experiments show robustness, theoretical characterization of how task heterogeneity affects regret bounds is incomplete.
- **Communication rounds**: The paper doesn't deeply analyze latency or asynchronous failure modes common in real federated settings.
- **Black-box requirement**: Requires expensive-to-evaluate objectives; not suitable for problems with cheap evaluations or differentiable surrogates.
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity**: Covariance rescaling factors and distribution reweighting schemes likely require tuning; sensitivity analysis is limited.

## Open Questions
1. How do regret guarantees degrade as task heterogeneity increases quantitatively? Can tighter bounds be proved under distributional divergence assumptions?
2. Can this extend to non-Gaussian posteriors or variational approximations to handle higher-dimensional spaces?
3. How should the server's reweighting strategy adapt dynamically to discovered optima or changing task diversity?
4. What happens under Byzantine failures (malicious agents sending adversarial distributions)? Can robustness certificates be derived?
5. Can the framework incorporate bandit feedback or partial information per agent (e.g., noisy evaluations, delayed rewards)?
6. How does performance scale with the number of agents and communication constraints in adversarial settings?

## Key Takeaway
Federated agents optimize expensive functions efficiently by sharing lean distributional beliefs instead of raw data, with theoretical safety guarantees.
