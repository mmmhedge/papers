# Selective Credibility-Limited Belief Update

**Authors:** Theofanis Aravanis, Costas D. Koutras
**Published:** 2026-07-30 | **arXiv:** [2607.28523v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28523v1)

## Summary
This paper develops a formal framework for how intelligent agents should update their beliefs when they receive information from potentially unreliable sources. Standard belief update assumes all information can be incorporated fully, but real agents often receive partial or conflicting information. The authors extend existing credibility-limited approaches to let agents selectively accept parts of compound information, creating a more realistic and expressive model of belief revision.

## Theoretical Background
Belief update is a classical problem in epistemic logic and artificial intelligence — how should an agent's knowledge change when the world changes? The standard framework is Katsuno-Mendelzon (KM) update, which defines how to revise beliefs when new facts become true. Real-world belief update is more complex: sources have varying reliability (credibility), and compound information (e.g., "A and B") might be only partially believable. Earlier work introduced credibility-limited update to handle unreliable sources, but treated information as atomic — you either accept all of "A and B" or none of it. This paper builds on the formal semantics of possible-worlds models (where beliefs are represented as sets of possible scenarios an agent considers true) and adds selective transformation of epistemic inputs.

## Core Contributions
1. **Selective credibility-limited belief update**: A framework allowing agents to transform compound epistemic inputs into weaker versions (logical consequences) before credibility-limited transitions, enabling selective acceptance of parts of information.

2. **Semantic and axiomatic characterization**: Formal definitions and proof of what axioms these operators satisfy, showing they form a coherent logical system.

3. **Two principled sub-classes**: 
   - Consistency-preserving operators: transformed inputs remain believable from the source world if the original input was consistent.
   - Maximal consistency-preserving operators: additionally ensure the selected proxy is as informative as possible.

4. **Unification result**: Shows credibility-limited update and classical KM update emerge as special cases, proving the framework is genuinely more expressive while generalizing prior work.

## Connections to Other Work
This extends [[Katsuno-Mendelzon belief update]] (the foundational classical framework) and prior credibility-limited approaches. It relates to broader themes in epistemic logic and multi-agent reasoning — how agents reason with uncertain or conflicting information. The selective transformation idea resonates with work on [[reinforcement-learning]] agents updating on noisy rewards and [[alignment]] literature on how models should handle conflicting training signals. The formal axiomatic approach mirrors techniques in knowledge representation and automated reasoning.

## Practical Applications
- **Multi-source information fusion**: Systems that combine data from sensors or databases with different reliability levels (medical diagnosis with conflicting test results, autonomous vehicles reconciling sensor disagreements).
- **Dialogue systems and chatbots**: Updating beliefs when users provide potentially contradictory or partial information.
- **Fact-checking and misinformation handling**: Incorporating new information while accounting for source credibility and partial truth.
- **Collaborative multi-agent systems**: Agents updating shared beliefs based on reports from teammates with varying trustworthiness.
- **Knowledge base maintenance**: Systems that integrate conflicting or incomplete updates from multiple knowledge sources.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **Credibility-aware data fusion platform**: Enterprise tool for integrating multi-source data (APIs, sensors, databases) with automatic source-weighting and selective belief update — marketed to IoT, robotics, or autonomous systems companies.

2. **Intelligent misinformation filter**: Browser extension or content platform that selectively incorporates claims from news sources by credibility and extracts maximally informative consistent facts for users.

3. **Multi-agent collaboration middleware**: Framework for robotics teams or distributed systems to resolve conflicting state estimates from agents with varying sensor quality using principled belief update logic.

4. **Medical decision support system**: Tool for clinicians combining test results and expert opinions with explicit credibility modeling, showing which parts of conflicting diagnoses are most reliable.

5. **Adaptive learning platform**: Educational AI that updates student knowledge models by selectively accepting partial feedback from assessments (quizzes, peer review) weighted by reliability.

## Technical Limitations
- **Computational complexity**: The paper does not analyze complexity of finding maximal consistency-preserving proxies; in real systems with large information spaces this could be intractable.
- **Specification of credibility and transformation**: Agents must specify credibility restrictions and transformation functions — the framework provides no guidance on how to elicit or learn these from data.
- **No learning or adaptation**: The framework is static; it does not address how credibility scores or transformation rules should be updated as agents observe outcomes.
- **Scalability to many sources**: How the approach scales with dozens or hundreds of conflicting sources is unclear.
- **Empirical validation absent**: Paper is purely formal; no experiments demonstrate whether the axioms match human or AI belief-update behavior in practice.

## Open Questions
- Can credibility scores and transformation functions be automatically learned from data or interaction history?
- How does selective belief update perform in large-scale information fusion tasks compared to classical approaches (e.g., Bayesian networks, weighted averaging)?
- Do the proposed axioms (especially maximal consistency-preservation) align with human intuitions or practical agent behavior in multi-source settings?
- Can the framework be extended to handle probabilistic or uncertain credibility weights rather than hard restrictions?
- What are the computational complexity bounds for finding optimal proxies, and are there efficient algorithms for structured domains?

## Key Takeaway
Agents can rationally accept parts of conflicting information by transforming it based on source credibility — this paper formalizes how.
