# Joint Communication-Control Strategy Optimization with Partially Nested Information Structures: The Linear-Quadratic Case

**Authors:** Haoyi You, Kaiqing Zhang
**Published:** 2026-08-13 | **arXiv:** [2608.13535v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13535v1)

## Summary
This paper solves the problem of how multiple agents in a networked system should coordinate their communication and control decisions jointly. The key innovation is showing that under "partially nested" information structures — where agents have hierarchical knowledge about each other's states — the optimization problem becomes tractable and admits closed-form solutions via dynamic programming, even when agents must decide what to communicate to each other.

## Theoretical Background
This work builds on **decentralized stochastic control** and the **common-information-based (CIB) framework**, which provides a way to reformulate decentralized decision problems into centralized ones by tracking what information is commonly known among agents. The paper focuses on **linear-quadratic (LQ) problems** — systems where dynamics are linear and costs are quadratic, a classical setting in control theory because it often admits closed-form solutions. The notion of **information structure** describes what each agent knows when making decisions; a **partially nested** structure is one where some agents have strictly more information than others in a hierarchical way. Prior work on decentralized LQ control with nested information structures showed these admit linear optimal strategies; this paper extends that to include optimizable communication.

## Core Contributions
1. **Formalization of JCCO**: The paper formally defines joint communication-control strategy optimization under the CIB framework and identifies conditions under which partial nestedness is preserved when optimizing communication — violating these conditions causes optimal strategies to become nonlinear.
2. **Dynamic programming solution with Riccati equations**: Develops a DP-based computational approach that yields closed-form Riccati equations for the optimal control strategies, making the problem tractable rather than infinite-dimensional.
3. **Open-loop and closed-loop communication**: Extends the method to handle both open-loop (non-adaptive) and closed-loop (adaptive) communication strategies, with the closed-loop case being more computationally efficient than naive CIB-belief approaches.
4. **Decentralized LQ control with output feedback**: As a byproduct, the approach also solves a standing problem in decentralized control: LQ systems with output feedback under partially nested structures in the CIB framework.

## Connections to Other Work
This extends classical **decentralized control** theory (which assumes information structures are fixed) by making communication strategies endogenous. It relates to broader **multi-agent coordination** literature but differs by keeping the problem analytically tractable rather than resorting to approximation or learning. The use of the CIB framework connects to recent work on **team problems** and **stochastic dynamic games**, where information asymmetry is central. The preservation of structural properties (nestedness) under optimization parallels similar results in [[game theory]] where certain equilibrium properties are robust to perturbations.

## Practical Applications
- **Power grid coordination**: Multiple substations or microgrids need to share state information and coordinate reactive/active power control; deciding what to communicate and how often could reduce communication overhead while maintaining stability.
- **Multi-robot formation control**: Teams of robots navigating together need to share position/velocity estimates; the framework determines optimal message passing rates and feedback control given bandwidth constraints.
- **Autonomous vehicle platoons**: Vehicles in a convoy decide jointly what kinematic data to share and how to adjust acceleration/braking in response, minimizing fuel use and communication bandwidth.
- **Process control in manufacturing**: Distributed sensors and actuators in industrial plants coordinate valve positions and feedback, reducing latency and communication costs.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **Decentralized control optimization software**: A tool for systems engineers designing multi-agent control systems; inputs system dynamics and information constraints; outputs communication protocols and control laws.
2. **Smart grid coordination platform**: SaaS for utilities to optimize communication and control between distributed energy resources (batteries, solar, wind) and grid operators under bandwidth constraints.
3. **Autonomous fleet management**: Software-as-a-service for logistics/delivery companies to optimize communication overhead and control latency in vehicle fleets using this framework.
4. **Industrial IoT optimization engine**: Cloud service that takes plant topology, sensor/actuator specs, and communication budgets, then computes optimal networked control strategies.
5. **Adaptive communication middleware**: Library/framework for embedded systems that dynamically adjusts which agents communicate and at what frequency based on running the optimization online.

## Technical Limitations
- **Linear-quadratic restriction**: Real systems are often nonlinear (e.g., power flows, vehicle dynamics at high speeds); the framework doesn't directly apply, though approximations may be possible.
- **Partially nested assumption**: Many practical systems have more complex information structures (e.g., agents communicate with multiple peers asynchronously); the framework requires a specific hierarchical order.
- **Computational scalability unclear**: The paper doesn't discuss how Riccati equations scale with the number of agents or state dimension; very large networks may still be intractable.
- **Perfect communication model**: Assumes noiseless, delay-free communication; real networks have latency, packet loss, and quantization, which would complicate the analysis.
- **Offline optimization**: The approach computes strategies offline; adapting to time-varying or uncertain system parameters would require re-solving the dynamic program.
- **No learning or robustness**: The framework doesn't learn from data or handle model misspecification; if true dynamics differ from the LQ model, closed-form strategies may fail.

## Open Questions
- Can the partial nestedness preservation conditions be relaxed or generalized to less hierarchical information structures?
- How do the solutions degrade when communication is noisy, delayed, or bandwidth-constrained in ways not captured by the information structure?
- Can the approach be extended to infinite-horizon problems with discounting or average costs (the paper likely focuses on finite-horizon)?
- Is there a way to iteratively refine the baseline communication protocol to improve the final solutions, or does it require manual specification?
- Can online or adaptive versions of these algorithms be developed that recompute strategies as the system evolves or uncertainties are resolved?

## Key Takeaway
Agents can jointly optimize communication and control in networked systems under hierarchical information structures using closed-form solutions from dynamic programming.
