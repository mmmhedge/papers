# Top-down Traffic Scenario Generation via Joint Initial-Goal Diffusion and Trajectory Infilling

**Authors:** Da Saem Lee, Yash Vardhan Pant, Sebastian Fischmeister
**Published:** 2026-08-11 | **arXiv:** [2608.11407v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11407v1)

## Summary
This paper introduces TrafficDiffuser, a framework that generates realistic traffic scenarios by jointly modeling agent initial positions and goal destinations using diffusion models, then filling in trajectories between them. This addresses a key bottleneck in autonomous vehicle testing: existing simulators require manual specification of starting conditions, limiting the diversity and realism of generated scenarios. The approach makes scenario generation more interpretable and scalable for simulation-based AV development.

## Theoretical Background
The work builds on diffusion models — generative models that learn to reverse a noise-to-data process, commonly used in image generation and recently adapted for sequential data. Prior work in autonomous driving has tackled trajectory generation and agent initialization separately: trajectory generation models predict how agents move between two points, while initialization methods determine where agents should start. The paper's key insight is that modeling initial states and goals jointly (rather than conditioning on fixed initial states) makes the problem more tractable and interpretable. It relies on the Argoverse 2 dataset, a large-scale motion prediction benchmark capturing real-world driving behavior.

## Core Contributions
1. **Joint initial-goal modeling**: Instead of conditioning trajectory generation on fixed initial states, the paper models initial position and goal destination as a joint distribution using diffusion, making scenario generation both more diverse and interpretable.
2. **Trajectory infilling as simplification**: Once initial-goal pairs are generated, trajectory generation becomes an infilling problem — predicting what happens *between* two known points — rather than open-ended generation, which is significantly easier.
3. **Empirical validation on Argoverse 2**: Demonstrates substantial improvements over prior initialization methods (55.3% reduction in speed distribution distance, 2.8% lower off-road rate) while capturing realistic distributions of real-world driving.
4. **Modular framework**: Shows the generated scenarios can be constrained by trajectory modes and integrated with existing trajectory models, making it practical for existing simulators.

## Practical Applications
1. **Autonomous vehicle testing**: Generate diverse, realistic traffic scenarios for simulation-based testing of AV perception and planning algorithms without costly real-world data collection.
2. **Safety validation**: Create corner-case scenarios (rare but critical driving situations) by constraining generated initial-goal pairs to high-risk configurations.
3. **Simulation for learning**: Use generated scenarios as training data for imitation learning or reinforcement learning policies for autonomous driving.
4. **Traffic flow analysis**: Generate plausible multi-agent traffic patterns for urban planning, intersection design, or traffic signal optimization studies.
5. **Synthetic data augmentation**: Supplement limited real-world datasets with generated scenarios that preserve distributional properties of real traffic.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **SIM-AV Platform**: A cloud-based simulation service that generates unlimited diverse traffic scenarios on-demand for AV companies to test their stacks, replacing expensive real-world testing fleets.
2. **Scenario-as-a-Service**: An API that generates interpretable, constraint-based traffic scenarios for different use cases (highway merging, urban intersections, pedestrian interactions), sold to AV and robotics companies.
3. **Safety Certification Tool**: Automated scenario generation focused on edge cases and failure modes, helping AV manufacturers meet regulatory testing requirements faster.
4. **Synthetic Data Pipeline**: A B2B tool that generates high-fidelity synthetic datasets (trajectories + sensor simulations) for computer vision and motion prediction model training, addressing data scarcity for smaller AV teams.
5. **Traffic Behavior Analyzer**: A tool that learns and generates traffic patterns from historical city data, helping urban planners and traffic engineers prototype new intersection designs or signal timing with realistic agent behavior.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Distribution shift**: The model is trained on Argoverse 2 (primarily highway and urban driving in specific geographies); performance on different road types, weather conditions, or driving cultures is untested.
2. **Interpretability of joint modeling**: While generating initial-goal pairs is more interpretable than raw agent initialization, the diffusion model's learned joint distribution is still a "black box" — it's unclear *why* certain initial-goal combinations are preferred.
3. **Scalability to many agents**: The paper focuses on independent agent generation; coordinating realistic multi-agent interactions (e.g., merging, lane-changing with dependencies) is not addressed.
4. **Goal semantics**: "Goals" are defined as end-of-trajectory positions, but this may not capture richer intent (e.g., "turn left at the intersection" vs. "park in this spot") that matters for realistic scenario diversity.
5. **Trajectory infilling quality**: Infilling assumes reasonable paths exist between given initial-goal pairs; impossible or dangerous initial-goal combinations could still be generated and require post-hoc filtering.
6. **Computational cost**: Training large diffusion models is expensive; unclear how well the approach transfers to new datasets or domains without retraining.

## Open Questions
1. How does joint initial-goal modeling scale to scenarios with many interacting agents? Can you enforce realistic agent-agent dependencies?
2. Can the framework incorporate higher-level semantic constraints (e.g., "generate a highway merging scenario" or "urban intersection at rush hour") beyond trajectory modes?
3. How sensitive is the model to out-of-distribution initial-goal pairs? Can you reliably detect or prevent physically impossible scenarios?
4. Could the diffusion model be extended to jointly model other scenario properties (weather, time of day, traffic density) alongside initial-goal pairs?
5. How does synthetic data from TrafficDiffuser affect downstream AV model performance compared to real-world data? Is there evidence of sim-to-real transfer?
6. Can interpretability techniques help explain *why* certain initial-goal combinations are assigned high probability by the diffusion model?

## Key Takeaway
Diffusion models can jointly generate realistic traffic initial-goal pairs, turning trajectory generation into simpler infilling and enabling scalable, interpretable scenario generation for autonomous vehicle testing.
