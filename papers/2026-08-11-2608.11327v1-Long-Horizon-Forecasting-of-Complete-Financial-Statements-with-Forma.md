# Long-Horizon Forecasting of Complete Financial Statements with Forma

**Authors:** Travis L. Johnson, Jiannan Jiang, Soumyabrata Chaudhuri, Yihao Chen, Lauren Falvey et al.
**Published:** 2026-08-11 | **arXiv:** [2608.11327v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11327v1)

## Summary
This paper introduces Forma, a transformer-based model that forecasts complete financial statements (78 line items) for 1–20 quarters ahead—a task no prior work has tackled jointly at this scale. The key insight is that specialist architectures beat general-purpose scale for this domain; Forma outperforms classical ML, gradient boosting, zero-shot foundation models, and frontier LLMs, with its advantage widening at longer horizons where valuation matters most.

## Theoretical Background
Financial statement forecasting traditionally relies on time-series models (ARIMA, GARCH) or classical regression, which treat each line item independently and struggle beyond one year. Recent work has applied [[transformers]] to financial prediction, but mostly for stock returns or individual metrics. Discounted-cash-flow (DCF) valuation—the standard approach for estimating firm value—depends critically on long-horizon free-cash-flow forecasts, yet most cash value lies beyond year 1, making 5+ year forecasts essential but notoriously hard. This paper frames statements as sets of (account, quarter, value) tuples, enabling Forma to learn joint distributions over accounting items and their temporal evolution without treating time as a strict sequence.

## Core Contributions
1. **ProForma-20Q benchmark**: The first reproducible, 20-quarter forecasting benchmark with 78 statement line items across anonymized firms, scored by change-space R² (predicting quarter-over-quarter changes rather than absolute values).
2. **Forma architecture**: A transformer that reads statements as unordered tuples, predicts masked tuples via Gaussian likelihood, and naturally produces calibrated predictive intervals.
3. **Long-horizon superiority**: Demonstrates that Forma's accuracy *increases* relative to baselines at longer horizons (the opposite of typical time-series degradation), precisely where DCF valuations are most sensitive.
4. **Accounting coherence**: Forecasts nearly satisfy accounting identities (Assets = Liabilities + Equity) without explicit constraints, and exact coherence can be enforced at negligible accuracy cost.
5. **Scenario analysis capability**: The tuple interface enables conditional forecasting (e.g., fixing revenue paths) without retraining, supporting what-if analysis for practitioners.

## Connections to Other Work
Forma advances beyond [[attention is all you need]]-style sequential modeling by treating statements as set-like objects, reducing inductive bias. It contrasts with [[RLHF]]-inspired approaches (e.g., LLM fine-tuning for finance) by showing that domain-specific inductive biases (tuple structure, Gaussian likelihoods, accounting constraints) outweigh raw model scale. The calibrated intervals relate to [[uncertainty quantification]] in deep learning; the joint forecasting resembles multivariate time-series work but scales to 78 correlated targets. Unlike zero-shot foundation models, Forma is trained on financial data, demonstrating that specialist architectures remain vital in high-stakes domains.

## Practical Applications
- **Equity valuation**: Analysts use 5–10 year DCF models; Forma's 20-quarter forecasts reduce terminal-value assumption risk and improve deal pricing.
- **Credit analysis**: Lenders forecast debt-service coverage ratios and leverage; Forma's statement projections support loan underwriting and covenant monitoring.
- **Corporate planning**: CFOs use long-range financial projections for budgeting and investor guidance; Forma enables rapid scenario testing (e.g., revenue down 10%, how does EBITDA respond?).
- **Regulatory reporting**: Auditors and regulators need plausible forward-looking statements; Forma can flag unrealistic filings or stress-test solvency.
- **M&A due diligence**: Forecasting target company financials under different acquisition scenarios (cost synergies, revenue retention, tax effects) is critical for deal pricing.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **Fintech valuation SaaS**: A web tool where M&A advisors, PEs, and equity researchers upload historical financials and get 20-quarter projections with sensitivity analysis and scenario builder—monetize via subscription or per-company licensing.
2. **Credit risk platform for SMBs**: Banks and alternative lenders use Forma to assess small-business loan applications; model auto-generates forward-looking P&Ls and cash-flow statements, improving approval speed and default prediction.
3. **Investor intelligence API**: Sell institutional-grade statement forecasts to hedge funds and quant shops, bundled with consensus estimate comparison and anomaly detection (e.g., flagging inconsistent industry peers).
4. **Corporate FP&A co-pilot**: A real-time planning tool embedded in Excel/Tableau for corporate finance teams; auto-fills forecast templates, version-controls scenarios, and alerts to plan inconsistencies.
5. **Accounting software add-on**: Partner with QuickBooks or NetSuite to embed Forma as a "forecast assistant," helping SMB accountants auto-generate reliable projections for bank applications and board meetings.

## Technical Limitations
- **Data dependency**: Forma requires 4+ years of historical statements; early-stage or newly public firms have sparse training data.
- **Structural breaks**: The model assumes accounting and business model continuity; M&A, restructuring, or new segments violate that—Forma may extrapolate stale patterns.
- **Accounting policy changes**: Shifts in revenue recognition (ASC 606) or lease accounting (IFRS 16) are not explicitly handled; the model may confuse real change with policy change.
- **Extreme scenarios**: Long-horizon forecasts inherit compounding error; 20 quarters is ~5 years, and the model has not been tested on high-inflation, severe recession, or hyperinflationary regimes.
- **Causality**: Forma is correlational; it cannot explain *why* forecasts change and may miss novel business drivers (e.g., a new product launch not yet in historical data).
- **Identity preservation**: While "nearly satisfying" accounting identities is strong, the paper doesn't fully address how constraint enforcement scales or degrades under distribution shift.

## Open Questions
1. **Out-of-distribution robustness**: How does Forma perform on firms entering new industries, pivoting business models, or facing unprecedented shocks (e.g., COVID, interest-rate regimes)?
2. **Explainability for practitioners**: Can we decompose Forma's forecasts into "fundamentals" (e.g., margin compression, asset turnover changes) that analysts can sanity-check?
3. **Real-time learning**: Can Forma adapt to new quarterly data without full retraining? Does incremental learning preserve calibration?
4. **Cross-company transfer**: Does a model trained on, say, tech firms transfer to retail? What about emerging markets vs. US GAAP?
5. **Ensemble with analyst views**: How should practitioners optimally blend Forma's forecasts with sell-side consensus or management guidance?
6. **Valuation impact**: A downstream question: do portfolios or deals priced using Forma-based DCF valuations outperform those using traditional point forecasts?

## Key Takeaway
Specialist transformers beat foundation models at multi-year financial statement forecasting, with accuracy improving at longer horizons where valuation matters most.
