# ExtractBench: A Benchmark for Schema-Guided Enterprise Document Extraction

**Authors:** Boyang Zhang, Adrian Lyjak, Eli Stewart, Zhaoqi Li, Simon Suo
**Published:** 2026-07-31 | **arXiv:** [2607.29677v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29677v1)

## Summary
This paper introduces ExtractBench, a benchmark for evaluating how well AI agents extract structured data from enterprise documents according to user-defined schemas. The benchmark is unique because it measures not just extraction accuracy but also whether agents can prove where they found their answers (grounding), handle long documents, and do so cost-effectively — addressing real pain points in enterprise automation.

## Theoretical Background
Schema-guided extraction builds on information extraction, a classical NLP task where systems identify and organize specific data from unstructured text. Modern approaches combine large language models (LLMs — neural networks trained on massive text to predict sequences) with agentic frameworks (systems that can break tasks into steps, call tools, and reason through problems). Prior work in document understanding has focused on either short-form extraction or visual document processing, but enterprise documents often mix complex layouts, long lists, and multi-page contexts that challenge current systems.

## Core Contributions
- **First comprehensive benchmark** combining four metrics simultaneously: value accuracy (F1), record completeness at scale, grounding evidence (both word and page level), and cost measurement
- **Scalable curation pipeline** mixing three validation methods: independent system agreement for real docs, known-value synthetic lists, and human verification for forms
- **Real-world scale and diversity**: 4,869 pages across 370 actual enterprise documents spanning 8 business domains and 67 document types
- **Empirical findings**: VLMs (vision language models) perform well on short documents but systematically truncate long lists; coding agents stay accurate but cost far more; LlamaExtract Agentic Plus balances accuracy and cost effectively
- **Public release** with code and data for reproducibility

## Connections to Other Work
This extends classical information extraction and [[Named Entity Recognition]] traditions into the agentic era. It complements benchmarks like CORD (receipt understanding) and DocVQA (document question-answering) by focusing on *schema fidelity* — the agent must follow user instructions precisely, not just find answers. Related to [[Retrieval-Augmented Generation]] in that grounding evidence mirrors the "show your work" requirement. Connects to broader [[agents]] research around tool use and reasoning.

## Practical Applications
- **Insurance claim processing**: Extract coverage limits, claim amounts, policy numbers from mixed-format documents with proof of source
- **Contract lifecycle management**: Pull obligations, dates, and payment terms from long legal documents while maintaining audit trails
- **Accounts payable automation**: Extract invoice line items, vendors, and amounts with verifiable grounding for compliance
- **Real estate document management**: Systematically extract property details, encumbrances, and transaction terms from deeds and title documents
- **Healthcare records abstraction**: Pull diagnosis codes, procedures, and patient demographics from clinical notes with source evidence for HIPAA compliance

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **Enterprise extraction-as-a-service with audit trails**: SaaS platform wrapping ExtractBench evaluation to let enterprises benchmark and compare extraction models on their own documents before deployment, with cost-accuracy tradeoffs visualized
2. **Vertical extraction APIs**: Pre-tuned extraction agents for specific industries (insurance, real estate, legal) that come with domain-specific schemas and templates, plus ExtractBench-style grading dashboards
3. **Extraction quality monitoring**: Continuous benchmarking tool that runs ExtractBench-style tests on production extraction pipelines to detect model drift, cost creep, or accuracy regression
4. **Schema inference engine**: Tool that learns optimal schemas from user documents and validates them against ExtractBench metrics, suggesting schema revisions to maximize extraction success
5. **Grounding visualization platform**: UI for compliance teams to review extracted data alongside source grounding evidence, built on ExtractBench's word/page-level metrics for audit-trail generation

## Technical Limitations
- **Synthetic list validation** (known values) may not capture all extraction failure modes in real financial or legal scenarios with novel structures
- **Human verification scope** unclear — what fraction of the 4,869 pages were manually spot-checked? Possible systematic bias if verifiers favored certain document types
- **Cost measurement** appears to be API pricing, not including human review loops or error correction cycles, which dominate real enterprise workflows
- **Schema diversity** limited to 67 document types — may not generalize to highly custom schemas enterprise clients invent mid-deployment
- **No discussion of multilingual documents** or non-Latin scripts common in global enterprises
- **Grounding metrics** (word/page F1) don't capture partial credit for near-correct source citations
- **Scalability**: unclear how performance degrades beyond the tested page/document lengths in the benchmark

## Open Questions
- How do extraction accuracies degrade as documents grow beyond the longest in ExtractBench? Is there a hard cutoff or graceful degradation?
- Can agentic systems learn to self-correct when grounding evidence conflicts with extracted values?
- How sensitive are the rankings to schema design choices? Would a different schema representation change model rankings significantly?
- What fraction of "truncated list" failures are due to token limits vs. reasoning failures? Can retrieval-augmented approaches solve this?
- How well do models trained on ExtractBench generalize to truly novel document types not seen during development?
- Can grounding evidence be used to automatically identify and flag low-confidence extractions before human review?

## Key Takeaway
New benchmark measures extraction accuracy, completeness, and cost together, revealing that LLM agents excel but visual models fail on long enterprise documents.
