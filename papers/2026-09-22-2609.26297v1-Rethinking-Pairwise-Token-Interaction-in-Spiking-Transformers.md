# Rethinking Pairwise Token Interaction in Spiking Transformers

**Authors:** Sicheng Shen, Dongcheng Zhao, Zhiyuan Li, Jinyan Yu, Qian Zhang et al.
**Published:** 2026-09-22 | **arXiv:** [2609.26297v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26297v1)

## Summary
This paper proposes Gated Spike Axial Propagation (GSAP), a new way for spiking neural networks to handle token interactions. Spiking neural networks use sparse, binary spike events to process information efficiently, but when applied to transformers, they suffer from communication bottlenecks because tokens only interact when their spike patterns match exactly. GSAP decouples information propagation from pattern matching, enabling distant tokens to communicate through structured sequential propagation rather than pairwise coincidence, potentially making neuromorphic transformers more practical for real-world tasks.

## Theoretical Background
Transformers rely on attention mechanisms — networks that learn which input tokens are most relevant to each other through learned query-key matching — to process sequences. Spiking neural networks mimic biological neurons by representing information as discrete spike events (on/off signals) rather than continuous values, offering energy efficiency but losing the flexible, dense representations that make transformers powerful. The fundamental tension is that spike-based query-key matching produces sparse, input-dependent interaction patterns; tokens can only exchange information when spike events happen to coincide, which limits long-range communication. This paper builds on prior work in neuromorphic computing and spiking neural networks (SNNs), which aim to create brain-inspired, energy-efficient AI systems.

## Core Contributions
1. **Identifies a fundamental mismatch**: Standard transformer attention mechanisms are poorly suited to spiking representations because pairwise spike matching creates bottlenecks for long-range communication.
2. **Introduces GSAP**: A spike-native token interaction mechanism that replaces direct query-key matching with a two-stage process: (a) spike-based context propagates along structured axes (horizontal and vertical), and (b) receiver-conditioned gates decide how much propagated context each token incorporates.
3. **Preserves efficiency**: Retains the sparse, event-driven nature of spiking systems while enabling more structured long-range communication than pairwise matching alone.
4. **Adds a lightweight local pathway**: Preserves fine-grained neighborhood information alongside axial propagation.

## Connections to Other Work
This work directly challenges the assumption that spiking transformers should inherit attention mechanisms from conventional transformers. It relates to broader efforts in neuromorphic AI and energy-efficient transformers, as well as axial attention mechanisms (which decompose attention along spatial axes in vision tasks). The propagate-then-select paradigm differs from [[Attention Is All You Need]]'s direct query-key matching, proposing instead a more structured, asynchronous communication protocol better suited to sparse binary representations. It also connects to research on efficient transformers and structured attention patterns in computer vision.

## Practical Applications
1. **Edge AI on neuromorphic hardware**: Running transformers on low-power neuromorphic chips (e.g., Intel Loihi, IBM TrueNorth) for real-time inference in IoT devices, robotics, and autonomous systems.
2. **Temporal event processing**: Processing asynchronous event camera data or other spike-like sensor streams (e.g., neuromorphic sensors) without converting to dense frames.
3. **Real-time video analysis**: Combining spiking representations with transformer-style reasoning for continuous video streams on embedded devices.
4. **Low-latency autonomous driving perception**: Neuromorphic vision systems that process events as they occur rather than waiting for full frame acquisitions.

## Product & Startup Ideas

2. **Event-based robotics perception platform**: A middleware stack for robots that fuses neuromorphic sensors (event cameras, DVS) with GSAP transformers for real-time navigation and manipulation.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Training complexity**: No discussion of how to train GSAP end-to-end; spiking networks are notoriously harder to train than conventional neural networks due to non-differentiability of spike events.
2. **Unproven on large-scale tasks**: Experiments likely limited to smaller datasets or simpler tasks compared to standard transformers; unclear if GSAP scales to language modeling or large vision tasks.
3. **Axial propagation assumption**: The choice of horizontal/vertical propagation axes is domain-specific and may not generalize well to all sequence structures or graph-like data.
4. **Comparison baseline**: No clear comparison to other recent neuromorphic transformer designs or simpler baselines (e.g., standard spiking networks without transformer structure).
5. **Hardware dependency**: Benefits depend on actual neuromorphic hardware; if running on GPUs, the overhead of sparse spike operations may negate energy savings.
6. **Gate complexity**: The receiver-conditioned gates add learnable parameters and require careful tuning; unclear how much of the gain comes from structural design vs. increased model capacity.

## Open Questions
1. How does GSAP perform on large-scale language and vision benchmarks compared to standard transformers and other spiking neural network designs?
2. Can GSAP be trained with standard backpropagation, or does it require surrogate gradient methods or other specialized training techniques?
3. How sensitive is performance to the choice of propagation axes and gate architectures? Can these be learned adaptively?
4. How does GSAP compare in terms of actual energy consumption and latency on real neuromorphic hardware vs. simulated spiking on GPUs?
5. Can GSAP extend to multi-head attention or other transformer variants (e.g., sparse attention, mixture of experts)?
6. How does information bottleneck (what percentage of propagated context actually gets selected by gates) affect learning dynamics?

## Key Takeaway
Spiking transformers need different token interaction mechanisms suited to sparse binary events, not just borrowed attention—structured axial propagation works better.
