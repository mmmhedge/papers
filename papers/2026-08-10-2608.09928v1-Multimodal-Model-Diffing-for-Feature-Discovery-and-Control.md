# Multimodal Model Diffing for Feature Discovery and Control

**Authors:** Hunar Batra, Lachin Naghashyar, Ashkan Khakzar, Philip Torr, Christian Schroeder de Witt et al.
**Published:** 2026-08-10 | **arXiv:** [2608.09928v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09928v1)

## Summary
This paper introduces MMDiff, a framework for understanding and controlling the internal features of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) that process both text and images. The work uses sparse autoencoders to decompose hidden neural states into interpretable directions, then compares these across different model versions to isolate which features were changed by multimodal training and how to steer them. This matters because MLLMs are increasingly deployed in high-stakes settings, yet we lack tools to audit their behavior or control specific capabilities like spatial reasoning or safety.

## Theoretical Background
The paper builds on two main foundations. First, **sparse autoencoders (SAEs)** — neural networks trained to compress hidden states into a small number of interpretable feature directions — have recently emerged as tools for understanding large language models by decomposing opaque activations into human-readable components. Second, **mechanistic interpretability** is the effort to understand *how* neural networks compute their outputs by reverse-engineering their internal mechanisms, moving beyond black-box analysis. The work also draws on **causal intervention methods** (removing or steering features to measure their effects) and **contrastive analysis** (comparing firing patterns across different input types to isolate task-relevant features). The paper extends these techniques from single-modality models to MLLMs, where visual and textual information interact in ways that haven't been well characterized before.

## Core Contributions
1. **Multimodal SAE training**: Extends sparse autoencoders to work on MLLMs, learning interpretable feature spaces that capture both visual and linguistic understanding.
2. **Model diffing for multimodal discovery**: Compares SAEs from a base language model against its multimodal-adapted version to directly identify *which* features were added or altered by multimodal training — a novel isolation technique.
3. **Per-token contrastive firing analysis**: Introduces a method to detect causal features for specific tasks by analyzing which feature directions fire selectively on task-relevant inputs (vs. distractor inputs), enabling fine-grained feature localization.
4. **Integrated control pipeline**: Demonstrates that identified features can be surgically removed or steered in a single forward pass, enabling targeted behavior modification without full retraining — with empirical validation on three MLLM families (LLaVA-MORE, PaliGemma 2, InternVL3.5).

## Connections to Other Work
This paper sits at the intersection of [[mechanistic-interpretability]] (understanding neural circuits) and [[alignment]] (ensuring models behave as intended). It extends prior work on SAE-based interpretability for LLMs (e.g., anthropic-scale papers on dictionary learning in transformers) to the multimodal setting. The causal intervention approach is related to [[Activation Patching]] and feature ablation methods, but applied to learned sparse directions rather than raw neurons. The safety applications — reducing adversarial attack success rates by 24% — connect to adversarial robustness and model auditing, though the paper doesn't deeply engage with adversarial training or certified defenses. The feature steering approach parallels [[RLHF]] and other control mechanisms, but operates at the interpretable feature level rather than through gradient-based optimization.

## Practical Applications
1. **MLLM auditing for safety**: Regulators or organizations deploying MLLMs (e.g., in healthcare, autonomous systems) can use MMDiff to audit which internal features drive unsafe behaviors (hallucination, bias in image understanding) and surgically disable them without losing performance on safe tasks.
2. **Selective capability degradation**: Companies may want to disable certain MLLM behaviors (e.g., optical character recognition on sensitive documents, or spatial reasoning in certain contexts) without retraining — MMDiff enables this at runtime.
3. **Multimodal adversarial robustness**: Security teams can identify and reinforce features that are vulnerable to adversarial images or vision-language attacks, improving robustness.
4. **Model debugging and improvement**: ML engineers can use feature discovery to understand why an MLLM fails on specific visual-spatial tasks and steer the relevant features to improve accuracy.
5. **Transparent AI systems**: For regulated industries, MMDiff provides a mechanism to document *why* a multimodal model makes decisions (which interpretable features drove the output), improving explainability.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **MLLMSafe™** — A SaaS platform that runs MMDiff on customer-deployed MLLMs, continuously monitors for unsafe feature activations (e.g., biased visual understanding, jailbreak-vulnerable features), and provides a dashboard for selective feature steering and real-time behavior control without retraining.
2. **FeatureAudit for MLLMs** — Enterprise software that integrates with MLLM deployment pipelines (Hugging Face, local models) to automatically train multimodal SAEs, compare against baseline versions, and generate interpretability reports for compliance (GDPR, AI Act) showing which features are responsible for sensitive outputs.
3. **Multimodal Model Surgery** — A developer tool and API that lets teams upload or connect to their MLLM, automatically discovers task-specific features via MMDiff, and exposes a no-code interface to enable/disable or steer features for A/B testing new behaviors without retraining.
4. **Adversarial Resistance Testing** — A consultancy/tool that applies MMDiff to customer MLLMs to identify vision-language attack vectors (adversarial images, jailbreaks) by isolating vulnerable feature directions, then recommends targeted steering or fine-tuning to harden models.
5. **Interpretable MLLM Marketplace** — A platform where MLLM creators publish pre-computed multimodal SAEs alongside their models, enabling users to browse, search, and understand the internal feature landscape ("OCR features," "spatial reasoning features," "safety features") before deployment, reducing black-box risk.

## Technical Limitations
1. **SAE training cost and fidelity**: Sparse autoencoders require significant compute to train well on large hidden states, and reconstruction quality is never perfect — important features may be missed or merged, especially in high-dimensional settings. The paper does not thoroughly characterize failure modes.
2. **Feature isolation is not causal proof**: The "diffing" approach assumes features are cleanly separable across model versions, but multimodal training may implicitly retrain many features. Contrastive firing analysis improves precision but is still correlational — true causality requires extensive ablation.
3. **Limited generalization of discovered features**: Features discovered on one MLLM family may not transfer to another, limiting the reusability of the SAE dictionaries themselves. The paper trains separate SAEs for each model, which doesn't scale.
4. **Single-layer control assumption**: The paper steers features at a single layer; interactions with other layers and downstream effects are not fully characterized. Steering early layers may have unpredictable impacts on later processing.
5. **Benchmark limitations**: Evaluation is on relatively narrow tasks (spatial understanding, OCR, VQA, specific safety attacks). Generalization to complex, real-world multimodal reasoning is unclear.
6. **No comparison to other interpretability methods**: The paper doesn't benchmark MMDiff against other feature discovery approaches (e.g., attention pattern analysis, gradient-based saliency) in terms of feature quality or interpretability.

## Open Questions
1. **Compositional feature interactions**: How do discovered features compose to produce complex multimodal behaviors? Can we build a computational graph of feature dependencies?
2. **Robustness of steering across inputs**: When features are steered, do the effects generalize to out-of-distribution images and text, or is steering brittle to domain shift?
3. **Cross-model feature alignment**: Can we learn a common feature space across multiple MLLMs, enabling transfer of discovered features and control strategies?
4. **Adversarial feature hardening**: If an attacker knows which features drive unsafe outputs (via MMDiff), can they craft adversarial inputs to re-activate disabled features? How much adversarial robustness does feature-level control actually provide?
5. **Scaling to larger models**: How does SAE training and feature discovery scale to 100B+ parameter MLLMs and very high-dimensional hidden states (e.g., 8k+ dims)?
6. **User studies on interpretability**: Do the discovered features actually match human intuitions about multimodal reasoning, or are they artifacts of the SAE training process?

## Key Takeaway
Sparse autoencoders can isolate and control specific multimodal behaviors in vision-language models, enabling safer, more auditable AI systems without retraining.
