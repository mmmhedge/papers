# SOTA: Stock Options Trading Agents Guided by Option-Implied Return Distributions

**Authors:** Yizhen Xie, Mengyang Liu
**Published:** 2026-10-07 | **arXiv:** [2610.10407v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10407v1)

## Summary
This paper presents SOTA, an AI agent system that trades stock options by selecting and combining strategies dynamically rather than being locked into a single fixed approach. The system uses language models fine-tuned with reinforcement learning to navigate thousands of option contracts and decide which strategies to deploy based on market conditions, achieving 18.3% returns over six months with controlled risk metrics.

## Theoretical Background
Option trading is a complex sequential decision problem: each underlying stock has thousands of possible contracts (calls and puts at different strike prices and expirations), and traders must simultaneously choose which contracts to hold and how to combine them into strategies. Traditional approaches sidestep this by fixing the strategy structure (e.g., always trading a straddle — buying both a call and put at the same strike). Recent advances in [[large-language-models]] enable agents to reason over unstructured context like news, and [[reinforcement-learning]] methods (especially post-training fine-tuned language models) can optimize complex sequential decisions. The paper builds on agent frameworks that decompose large action spaces by abstracting them into interpretable policy layers, combined with supervised fine-tuning and RL to improve trading performance.

## Core Contributions
1. **Strategy-level abstraction**: SOTA abstracts the combinatorial explosion of individual contracts into high-level strategy decisions (e.g., "bull call spread," "iron condor"), reducing a potentially infinite action space to manageable choices.
2. **Deterministic resolvers**: Implements concrete portfolio positions from strategy selections, decoupling agent reasoning from implementation details.
3. **Post-training pipeline**: Applies supervised fine-tuning on frontier trajectories followed by reinforcement learning to a 27B language model, tailored for options trading.
4. **Asymmetric news effect discovery**: Documents that news improves training trajectories but harms out-of-sample performance when retained during RL (18.3% return drops to -2.7%), suggesting overfitting to news signals.

## Connections to Other Work
This research sits at the intersection of [[agents]], [[reinforcement-learning]], and [[quantitative-finance]]. It extends agent reasoning frameworks (e.g., ReAct-style reasoning over context) to the derivatives market, where action spaces are exponentially larger than equity trading. The approach parallels recent work in [[algorithmic-trading]] using deep RL, but focuses on the structured strategy selection problem unique to options. The finding that news degrades RL performance relates to broader questions in [[mechanistic-interpretability]] about what signals models actually learn from versus memorize. The system echoes portfolio optimization work but adds an agentic decision layer rather than pure mathematical optimization.

## Practical Applications
1. **Hedge fund strategy automation**: Automate complex multi-leg option portfolio selection for institutional investors managing large books.
2. **Retail trading platforms**: Embed SOTA-like agents in brokerages to guide retail traders on which strategies to execute based on market regime.
3. **Risk management**: Use the agent to dynamically rebalance hedging strategies (protective puts, collars) as volatility and market conditions shift.
4. **Market-making in options**: High-frequency desks could use the framework to dynamically select which market-making strategies to deploy across the options surface.
5. **Volatility arbitrage**: Automatically identify and trade mean-reversion strategies in implied volatility across strikes and expirations.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **AI Options Coach SaaS**: Web platform where retail options traders submit their portfolio and get real-time strategy recommendations from SOTA, with automated order integration.
2. **Institutional Options Optimizer**: Offer hedge funds and prop traders an API-driven agent that monitors market data, news, and volatility surfaces and autonomously suggests rebalancing trades with expected return/risk estimates.
3. **Options Backtesting Engine**: Provide a freemium SaaS for backtesting strategies using SOTA's abstractions; monetize via premium features like live trading and white-label embedding.
4. **News-Conditioned Trading Alert System**: Specialized product that curates financial news and generates options trading signals, but explicitly trains models to ignore news-specific overfitting (capitalizing on SOTA's finding).
5. **Robo-advisor for Options**: Consumer app that lets users set risk tolerance and automates options strategy selection, positioning SOTA as the "Wealthfront for options."

## Technical Limitations
1. **Out-of-sample generalization**: The sharp drop from 18.3% returns to -2.7% when news is retained during RL suggests the model is brittle and overfits to training data. Robustness across market regimes is unclear.
2. **Small evaluation universe**: Only nine large-cap equities plus SPY. Performance on micro-cap, illiquid, or volatile stocks is unknown.
3. **News data leakage**: The asymmetric news effect is suspicious — it may indicate that news was forward-looking or not properly time-aligned in the RL phase, rather than a fundamental principle.
4. **Transaction costs not detailed**: No clear accounting of bid-ask spreads, commissions, or slippage, which are material for options trading.
5. **Market regime limitations**: Six-month out-of-sample is limited; performance during crises, regime shifts, or extreme volatility is untested.
6. **Strategy space constraints**: The abstraction to strategy-level decisions is fixed; the agent cannot invent new structures or adapt the decomposition itself.

## Open Questions
1. **Why does news hurt RL?** Is it data leakage, spurious correlation, or does the model genuinely overfit to news signals? Can news be incorporated in a regime-aware way?
2. **How does SOTA perform across market cycles?** What happens in high-volatility, crash, or low-volume regimes?
3. **Can the strategy abstraction be learned?** Rather than pre-defining strategies, could the agent learn which combinations of primitives (call, put, strike, expiry) to group together?
4. **How sensitive is performance to hyperparameters and initialization?** Is 18.3% reproducible or is there high variance?
5. **Can multi-agent coordination improve results?** Would deploying multiple SOTA instances with different risk tolerances or time horizons outperform a single agent?
6. **How does it generalize to non-U.S. options or exotic strategies?** What about American vs. European exercise or early-exercise features?

## Key Takeaway
LLM-based agents can outperform static strategies in options trading by dynamically selecting among approaches, but must guard against overfitting to news signals in training.
