# Where-OPD: Spatially Guided On-Policy Self-Distillation of MLLMs with Synthetic Scenes

**Authors:** Sophia Sirko-Galouchenko, Monika Wysoczanska, Andrei Bursuc, Nicolas Thome, Spyros Gidaris
**Published:** 2026-10-01 | **arXiv:** [2610.02117v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02117v1)

## Summary
This paper introduces Where-OPD, a training method that improves multimodal large language models (MLLMs — AI systems that understand both text and images) by having them learn from a smarter version of themselves. The key innovation is using spatial guidance — telling the teacher model *where* in an image to look — during training on synthetic scenes, so the student learns fine-grained visual reasoning without needing human annotations. The results transfer surprisingly well to real-world images, showing gains of 3+ points on counting, document, and chart understanding tasks.

## Theoretical Background
The paper builds on two main ideas: (1) **on-policy self-distillation** — a technique where a frozen or slow-updating copy of a model teaches a trainable version of itself, recently shown effective for reasoning in language models; and (2) **privileged information during training** — the idea that a teacher can access extra information (like image crops or annotations) that the student must learn to infer from limited input. Prior work applied privileged visual crops to MLLMs but required human-annotated grounding data or external teacher models. This paper replaces those expensive annotations with procedurally generated synthetic scenes that come with automatic object identities and spatial coordinates — metadata available "for free" in simulation.

## Core Contributions
1. **Spatially grounded self-distillation for MLLMs**: A novel form of privileged information (text-based spatial guidance like "object X is at region Y") rather than visual crops or external models.
2. **Synthetic-to-real transfer without task alignment**: Post-training uses only synthetic scenes, yet improvements generalize to real-world benchmarks on different tasks (counting, documents, charts, and real-world perception).
3. **Annotation-free scalability**: Eliminates the need for human-annotated grounding or external teacher models by leveraging procedural generation.
4. **Empirical validation**: Consistent improvements across multiple MLLM architectures on CVBench, V*, ZoomBench, BLINK, HR-Bench, and MME-RealWorld.

## Connections to Other Work
This work extends [[Reinforcement Learning|on-policy self-distillation]] approaches (recently applied to language model reasoning) to the multimodal setting. It differs from prior **privileged information** methods (e.g., using image crops) by using spatial text guidance instead, making it simpler and annotation-free. The synthetic-to-real transfer aspect connects to domain generalization and data augmentation literature, though the finding that *spatial grounding* in synthetic data transfers broadly is novel. Related to [[Vision-Language Models|vision-language alignment]] and **fine-grained visual reasoning** in MLLMs.

## Practical Applications
1. **Document and form understanding in enterprise software**: Improving OCR and data extraction from invoices, contracts, and tax forms without retraining on annotated real documents.
2. **Retail inventory and shelf auditing**: Training visual inspection systems to count products and locate items in shelves using synthetic store layouts, then deploying to real stores.
3. **Medical imaging analysis**: Post-training radiology or pathology models on synthetic scenes to improve localization and region-level reasoning, reducing reliance on expensive specialist annotations.
4. **Autonomous systems**: Improving spatial reasoning in robotics and autonomous vehicles using synthetic training scenes that transfer to real-world perception.
5. **Accessibility tools**: Enhancing image description systems to better articulate spatial relationships and locate objects of interest for visually impaired users.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **SyntheticTeach**: A platform that generates synthetic training scenes for domain-specific MLLMs (retail, healthcare, manufacturing) and applies on-policy self-distillation to improve real-world deployment without annotation cost.
2. **RegionReason AI**: A fine-tuning service that takes a client's MLLM and post-trains it on procedurally generated synthetic scenes tailored to their use case (e.g., chart analysis, document processing), delivering a stronger model in weeks instead of months of annotation.
3. **ZeroAnnotation Vision**: Offers pre-trained MLLM checkpoints post-trained with Where-OPD on diverse synthetic tasks (counting, grids, layouts) — customers can directly deploy or further fine-tune with minimal real-world data.
4. **VisualAudit.ai**: Automated quality control software for manufacturing that uses Where-OPD-trained models to locate defects and anomalies in products, trained on synthetic production simulations.
5. **DocuMind Pro**: Enterprise document processing tool (invoices, medical records, insurance forms) using Where-OPD to improve region-level understanding and extraction accuracy without annotating thousands of real documents.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Synthetic scene realism gap**: Procedural generation may not capture all visual complexities, lighting conditions, or occlusion patterns in real data; transfer gains are significant but capped by how well synthetic distributions match real distributions.
2. **Task generalization unclear**: Paper shows transfer across counting, documents, and charts, but unclear whether the approach works equally well for very different tasks (e.g., dense pose estimation, instance segmentation) or entirely new domains.
3. **Spatial guidance design choices**: The paper doesn't deeply explore how sensitive results are to the *format* of spatial guidance (coordinates, bounding boxes, region descriptions) — this hyperparameter may need tuning per task.
4. **Computational cost not fully disclosed**: No analysis of the compute overhead of on-policy self-distillation (running both teacher and student) compared to standard fine-tuning.
5. **Limited to rectangular/grid-like scenes**: Synthetic scene generation likely works best for structured layouts (documents, charts, grids); applicability to unstructured natural images (outdoor scenes, crowds) is unclear.

## Open Questions
1. **What properties of spatial guidance matter most?** Is it the *precision* of coordinates, the *granularity* of regions, or the *semantics* of object labels? An ablation study varying each would clarify.
2. **Can this extend to video?** Procedural generation is cheap for images; can spatially grounded self-distillation improve MLLMs for video understanding, and does synthetic video transfer to real video?
3. **Does this help with hallucination or grounding accuracy?** The paper focuses on downstream task performance; does spatial guidance reduce false object claims or improve localization calibration?
4. **How does performance scale with scene complexity?** Do more complex synthetic scenes (more objects, occlusions, fine details) yield better transfer, or is there a sweet spot?
5. **Can spatial guidance be learned vs. provided?** Instead of giving coordinates, could the model learn to generate spatial guidance itself during teacher inference, making the approach even more scalable?

## Key Takeaway
Self-training MLLMs with spatial guidance on synthetic scenes boosts real-world perception without annotations.
