# Persistent Convolution: A Topological Framework for AI Alignment Testing and Semantic Space Characterization

**Authors:** Tyler Ashoff, Jordan Rodu
**Published:** 2026-07-31 | **arXiv:** [2607.29008v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29008v1)

## Summary
This paper proposes a topology-based statistical framework to test whether opaque AI models (like neural networks) align with human-curated knowledge structures and semantic concepts. Rather than trusting a model's outputs alone, the authors develop formal tests on the model's internal embedding space to check whether it has learned the "right" concepts in the "right" way — addressing a critical gap between performance and interpretability in modern AI deployment.

## Theoretical Background
The work builds on several foundational ideas: embedding spaces (high-dimensional vector representations where semantically similar concepts cluster together), topological data analysis (mathematical tools from topology that characterize the shape and structure of data clouds), and alignment testing (verification that a system behaves according to human intentions). The paper connects to possibility theory (a framework for reasoning under uncertainty that extends probability theory) and decision theory (the mathematics of rational choice under uncertainty). Prior work in mechanistic interpretability has sought to understand internal model representations, while knowledge graph alignment has traditionally relied on outcome-level comparisons. This paper bridges those domains by using formal statistical tests on embedding geometry to detect misalignment before deployment.

## Core Contributions
1. **Persistent Homology for Alignment**: Develops a topological alignment test using persistent homology (a technique that tracks how the topological structure of data changes across scales) to verify whether a model's semantic space respects human-curated knowledge structures.
2. **Multi-modal Alignment Framework**: Extends the method to handle multiple input modalities (e.g., text + images), allowing joint testing across different data types.
3. **Decision-Theoretic Integration**: Connects the topological results to a unified framework from data collection through deployment, embedding possibility theory into the alignment pipeline.
4. **Practical Deployment Tool**: Provides an interpretable model comparison and selection technique that augments standard performance metrics and output-level reasoning.

## Connections to Other Work
This relates to [[mechanistic-interpretability]] work (e.g., probing studies that examine learned representations) but uses topological rigor instead of post-hoc attribution. It complements [[RLHF]] and other [[alignment]] approaches by offering a pre-deployment diagnostic, rather than a training objective. The embedding-space focus connects to recent work on concept activation vectors and representation geometry. Unlike adversarial robustness testing (which checks worst-case inputs), this approach characterizes structural properties of the learned semantic space itself. The knowledge graph angle links to symbolic AI and structured knowledge representation.

## Practical Applications
- **Model Selection in Production**: When choosing between two language models or vision systems, run the alignment test on a curated knowledge ontology (e.g., medical concepts, legal terms) to verify both capture the same semantic distinctions before deployment.
- **Regulatory Compliance**: Financial or healthcare institutions can audit whether an AI system's internal representations respect domain-specific concept hierarchies (e.g., credit risk categories, disease classifications) required by regulators.
- **Multi-modal AI Validation**: Before deploying a vision-language model, test whether its joint embedding space aligns with human-expert knowledge about how visual and textual concepts should relate.
- **Foundation Model Evaluation**: Benchmark new large language models on knowledge structure alignment rather than just downstream task accuracy, detecting hidden representational quirks.
- **AI Safety Checkpoints**: Insert alignment tests as gates in CI/CD pipelines to catch subtle semantic drift during model updates or fine-tuning.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **AlignmentOS**: A SaaS platform offering pre-built topological alignment tests for common domains (healthcare, finance, law). Users upload a knowledge graph and their model; get a report on structural misalignment.
2. **EmbeddingAudit**: A compliance tool for regulated industries. Automatically checks whether AI models in production maintain alignment with statutory knowledge hierarchies (e.g., FDA drug classifications, SEC definitions).
3. **SemanticGate**: A model governance layer integrated into deployment pipelines. Blocks models that fail topology-based alignment checks, with explainable violation reports for data teams.
4. **MultimodalValidator**: Specialized for vision-language and cross-modal systems. Tests whether alignment holds across input types, helping teams detect modality-specific semantic drift.
5. **KnowledgeGraphSync**: Tools for enterprises to maintain curated knowledge ontologies and automatically run alignment tests against new models, surfacing where the model "disagrees" with canonical definitions.

## Technical Limitations
- **Knowledge Graph Dependency**: The method requires a high-quality, human-curated knowledge structure upfront. If the graph is incomplete, biased, or misses important distinctions, alignment tests may be misleading.
- **Scaling to High Dimensions**: Persistent homology can be computationally expensive for very large embedding spaces and high cardinality concepts; computational complexity may limit application to enormous models.
- **Abstraction Gap**: Testing alignment at the embedding level doesn't guarantee downstream behavioral alignment — a model can have "correct" concept geometry but still make poor decisions.
- **Multimodality Complexity**: The multi-modal extension's integration assumptions (how to compare topologies across different modality embeddings) may not generalize to novel modality combinations.
- **Limited Guidance on Hyperparameters**: The paper likely doesn't fully specify how to choose topological thresholds or which statistical tests are most robust across different domains and model architectures.
- **Assumption of Separability**: The method assumes concepts *should* be topologically distinct; in cases where concepts genuinely blur or overlap (e.g., art vs. design), tests may flag false positives.

## Open Questions
- How sensitive is the topological alignment signal to the specific choice of distance metric in embedding space? Would results differ under Euclidean vs. cosine vs. other geometries?
- Can the method detect alignment failures that don't show up as obvious embedding-space anomalies — e.g., models that have correct geometry but inverted semantics?
- How do alignment tests behave when the model and knowledge graph are from different eras or cultures, encoding different concept hierarchies?
- What is the sample complexity? How many concepts or examples are needed in the curated knowledge structure to get a robust alignment signal?
- Can this framework extend to temporal or causal knowledge graphs where concepts have process-like relationships, not just static similarity?
- How does alignment on a static knowledge graph generalize to out-of-distribution queries or emergent reasoning the knowledge graph doesn't explicitly cover?

## Key Takeaway
Topology-based testing on model embeddings offers a practical, formal way to verify AI systems align with human knowledge before deployment.
