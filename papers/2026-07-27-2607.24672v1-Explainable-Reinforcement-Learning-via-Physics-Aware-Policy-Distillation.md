# Explainable Reinforcement Learning via Physics-Aware Policy Distillation

**Authors:** Shaker Al-Tamari, Waled Kadour
**Published:** 2026-07-27 | **arXiv:** [2607.24672v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24672v1)

## Summary
This paper makes Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) systems interpretable by distilling a high-performance continuous control agent into a simple decision tree that humans can understand. The authors show they can preserve the original agent's performance while gaining explainability — a critical step toward deploying AI safely in robotics and autonomous systems where regulatory approval and human trust are non-negotiable.

## Theoretical Background
Policy distillation is a technique where a complex "teacher" neural network is compressed into a simpler "student" model by training the student to mimic the teacher's behavior. In reinforcement learning, the teacher is typically a high-performing agent trained via deep neural networks (like TD3 — Twin Delayed Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient, an algorithm for continuous control tasks). The Inverted Pendulum is a classic control benchmark: a pole balanced on a movable cart, easily solved mathematically but useful for validating RL algorithms. The paper adds physics-aware features (domain knowledge about the system dynamics) and "Noisy Oracle Rollouts" (synthetic training data generated by the teacher agent with controlled noise) to improve distillation quality. The goal is interpretability — the ability to understand *why* an AI system makes decisions — which is essential for safety-critical applications.

## Core Contributions
1. Demonstrates that a high-performing continuous RL agent (TD3) can be successfully distilled into a shallow decision tree without significant performance loss on the Inverted Pendulum task.
2. Introduces physics-aware feature engineering and "Noisy Oracle Rollouts" as practical techniques to improve policy distillation in continuous control domains.
3. Provides a control-theoretic analysis showing the trade-off between continuous and discrete actuation: discrete rule-based control induces Bang-Bang control (switching between extremes) and creates stable bimodal limit cycles rather than smooth trajectories.
4. Validates that the interpretable student policy maintains BIBO stability (bounded inputs produce bounded outputs), making it safe for deployment in safety-critical settings.

## Connections to Other Work
This work sits at the intersection of [[Interpretability in Deep Learning]], [[Policy Distillation]], and [[Safe Reinforcement Learning]]. It relates to broader efforts in mechanistic interpretability (understanding how neural networks make decisions) but applies them specifically to RL. The use of decision trees as interpretable surrogates echoes classical explainable AI methods, while the physics-aware approach connects to [[Physics-Informed Neural Networks]] and domain-driven machine learning. The stability analysis leverages classical control theory, bridging modern RL with rigorous formal verification methods used in safety-critical systems.

## Practical Applications
- **Autonomous Driving**: Decision tree policies can be audited before deployment, critical for regulatory approval (e.g., SAE Level 3+ autonomy).
- **Surgical Robotics**: Surgeons need to understand and trust robot arm decisions in real-time; an interpretable policy enables overrides and debugging.
- **Industrial Process Control**: Chemical plants or power grids regulated by safety standards can deploy RL-derived controllers that pass formal verification audits.
- **Aerospace**: Aircraft autopilot systems require explainability for certification; tree-based policies replace opaque neural networks.
- **Medical Device Control**: Ventilators or infusion pumps using RL can be made interpretable, helping clinicians understand and challenge algorithmic decisions.

## Technical Limitations
- **Limited to Simple Domains**: The Inverted Pendulum is a low-dimensional toy problem; scaling to high-dimensional continuous control (e.g., humanoid locomotion) is unproven — decision trees may become too large to interpret.
- **Loss of Performance in Complex Tasks**: The paper shows performance preservation on a simple benchmark; more complex domains may require much larger trees or approximation errors.
- **Physics Feature Engineering**: Requires manual domain expertise to design meaningful physics-aware features; generalizing the approach to novel domains is non-trivial.
- **Discrete Control Overhead**: Converting continuous actions to discrete rules incurs Bang-Bang switching and energy inefficiency — acceptable for inverted pendulums but potentially problematic for soft robotics or energy-constrained systems.
- **Stability Guarantees Only Local**: BIBO stability is verified in a specific operating region; global stability across the full state space remains uncertain.
- **Teacher Dependency**: The student can only be as good as the teacher agent; if the teacher overfits or is suboptimal, the student inherits those flaws.

## Open Questions
- Can this approach scale to high-dimensional continuous control tasks (e.g., 50+ state dimensions) while remaining interpretable?
- How sensitive is distillation quality to the choice and design of physics-aware features? Is there a principled way to select them automatically?
- Can hybrid approaches (e.g., decision trees + small neural network branches) maintain interpretability while improving performance on harder tasks?
- How does noisy oracle rollout quality affect convergence and final policy accuracy? Is there an optimal noise level?
- Can formal verification methods prove safety properties (e.g., never exceeding force/torque limits) of the distilled policy *automatically*, without manual modeling?
- Does this approach extend to stochastic environments or partially observable systems where the teacher must account for uncertainty?

## Key Takeaway
Neural RL agents can be converted into interpretable decision trees without losing performance, enabling safe deployment in regulated industries.
