# CAMOS: Coupled Oscillatory State-Space Model for Multimodal Clinical Time-Series

**Authors:** Maxx Richard Rahman, Mostafa Hammouda, Wolfgang Maass
**Published:** 2026-09-30 | **arXiv:** [2609.39484v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39484v1)

## Summary
This paper introduces CAMOS, a neural state-space model designed to handle multimodal clinical time-series data that is irregular, incomplete, and heterogeneously sampled. Unlike standard linear models that treat missing data as simple masking, CAMOS allows the transition dynamics themselves to adapt based on which measurements are actually present, enabling it to capture interactions between modalities. The work is clinically motivated by real datasets like ADNI where half of imaging visits lack certain modalities.

## Theoretical Background
State-space models (SSMs) are a classical framework for sequential data: they maintain a latent state that evolves through time via a transition operator, then produce observations from that state. Recent work has revived SSMs with neural network variants — particularly oscillatory SSMs that use coupled differential equations with second-order dynamics for better long-range expressivity than RNNs. The paper's key theoretical insight is grounded in linear algebra: they prove that if a linear SSM's transition operator is independent of which modalities were measured, the resulting latent state must be an additive function of the availability indicators, preventing any non-linear interaction between jointly present or jointly absent modalities. They address this by gating the coupling matrix itself — the heart of the differential equation — through availability patterns.

## Core Contributions
1. **Theoretical impossibility result**: Proves linear SSMs with fixed transition operators cannot represent modal interactions under missingness, providing formal justification for a more expressive architecture.
2. **CAMOS architecture**: A second-order oscillatory SSM where the coupling matrix is availability-gated, making the dynamics themselves a function of the measurement pattern.
3. **Theoretical restoration**: Develops three technical guarantees for coupled oscillatory models: (a) per-channel Gershgorin budgets ensuring positive-definite effective stiffness across all 2^M availability patterns, (b) energy arguments that charge amplification to availability transitions rather than sequence length, and (c) channel factorization preserving parallel scan exactness.
4. **Empirical validation**: Demonstrates superior performance on ADNI across same-visit staging, landmark prediction, longitudinal forecasting, and zero-shot transfer to OASIS-3 without majority-class collapse.

## Connections to Other Work
This work extends recent neural oscillatory SSM literature (e.g., S4, S5 models that use structured state-spaces for efficiency) to the multimodal incomplete-data setting. It addresses a gap left by clinical fusion models and simple masking-based approaches. The theoretical analysis mirrors stability arguments in numerical ODE solvers and structured matrix theory but applies them to the specific challenge of availability-dependent dynamics. The work also relates to broader [[deep-learning]] literature on handling missing data, though most prior work treats missingness as a separate problem rather than integrating it into the transition function itself.

## Practical Applications
1. **Neurodegenerative disease monitoring**: ADNI-like cohort studies tracking Alzheimer's progression where MRI, PET, and CSF biomarkers are collected asynchronously and incompletely — CAMOS can forecast cognitive decline despite sparse imaging.
2. **ICU patient monitoring**: Integrate vital signs (continuous), lab results (periodic), and imaging (rare) to predict deterioration risk without imputation.
3. **Multi-sensor IoT and wearable health**: Smartwatches, continuous glucose monitors, and periodic clinical labs for chronic disease management.
4. **Longitudinal electronic health records**: Fuse diagnoses, medications, labs, and imaging visits to predict readmissions or complications.
5. **Pharmaceutical trial monitoring**: Track heterogeneous safety biomarkers collected on different schedules to detect adverse signals early.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **Clinical risk stratification platform**: SaaS tool for healthcare systems to auto-ingest EHR data (multimodal, irregular) and produce per-patient risk scores for expensive interventions (ICU admission, readmission, adverse events). CAMOS's ability to handle missingness without imputation cuts data prep time.
2. **Neurodegenerative disease digital biomarker company**: Partner with pharma/CROs to deploy CAMOS on longitudinal cognitive + imaging cohorts to identify disease progression subgroups and enable adaptive trial designs faster than traditional statistical endpoints.
3. **Wearable + clinical lab fusion platform**: Combine continuous wearable signals with infrequent blood work to deliver personalized health coaching for chronic disease (diabetes, heart failure). The availability-gated dynamics unlock patterns invisible in either modality alone.
4. **Predictive hospital operations**: Embed CAMOS in EHR systems to forecast bed demand, infection risk, and discharge readiness using heterogeneous patient data collected on clinical schedules, not experimental convenience.
5. **Biotech AI-as-a-service for cohort analysis**: Offer a hosted model fine-tuned on client longitudinal data (genetics, imaging, labs, clinical notes) to enable post-hoc discovery of patient subtypes and treatment response predictors without sharing raw data.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Scalability to very high dimensional multimodal data**: The gating mechanism operates on all 2^M availability patterns; as modalities grow (M > 20), computational cost and memory scale exponentially unless sparse patterns are common. Not clear how to scale elegantly.
2. **Unknown modality mechanism**: Assumes missingness is driven by clinical protocol or practicality, not patient outcomes (MCAR/MAR). If sicker patients systematically skip imaging (MNAR), the model may encode confounding as predictive signal.
3. **No explicit imputation or reconstruction**: CAMOS handles missing modalities in dynamics but does not reconstruct unobserved values; downstream tasks requiring filled-in data still need separate imputation.
4. **Interpretability**: The availability-gated coupling matrix is non-linear and high-dimensional. Understanding *which* modal interactions the model learned is non-trivial.
5. **Generalization to novel modality patterns**: CAMOS is trained on observed availability patterns. If deployment encounters a new missingness pattern (e.g., a new lab test added to clinic protocol), behavior is untested.
6. **Assumption of oscillatory dynamics**: Not all clinical processes follow second-order dynamics; the inductive bias may be mismatched for fast-mixing processes (e.g., acute inflammation) or unit-root trends (e.g., chronic decline).

## Open Questions
1. **What structure in the learned coupling matrices corresponds to clinically meaningful modality interactions?** Can we extract or visualize which pairs of modalities the model found synergistic?
2. **How does CAMOS compare to simple probabilistic approaches that explicitly model missing-data mechanisms** (e.g., Bayesian SSMs with missing-data priors)? Is the availability-gating strictly better or trading interpretability for accuracy?
3. **Can the 2^M explosion be mitigated through low-rank or sparse factorizations** of the availability-gated coupling matrix, and does this preserve the theoretical guarantees?
4. **Does the model learn different oscillation frequencies for different modalities**, and do these align with known temporal scales of biological processes (e.g., circadian, menstrual, disease progression)?
5. **How sensitive is the model to the choice of Gershgorin budget thresholds?** Is there a principled data-driven way to set them, or must they be tuned per cohort?
6. **Can CAMOS be extended to handle mixed modality types** (continuous time-series, discrete events, categorical measurements) without architectural redesign?

## Key Takeaway
Availability-gated oscillatory dynamics let neural state-space models capture interactions between multimodal clinical data even when measurements are sparse and irregular.
