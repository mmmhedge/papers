# Forecast Accuracy Is Not Trading Profit: Evolving Small Recurrent Networks for Stock Return Prediction

**Authors:** Jonathan Chang, Zimeng Lyu
**Published:** 2026-10-06 | **arXiv:** [2610.07825v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07825v1)

## Summary
This paper challenges the assumption that better forecasting accuracy leads to better trading profits. The authors compare different neural network architectures—transformers, recurrent networks, and others—on stock return prediction, and find that small networks evolved via neuroevolution outperform larger models on both forecast accuracy *and* actual trading returns, while maintaining extreme computational efficiency. The key insight: what matters for trading is not pointwise prediction error, but how forecasts align with the decision horizon and after accounting for transaction costs.

## Theoretical Background
The paper sits at the intersection of two debates. First, time series forecasting traditionally uses metrics like mean squared error (MSE) or mean absolute error (MAE)—measures of how close a prediction is to the true value in isolation. But in finance, these metrics don't directly translate to profit because trading decisions depend on risk, costs, and portfolio construction. Second, recent years have seen transformers (neural networks using attention mechanisms to weight different parts of a sequence) dominate many forecasting benchmarks, but there's ongoing discussion about whether they truly outperform simpler recurrent neural networks (RNNs—networks with memory that process sequences step-by-step) in practice. The authors also draw on neuroevolution, a machine learning approach that evolves neural network architectures automatically rather than hand-designing them.

## Core Contributions
1. **Horizon-performance mismatch**: Demonstrates empirically that rank IC (Information Coefficient, a standard finance metric) diverges sharply across prediction horizons—evolved small networks maintain performance at longer horizons (10 days) while larger models decay. This explains why forecast accuracy doesn't predict trading profit.
2. **Neuroevolution outperforms standard architectures**: Shows that evolved small RNNs (66 weights) beat transformers (up to 817K parameters), GPT-style mixing layers, and fixed RNNs on both accuracy and net returns across four mid-cap portfolios over three years.
3. **Computational-financial efficiency trade-off**: Proves that extreme miniaturization (CPU-only, Raspberry Pi inference in 10.8 microseconds) is possible without sacrificing predictive edge, contrary to the scaling hypothesis in deep learning.
4. **Real transaction cost accounting**: Unlike many ML finance papers, includes actual position formation costs and bid-ask spreads—showing that the second-best forecaster actually loses money once costs are applied.

## Connections to Other Work
This work relates to [[Efficient Markets Hypothesis]] debates and critiques of the "high accuracy = high profit" narrative in quantitative finance. It parallels critiques of [[transformer-scaling]] and the dominance of large models, echoing arguments that model size may not be the right optimization target for all domains. The neuroevolution approach connects to broader [[neural architecture search]] literature but applies it to a financial decision problem rather than accuracy alone. It also touches on [[market microstructure]] by treating transaction costs as a first-class constraint rather than an afterthought.

## Practical Applications
1. **Retail trading bots on edge devices**: Deploy trading algorithms on smartphones or embedded systems without cloud infrastructure, reducing latency and operational risk.
2. **Low-latency proprietary trading**: Microsecond-level inference times enable ultra-fast order execution in competitive markets where speed matters.
3. **Resource-constrained hedge funds**: Smaller firms without GPU budgets can run competitive forecasting and execution pipelines on CPU infrastructure.
4. **Decentralized finance (DeFi) arbitrage**: Small, efficient models can be deployed on-chain or in bandwidth-limited environments for automated market-making or arbitrage.
5. **Portfolio rebalancing at scale**: Train a single model across many securities (the "pooled panel" approach) to efficiently rebalance thousands of positions with minimal computational cost.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **EdgeTrade**: A SaaS platform that takes historical price data and auto-generates tiny, optimal trading models via neuroevolution, then deploys them to users' devices (laptop, phone, Pi) with live market feeds—competing on simplicity and cost against heavyweight algorithmic trading platforms.
2. **NeuroQuant**: An institutional quant research tool that benchmarks model architectures not on forecast error but on *realistic* trading P&L (including costs, slippage, market impact), letting asset managers detect overfitting that traditional metrics miss.
3. **MicroHedge**: A mobile app for retail investors that runs a lightweight evolved RNN model locally to generate daily long/short signals for a specified universe, with zero cloud dependency and full privacy.
4. **TinyAlpha**: A consulting service that designs and validates minimalist ML trading strategies for constrained environments (emerging markets with poor connectivity, regulatory sandboxes, high-frequency venues with strict latency budgets).
5. **ArchitectureAudit**: A B2B platform that takes a client's existing forecasting model and runs neuroevolution to find a 100–1000x smaller proxy with equivalent or better P&L, then benchmarks the savings in inference cost and latency.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Limited data regime**: Only three years of mid-cap data tested; results may not generalize to different asset classes (crypto, bonds, commodities), market regimes (crisis vs. calm), or longer lookback windows.
2. **Single-model pooling**: Training one network across a whole universe assumes correlations and patterns are stable across sectors; may fail if market structure shifts (e.g., sector rotation).
3. **No market impact modeling**: The strategy assumes positions can be executed at quoted prices; large-scale deployment of many such signals simultaneously could move prices, destroying the edge.
4. **Neuroevolution reproducibility**: The paper doesn't report variance across random seeds or population initializations; we don't know if the found architectures are robust or lucky.
5. **Overfitting risk**: No explicit out-of-sample validation on held-out years; the three-year window might be cherry-picked or coincidentally favorable.
6. **Horizon-specific tuning**: The strong results at 10-day horizons may not translate to intraday or longer-term strategies without retuning.

## Open Questions
1. **Why does neuroevolution work better?** What structural properties of the evolved networks (sparsity, attention patterns, gating mechanisms) give them the forecasting-to-profit edge? A mechanistic analysis would strengthen the contribution.
2. **Does the advantage persist out-of-sample?** Test on held-out years (2027+) to rule out overfitting to the 2022–2024 period.
3. **Cross-asset and regime robustness**: Do the same tiny architectures work for other sectors, geographies, or asset classes (small-cap, international, crypto)?
4. **Scalability of neuroevolution**: How does CPU search time scale if the universe grows (1000s of stocks vs. hundreds)? Is 16 minutes still realistic?
5. **Explainability**: Can we reverse-engineer the evolved networks to understand what patterns they learned? Are they exploiting well-known anomalies or discovering novel signals?
6. **Comparison to other lightweight architectures**: What about other minimal models (linear + basis functions, hand-designed RNNs with fewer parameters, quantized transformers)?

## Key Takeaway
Tiny evolved neural networks beat massive transformers at both predicting stock returns and generating actual trading profit.
