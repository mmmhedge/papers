# Bi-FORK: Generative Modeling of High-Dimensional Bifurcating Systems

**Authors:** Anna Zimmel, Fleur Hendriks, Markus Holzleitner, Florian Sestak, Martin Weichselbaumer et al.
**Published:** 2026-10-08 | **arXiv:** [2610.12449v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12449v1)

## Summary
This paper introduces Bi-FORK, a generative framework that learns physical systems where one input can produce multiple valid outputs — a problem called bifurcation that breaks standard machine learning assumptions. The work uses latent flow matching and a repulsion-guided sampling strategy to generate complete trajectories through these "branching" solution spaces, scaling to systems with hundreds of thousands of dimensions. This matters because bifurcations appear everywhere in engineering and climate science, yet existing neural surrogates (learned replacements for expensive simulations) can't handle them.

## Theoretical Background
Traditional learned physics models assume a one-to-one input–output mapping: given a physical state, they predict a single future state. This breaks down at bifurcation points — moments where a system becomes unstable and splits into multiple equally valid branches (like a buckling beam that can bend left or right). The paper builds on [[generative-models|generative modeling]] approaches, specifically **flow matching** (a technique for learning smooth transformations between data distributions) and **latent variable models** (where a lower-dimensional "latent space" captures variation). The core insight is treating solution branches as distinct modes in a learned distribution, sampled from latent space rather than directly predicting outputs. Prior work on neural surrogates for PDEs (partial differential equations) focused on deterministic mappings; this extends that to one-to-many relationships.

## Core Contributions
1. **Bi-FORK framework**: A generative model designed specifically for bifurcating systems, combining latent flow matching (for coherent trajectories) with **repulsion-guided sampling** (a novel mechanism to pull samples toward different solution branches in a single forward pass, rather than requiring multiple passes or post-hoc clustering).

2. **Scalability breakthrough**: Handles systems discretized at 260,000 points — several orders of magnitude beyond prior generative approaches to bifurcation problems.

3. **Unified treatment across bifurcation types**: Demonstrated on continuous (buckling beams), discrete (mechanical metamaterials), and field-valued (Allen-Cahn phase separation) bifurcations, showing the method is not tailored to one physics regime.

4. **Space-time coherence preservation**: Unlike naive generative approaches, maintains physical consistency across both spatial and temporal dimensions in trajectories.

## Connections to Other Work
This extends [[neural operator learning|neural operators]] (like DeepONet, FNO) and physics-informed neural networks ([[PINN|PINNs]]) by recognizing they're unsuitable for multimodal solution landscapes. It relates to broader challenges in [[mechanistic-interpretability|understanding when and why neural models fail]], particularly at phase transitions or instabilities. The repulsion-guided sampling strategy connects to [[mixture-of-experts|mixture models]] and mode-seeking inference in probabilistic modeling. The latent flow matching backbone draws from recent diffusion and flow-based generative models (continuous normalizing flows). Philosophically, this addresses a blind spot in the neural surrogate literature: most engineering and climate applications are deterministic at the equation level, but epistemic uncertainty around which branch a system takes is physically meaningful.

## Practical Applications
1. **Engineering design under uncertainty**: Structural engineers can use Bi-FORK to rapidly evaluate all possible failure modes (e.g., buckling patterns) of a beam or bridge under load, informing robust design without exhaustive simulation.

2. **Climate and weather prediction**: Phase transitions in climate systems (e.g., Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation collapse) are bifurcations; generative forecasting of alternative equilibrium states could improve risk assessment for decadal predictions.

3. **Material discovery**: Mechanical metamaterials exhibit bifurcating behavior under stress; amortized sampling of solution branches accelerates the search for materials with target nonlinear properties (e.g., tunable stiffness).

4. **Real-time control under instability**: In fluid dynamics or plasma confinement, a fast surrogate that predicts multiple plausible futures at bifurcation points could guide adaptive control strategies.

5. **Uncertainty quantification in inverse problems**: Given experimental observations, engineers need to infer which branch a system followed; this generative approach naturally captures posterior distributions over branches.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Training data requirements**: Bi-FORK likely needs high-quality, densely sampled trajectories across multiple branches to learn the latent space; costly to generate via direct simulation, especially for expensive 3D physics.

2. **Branch discovery completeness**: No guarantee that repulsion-guided sampling finds *all* branches, especially in very high dimensions with many modes. May miss rare or "thin" solution branches.

3. **Latent space interpretability**: The learned latent codes may not have clear physical meaning (e.g., branch index), limiting user control over which solution to sample.

4. **Generalization across parameters**: If physical parameters (e.g., material properties, load magnitude) vary, a single trained model may not extrapolate to parameter regimes far from training data.

5. **Validation of multimodality**: Evaluating whether the model truly captures all distinct physical branches (vs. spurious diversity) requires expensive ground-truth solutions, especially for complex PDEs.

6. **Scalability of latent space**: At 260k points, even latent representations become large; unclear how much further it scales and whether computational cost remains practical.

## Open Questions
1. **How does the dimensionality of latent space scale with system size and number of branches?** Is there a principled way to set it, or does it require expensive tuning?

2. **Can Bi-FORK be extended to parametric bifurcations?** I.e., can a single model learn solution manifolds across a range of physical parameters (load, temperature, etc.), and if so, how does performance degrade?

3. **How sensitive is branch recovery to the hyperparameters of repulsion-guided sampling?** Is there a principled way to weight exploration vs. staying near realistic solutions?

4. **Can this framework detect and represent bifurcations it hasn't explicitly seen in training?** E.g., generalize to a geometry or loading regime that exhibits new bifurcation structure.

5. **What is the sample efficiency compared to direct simulation?** At what problem size does Bi-FORK become faster than just running the PDE solver multiple times?

6. **How does performance scale for systems with many (10+) solution branches?** Does the model still recover them, or does multimodality become intractable?

## Key Takeaway
A generative AI framework that learns to explore multiple equally-valid futures in unstable physical systems, enabling fast surrogate modeling where standard neural networks fail.
