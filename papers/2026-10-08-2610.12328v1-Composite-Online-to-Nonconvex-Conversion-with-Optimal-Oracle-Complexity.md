# Composite Online-to-Nonconvex Conversion with Optimal Oracle Complexity

**Authors:** Mingyi Li, Taira Tsuchiya, Kenji Yamanishi
**Published:** 2026-10-08 | **arXiv:** [2610.12328v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12328v1)

## Summary
This paper solves a fundamental problem in nonconvex optimization: how to efficiently find stationary points when your objective combines a nonsmooth, possibly nonconvex function with a convex regularizer (like L2 regularization). The authors prove that adding the regularizer doesn't increase the number of gradient queries needed — a surprising and practically important result for training neural networks and solving constrained problems.

## Theoretical Background
Nonconvex optimization is central to deep learning, where neural network objectives are rarely convex. Most prior work focuses on *smooth* nonconvex problems (where gradients don't jump), but real regularization and constraints introduce *nonsmooth* components. The paper builds on the [[online-to-nonconvex]] conversion framework — a recent technique that borrows from online learning to achieve optimal rates in nonconvex settings.

Key concepts: A **Goldstein stationarity condition** is a generalization of "critical point" for nonsmooth functions; **oracle complexity** measures how many gradient or function evaluations an algorithm needs; **regularizers** are penalty terms (like L2 weight decay) that prevent overfitting.

Prior work achieved suboptimal rates for composite (regularized) nonconvex problems under zeroth-order access (function values only). No first-order (gradient-based) bounds were known. The online-to-nonconvex framework worked for noncomposite problems but required new machinery to handle the regularizer.

## Core Contributions
1. **Extended online-to-nonconvex to composite optimization**: Designed new loss functions for the online learner that incorporate the regularizer directly — not a linearized approximation — enabling regret analysis.

2. **Optimal oracle complexity for composite nonsmooth nonconvex problems**: Proved $O(\delta^{-1}\varepsilon^{-3})$ gradient complexity and $O(d\delta^{-1}\varepsilon^{-3})$ zeroth-order complexity, matching noncomposite rates and closing a gap in the literature.

3. **First theoretical guarantee under first-order access**: Previous results for this setting didn't exist; this provides the first principled complexity bound.

4. **Smooth case analysis**: Extended results to smooth objectives with improved rates.

## Connections to Other Work
Relates directly to [[RLHF]] and [[reinforcement-learning]] training pipelines where regularization is standard. Connects to [[mechanistic-interpretability]] studies that rely on regularized loss landscapes. Also relevant to constrained optimization (e.g., in computer vision with [[computer-vision]] pipelines) where regularization enforces structural priors.

The online-to-nonconvex framework sits at the intersection of online learning and nonconvex optimization — a modern approach that sidesteps traditional descent methods' difficulties with nonsmooth objectives.

## Practical Applications
- **Neural network training with weight decay**: Shows that L2 regularization doesn't harm sample efficiency of SGD-like methods.
- **Constrained optimization in ML**: Regularizers represent constraint approximations; the result justifies their use without complexity penalty.
- **Robust model training**: Adversarial regularizers (which are nonsmooth) can be added without theoretical cost.
- **Federated learning**: Many federated algorithms apply regularization; this guarantees efficiency.
- **Online learning with composite losses**: Any system that combines learned losses with fixed convex penalties (e.g., fairness constraints).

## Technical Limitations
- **Goldstein stationarity** is weaker than true critical points for nonsmooth functions; practical implications for solution quality are unclear.
- **Dimension dependence in zeroth-order case**: The $O(d\delta^{-1}\varepsilon^{-3})$ bound grows linearly in dimension $d$, making high-dimensional zeroth-order optimization impractical.
- **Goldstein radius $\delta$ is problem-dependent**: The bound depends on an uncontrolled quantity that varies by problem; practitioners need guidance on estimating it.
- **No lower bounds provided**: The paper proves matching upper bounds to noncomposite rates, but doesn't prove these are fundamental limits for composite problems.
- **Experiments limited**: Numerical validation is minimal; practical performance on real neural networks vs. existing methods (SGD, Adam) is not shown.
- **Nonsmooth function must be Lipschitz**: Unbounded functions (e.g., ReLU-like activations in some regimes) may violate assumptions.

## Open Questions
1. Can the $\delta^{-1}$ dependence be improved or removed? Is it an artifact of Goldstein conditions or fundamental?
2. Does the theory extend to *stochastic* regularizers (e.g., dropout-like randomization)?
3. How does this compare empirically to modern adaptive methods (Adam, RMSprop) on realistic neural networks?
4. Can the dimension dependence in zeroth-order access be improved, or is it information-theoretic?
5. Does this framework extend to non-convex regularizers (e.g., L0 sparsity), which matter in practice?
6. What happens with composite objectives where the nonsmooth part is also nonconvex?

## Key Takeaway
Adding convex regularization to nonconvex learning doesn't increase sample complexity—a theoretical guarantee useful for practical constrained optimization.
