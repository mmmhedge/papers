# AI and Exchange Rate Predictability

**Authors:** Amin Izadyar
**Published:** 2026-08-01 | **arXiv:** [2608.00761v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00761v1)

## Summary
This paper uses generative AI models (ChatGPT and DeepSeek) to predict currency exchange rates by reasoning about economic fundamentals, solving a decades-old puzzle in finance called the exchange rate disconnect. The approach generates trading signals with a Sharpe ratio above 0.7 annually, suggesting that large language models can extract economically meaningful insights from macroeconomic data that traditional methods miss.

## Theoretical Background
The exchange rate disconnect puzzle, identified by Meese and Rogoff (1983), observes that standard economic models (based on purchasing power parity, interest rate differentials, and other fundamentals) fail to predict currency movements better than a random walk. This has been a central empirical anomaly in international finance for 40+ years. The paper builds on the Taylor rule framework — a monetary policy rule that central banks use to set interest rates based on inflation and output gaps — as a theoretical bridge connecting macroeconomic fundamentals to exchange rates. The core idea is that if currency markets are efficient, exchange rates should reflect differences in monetary policy stance across countries. Large language models — neural networks trained on vast text corpora that can reason over and synthesize complex information — are applied here as reasoning engines rather than pure prediction models.

## Core Contributions
1. **Demonstrates LLM reasoning as a macroeconomic interpreter**: Rather than training a supervised model, the paper uses generative AI to synthesize economic releases and produce a "fundamental strength" score for currencies. This is reasoning-based rather than pattern-matching.
2. **Breaks the exchange rate disconnect**: Shows that AI-extracted fundamentals have significant cross-sectional predictive power, where traditional models fail.
3. **Generates economically significant returns**: A simple long/short strategy yields a Sharpe ratio of 0.7+, with excess returns that survive controls for standard currency risk factors.
4. **Validates against look-ahead bias**: Runs multiple robustness checks to confirm predictability comes from reasoning, not memorization or data leakage.
5. **Identifies the Taylor rule as the mechanism**: Provides evidence that the Taylor rule framework explains how fundamentals transmit to exchange rates.

## Connections to Other Work
This connects to the broader literature on [[RLHF]] and [[Alignment]] by showing that LLMs can be used as economic reasoners without fine-tuning. It relates to [[Mechanistic Interpretability]] questions: *how* do LLMs extract signal from noisy macroeconomic data? The paper implicitly addresses [[Market Microstructure]] inefficiencies — if AI can reason about fundamentals in real time, why don't markets price these in faster? It also challenges [[Algorithmic Trading]] assumptions; most quantitative strategies use supervised learning or rules-based logic, but this shows reasoning-based synthesis can outperform.

## Practical Applications
1. **Currency hedge funds**: Portfolio managers can use AI-synthesized fundamental signals to time long/short FX positions for institutions with macro views.
2. **Corporate FX treasury**: Multinational firms can optimize timing of foreign exchange hedges based on AI-assessed fundamental strength of currencies.
3. **Central bank monitoring**: Policy makers can use the AI scores as an early warning system for currency misalignments or capital flow risks.
4. **Carry trade optimization**: Traders can layer AI fundamental signals onto traditional carry strategies to reduce drawdowns during reversals.
5. **Emerging market alpha**: The approach is particularly valuable in emerging markets where data quality is lower but AI can synthesize multiple signals into a coherent view.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **"FundamentalAI"** — A SaaS platform that ingests real-time economic releases and uses LLMs to score currency fundamental strength, feeding into hedge fund PM dashboards and alert systems.
2. **"Taylor Bot"** — An AI-powered trading assistant for FX desks that flags when the Taylor rule is misaligned with exchange rate prices, suggesting trade opportunities.
3. **"MacroLens"** — A research tool for institutional investors that uses LLMs to synthesize economic data into narrative summaries and momentum signals across currency pairs.
4. **"EM Currency Shield"** — Targeted product for emerging market central banks / treasuries that provides real-time AI assessment of their currency's fundamental strength vs. peers, with policy recommendations.
5. **"Quant-as-a-Service FX"** — White-label API that wraps the AI reasoning engine for brokers, enabling retail platforms to offer AI-driven currency signals and automated strategies.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Generalization risk**: Models trained on ChatGPT and DeepSeek may reflect their specific training data and instruction-tuning biases. Performance could degrade with different LLMs or future versions.
2. **Look-ahead bias not fully resolved**: While the paper runs robustness checks, using LLMs to reason about economic data introduces subtle temporal contamination risks. The models may have seen aggregate economic commentary that partially prices in future moves.
3. **Market efficiency assumption**: The strategy assumes mispricing persists. If this becomes widely adopted, competitive arbitrage will erase the edge.
4. **Data quality for emerging markets**: Economic data for non-major currency pairs is sparse, noisier, and released irregularly. Scalability beyond major pairs is uncertain.
5. **Interpretation opacity**: LLM reasoning is not fully interpretable. We don't know exactly which data points drive the fundamental scores, limiting robustness testing and regulatory compliance in some jurisdictions.
6. **Transaction costs not modeled**: The Sharpe ratio is measured on gross returns; realistic net returns (spreads, commissions, slippage) would be significantly lower.

## Open Questions
1. **Out-of-sample temporal stability**: Does this work in regimes with low or negative real rates? During crisis periods? The paper tests on historical data — prospective testing is essential.
2. **What information are LLMs extracting that markets miss?** Can mechanistic interpretability techniques reveal which economic signals drive predictions?
3. **Latency and real-time implementation**: How quickly can the LLM reasoning run? Is it fast enough to trade on intraday or only swing timeframes?
4. **Cross-asset spillovers**: Does AI-assessed FX fundamental strength predict equity returns, bond spreads, or commodity prices for the same country?
5. **Policy robustness**: Do results hold when central banks deviate from the Taylor rule (as during unconventional monetary policy)? Can the model adapt?
6. **Scalability to smaller LLMs**: Do cheaper, smaller models work equally well, or is reasoning quality size-dependent?

## Key Takeaway
Generative AI reasoning about economic data predicts currency returns and solves a forty-year finance puzzle by uncovering a stable long/short strategy.
