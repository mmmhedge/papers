# A Spiking Neural Network Model of Elementary Self-Consciousness via Endogenous Default Mode Network Dynamics

**Authors:** R. Lahoz-Beltra
**Published:** 2026-09-24 | **arXiv:** [2609.29984v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29984v1)

## Summary
This paper builds a large computational model of the brain's Default Mode Network—the system active when we're at rest—using 10,000 spiking neurons to explore how self-awareness might emerge. The key idea is that self-consciousness arises from the interaction between an autonomous, internally-driven network (the DMN) and sensory input, creating a stable "sense of self" even without external stimulation. This matters because understanding the neural basis of self-awareness is a foundational question in neuroscience and cognitive science.

## Theoretical Background
The work rests on several established concepts. **Spiking neural networks (SNNs)** are computational models where neurons fire discrete action potentials (spikes) at specific times, more biologically realistic than standard artificial neural networks. The paper uses **Izhikevich dynamics**, a simplified but neuroscientifically plausible model of neuronal behavior that can produce diverse firing patterns with few equations. The **Default Mode Network** is a well-documented set of brain regions that activate during rest and self-referential thought—a discovery from functional neuroimaging over the past two decades. **Tonic neuromodulation** refers to steady chemical signals from the brainstem (like dopamine or norepinephrine) that set the baseline excitability of neural circuits. The theoretical premise is that self-consciousness depends on a stable internal representation that persists even when external inputs are absent.

## Core Contributions
1. **Explicit hierarchical SNN architecture for self-representation**: The model concretely implements a two-layer structure where top-down DMN projections dominate bottom-up sensory signals, a specific architectural hypothesis for how self might be coded.
2. **Mechanistic account of endogenous pacemaker activity**: Demonstrates mathematically how intrinsic bursting neurons (with autonomous rhythms driven by tonic currents) can sustain self-referential activity independent of external perturbations.
3. **Integration of brainstem neuromodulation**: Models ascending noradrenergic/dopaminergic tones as continuous currents shaping network excitability—a bridge between systems neuroscience and computational modeling.
4. **Quantitative framework linking DMN dynamics to elementary self-consciousness**: Provides a testable, simulation-based model that makes explicit predictions about which neural mechanisms enable persistent self-representation.

## Connections to Other Work
This work engages with classical neuroscience traditions (network dynamics, neuromodulation) but sits somewhat orthogonally to the current ML/AI tags. It relates conceptually to:
- **Embodied cognition** literature emphasizing that mind is grounded in neural physiology, not abstract computation
- **Integrated Information Theory (IIT)** and other frameworks seeking neural correlates of consciousness
- Historical work on **central pattern generators** and autonomous rhythmic circuits in animal nervous systems
- Recent interest in **neuromorphic computing** and brain-inspired architectures (though this paper is more neuroscience-forward than engineering-forward)

The paper does *not* engage with modern deep learning, transformers, or large language models—it is fundamentally a computational neuroscience study.

## Practical Applications
1. **Neurofeedback and consciousness assessment**: Diagnostic tools for detecting consciousness levels in coma or locked-in patients by measuring DMN-like endogenous rhythms in EEG/fMRI.
2. **Neuromorphic chip design**: Informing analog or mixed-signal neuromorphic processors that replicate DMN dynamics for ultra-low-power embodied AI systems (e.g., brain-on-chip implants).
3. **Anesthesia mechanisms**: Predicting how anesthetics disrupt self-consciousness by modeling suppression of tonic neuromodulation or DMN-sensory coupling.
4. **Schizophrenia and autism spectrum modeling**: Testing hypotheses about disrupted self-referential processing in conditions involving altered DMN function.
5. **Robotic self-awareness**: Embedding DMN-like pacemaker circuits in autonomous robots to create rudimentary self-models independent of immediate sensory feedback.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **DMN-Monitor**: A wearable EEG device with embedded SNN simulation that detects disruptions in endogenous DMN rhythms as early warnings for dissociation, depression, or anesthesia depth during surgery.
2. **Neuromorphic Self-Awareness Chip**: Licensed IP for neuromorphic processor manufacturers (Intel Loihi, BrainScaleS) to embed self-model circuits in edge AI devices, enabling embodied robots with persistent task continuity.
3. **Virtual Patient Simulator**: Clinical training software using the model to simulate patient consciousness states (alert, drowsy, unconscious) with realistic neural dynamics for anesthesiology education.
4. **Consciousness Biomarker Assay**: B2B service for pharmaceutical companies testing consciousness-related side effects of novel drugs by running large-scale SNNs with compound-induced parameter perturbations.
5. **Brain-Computer Interface (BCI) Decoder**: Next-gen BCI software that uses DMN pacemaker signatures to decode intended actions in locked-in patients with higher fidelity than current systems.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Scale and biological realism gap**: 10,000 neurons is orders of magnitude smaller than actual brain networks; the DMN alone involves millions. Unclear if emergent properties scale linearly.
2. **Oversimplified neuromodulation**: Tonic currents are a crude proxy for complex neuroendocrine systems (serotonin, acetylcholine, endocannabinoids); missing receptor dynamics, uptake, and spatial diffusion.
3. **No empirical validation**: The model is a proof-of-concept; no direct comparison to real neural recordings (fMRI, multi-electrode arrays) from conscious vs. unconscious subjects.
4. **Phenomenological gap**: The paper claims to model "self-consciousness," but demonstrates only persistent activity patterns. The explanatory link between neural dynamics and subjective experience (the "hard problem") remains unaddressed.
5. **Fixed connectivity assumptions**: Synaptic weights are static; no learning or plasticity mechanisms, so the network cannot adapt or develop representations over time.
6. **Numerical integration artifacts**: Modified Euler schemes can introduce stability or accuracy errors; no validation against ground-truth solutions or alternative solvers.

## Open Questions
1. **Does the model exhibit criticality?** Computational neuroscience often finds consciousness near phase transitions or critical points. Does tuning tonic current strength reveal a bifurcation where self-awareness "switches on"?
2. **How does sensory perturbation strength affect self-representation?** A parameter sweep varying input magnitude could test whether weak stimuli preserve DMN coherence while strong stimuli disrupt it (as in anesthesia).
3. **Can hierarchical DMN structure be derived from learning principles?** Rather than hand-coded, could the DMN-to-sensory weight ratio emerge from unsupervised or self-supervised learning rules?
4. **Multi-scale validation**: Embed the SNN in a larger framework (e.g., coupled to a simulated body and environment) and test whether the model generalizes to sensorimotor integration.
5. **Comparative modeling**: Build analogous SNNs for simple organisms (C. elegans, larval zebrafish) with known connectomics and test whether the framework predicts their capacity for elementary self-representation.

## Key Takeaway
Spiking neural network with autonomous pacemaker layer may explain how brain maintains self-awareness independent of external input.
