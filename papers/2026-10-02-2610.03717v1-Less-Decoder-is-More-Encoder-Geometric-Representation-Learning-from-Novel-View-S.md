# Less Decoder is More Encoder: Geometric Representation Learning from Novel View Synthesis

**Authors:** Keerthi Kaashyap, Dennis Anthony, Akshay Krishnan, Nhi Ngoc Nguyen, Jeremy Collins et al.
**Published:** 2026-10-02 | **arXiv:** [2610.03717v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03717v1)

## Summary
This paper argues that existing view synthesis methods produce poor geometric representations not because of insufficient training signal, but because of architectural choices that dilute what the encoder learns. The authors introduce SNAP, a self-supervised approach that uses a pose-conditioned decoder and latent-space reconstruction to learn transferable 3D geometric features from multi-view images. The method is competitive with supervised geometry methods while enabling downstream tasks like pose estimation, depth prediction, and robot manipulation.

## Theoretical Background
Novel View Synthesis (NVS) — generating plausible images from unseen camera viewpoints — has long been thought to force models to learn 3D scene structure. Recent work uses transformers (neural networks with attention mechanisms that process sequences efficiently) as encoders and decoders for this task. The implicit assumption was that NVS supervision would naturally produce rich geometric representations. However, this paper challenges that assumption by examining which architectural components actually harm representation quality. The work builds on self-supervised learning theory, which proposes that solving proxy tasks (like reconstruction) can yield useful general-purpose features without hand-labeled data.

## Core Contributions
1. **Diagnosis of the problem**: Identifies that spatially expressive decoders (which can reconstruct images from any location) and pixel-space reconstruction targets actively suppress transferable geometric knowledge in the encoder, rather than encouraging it.
2. **SNAP architecture**: Proposes a pose-conditioned local decoder that only reconstructs image patches visible from specific camera poses, and a latent-space (not pixel-space) reconstruction objective. This forces the encoder to handle geometric reasoning while the decoder stays simple.
3. **Emergent viewpoint invariance**: Shows that SNAP's learned features exhibit robustness to camera viewpoint changes without explicit supervision for this property, approaching the performance of heavily supervised methods.
4. **Empirical validation**: Demonstrates competitive performance across five diverse downstream tasks (localization, pose estimation, correspondence, depth, manipulation) with lower compute and data budgets than baselines.

## Connections to Other Work
This work relates to broader themes in self-supervised vision learning (like [[contrastive learning]] and [[masked image modeling]]), which ask what proxy tasks extract useful features. It contrasts with decoder-heavy generative approaches in vision (VAEs, diffusion models) that optimize for reconstruction quality rather than representation transferability. The paper also connects to the broader principle in deep learning that architectural inductive biases shape what gets learned — similar findings appear in [[mechanistic interpretability]] work examining how model design constrains learned representations. The pose-conditioning mechanism echoes [[equivariance]] work in geometric deep learning.

## Practical Applications
1. **Autonomous navigation**: Robots need to localize in environments and estimate 6D poses; SNAP features enable this without dense supervision.
2. **3D scene understanding**: AR/VR applications require understanding scene geometry from sparse camera views; learned features reduce annotation burden.
3. **Structure-from-motion**: Classical computer vision pipelines for reconstructing 3D scenes can be seeded with SNAP's correspondence predictions for better initial estimates.
4. **Robot manipulation**: Grasping and reaching require understanding 3D spatial relationships; the paper shows SNAP enables manipulation tasks with minimal task-specific training.
5. **Visual localization at scale**: Large indoor/outdoor environments need efficient pose estimation; self-supervised features reduce the cost of building localization systems.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **Self-supervised 3D reconstruction SaaS**: A cloud service that takes multi-view image sets and automatically produces high-quality 3D models and pose estimates without manual annotation, using SNAP-style encoders.
2. **Robotics perception platform**: Sell pre-trained SNAP encoders as a foundation model for robot companies, reducing their need for geometry-specific labeling pipelines.
3. **AR localization engine**: Lightweight visual localization software for mobile AR that works in GPS-denied environments by leveraging SNAP's viewpoint-invariant features.
4. **Autonomous vehicle perception backbone**: Build a next-generation perception stack for AVs that uses unsupervised geometric reasoning to reduce reliance on expensive 3D annotations.
5. **Synthetic data generation for vision**: A tool that generates task-specific training data by training NVS models on unlabeled video, then using SNAP-style encoders to extract features for downstream applications.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Dependence on multi-view data**: The method requires multiple views of the same scene during training; applications with single-image input cannot directly use the training objective.
2. **Camera pose assumption**: The approach assumes accurate camera poses are available during training. Errors in pose estimation could degrade learned representations.
3. **Patch-level features**: Emergent invariance is demonstrated at the patch level; whether this transfers cleanly to pixel-level tasks (e.g., dense semantic segmentation) is unclear.
4. **Scale to complex scenes**: Evaluation is on relatively controlled datasets; behavior on large-scale outdoor scenes with dynamic objects, occlusions, and appearance changes is not thoroughly explored.
5. **Computational cost of training**: While inference may be efficient, the paper doesn't fully characterize training costs compared to baselines, which could affect adoption.
6. **Latent-space design choices**: The choice of latent dimensionality and reconstruction loss specifics appear somewhat ad-hoc; sensitivity analysis is limited.

## Open Questions
1. How does SNAP scale to videos with dynamic scenes and moving objects, or does it require static scene assumptions?
2. Can the approach work with sparse, irregular multi-view samples (e.g., from unstructured internet image collections) rather than dense multi-view captures?
3. Does the viewpoint invariance property hold under more extreme camera transformations (extreme viewpoint shifts, motion blur, lighting changes)?
4. How does performance degrade as a function of pose estimation error? What are the robustness thresholds?
5. Can the latent-space reconstruction objective be combined with other self-supervised losses (e.g., contrastive learning) to further improve downstream performance?
6. How does SNAP compare to recent foundation models trained on internet-scale data? Is there a regime where supervised pre-training dominates?

## Key Takeaway
Restricting decoder expressivity forces encoders to learn better 3D geometry; SNAP achieves competitive geometric representations through architectural simplicity and latent-space reconstruction.
