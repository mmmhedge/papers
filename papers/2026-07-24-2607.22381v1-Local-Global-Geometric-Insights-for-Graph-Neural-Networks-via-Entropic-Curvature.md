# Local-Global Geometric Insights for Graph Neural Networks via Entropic Curvature

**Authors:** Rachid Caich, Yassine Abbahaddou
**Published:** 2026-07-24 | **arXiv:** [2607.22381v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22381v1)

## Summary
This paper introduces Entropic Curvature, a new mathematical tool for understanding how information flows across entire graphs in neural networks. The work solves a key limitation of existing curvature measures — they only look at local connections and miss what happens globally — by using transport theory (how probability distributions move through space) to capture long-distance information propagation. The authors show this unifies two major GNN failure modes (oversmoothing and oversquashing) and provide practical mechanisms to improve graph neural network performance.

## Theoretical Background
Graph Neural Networks process data on graphs by iteratively updating node representations using neighborhood information. Two critical problems emerged: oversmoothing (node features become indistinguishable after many layers) and oversquashing (distant nodes cannot communicate efficiently). Prior work introduced curvature notions — Ollivier-Ricci and Forman curvature — borrowed from differential geometry to diagnose these issues, but these measures only compare local neighborhoods and don't reveal global information flow patterns.

This paper extends the Lott-Sturm-Villani framework, which defines curvature in continuous spaces using optimal transport (finding efficient ways to move mass between distributions), to discrete graphs. The key insight is displacement convexity of entropy — a measure of how disorder spreads — along Wasserstein geodesics (optimal transport paths). Poincaré inequalities are classical tools from analysis that bound how quickly functions decay or diverge on a space.

## Core Contributions
1. **Entropic Curvature definition**: A novel global curvature measure on graphs derived from the Lott-Sturm-Villani framework, capturing how entropy (disorder) spreads via optimal transport over entire graphs.

2. **Weak Entropic Curvature proxy**: A computationally tractable lower bound that maintains theoretical guarantees while remaining implementable.

3. **Three theoretical guarantees**:
   - Poincaré-type inequality that certifies protection against oversmoothing
   - Transport-entropy generalization bound relating curvature to learning performance
   - Expansion paradox proving sparsity, spectral expansion, and positive curvature cannot simultaneously exist — unifying oversmoothing and oversquashing as opposite points on a curvature spectrum

4. **Three practical mechanisms**: E-Gate aggregator (adaptive message passing), ENT structural encoding (graph positional information), and Midpoint-Completion Rewiring (graph topology improvement).

5. **Empirical validation**: Benchmarking on six node-classification and graph-classification datasets against five baselines.

## Practical Applications
- **Recommendation systems**: Reducing feature collapse when stacking many GNN layers to capture long-range user-item relationships.
- **Molecular property prediction**: Ensuring chemically distant atoms can still influence predictions without information bottlenecks.
- **Social network analysis**: Preserving signal diversity in large-scale networks where simple message passing degrades.
- **Knowledge graph reasoning**: Maintaining distinct entity representations across multi-hop reasoning chains.
- **Traffic flow prediction**: Capturing dependencies between distant locations in road networks without losing spatial diversity.

## Technical Limitations
- **Computational overhead**: Computing optimal transport on large graphs is expensive; the "weak" proxy helps but scalability to billion-node graphs is unclear.
- **Discrete approximation**: Extending continuous Lott-Sturm-Villani theory to discrete graphs requires approximations whose tightness degrades with irregular graph structure.
- **Limited empirical gains**: While theoretically motivated, the absolute performance improvements over existing methods on standard benchmarks may be modest.
- **Assumptions on graph structure**: Theory assumes certain regularity; behavior on highly heterogeneous (power-law) graphs is unexplored.
- **Rewiring cost**: Mechanisms like MCR incur graph modification overhead that may dominate benefit for small or static graphs.
- **Limited scope**: Focus is on node and graph classification; applicability to edge prediction, generative tasks, or dynamic graphs less clear.

## Open Questions
- How does entropic curvature behave on power-law and scale-free graphs common in real applications?
- Can the weak proxy bound be tightened, and what is the gap between theory and practice?
- How do the three mechanisms (E-Gate, ENT, MCR) interact, and can they be optimized jointly?
- Does entropic curvature transfer across datasets, or must it be recomputed per graph?
- Can this framework extend to [[heterogeneous graphs]], [[temporal graphs]], and [[hypergraphs]]?
- What is the relationship between entropic curvature and other geometric properties like dimension or Gromov-hyperbolicity?

## Key Takeaway
Entropic curvature unifies graph neural network failure modes using global optimal transport, enabling better layer design and graph rewiring.
