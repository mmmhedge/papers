# Bayesian Partner Modelling enables Adaptive Replanning for LLM Coordination

**Authors:** Harsh Goel, Aditya Sai Ellendula, Vaishnav Tadiparthi, Ehsan Moradi Pari, Hossein Nourkhiz Mahjoub et al.
**Published:** 2026-08-19 | **arXiv:** [2608.18490v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18490v1)

## Summary
This paper tackles a key challenge in multi-agent LLM systems: agents often continue executing outdated plans when their teammates' strategies change mid-task. The authors introduce BayesBeliefAgent, which combines hierarchical LLM planning with Bayesian tracking to intelligently decide *when* to replan based on contradictions in observed partner behavior, rather than replanning constantly or ignoring strategy shifts. The method dramatically reduces unnecessary replans while keeping agents aligned with their teammates' actual strategies.

## Theoretical Background
Multi-agent coordination traditionally relies on either explicit communication or implicit partner modeling. This work builds on two threads: (1) **partner modeling** in cooperative games — inferring what strategies or skills teammates are executing — and (2) **hierarchical planning** in LLMs, where agents decompose tasks into sub-skills that execute over multiple steps. The key insight is that agents executing temporally extended skills (multi-step behaviors) are naturally "stuck" in a plan once committed; the challenge is knowing when commitment should be interrupted. The paper uses **Bayesian inference** to track partner state probabilistically, allowing uncertainty quantification, and pairs this with LLM-based planners that operate at the skill level rather than primitive actions. The **belief-action gap** metric — the fraction of decisions where the agent has correct information but acts suboptimally — is a novel framing to measure the lag between perception and response.

## Core Contributions
1. **Contradiction-conditioned replanning**: Rather than replanning constantly or passively, the agent replan only when partner actions directly contradict its current belief about their skill. This is more efficient than heuristic triggers.
2. **BayesBeliefAgent architecture**: A modular system pairing a hierarchical LLM planner with a Bayesian partner tracker, allowing skill-level reasoning rather than primitive action reasoning.
3. **Belief-action gap metric**: A quantitative measure of coordination lag that isolates the cost of slow adaptation from the cost of poor strategy. Existing work measures only task reward, missing this nuance.
4. **Empirical validation**: Demonstrates that contradiction-conditioned control achieves comparable task performance to heuristic replanning while using ~10× fewer replans across Overcooked benchmarks.

## Connections to Other Work
This relates to [[Theory of Mind]] reasoning in multi-agent RL and recent work on LLM-based agent coordination (e.g., LLM agents in Overcooked or cooperative games). It extends passive [[partner modeling]] — agents tracking what teammates do — to *active* interruption logic. It also connects to [[hierarchical reinforcement learning]], which structures plans into temporal abstraction; the novelty here is applying that framing to LLM agents and tying it to explicit Bayesian uncertainty. The work is distinct from constant replanning approaches (which are computationally expensive) and from static partner assumptions (which miss mid-task shifts).

## Practical Applications
1. **Robotic teams in dynamic environments**: Robots collaborating on assembly lines where humans or other robots may switch strategies mid-task (e.g., changing tool choices). The agent avoids wasting time on outdated sub-plans.
2. **Human-AI collaboration in real-time strategy games**: AI teammates in games like Dota 2 or StarCraft that must adapt to human player strategy changes without constant re-computation.
3. **Emergency response coordination**: Multiple AI agents and human operators managing disasters; when a teammate's operational focus shifts, detecting that shift and re-coordinating quickly is critical.
4. **Logistics and supply chain**: Autonomous vehicles or warehousing agents coordinating handoffs; if a partner's routing strategy or capacity changes, detecting it without over-replanning saves energy and time.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Assumption of discrete skills**: The method assumes agents can identify and track discrete partner "skills" (e.g., "cutting" vs. "plating" in Overcooked). Real-world behaviors may be continuous or ambiguous, making skill identification harder.
2. **Limited to contradiction detection**: The method only replan when actions *contradict* the current belief. Subtle strategy shifts that don't immediately contradict may go unnoticed.
3. **Overcooked as primary benchmark**: Overcooked is a highly structured, turn-based cooperative game. Generalization to real-time, stochastic, or adversarial multi-agent environments is unclear.
4. **Bayesian tractability**: The Bayesian tracking module assumes a tractable posterior over partner skills. In high-dimensional or open-ended environments (e.g., natural language negotiation), maintaining a well-calibrated belief may become intractable.
5. **LLM hallucination and consistency**: LLM planners are known to be inconsistent and sometimes generate nonsensical plans. The paper doesn't address how hallucinations or plan instability interact with the belief-tracking module.
6. **Computational cost of LLM inference**: While replanning is reduced, each LLM call for hierarchical planning is still expensive compared to lightweight discrete planners. Real-time constraints may limit applicability.

## Open Questions
1. How does the method scale to settings with >2 agents or asymmetric information (agents don't observe all partners' actions directly)?
2. Can the approach handle gradual or continuous strategy shifts, not just discrete contradictions?
3. What is the sample efficiency of the Bayesian tracker? How many partner actions are needed to accurately infer skill before the tracker makes costly errors?
4. How does belief-tracking robustness degrade under noisy or adversarial partner behavior (e.g., agents deliberately trying to confuse teammates)?
5. Can the method be extended to competitive or mixed-motive settings where partners don't have aligned objectives?
6. How sensitive is performance to the choice of Bayesian prior over partner skills? Can this be learned or adapted online?

## Key Takeaway
LLM agents can collaborate more efficiently by replanning only when a teammate's behavior contradicts their inferred strategy, rather than replanning constantly.
