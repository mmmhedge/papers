# Spiking neural networks for streaming qubit readout

**Authors:** Barry M. Dillon, Aqib Javed, Jim Harkin, Patryk Dabkowski, Benjamin Lienhard
**Published:** 2026-10-01 | **arXiv:** [2610.02129v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02129v1)

## Summary
This paper develops spiking neural networks (SNNs) — neural networks that process information as sparse temporal spikes rather than continuous activations — to read quantum qubit states in real time during superconducting quantum processors. The key innovation is processing measurement signals in successive chunks and streaming classification scores as data arrive, rather than waiting for the full measurement window; this approach beats traditional matched-filter methods and matches the accuracy of standard neural networks while running on FPGA hardware fast enough for real quantum feedback loops.

## Theoretical Background
Qubit readout in superconducting quantum processors is the measurement step that collapses a quantum state into classical 0 or 1. Conventional readout uses matched filtering — a signal-processing technique that correlates the noisy measured trace against an ideal template to maximize signal-to-noise ratio. However, real readout signals contain multiple overlapping phenomena: crosstalk (one qubit's measurement affecting others), relaxation (the qubit decaying back to its ground state during measurement), and other nonidealities that are multivariate and dynamic. Spiking neural networks are inspired by biological neurons and encode information in the timing and frequency of discrete spike events rather than continuous neuron activations. This temporal, event-driven nature makes SNNs naturally suited to streaming computation with lower latency and power consumption than standard artificial neural networks (ANNs). The paper uses quantization-aware training (reducing neural network precision for hardware efficiency) and hls4ml (a tool that compiles neural networks into FPGA-deployable hardware code).

## Core Contributions
1. **First application of SNNs to qubit readout**: Demonstrates that spiking networks can classify qubit states from multiplexed readout signals, outperforming matched filters and matching ANN accuracy.
2. **Streaming readout with time-resolved confidence**: Unlike standard ANNs that require the full trace, SNNs update their classification score chunk-by-chunk, giving a real-time, evolving estimate of qubit state — valuable for feedback control.
3. **FPGA-feasible latency**: Proves that quantized SNN inference can complete a classification update before the next chunk of data arrives, enabling subcycle-latency quantum control on real hardware.
4. **End-to-end hardware synthesis pipeline**: Shows practical deployment using quantization-aware training + hls4ml, bridging neural network design and FPGA constraints.

## Connections to Other Work
This work sits at the intersection of neural network-based quantum control and low-latency edge inference. It extends classical matched filtering (standard in quantum readout) by leveraging modern deep learning (specifically the temporal-streaming properties of SNNs). The use of SNNs echoes broader interest in neuromorphic computing for time-critical inference; however, prior SNN work has focused on computer-vision and sensorimotor tasks, not quantum measurement. The hardware-aware training (quantization-aware training + hls4ml) echoes efficiency-focused research in embedded neural networks but applies it to a novel quantum domain. The paper implicitly addresses [[reinforcement-learning]] and quantum feedback: faster, more accurate readout directly improves real-time feedback policies for quantum error correction.

## Practical Applications
1. **Quantum error correction**: Fast, accurate readout is the bottleneck for practical quantum error correction codes; lower-latency readout enables tighter feedback loops and higher logical error thresholds.
2. **Superconducting qubit calibration**: Real-time, chunk-by-chunk confidence scores let calibration algorithms adapt mid-measurement and detect transient faults.
3. **Multiplexed quantum processors**: As qubit counts grow, crosstalk becomes severe; the SNN's multivariate approach naturally handles frequency-multiplexed readout of tens to hundreds of qubits.
4. **Cryogenic FPGA deployment**: SNNs' lower power and latency suit operation inside dilution refrigerators where classical electronics are thermally constrained.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Limited to superconducting qubits**: The approach is tailored to frequency-multiplexed superconducting readout; generalization to trapped ions, photonic, or other platforms is unclear.
2. **Training data requirements**: SNNs must be trained on measured traces from the specific device; the method does not port directly across different qubit chips or architectures without retraining.
3. **FPGA synthesis overhead**: Quantization and hls4ml compilation may lose accuracy compared to high-precision SNNs; the paper doesn't deeply analyze the trade-off across bit-widths or network sizes.
4. **Chunk-size and timing sensitivity**: Performance depends on choosing the right temporal chunk size; too coarse and you lose temporal resolution, too fine and latency becomes tight. No principled method for this choice is provided.
5. **Scalability to large multiplexing**: Unclear how SNNs scale to 100+ qubits with severe crosstalk; the experiments are likely on smaller systems (the paper does not specify qubit count).
6. **No theoretical guarantees**: Unlike matched filtering, which has optimal detection theory, SNNs are empirical. No analysis of fundamental speed-accuracy limits is given.

## Open Questions
1. How does performance scale with the number of multiplexed qubits? Is there a crosstalk saturation point?
2. Can transfer learning reduce retraining time if a new qubit is added to the processor?
3. What is the theoretical lower bound on readout latency given a fixed SNR and chunk size? How does it compare to matched filtering?
4. Can SNNs outperform matched filters in the presence of phase drift or slow parameter drift during the measurement window?
5. How sensitive are SNNs to hyperparameters (network depth, neuron count, threshold, time constant)? Is there a design methodology?
6. Can SNNs be combined with classical feedback to adaptively refine the readout window in real time?
7. Do SNNs provide meaningful uncertainty estimates (e.g., soft confidence scores) that calibration or error-correction algorithms can exploit?

## Key Takeaway
Spiking neural networks enable real-time, low-latency qubit readout on FPGAs by streaming classification scores as measurement data arrive, outperforming traditional methods for quantum control.
