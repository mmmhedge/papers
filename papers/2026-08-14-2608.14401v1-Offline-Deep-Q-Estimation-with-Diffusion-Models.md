# Offline Deep Q* Estimation with Diffusion Models

**Authors:** Xiaohong Chen, Yuling Jiao, Lican Kang, Jerry Zhijian Yang, Chen Zhong
**Published:** 2026-08-14 | **arXiv:** [2608.14401v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14401v1)

## Summary
This paper tackles a core problem in offline reinforcement learning: estimating the optimal action-value function (Q*) when you can only learn from a fixed dataset, without interacting with the environment. The key innovation is using diffusion models — generative neural networks that learn complex distributions — to first estimate the unknown reward function and state transitions, then using those estimates to solve the Bellman equation (the recursive relationship that defines optimal decision-making). The paper provides rigorous theoretical guarantees showing the method converges at a specific rate and demonstrates it works well in practice.

## Theoretical Background
Offline reinforcement learning addresses a practical constraint: in many real-world settings (healthcare, robotics, finance), you cannot afford to experiment freely, so you must learn from historical data alone. The standard approach uses the Bellman equation — a recursive equation that relates the value of a state to immediate rewards and future values. In offline settings, both the reward function and transition kernel (how the environment evolves) are unknown, making the optimal Bellman operator unavailable directly from data.

This paper builds on two key threads: (1) recent work using diffusion models — neural networks trained to reverse noise and generate samples from learned distributions — for conditional density estimation in RL, and (2) deep neural network approximation theory, which analyzes how well neural networks can solve functional equations like the Bellman equation. The contribution decouples these: first learn the environment's dynamics and rewards as distributions using diffusion models, then use those as surrogates in the Bellman equation.

## Core Contributions
1. **Novel operator-learning framework**: Explicitly decouples estimation of the Bellman operator (environment learning) from value function learning, rather than end-to-end learning.

2. **Theoretical guarantees without strong assumptions**: Provides sharp nonasymptotic convergence rates that do *not* rely on "completeness" — a strong assumption commonly required in deep RL theory that essentially assumes the function class can represent all possible value functions.

3. **End-to-end analysis of diffusion-based estimation**: Establishes that conditional diffusion models achieve convergence in total variation distance (a strong probabilistic measure) at a rate depending on state/action dimensions and smoothness of Q*.

4. **Explicit oracle rates**: Derives concrete convergence rates ($\widetilde{\mathcal O}(n^{-2\beta/(d_x + d_a + 2\beta)})$ for Bellman residual, improving to $\widetilde{\mathcal O}(n^{-\beta/(d_x + d_a + 2\beta)})$ in $L^2$) where $\beta$ is the smoothness of the optimal value function.

## Connections to Other Work
This work sits at the intersection of [[offline reinforcement learning]], [[diffusion models]], and [[deep RL theory]]. It relates to:
- Prior work using generative models for RL (GANs, VAEs, autoregressive models), but applies the recent success of diffusion models
- Classical offline RL methods like batch Q-learning and behavior-cloning, which also avoid direct operator access
- Theoretical advances in [[neural network function approximation]], particularly without completeness assumptions
- Concurrent work on diffusion-based planning and model-based RL

The key distinction is that most prior work either avoids theory or makes completeness assumptions; this paper provides rates without that restriction.

## Practical Applications
1. **Healthcare treatment planning**: Hospitals have historical patient data but cannot experiment on new treatment regimens. This method learns from past outcomes to estimate optimal treatment policies.

2. **Autonomous vehicle sim-to-real transfer**: Use simulation and real driving logs (without safety-critical exploration) to learn control policies that transfer to new environments.

3. **Robotics manipulation**: Train robot control from logged human demonstrations and simulation without further real-world interaction.

4. **Recommendation systems**: Learn user preference models from historical interaction logs to optimize ranking policies without A/B testing every variant.

5. **Financial portfolio optimization**: Estimate optimal trading policies from historical market data and recorded trading logs without live experimentation.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **Offline RL as a service (RL-as-a-Service for enterprises)**: A cloud platform where companies upload historical operational data (e.g., supply chain logs, manufacturing decisions), and the system returns an optimized policy via diffusion-based Q* estimation. Target: logistics, healthcare operations.

2. **Safer robot training platform**: Software toolkit that converts robot learning from interactive (risky) to offline (safe) by using this method on existing teleoperation/simulation data. Market: manufacturing automation, surgical robotics.

3. **Clinical decision support**: SaaS for hospitals that ingests patient records and suggests evidence-based treatment protocols by estimating Q* from historical outcomes, certified by this convergence theory.

4. **Synthetic data generation for RL**: Use the estimated environment models (rewards + transitions from diffusion) to generate unlimited synthetic rollouts for downstream RL agents, reducing sample complexity.

5. **Financial strategy backtesting engine**: Tool for hedge funds / trading desks to test policy optimization on historical market data with theoretical guarantees, reducing live-trading risk during policy deployment.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Curse of dimensionality**: The convergence rate decays with dimension ($d_x + d_a$) — in high-dimensional state/action spaces, convergence slows significantly. Methods for deep state representations (e.g., visual inputs) are not addressed.

2. **Smoothness assumption**: Requires Q* to be Hölder smooth (β > 0), which may not hold near discontinuities or value cliffs in practice. No guidance on verifying or relaxing this.

3. **Concentrability condition**: The final $L^2$ rate requires a "concentrability" assumption (roughly: the offline data distribution must sufficiently cover the optimal policy's induced distribution). This can fail badly with poor historical data.

4. **Two-stage estimation error**: Errors in diffusion-based reward/transition estimation propagate into the value function. The paper bounds this but doesn't study robustness to model misspecification.

5. **Computational cost**: Training conditional diffusion models is expensive (requires many sampling/denoising steps). Scalability to large-scale problems is unclear.

6. **Limited empirical scope**: Experiments appear to be in standard benchmarks; real offline RL often involves distribution shift, missing features, or heterogeneous data that aren't tested here.

## Open Questions
1. **Dimensionality reduction**: Can representation learning (e.g., learning a low-dimensional latent state) be combined with this framework to beat the curse of dimensionality?

2. **Model misspecification**: What happens if the estimated reward/transition kernels are biased (not just noisy)? Can the method be made robust?

3. **Data-dependent concentrability**: Can you weaken the concentrability condition or provide algorithms that learn *which* regions of the state space are reliable?

4. **Finite-sample empirics**: How many samples does the method need in practice relative to standard offline RL on real benchmarks? Does theory predict practice?

5. **Partial observability**: How does this extend to settings where states are not fully observed (POMDPs)?

6. **Offline-to-online**: Can you smoothly transition from offline learning (this paper) to online fine-tuning when interaction becomes available?

## Key Takeaway
Diffusion models can learn environment dynamics offline, then plug into Bellman equations with convergence guarantees that don't require strong completeness assumptions.
