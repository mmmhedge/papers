# Lapis: Laplacian Spiking Attention via First-Spike Timing and Membrane Leakage

**Authors:** Kaiwen Tang, Jiaqi Zheng, Zixuan Zhu, Yiqun Wang, Zhanglu Yan et al.
**Published:** 2026-08-12 | **arXiv:** [2608.11865v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11865v1)

## Summary
This paper introduces Lapis, a new attention mechanism designed specifically for spiking neural networks (SNNs) — biologically-inspired networks that process information via sparse spike timing rather than continuous activations. Instead of using standard dot-product scoring like transformers do, Lapis scores token relationships using the distance between first-spike arrival times, mapped through a Laplacian kernel. The key insight is that this approach uses only addition and subtraction, not multiplication, making it dramatically more energy-efficient while maintaining competitive accuracy on image classification tasks.

## Theoretical Background
Spiking neural networks are inspired by biological neurons and represent information as precise timing of discrete events (spikes), contrasting with traditional artificial neural networks that use continuous values. Self-attention — the mechanism powering modern transformers — computes relationships between elements by scoring query-key pairs, typically via dot products. Time-to-first-spike coding is a method for encoding information in SNNs where a neuron's input is represented by how quickly it fires. Leaky integrate-and-fire (LIF) neurons are the standard SNN building block, modeling how biological neurons accumulate input over time and fire when reaching a threshold. The paper leverages the mathematical relationship between distance-based scoring and LIF membrane dynamics to design attention that feels native to spiking computation.

## Core Contributions
1. **Lapis attention mechanism**: A novel scoring function that replaces dot products with L1 distances between query/key first-spike latency vectors, eliminating all query-key channel multiplication.
2. **Laplacian kernel mapping**: Uses exponential decay (Laplacian kernel) to convert latency distances to attention affinities, with the kernel shape matching actual LIF membrane impulse responses.
3. **Hardware-aligned design**: Row normalization becomes a simple bit-shift operation under power-of-two rounding, and the entire scoring uses only subtraction, absolute value, and accumulation — operations native to spiking hardware.
4. **Empirical validation**: Demonstrates 96.56% accuracy on CIFAR-10 (0.53 points below dense attention) and 14.5x energy reduction on ImageNet-1K relative to dense dot-product attention, with a 6-bit deployed model achieving 83.25% ImageNet-1K accuracy at 3.28mJ per image.

## Connections to Other Work
This work sits at the intersection of [[Attention Is All You Need]]-style transformer architectures and spiking neural networks. It addresses a long-standing challenge in spiking vision transformers: prior work either simplified attention scoring (losing expressive power) or replaced it with discrete operators (misaligned with SNN dynamics). Lapis bridges this by making attention mechanistically coherent with SNN computation, similar in spirit to work on designing neural architectures that respect hardware constraints. The energy efficiency gains connect to broader efforts in neuromorphic computing and efficient vision models, though those typically target dense networks or other paradigms. The use of time-to-first-spike coding echoes classical rate coding and temporal coding in computational neuroscience.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Accuracy gap on complex datasets**: 0.53-point ImageNet gap vs. dense attention is non-trivial; larger models or more complex tasks may widen this.
2. **First-spike encoding assumptions**: Time-to-first-spike coding requires carefully tuned input scaling and threshold levels; performance degrades if input distributions shift or vary across deployment contexts.
3. **Limited to image classification**: Paper focuses on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet; generalization to detection, segmentation, or other vision tasks (which may need richer temporal dynamics) is unexplored.
4. **Neuromorphic hardware dependency**: Energy gains assume actual neuromorphic hardware (Loihi, TrueNorth, future ASICs); on standard GPUs/TPUs, SNNs may underperform due to inherent sparsity-computation mismatch.
5. **Membrane latency in practice**: Real hardware may introduce latency variability and noise in spike timing; robustness to realistic neural dynamics (jitter, mismatch) is not characterized.
6. **Training complexity**: Spiking networks require surrogate gradient training and careful hyperparameter tuning; no discussion of training stability or convergence rates relative to dense networks.

## Open Questions
1. How does Lapis attention scale to larger models (ViT-Large or ViT-Huge scale) and pretraining tasks (ImageNet-21K, foundation models)? Does the accuracy gap grow?
2. Can Lapis be extended to other modalities (NLP, multi-modal) where spike timing has less semantic meaning, or is it vision-specific?
3. How sensitive is performance to first-spike encoding hyperparameters (threshold, input scaling) across different image statistics (lighting, resolution, domain)?
4. What is the theoretical connection between Laplacian kernels and optimal attention weighting in SNNs? Can this be formalized to derive other kernels?
5. Can Lapis be combined with [[RLHF]] or other fine-tuning methods for spiking networks, or does spike-based attention break common training paradigms?
6. How does Lapis perform under adversarial perturbations or distribution shifts compared to dense attention? Is spike timing more or less robust?
7. Can temporal dynamics (multi-step spike sequences) enrich the scoring, or is first-spike-only optimal?

## Key Takeaway
Spiking networks can score attention using spike timing distance instead of multiplication, achieving 14× energy savings with minimal accuracy loss on ImageNet.
