# Form and Void: Entangled Composition through an Autonomous AI Agent

**Authors:** Shiwen Wang, Jian Yang, Xu Wang, Xincan Wang, Weiming Dong
**Published:** 2026-10-01 | **arXiv:** [2610.02045v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02045v1)

## Summary
This paper introduces FaV-A, an AI agent that generates images with sophisticated positive-negative space composition—where foreground objects and background empty space are intentionally designed to work together visually. Current text-to-image models struggle with this because they must coordinate two interdependent concepts that share a boundary. FaV-A solves this by breaking the task into stages: first generating a base object, then analyzing it to find complementary negative space, then composing them together—outperforming single-pass prompting.

## Theoretical Background
The work builds on two main foundations. First, the principle of positive-negative space (also called figure-ground relationship) from visual design and art—where empty space is as compositionally important as filled space, creating visual balance and layered meaning. Second, recent advances in [[Generative Models|text-to-image generation]] and multimodal large language models (MLLMs)—neural networks trained on paired images and text that can generate images from descriptions or analyze images semantically. The paper recognizes that while these models are powerful, they treat composition as a monolithic problem, not as a multi-step reasoning task.

## Core Contributions
1. **FaV-A agent design**: A staged workflow that explicitly decomposes positive-negative space generation into sequential reasoning steps (base object → shape analysis → negative space identification → compositional instruction generation), rather than attempting it in a single pass.
2. **Progressive generation framework**: A new paradigm showing that coordinated semantic control over entangled concepts is better solved through iterative refinement with agent-based planning.
3. **Empirical validation**: Demonstration that staged generation with multimodal reasoning outperforms zero-shot direct prompting for this specific compositional challenge.

## Connections to Other Work
This relates to [[Agents|agent-based image generation]] and the broader trend of decomposing complex generation tasks into simpler sub-problems (similar to chain-of-thought reasoning in language models). It extends recent work on [[Generative Models|text-to-image diffusion models]] by adding compositional intelligence. The work also connects to [[Multi-Agent Systems|multi-step visual planning]] where agents reason about spatial relationships. Unlike simple prompting-based approaches, FaV-A treats composition as a planning problem requiring analysis of intermediate outputs.

## Practical Applications
1. **Professional design tools**: Graphic design and UX/UI software that auto-generates compositionally sound layouts with meaningful negative space.
2. **Film and animation pre-visualization**: Storyboard generation where positive-negative space relationships enhance visual narrative and cinematic balance.
3. **Advertising creative generation**: Automated poster and banner creation that balances subject matter with strategic empty space for visual impact.
4. **Architectural visualization**: Interior and exterior design renderings where negative space (void, open areas) is explicitly designed alongside structures.
5. **Web and print design automation**: Layout generation for marketing collateral, book covers, and editorial design.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **CompositionAI**: A design-focused SaaS tool that generates marketing visuals (social media posts, ads, banners) with automatically balanced positive-negative space, integrated with Figma/Adobe Suite.
2. **StoryboardBot**: An agent-driven service for film/game studios that generates cinematic pre-visualization with intentional compositional depth for faster pre-production.
3. **LayoutGPT**: An enterprise tool for publishers and agencies that auto-generates editorial layouts (magazine spreads, newsletters) with professional compositional balance.
4. **ArchViz Pro**: Architecture/real estate visualization platform using FaV-A principles to render spaces with clear emphasis on void and structure for presentations.
5. **DesignReview API**: A backend service that analyzes user-uploaded designs and regenerates them with improved positive-negative space balance, sellable to design platforms.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Boundary coordination complexity**: The paper doesn't fully address how precisely the agent identifies where positive and negative space should meet—this boundary definition may be ambiguous in complex scenes.
2. **Scalability with scene complexity**: The staged approach may break down with multiple objects or intricate foreground-background relationships; unclear how it generalizes beyond single-object focus.
3. **Semantic ambiguity**: The "negative space semantics" identification step relies on MLLM reasoning, which may fail on abstract or unconventional design intent.
4. **Evaluation metrics**: The paper likely relies on human judgment or limited quantitative metrics; no standardized metric for "good" positive-negative composition exists.
5. **Computational cost**: Multi-stage generation is slower than single-pass; trade-off between quality and latency not thoroughly explored.
6. **Dependency on base model quality**: Success is bottlenecked by the initial text-to-image model's ability to generate coherent objects.

## Open Questions
1. How does FaV-A perform on scenes with multiple foreground objects or competing semantic elements? Does the staged approach generalize?
2. Can the agent learn to *refine* compositions iteratively (e.g., regenerate negative space semantics based on aesthetic feedback)?
3. How sensitive is the approach to MLLM reasoning errors? What happens when shape analysis misinterprets object geometry?
4. Could reinforcement learning or human preference signals help the agent optimize for compositions that humans rate as more balanced?
5. Does the staged workflow benefit from learned weighting or attention between stages, or is the current pipeline fixed?
6. How does this approach compare to end-to-end fine-tuned models trained on annotated positive-negative space datasets?

## Key Takeaway
An AI agent that designs better image compositions by breaking positive-negative space generation into planned reasoning steps rather than single prompts.
