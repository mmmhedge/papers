# FastBench: Can Streaming VLMs Perceive High-Dynamic Real-World Streams?

**Authors:** Yuxuan Hu, Weikang Shi, Yang Bo, Xudong Lu, Xintong Guo et al.
**Published:** 2026-10-08 | **arXiv:** [2610.12427v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12427v1)

## Summary
This paper introduces FastBench, a benchmark designed to test whether streaming video language models can understand fast-moving, dynamic real-world events. Current models struggle with this task because they must choose between keeping high temporal resolution (many frames per second) or maintaining long temporal memory—a fundamental trade-off under limited computational budgets. The paper shows even state-of-the-art models achieve only ~50% accuracy on high-dynamic scenarios, revealing a critical gap in streaming video understanding.

## Theoretical Background
Streaming video language models (VLMs) extend large language models by ingesting continuous video feeds and answering questions about them. These models use attention mechanisms (similar to [[Transformers]]) to process visual and textual information jointly. The core challenge is the *context budget constraint*: a model has a fixed amount of memory to store past frames, so it must make hard choices about frame sampling rate (e.g., 2 FPS vs. 24 FPS), spatial resolution, and how far back in time to retain history. Prior work has benchmarked VLMs on static images and low-dynamic videos where events unfold slowly; FastBench addresses high-dynamic scenarios where critical events (e.g., a ball being caught, a person falling) occur in milliseconds and may be missed by sparse sampling.

## Core Contributions
1. **FastBench benchmark**: 306 QA pairs grounded in high-FPS video clips, with a rigorous pipeline that filters questions to ensure they require high-temporal-resolution perception, verifies answers using trajectory tracking tools (SAM3, CoTracker3), and includes human inspection and evidence intervals.
2. **Taxonomy of temporal perception**: Organizes questions by capability (e.g., object localization, action recognition, state change) and temporal scope (forward-looking, instantaneous, backward-looking).
3. **ProactiveFrame baseline**: A training-free method that dynamically adjusts frame sampling rates through a dual-tier sliding window—keeping recent frames dense while compressing older history into sparse samples. Shows 5.4 percentage point improvement over uniform sparse sampling.
4. **Empirical finding**: Even top models (Gemini-3.5-Flash at 50.7%) significantly underperform, and performance saturates as history is compressed, suggesting models lack the ability to autonomously decide *when* to request finer temporal granularity.

## Connections to Other Work
FastBench complements existing [[Video Understanding]] benchmarks (e.g., VideoQA datasets) by focusing specifically on the temporal resolution vs. memory trade-off. It relates to work on [[Attention Mechanisms]] and context window optimization in [[Transformers]], and to [[Adaptive Computation]] methods that adjust model behavior based on input complexity. The trajectory-grounded verification approach parallels recent work in [[Video Object Tracking]] (CoTracker, SAM) used for ground-truth verification. ProactiveFrame echoes ideas in token pruning and dynamic attention in language models, but applied to frame-level streaming video.

## Practical Applications
1. **Autonomous vehicle perception**: Detecting sudden pedestrian movements or obstacles requires high-temporal-resolution video understanding under real-time constraints.
2. **Sports analytics**: Tracking fast ball movements, player interactions, and split-second plays for real-time commentary or coaching feedback.
3. **Industrial safety monitoring**: Detecting rapid accidents, spills, or machinery failures in factory video streams.
4. **Surveillance and security**: Identifying threats or unusual behaviors in live camera feeds while managing bandwidth and latency.
5. **Robotics**: Enabling robots to react to fast-moving objects or dynamic obstacles in real-world environments.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **Adaptive streaming video API**: A service that learns optimal frame-sampling strategies per use case (sports, traffic, industrial), using FastBench-style evaluation to reduce bandwidth while maintaining perception quality.
2. **Real-time sports analytics platform**: Leverage ProactiveFrame-like methods to generate instant instant highlights, plays, and insights from live multi-camera feeds with minimal latency.
3. **Autonomous vehicle safety testing suite**: Benchmark and certify VLM-based perception systems on high-dynamic driving scenarios (pedestrian jumps, sudden braking); sell certification to OEMs.
4. **Smart surveillance system optimizer**: Automatically tune frame rates and model selection per camera location and threat model, reducing computational cost while meeting safety SLAs.
5. **Video compression + understanding middleware**: A wrapper that compresses video streams intelligently (denser keyframes where events happen fast) and pairs it with a lightweight VLM for edge deployment on cameras.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Context budget realism**: The paper assumes a fixed context window, but doesn't explore how models could request variable-length buffers or dynamically re-prioritize memory allocation—core challenges in production systems.
2. **Ground-truth dependencies**: Answer verification relies on trajectory tools (SAM3, CoTracker3) that may themselves fail on occlusions, fast motion, or ambiguous objects, introducing verification bias.
3. **Domain and dataset size**: 306 QA pairs across eight domains is relatively small; generalization to unseen domains or rare high-dynamic events is unclear.
4. **Training vs. inference trade-offs**: ProactiveFrame is training-free, which is convenient but may leave significant performance on the table compared to a model *trained* to anticipate when high-temporal resolution is needed.
5. **Temporal scopes**: The benchmark doesn't capture scenarios where *future* prediction (predicting where a ball will go) requires understanding of current high-FPS motion—only retrospective QA.

## Open Questions
1. **Can models learn to request high-FPS sampling?** Train a VLM end-to-end to emit control signals (e.g., "increase frame rate now") and see if it learns adaptive perception.
2. **How does context-length scaling affect high-dynamic understanding?** Test models with vastly larger context windows to isolate whether history compression is the bottleneck.
3. **What if we augment inputs with motion signals?** Provide optical flow or frame differences as explicit channels; does that reduce need for high-FPS raw frames?
4. **Cross-domain generalization:** Evaluate models trained on FastBench on held-out high-dynamic datasets (e.g., robotics, autonomous driving) to measure transfer.
5. **Multimodal fusion:** Can audio (e.g., a ball's impact sound) or inertial sensors complement sparse video to recover high-dynamic understanding?

## Key Takeaway
Streaming video models miss fast events due to memory constraints; even top models achieve only 50% accuracy on high-dynamic scenes, indicating a fundamental architectural challenge unsolved by current approaches.
