# Distilling Graph Geometry: Knowledge Gap from GNNs to MLPs

**Authors:** Zhewei Chen, Hao Zhu, Jiaojiao Jiang, Ahad N. Zehmakan
**Published:** 2026-10-07 | **arXiv:** [2610.10520v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10520v1)

## Summary
This paper addresses how to distill (compress) graph neural networks — models that learn from structured relational data — into simpler MLPs (multi-layer perceptrons) that don't need the graph structure at inference time. The key insight is that existing distillation methods fail because they don't explicitly preserve the graph's geometric structure; the authors identify two failure modes (underfitting on sparse graphs, overfitting on dense graphs) and propose G²MLP, which uses Ricci curvature to guide where the student should align with the teacher, consistently improving accuracy while keeping the deployed model lightweight.

## Theoretical Background
Graph neural networks (GNNs) process relational data by iteratively aggregating information from neighboring nodes through "message-passing" — a mechanism inspired by how information flows through networks. Knowledge distillation is a technique where a smaller, simpler "student" model learns to mimic a larger, more accurate "teacher" model, usually to enable efficient deployment. The paper builds on spectral analysis of neural network representations — studying the eigenvalue structure of learned feature matrices — and introduces Ollivier-Ricci curvature, a geometric measure from differential geometry that quantifies how curved a space is locally. The core problem: when you distill a GNN to an MLP, you lose the graph signal but existing methods don't explicitly specify what geometric properties the student should preserve from the teacher.

## Core Contributions
1. **Identification of two spectral failure modes**: On sparse graphs, students suffer from spectral underfit (missing important high-energy directions in the teacher's representation space, especially near graph boundaries); on dense graphs, they suffer from spectral overfit (keeping spurious directions the teacher has actually collapsed through aggregation).

2. **Energy-weighted alignment objective**: Frames GNN-to-MLP distillation as an explicit teacher-student alignment problem in spectral space, weighted by the "energy" (significance) of different directions.

3. **Curvature-guided supervision allocation**: Uses Ollivier-Ricci curvature to identify *where* spectral errors concentrate and automatically balances prediction-level loss against representation-level alignment loss — a principled way to say "preserve geometry here, not there."

4. **Empirical validation across settings**: Demonstrates consistent improvements over baselines on node-classification tasks, with transferability to Graph Transformers and link prediction without architectural changes.

## Connections to Other Work
This work sits at the intersection of [[Knowledge Distillation]] in deep learning and [[Graph Neural Networks]]. It relates to broader efforts in [[Model Compression]] and efficient inference. The use of geometric measures (Ricci curvature) echoes recent work applying differential geometry to understand neural network loss landscapes and generalization. It differs from prior GNN distillation by explicitly modeling geometry rather than relying purely on prediction matching or confidence reweighting.

## Practical Applications
1. **Mobile/edge deployment of graph models**: Deploy social network or recommendation models on-device by distilling a GNN teacher into a lightweight MLP student that doesn't need graph access.
2. **Real-time inference for recommendation systems**: Reduce latency in production recommender systems (e.g., Spotify, Netflix) by avoiding graph traversal at inference.
3. **Privacy-preserving graph learning**: Share a distilled MLP student without revealing the underlying graph structure, useful in federated or sensitive applications.
4. **Faster inference in knowledge graphs**: Distill GNN-based link predictors or entity classifiers (e.g., for biomedical or academic knowledge graphs) into MLPs for rapid retrieval.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **Graph Inference as a Service (GIaaS)**: A platform that automatically distills customer GNN models into lightweight MLPs optimized for their graph structure (sparse/dense), monetizing via API calls or per-model-optimization fees.
2. **Edge ML Compiler**: A compiler that takes trained GNN models and automatically generates optimized MLP equivalents for embedded systems, IoT, or mobile — targeting robotics, autonomous vehicles, or smart city applications.
3. **Geometric Model Optimization SaaS**: Offer distillation-as-a-service specifically for graph data (social networks, recommendation graphs, knowledge graphs) with curvature-aware tuning; target mid-market SaaS companies.
4. **Hyperlocal Recommendation Engine**: Deploy distilled graph models at the edge in retail or local services to provide fast, graph-aware personalization without cloud latency.
5. **Privacy-First Analytics**: Provide tools for organizations to share graph insights (via distilled MLPs) without exposing raw graph topology, useful for healthcare networks, supply chains, or consortiums.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Curvature computation overhead**: Computing Ollivier-Ricci curvature is expensive (computationally demanding); the paper doesn't fully address how this scales to very large graphs or how to approximate it.
2. **Teacher quality assumption**: Distillation quality depends on the teacher GNN's accuracy — garbage teacher, garbage student. No analysis of robustness to weak or poorly-trained teachers.
3. **Graph structure assumption at training time**: The method still requires graph access during training; only the deployed MLP is graph-free. This doesn't help if the graph changes over time.
4. **Limited theoretical guarantees**: While motivated by spectral analysis, no formal bounds on student-teacher alignment or worst-case performance loss.
5. **Sensitivity to hyperparameter allocation**: The balance between prediction and representation loss (guided by curvature) introduces another tuning knob; unclear how sensitive results are to this choice across domains.
6. **Sparse graph focus**: Method is validated mainly on standard benchmarks; unclear performance on very sparse, power-law, or highly dynamic graphs.

## Open Questions
1. **Scalability of curvature**: Can Ollivier-Ricci curvature be approximated or estimated efficiently for billion-node graphs? What is the computational complexity curve?
2. **Temporal distillation**: How should the method adapt when the underlying graph changes over time (e.g., social networks, biological networks)? Can you continuously retrain the MLP student?
3. **Domain transfer**: Does curvature-guided distillation transfer across different types of graphs (e.g., from social graphs to citation graphs)? Can you pre-train a distillation strategy?
4. **Theoretical characterization**: Can you formally bound the performance gap between teacher GNN and distilled MLP as a function of graph properties (sparsity, diameter, curvature distribution)?
5. **Heterogeneous graphs**: The paper focuses on homogeneous graphs; how do you handle graphs with multiple node and edge types? Does curvature guidance still work?
6. **Link to architecture**: Does the choice of MLP architecture (depth, width) interact with curvature-guided alignment? Should architecture also be informed by graph geometry?

## Key Takeaway
Distilling GNNs to MLPs fails because it ignores graph geometry; using curvature to guide where to preserve structure fixes spectral errors and improves accuracy.
