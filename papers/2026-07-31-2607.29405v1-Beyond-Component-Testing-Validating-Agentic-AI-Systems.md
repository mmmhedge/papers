# Beyond Component Testing: Validating Agentic AI Systems

**Authors:** Fabio Orazio Mirto, Luca D'Agati, Giuseppe Tricomi, Stefano Silvestri, Francesco Longo et al.
**Published:** 2026-07-31 | **arXiv:** [2607.29405v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29405v1)

## Summary
This survey examines how to validate agentic AI systems—software that operates autonomously over multiple steps, planning actions, using tools, and adapting to changing conditions. Existing validation methods focus on isolated components and single input-output pairs, but autonomous agents fail in ways that unfold over time and depend on context; the paper synthesizes 257 papers to build a taxonomy of validation challenges and maps where current approaches fall short, with a focus on safety-critical domains like healthcare and autonomous vehicles.

## Theoretical Background
Agent validation traditionally inherited methods from software testing and machine learning evaluation, both of which assume bounded, deterministic, or stateless behavior. However, agentic systems combine planning algorithms (drawn from classical AI), reinforcement learning (learning from reward signals), tool integration (grounding agents in real systems), and memory mechanisms—creating systems whose correctness depends not just on individual decisions but on coherent trajectories over time. Prior work on [[RLHF]] and agent alignment has shown that optimizing for narrow metrics often fails to capture emergent behaviors in long-horizon tasks. Runtime monitoring and cyber-physical systems assurance offer templates for continuous validation, but have not been systematically adapted to LLM-based agents. The paper builds on distributed systems assurance, formal verification, and safety engineering disciplines.

## Core Contributions
- **Five-dimension taxonomy** for agent validation: behavioral (does the agent do what it should?), safety (does it avoid harmful outcomes?), temporal (are constraints maintained over time?), regulatory (is system behavior auditable and explainable?), and multi-agent (how do multiple agents interact safely?).
- **Systematic coverage gap analysis** showing behavioral evaluation is mature while temporal validity, runtime evidence preservation, regulatory legibility, and multi-agent assurance are severely under-developed.
- **Three operational case studies** (medical triage, manufacturing, autonomous mobility) grounding the taxonomy in concrete failure modes and regulatory requirements.
- **Lifecycle research agenda** proposing bounded-autonomy specifications, adversarial trajectory generation, continuous runtime monitoring, and audit-ready evidence structures as priorities.

## Connections to Other Work
This work sits at the intersection of [[alignment]] (ensuring agents behave as intended), multi-agent coordination problems (handling interactions between autonomous systems), and classical software assurance. It extends critiques of component-level evaluation seen in mechanistic interpretability and [[RLHF]] literature—the insight that local correctness doesn't guarantee global safety. The taxonomy echoes formal methods for cyber-physical systems and extends runtime monitoring practices from distributed systems. Relatedly, work on adversarial robustness and out-of-distribution detection becomes critical when agents operate in open environments. The regulatory dimension connects to emerging AI governance frameworks (EU AI Act, algorithmic accountability standards).

## Practical Applications
- **Healthcare operations**: Validating clinical decision-support agents that coordinate diagnostic tools, medication selection, and patient monitoring over patient episodes rather than single queries.
- **Manufacturing and maintenance**: Ensuring industrial robots and process-control agents safely handle equipment failures, worker interactions, and dynamic scheduling without catastrophic failures.
- **Autonomous vehicle fleets**: Testing multi-agent traffic coordination, fallback behaviors during sensor failure, and interaction safety with human drivers and infrastructure.
- **Financial trading agents**: Validating that autonomous traders maintain risk constraints, don't trigger flash crashes through coordinated actions, and preserve regulatory compliance over trading sessions.
- **Supply chain optimization**: Testing agents that dynamically replan logistics and procurement while respecting inventory constraints and contract obligations across time.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **Agent Auditor SaaS** – Continuous runtime monitoring and evidence logging for deployed agentic systems, generating audit trails and regulatory reports (compliance-as-a-service for AI operations).
2. **Trajectory Fuzzing Engine** – Automated adversarial trajectory generation to find failure modes in multi-step agent behaviors before deployment; stress-tests agents against edge cases and multi-agent collisions.
3. **Agent Specification IDE** – Formal specification environment for bounded-autonomy constraints (hard rules, safety zones, resource limits) with automated validation and compliance checking.
4. **Temporal Safety Analyzer** – Real-time dashboard and anomaly detection for long-horizon agent behaviors, surfacing when temporal constraints or accumulated side effects violate safety bounds.
5. **Multi-Agent Simulation Testbed** – Industry-specific simulation environments (healthcare, manufacturing, transport) pre-wired with common agent patterns, regulatory datasets, and failure catalogs for rapid validation.

## Technical Limitations
- **No ground truth for trajectory safety**: Unlike component testing, validating a trajectory as "correct" requires agreement on what long-term outcomes matter—often domain-dependent and contested (e.g., fairness in healthcare).
- **Combinatorial explosion**: Testing multi-agent interactions grows exponentially; cannot exhaustively validate all interaction patterns.
- **Non-determinism and stochasticity**: Agents using language models or randomized algorithms make validation non-repeatable; statistical validation requires many trajectories and long runtimes.
- **Delayed feedback**: Failures in agentic systems often appear far downstream (e.g., resource depletion after 100 decisions); attribution and root cause are hard.
- **Regulatory mismatch**: Current AI regulations (GDPR, EU AI Act) were written for supervised systems; applying them to autonomous, adaptive agents with emergent behavior remains unclear.
- **Environment coupling**: Agent behavior depends on real-world environment dynamics that are difficult to model; simulation may not capture critical failure modes.

## Open Questions
- How should we formally specify "bounded autonomy" for agents in practice? What language or logic captures both safety constraints and flexibility?
- Can we design adversarial trajectory generation methods that scale to realistic, high-dimensional action spaces?
- How should runtime evidence be structured so it is both useful for safety monitoring and auditable for regulators?
- What are the minimal temporal properties (e.g., invariants, stability bounds) that define safety for long-horizon agent behavior?
- How do we validate multi-agent systems where collective behavior emerges from local interactions and is difficult to predict?
- Can we adapt formal verification techniques (model checking, theorem proving) to LLM-based agents with learned policies?

## Key Takeaway
Autonomous AI systems need validation across time and context—not just testing isolated components—requiring new monitoring, specification, and audit methods.
