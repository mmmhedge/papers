# Benchmarking the Benchmarks: Evaluating Benchmarks for Conversational Agents

**Authors:** Noam Koren, Roy Bar-Haim, Abigail Goldsteen
**Published:** 2026-08-06 | **arXiv:** [2608.06329v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06329v1)

## Summary
Task-oriented conversational agents are evaluated using curated or automatically generated benchmarks, yet benchmark quality is rarely assessed. Poor benchmarks may contain inconsistent tasks, simplistic scenarios, or limited policy coverage, leading to unreliable evaluations. We introduce a reference-free framework that uses LLM judges to assess benchmark consistency, complexity, and policy coverage, while providing actionable diagnostics of weaknesses.

## Theoretical Background
AI agents combine a language model with tools, memory, and an execution loop. Their quality depends not only on individual answers but on decisions made across a complete trajectory, which makes evaluation and debugging harder than ordinary text generation.

The paper is positioned against prior approaches described in its abstract and asks whether a more targeted representation, evaluation method, or optimization procedure can improve the relevant trade-off.

## Core Contributions
1. We introduce a reference-free framework that uses LLM judges to assess benchmark consistency, complexity, and policy coverage, while providing actionable diagnostics of weaknesses.
2. Across domains and judge models, the proposed metrics consistently distinguish between benchmark quality levels.
3. We further demonstrate the framework's applicability to manually curated benchmarks.

## Technical Limitations
This note is based on the title, metadata, and abstract rather than a full-paper audit. The abstract cannot establish implementation completeness, statistical robustness, or whether all baselines were tuned comparably. Reported gains may depend on the selected datasets, models, task definitions, and compute environment. Deployment would require independent validation on out-of-distribution inputs, measurement of total runtime and cost, and analysis of failure cases. Any high-stakes use would also need human oversight and domain-specific governance.
