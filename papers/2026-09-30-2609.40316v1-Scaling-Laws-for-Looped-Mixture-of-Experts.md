# Scaling Laws for Looped Mixture of Experts

**Authors:** Yanbei Chen, Anirudh Goyal, Raghuraman Krishnamoorthi
**Published:** 2026-09-30 | **arXiv:** [2609.40316v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40316v1)

## Summary
This paper derives the first unified scaling laws that jointly model recurrence (looped inference) and sparsity (Mixture-of-Experts) in transformer models, alongside model size and data. The work shows that recurrence and sparsity are complementary efficiency axes — combining them yields ~2–3× parameter efficiency gains — and provides a principled framework for designing efficient models under compute budgets.

## Theoretical Background
Scaling laws predict how model loss improves with increases in parameters, data, and compute — they are empirical power laws fitted from training runs and guide resource allocation in large model development. Looped transformers (also called recurrent transformers) reuse the same parameters across multiple inference steps, increasing computational depth without growing parameters. Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) selectively activates sparse expert networks, maintaining model capacity while holding constant the compute per forward pass. Prior work modeled these mechanisms separately; this paper introduces a sparsity-conditional recurrence mapping that characterizes how the effective parameters gained from looping increase when sparsity is applied.

## Core Contributions
1. **Loop Scaling Laws**: A novel empirical scaling law that jointly captures recurrence, sparsity, model size, and data — the first to do so.
2. **Sparsity-Conditional Recurrence Mapping**: A bounded function showing how MoE sparsity amplifies the parameter-efficiency gains from looping.
3. **Unified Framework**: Demonstrates that standard dense and MoE-only scaling laws are special cases of the looped laws.
4. **Trillion-Token Validation**: Empirical evidence that looped MoE gains hold at scale; a looped MoE with 1 billion parameters can match a 2 billion parameter non-looped MoE on reasoning tasks.
5. **Design Guidance**: Practical recipes for allocating recurrence depth and sparse expert scaling under fixed compute and memory constraints.

## Connections to Other Work
This work extends the classical [[Chinchilla scaling laws]] and recent MoE scaling research by introducing recurrence as a third efficiency axis. It complements [[sparse transformers]] and [[conditional computation]] literature by showing recurrence and sparsity interact synergistically. The sparsity-conditional mapping relates to gating mechanisms in MoE systems and to the broader optimization problem of allocating compute across model width, depth, and recurrence.

## Practical Applications
- **Inference-constrained scenarios**: Edge devices and mobile applications where parameter count is limited but latency tolerance allows multiple recurrent steps.
- **Long-context reasoning**: Multi-hop reasoning tasks where looped depth provides computational benefit; shown empirically on reasoning benchmarks.
- **Cost-optimized training**: Organizations with fixed training budgets can use the scaling laws to decide whether to invest in larger models, more data, or recurrent looping.
- **Test-time scaling**: Using recurrence at inference to adaptively increase compute without retraining, enabling dynamic quality-cost tradeoffs.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **Efficient LLM inference engine**: A specialized serving framework that deploys looped MoE models using the scaling laws to auto-tune recurrence depth based on latency and accuracy targets.
2. **Model sizing consultant tool**: SaaS that takes a compute budget (FLOP/memory constraints) and outputs optimal model architecture parameters (width, depth, loop count, expert count).
3. **On-device reasoning assistant**: Mobile/edge app powered by small looped MoE models that can solve multi-step reasoning tasks by trading off latency for reasoning depth.
4. **Research benchmark suite**: Publish a standardized benchmark for looped MoE architectures, enabling reproducible scaling law research and attracting academic/industry collaborators.
5. **Adaptive inference middleware**: Middleware that monitors latency at inference time and dynamically adjusts recurrence loops to meet QoS targets without model retraining.

## Technical Limitations
- **Empirical fitting**: Scaling laws are fit to specific architectures and training regimes; generalization to novel MoE designs, attention patterns, or training procedures is unclear.
- **Reasoning-specific gains**: ~2× efficiency gains are demonstrated mainly on reasoning benchmarks; gains on other modalities (language modeling, generation) are less thoroughly characterized.
- **Recurrence overhead**: The paper does not deeply analyze wall-clock latency or memory footprint during looped inference — only parameter efficiency. Actual throughput may be limited by sequential computation.
- **Architectural coupling**: The interaction between recurrence depth, MoE gating complexity, and attention mechanisms is not fully decomposed; results may be sensitive to specific architectural choices.
- **Trillion-token scale**: Although validated at 1T tokens, the laws may not hold beyond this regime or under different training curricula.

## Open Questions
1. How do the scaling laws extend to multi-head or hierarchical MoE (e.g., expert-of-experts)?
2. Do the gains generalize to vision transformers and multimodal models, or are they primarily a language-modeling phenomenon?
3. What is the optimal schedule for increasing recurrence depth during training, and how does it interact with curriculum learning?
4. Can looped MoE reduce activation memory during training (not just inference), making it viable for training very large models?
5. How do the scaling laws change under different sparsity patterns (e.g., learned gating vs. top-k selection)?

## Key Takeaway
Combining recurrence and sparsity in transformers yields 2–3× parameter efficiency; scaled laws now guide principled design under compute constraints.
