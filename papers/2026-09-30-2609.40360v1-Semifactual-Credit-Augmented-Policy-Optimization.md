# Semifactual Credit-Augmented Policy Optimization

**Authors:** Junshu Pan, Zhizhang Fu, Shulin Huang, Yiran Ding, Zifan Cheng et al.
**Published:** 2026-09-30 | **arXiv:** [2609.40360v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40360v1)

## Summary
This paper addresses a fundamental problem in reinforcement learning for language models: when models learn to solve reasoning tasks, they often become overly sensitive to irrelevant prompt features (like wording changes that don't affect the problem). The authors propose SCAPO, a new training method that assigns credit to individual tokens based on their stability under prompt variations, improving mathematical reasoning accuracy by 4–6 percentage points on competitive benchmarks.

## Theoretical Background
The work builds on two key ideas. First, **reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR)** — a recent approach that trains language models by giving them explicit feedback when their reasoning is correct. Second, **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)** — a policy gradient method that scores each generated response and distributes the same advantage signal to all tokens equally. The paper also draws on causal inference: **semifactual interventions** are minimal prompt rewrites that change surface features (like synonym swaps) while preserving the underlying math problem and answer. This differs from counterfactual reasoning (imagining wholly different scenarios) and helps isolate which tokens genuinely matter for solving the task.

## Core Contributions
1. **Diagnosis via semifactual analysis**: The authors systematically measure how sensitive token probabilities are to task-irrelevant prompt variations. They show substantial variation — some tokens drift significantly while others remain stable.

2. **Token-level stability scoring**: Unlike GRPO (which treats all tokens equally), SCAPO computes a normalized "stability score" for each token based on how its probability changes under semifactual interventions.

3. **Refined credit assignment**: During training, tokens with low stability receive reduced advantages, discouraging the model from relying on spurious correlations. Notably, stability doesn't grant *extra* credit — it only downweights unstable signals.

4. **Empirical gains**: On AIME 2024–2026 (competitive math contest problems), SCAPO outperforms GRPO by 5.63 pp on Qwen3-4B and 4.17 pp on Qwen3-1.7B. Improvements hold across math benchmarks and out-of-distribution tasks.

## Connections to Other Work
This paper sits at the intersection of three areas:
- **[[Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards]]** (the base framework for reward-signaled LLM training)
- **[[Group Relative Policy Optimization]]** (the baseline method being refined)
- **Causal inference and robustness** (using semifactual perturbations to isolate true causal contributors, akin to work on adversarial robustness and spurious correlation removal)

It also relates to broader efforts in [[mechanistic-interpretability]] and alignment: understanding which internal model features drive predictions and reducing dependence on task-irrelevant ones. The focus on token-level credit assignment echoes actor-critic methods in classical RL.

## Practical Applications
1. **Mathematics tutoring and homework checking**: Deploy models that reliably solve math problems regardless of how questions are phrased (synonyms, style variation, font changes).
2. **Exam-like AI assistants**: Tools that must generalize across diverse prompt styles on competitive exams or standardized tests (SAT, GRE, AIME).
3. **Robust code generation**: Train models to generate code reliably by discouraging dependence on superficial formatting or naming conventions.
4. **Multilingual reasoning**: Reduce sensitivity to translation artifacts when solving problems originally posed in other languages.
5. **Enterprise document processing**: Finance/legal AI that must reason consistently across different document formats and writing styles.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Semifactual intervention design**: The method requires human or automatic paraphrasing to generate valid semifactual prompts. If paraphrases accidentally change the problem or are poor quality, stability scores become misleading.

2. **Computational cost**: Computing token stability requires forward passes under multiple prompt variants for each training example. This increases training time significantly compared to vanilla GRPO; the paper does not detail wall-clock overhead.

3. **Reward model assumption**: SCAPO assumes a verifiable reward signal (correct/incorrect answer). It cannot be applied to open-ended generation (creative writing, open-form summarization) unless a proxy reward exists.

4. **Limited scope tested**: Evaluation focuses on mathematical reasoning on specific benchmarks. Generalization to other reasoning domains (physics, logic, language understanding) remains unclear.

5. **Small model focus**: Results shown only on models up to 4B parameters. Scaling behavior on larger models (10B+, 100B+) is unknown.

6. **Causal claim validity**: The paper assumes semifactual perturbations correctly isolate causal tokens. If multiple tokens interact non-linearly, or if paraphrasing inadvertently removes implicit context, the stability measure may be noisy.

## Open Questions
1. How does computational cost scale with the number of semifactual variants, and what is the optimal number per example?
2. Can SCAPO be combined with other alignment techniques (RLHF, constitutional AI) or is it specific to RLVR?
3. Does token-level stability transfer across models of different sizes or architectures?
4. How sensitive are results to the choice of semifactual intervention method? (e.g., rule-based vs. learned paraphrasing)
5. Can semifactual stability be estimated *without* extra forward passes (e.g., via attention patterns or gradients)?
6. Do the gains persist when training data or prompts are naturally diverse (reducing the problem of spurious dependence)?
7. Can SCAPO improve other RL objectives beyond GRPO, such as DPO or IPO variants?

## Key Takeaway
Assigning credit to individual reasoning tokens based on stability under prompt rewrites improves LLM math accuracy and generalization better than treating all tokens equally.
