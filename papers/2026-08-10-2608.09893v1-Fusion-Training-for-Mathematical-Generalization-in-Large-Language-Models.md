# Fusion Training for Mathematical Generalization in Large Language Models

**Authors:** Congfeng Cao, Pengyu Zhang, Jelke Bloem
**Published:** 2026-08-10 | **arXiv:** [2608.09893v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09893v1)

## Summary
This paper systematically studies how to train large language models that can switch between two modes: a fast "non-thinking" mode for simple responses and a slow "thinking" mode for complex reasoning, especially in mathematics. The key finding is that these two modes are in tension—training the model to be good at quick answers actually makes it worse at reasoning—and the best training approach depends on balancing data and schedule choices.

## Theoretical Background
Large language models (neural networks trained to predict sequences of text) increasingly face a dual demand: users want both fast responses for straightforward questions and the ability to work through difficult problems step-by-step. Prior work explored "chain-of-thought" reasoning (having models write out reasoning steps before answering) and model scaling, but less attention was paid to *jointly optimizing* two distinct behavioral modes within a single model. This paper builds on the concept of Thinking Mode Fusion (TMF)—a training framework that explicitly partitions model objectives—and extends it by studying the under-explored training dynamics: what ratio of data should each mode receive, and in what order should they be trained?

## Core Contributions
1. **Systematic empirical study**: Quantifies the trade-off between non-thinking and thinking modes across multiple data ratios and three training schedules (e.g., alternating, sequential, interleaved), going beyond prior anecdotal observations.
2. **Asymmetric interaction finding**: Demonstrates that increasing non-thinking supervision *reduces* thinking-mode accuracy—a directional asymmetry that suggests the two modes compete for model capacity or attention.
3. **Schedule-dependent optimization**: Shows that no single training schedule is universally optimal; the best approach depends on the data ratio, providing a framework for practitioners to tune training.
4. **Benchmark and reproducibility**: Releases Fusion Bench, a benchmark with controlled data ratios and schedules, enabling reproducible research on mode fusion.

## Connections to Other Work
This work relates to [[RLHF]] (reinforcement learning from human feedback) and [[chain-of-thought prompting]], which also aim to improve reasoning without scaling model size. It connects to multi-task learning literature, which has long studied interference between objectives. The tension between modes echoes the broader alignment challenge: balancing multiple desiderata (speed vs. accuracy, brevity vs. correctness) within a single model. It also touches on mechanistic interpretability questions about whether thinking and non-thinking modes rely on separate or shared model components.

## Practical Applications
- **Educational software**: Math tutoring apps that give quick hints but can also show full step-by-step solutions when requested.
- **Coding assistants**: Providing fast autocomplete suggestions while supporting a "debug mode" that reasons through complex algorithmic problems.
- **Medical diagnosis systems**: Quick screening decisions for routine cases, with capability to produce detailed reasoning for edge cases requiring review.
- **Enterprise search**: Fast snippet answers for common queries, with long-form synthesis for complex research tasks.
- **Conversational AI**: Customer service that responds instantly to FAQs but can shift to deliberative reasoning for novel issues.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **MathTutor Pro**: A freemium app leveraging fusion training to provide instant answers for homework plus detailed reasoning, with analytics on which mode students use most.
2. **CodeReasoner IDE**: An IDE plugin that offers fast code completion by default but switches to detailed step-by-step problem-solving when flagged by the user or on complex functions.
3. **Clinical Decision Support SaaS**: A medical platform that flags diagnoses quickly in high-volume settings but generates explainable reasoning reports for liability and audit trails.
4. **Research Intelligence Agent**: A B2B tool for law firms / consulting that rapidly extracts relevant precedents but can produce full legal or strategic reasoning on demand, with different modes priced differently.
5. **Adaptive Tutoring Platform**: Dynamically selects between fast feedback and slow reasoning based on student performance, using A/B testing to optimize data ratios per student cohort.

## Technical Limitations
- **Mathematical focus**: Results are primarily from math benchmarks; generalization to other domains (code, creative writing, science) is unclear.
- **Single-model bottleneck**: The paper assumes a single model must serve both modes; separate specialized models might avoid the trade-off entirely.
- **Data ratio search**: The paper explores *discrete* ratios and schedules, not continuous optimization or learning-rate scheduling per mode.
- **Scalability unknown**: Unclear how findings scale to very large models (1T+ parameters) or very long reasoning chains; the trade-off might shift.
- **Mode detection**: In practice, systems need to *decide* when to invoke thinking mode; the paper does not address automated mode selection.
- **No interpretability**: The paper quantifies the tension but does not explain *why* non-thinking supervision hurts thinking mode—whether it's capacity competition, gradient interference, or something else.

## Open Questions
1. Is the non-thinking–thinking tension *fundamental* (rooted in model capacity) or *trainable away* (via better optimization)?
2. Do thinking and non-thinking modes rely on overlapping or separable model components (e.g., different layers or attention heads)? Can mechanistic interpretability pinpoint the bottleneck?
3. How do these findings scale to longer reasoning chains, larger models, and multimodal tasks?
4. Can meta-learning or adaptive training schedules automatically optimize the ratio and schedule for a given task distribution?
5. How does this interact with other training paradigms like [[RLHF]] or curriculum learning?

## Key Takeaway
Training LLMs with both fast and reasoning modes requires balancing competing demands; the best trade-off depends on data ratio and training order.
