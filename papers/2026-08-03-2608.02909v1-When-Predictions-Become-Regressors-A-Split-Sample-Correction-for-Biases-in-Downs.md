# When Predictions Become Regressors: A Split-Sample Correction for Biases in Downstream Inference

**Authors:** Nathan Canen, Ted Enamorado
**Published:** 2026-08-03 | **arXiv:** [2608.02909v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02909v1)

## Summary
Prediction-based methods, including Large Language Models (LLMs) and other machine learning techniques, are often used to construct measures of political phenomena that are difficult to quantify directly, such as policy positions in manifestos or emotions expressed on social media. In many applications, these prediction-generated measures are used as explanatory variables in regression models, even though they are measured with error. This leads to biased estimates.

## Theoretical Background
High-stakes predictive systems require traceable inputs, calibrated uncertainty, and evidence that results generalize beyond development data.

The paper addresses the trade-off identified in its abstract through a targeted method, representation, or evaluation framework.

## Core Contributions
1. In this paper, we propose a simple solution to these biases: instrumental variables constructed from multiple measures created on independent splits of the original data.
2. Through simulations, we show that this approach recovers estimates close to the true values, even in relatively small samples, while the standard approach can produce substantial bias in practice.
