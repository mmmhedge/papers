# Improving Diversity in LLM Short Story Generation

**Authors:** Zahra Solati Dehkordi, Vasileios Lampos
**Published:** 2026-10-05 | **arXiv:** [2610.06729v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06729v1)

## Summary
This paper addresses a key weakness of large language models: they tend to generate similar, repetitive outputs even when asked creative questions. The authors propose DivLM, a training framework that makes LLMs produce more varied short stories across genre, tone, style, and character names, while keeping the stories well-written and following instructions. This matters because diversity is essential for creative applications—users want different stories, not variations of the same narrative.

## Theoretical Background
Large language models (LLMs) — neural networks trained on massive text corpora to predict the next word — are known to suffer from "mode collapse," where they converge to high-probability outputs at the expense of variation. Prior work has shown that standard instruction-tuning (teaching models to follow user requests) can actually reduce diversity by narrowing the distribution of generated text. The paper builds on two established ideas: (1) continued pre-training on domain-specific corpora (in this case, creative writing) to shift model behavior toward a target distribution, and (2) reinforcement learning (RL) with custom reward functions to optimize for multiple objectives simultaneously — here, balancing diversity gains against maintaining quality and instruction-following.

## Core Contributions
DivLM introduces a two-phase post-training framework:
1. **Continued pre-training with weight residuals**: The authors fine-tune an LLM on creative writing data while using weight residuals (storing changes separately) to preserve instruction-following ability learned during initial training.
2. **Composite RL reward function**: They design a multi-dimensional reward that jointly scores diversity across genre, tone, style, and named entities alongside quality metrics, then optimize this composite objective using RL.
3. **Empirical validation**: Experiments on two LLM families show >9% average improvement in diversity metrics without degrading quality or human-story similarity.

The novelty lies in the *joint optimization* — explicitly targeting multiple diversity dimensions simultaneously rather than pursuing single-objective improvements.

## Connections to Other Work
This work sits at the intersection of [[Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF)]] and [[creative text generation]]. It extends standard instruction-tuning (seen in models like GPT-3.5, ChatGPT) by using RL to optimize for attributes beyond accuracy. The weight residuals technique is related to parameter-efficient fine-tuning approaches like LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation), which preserve base model knowledge during adaptation. The composite reward design echoes multi-objective optimization in RL, where agents must balance competing goals—a pattern also explored in [[alignment]] research where models must satisfy multiple human values simultaneously.

## Practical Applications
- **Interactive storytelling platforms**: Games or narrative apps where players want different story outcomes based on identical prompts, rather than seeing the same plot repeatedly.
- **Content generation for marketing**: Agencies can generate multiple ad copy variants or product descriptions with genuine variation, reducing content staleness in feed-based systems.
- **Educational writing tools**: Systems that help students learn by generating diverse example essays, explanations, or narrative responses to creative prompts.
- **Entertainment (streaming/publishing)**: Automated short-story generation for serialized content, where variety keeps audiences engaged across multiple episodes.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **"StorySpin"** — A creative writing SaaS that generates 5–10 completely different short stories from a single prompt; writers cherry-pick or remix them. Target: indie authors, content mills, educational publishers.
2. **Diversity-first ad copy generator** — B2B tool for e-commerce that auto-generates 20+ distinct product descriptions and ad variants from a single product listing, optimized for A/B testing and platform-specific tone (TikTok vs. LinkedIn).
3. **AI writing coach for screenwriters** — Desktop app that suggests alternate scene descriptions, dialogue, and plot branches while preserving story logic; writers explore variations without losing coherence.
4. **"Verse"** — Prompt-to-stories API for game studios; generates diverse NPC dialogue, side quests, and world-lore snippets on demand with controlled tone and theme variety.
5. **Educational narrative generator** — Textbook publisher tool that auto-creates diverse practice stories for language learning, history, or ethics courses; same scenario told in multiple genres/styles for deeper learning.

## Technical Limitations
- **Reward function design**: The composite reward relies on hand-crafted metrics for diversity (genre, tone, style, entities). These may not capture all dimensions readers care about, and weighting between them is somewhat arbitrary.
- **Scalability to longer narratives**: Experiments focus on short stories; it's unclear if the method scales to novel-length generation, where maintaining coherence across 50k+ tokens while increasing diversity becomes harder.
- **Generalization beyond creative writing**: The framework is validated only on short story generation; it's unknown whether the same approach works for non-narrative domains (e.g., technical writing, dialogue agents).
- **Computational cost**: RL fine-tuning is expensive; the paper doesn't detail wall-clock training time or compare to simpler baselines (e.g., temperature/sampling adjustments, decoding-time diversity tricks).
- **Instruction-following trade-off**: While the authors claim instruction-following is "preserved," there may still be subtle cases where enforcing diversity overrides user-specified constraints.

## Open Questions
- How does diversity scale with story length? Do the improvements hold for 5,000+ token stories, or does coherence degrade?
- Can you decompose the reward function to identify which diversity dimension (genre vs. tone vs. entities) is easiest to optimize? Which matters most to human readers?
- How sensitive is the method to reward function weights? A small ablation study across weight combinations would clarify robustness.
- Does this approach transfer to other creative domains—poetry, dialogue, world-building—or is it specific to narrative structure?
- What happens when diversity goals conflict with factual accuracy or safety constraints? Can the framework handle hard constraints (e.g., "never generate violent content") while optimizing soft diversity objectives?

## Key Takeaway
Two-phase training with RL makes language models tell more varied stories without losing quality.
