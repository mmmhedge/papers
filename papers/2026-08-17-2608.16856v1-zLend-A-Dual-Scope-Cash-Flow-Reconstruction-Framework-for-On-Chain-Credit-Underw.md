# zLend: A Dual-Scope Cash-Flow Reconstruction Framework for On-Chain Credit Underwriting

**Authors:** Girish G N, Ashutosh Sahoo, Akshay SP, Gurukiran S, Dhanashekar Kandaswamy
**Published:** 2026-08-17 | **arXiv:** [2608.16856v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16856v1)

## Summary
zLend is a deployed system that evaluates creditworthiness for decentralized lending by reconstructing a borrower's cash-flow history directly from public blockchain transactions, without traditional credit bureaus or income verification. The key innovation is analyzing two separate views of a wallet—its total token holdings and its liquid stablecoin reserves—to catch borrowers who appear wealthy but lack actual spending power, then deriving repayment-capacity signals (liquidity coverage, cash-flow volatility, drawdown patterns) from these reconstructions.

## Theoretical Background
The paper builds on quantitative finance's toolkit for risk assessment, adapting drawdown-and-recovery statistics (tools originally developed to measure portfolio losses and rebounds) to blockchain wallets. It relies on the observation that in decentralized finance (DeFi), traditional underwriting signals—income statements, credit history, asset verification—are unavailable; instead, all information is encoded in immutable, public transaction records. The framework is grounded in the principle that total wealth and liquid, immediately-accessible balance are distinct risk dimensions; conflating them leads to mispriced default probability.

## Core Contributions
1. **Dual-scope reconstruction methodology**: Explicitly separating analysis of a wallet's stablecoin liquidity versus total token holdings to detect liquidity mismatches (wealthy-looking wallets with depleted reserves).
2. **Adapted risk metrics for blockchain**: Operationalizing drawdown-and-recovery statistics (a quantitative finance concept) on intra-wallet token transfers; introducing recurring-counterparty detection to infer salary-like payment patterns purely from transfer timing.
3. **Formal specification and production validation**: Rigorous documentation of the underwriting pipeline with cross-language migration verified to numerical tolerance 1e-9 and validation against 78 field assertions—evidence of engineering maturity.
4. **Sensitivity analysis of the tier function**: Demonstrating that loan size materially affects tier assignment for real wallets (4 of 6 reference wallets change tier across USD 10–25k range), and that multiple criteria bind on different wallets, meaning no single heuristic dominates.

## Connections to Other Work
zLend occupies a unique position at the intersection of [[risk-management]] and [[DeFi]]. It extends classical credit underwriting (which relies on verified income and liability records) to an environment where only transaction data exists—a challenge also tackled in recent work on on-chain identity and reputation. The use of drawdown statistics connects to portfolio risk measurement (e.g., Sortino ratio, conditional value-at-risk frameworks). The system implicitly assumes that transfer patterns encode behavioral and financial intent—a premise shared by broader research into blockchain forensics and wallet classification, though zLend focuses on positive prediction of repayment capacity rather than illicit activity detection.

## Practical Applications
1. **DeFi lending protocols**: Direct integration (confirmed via third-party API integrations) to automate collateral requirements or interest rates based on inferred credit quality.
2. **Cross-chain credit scoring**: Aggregating multi-wallet transaction histories to assign credit tiers to users active across multiple blockchains.
3. **Undercollateralized loan markets**: Enabling lower collateral ratios for borrowers with demonstrably stable, recurring income-like cash flows.
4. **Risk-adjusted staking platforms**: Assessing validator or liquidity provider quality before allocating capital.
5. **On-chain insurance underwriting**: Pricing premiums for smart contract or counterparty risk based on wallet liquidity patterns.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **On-Chain Credit Bureau**: A subscription service providing real-time credit scores for DeFi borrowers, with alerts to lenders when liquidity coverage drops below thresholds, monetized via data licensing and API tiers.
2. **Decentralized Lending Aggregator**: A platform that automatically routes loans to the cheapest source (peer-to-peer, AMM, bank) based on zLend credit tiers, capturing spread and charging a fee to borrowers.
3. **Undercollateralized Loan Marketplace**: A peer-to-peer lending protocol using zLend tiers to set collateral requirements dynamically, targeting retail DeFi users who cannot post 200% collateral.
4. **Blockchain-Native Bank**: A neobank that accepts stablecoin deposits and offers credit lines to borrowers with strong zLend scores, hedging risk via on-chain derivatives.
5. **Enterprise Cash-Flow Analytics for Web3**: B2B SaaS tool for crypto-native businesses to audit internal cash-flow health and share it with lenders and investors in a zLend-compatible format.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Stablecoin-centric bias**: The framework's reliance on a "fixed stablecoin basket" assumes fungible stablecoins are interchangeable, missing collateral quality differences (e.g., USDC vs. algorithmic stablecoins, regulatory risk).
2. **No off-chain income**: Cannot detect actual salary deposits or real-world income, only on-chain patterns; vulnerable to sophisticated obfuscation (e.g., funds flowing through mixers or multiple wallets).
3. **Short-duration signals**: Repayment-capacity metrics are derived from daily balance history, potentially missing seasonal or multi-month cycles in real businesses.
4. **Liquidity mismatch detection is simplistic**: Flagging wallets with large aggregate holdings but low stablecoin reserves assumes all non-stablecoin assets are illiquid; decentralized tokens held at major exchanges (with real redemption capability) may be misclassified.
5. **Transfer timing inference**: Recurring-counterparty detection relies on regularity; one-time large transfers or irregular freelance income would be missed.
6. **No behavioral/intent signal**: The pipeline is purely mechanical; it cannot distinguish between a user temporarily moving funds to a cold wallet versus actual inability to pay.

## Open Questions
1. **How does zLend's tier assignment correlate with actual defaults in deployed lending?** The paper validates internal consistency but does not report lift over random or simpler baselines in a live portfolio.
2. **What is the optimal choice of the fixed stablecoin basket?** How sensitive is the framework to including/excluding emerging or regional stablecoins?
3. **Can drawdown-and-recovery statistics be integrated with on-chain collateral liquidation prices** to capture tail risk (e.g., when a borrower's collateral crashes simultaneously with a repayment deadline)?
4. **Does the framework generalize to non-English-speaking or emerging-market blockchains** where transaction volume and token diversity differ sharply?
5. **How does zLend perform for borrowers using smart contracts or intent-settlement networks** (which may hide true cash-flow state from raw transfers)?
6. **Can recurring-counterparty detection be strengthened** by incorporating graph structure (cluster-based salary networks) rather than timing alone?

## Key Takeaway
On-chain credit scoring is possible: reconsting cash-flow from blockchain transactions reveals repayment capacity by separating liquid reserves from total wealth.
