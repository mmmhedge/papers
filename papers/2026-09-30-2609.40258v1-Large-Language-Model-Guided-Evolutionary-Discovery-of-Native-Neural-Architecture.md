# Large Language Model-Guided Evolutionary Discovery of Native Neural Architectures for Spiking Sequence Modeling

**Authors:** Ruoyu Zhao, Jiaqi Wu, Chenyu Zhu, Zhichao Lu
**Published:** 2026-09-30 | **arXiv:** [2609.40258v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40258v1)

## Summary
This paper proposes OpenArchEvo, a system that uses large language models to automatically discover new neural network architectures optimized for spiking neural networks (SNNs)—a type of brain-inspired network that uses sparse, event-driven computation for energy efficiency. The key innovation is searching over executable code in an open program space rather than predefined configuration spaces, enabling discovery of fundamentally new architectural mechanisms. The discovered architectures achieve 50× better energy efficiency than standard transformer baselines.

## Theoretical Background
Spiking neural networks (SNNs) differ from standard artificial neural networks by processing information through discrete spike events rather than continuous activations, mimicking biological neurons. This sparse, event-driven computation offers dramatic energy savings on neuromorphic hardware. However, SNNs have temporal dynamics (how neuron state evolves over time) that differ fundamentally from feedforward models, making it unclear how to design sequence models (networks that process sequential data) optimally for them. Existing SNN sequence models typically adapt architectures from transformers or recurrent networks designed for standard continuous-valued networks, which may not exploit spike-based communication effectively.

Neural architecture search (NAS) — the problem of automatically designing network structures — traditionally uses evolutionary algorithms or reinforcement learning within predefined configuration spaces (e.g., "choose layer type A, B, or C"). This constrains discovery to variations within hand-designed templates. The paper leverages large language models (LLMs) — neural networks trained on vast text corpora that can generate and reason about code — to generate and evolve architecture designs as executable Python-like code, enabling discovery beyond predefined templates.

## Core Contributions
1. **Open program space for NAS**: Instead of evolving within fixed configuration spaces, the system generates and evolves executable architecture code, enabling discovery of genuinely novel mechanisms (e.g., spike-activity-dependent gating, residual pathways for spikes).

2. **Three-view representation for efficient search**: The system represents candidates via code, design rationale (LLM-generated explanations), and a behavioral fingerprint (abstract summary of computational behavior). This enables novelty estimation and performance prediction without costly training every candidate.

3. **Multi-objective surrogate-guided search**: Uses predicted performance and estimated novelty as joint objectives, leveraging surrogate models (learned approximations) to prioritize which architectures warrant expensive GPU training, reducing search cost to ~132 V100 GPU-days.

4. **Discovered native SNN architectures**: Identifies multiple new designs including NeuroGate (outperforms ANN baseline DeltaNet on WikiText-103) and LoopMem (50.6× energy reduction vs. dense Transformer baseline).

## Connections to Other Work
This work bridges several areas:
- **Neural architecture search**: Related to prior NAS methods like [[DARTS]], [[ENAS]], and evolutionary NAS, but extends beyond fixed search spaces.
- **Spiking neural networks**: Builds on SNN sequence modeling work (e.g., event-based RNNs, SNN transformers) by automating architecture discovery rather than manual design.
- **LLM-guided program synthesis**: Connects to recent work using LLMs for code generation and algorithm discovery (e.g., [[AlphaCode]]), but applies it to architecture design with performance constraints.
- **Multi-objective optimization**: Employs Pareto front exploration of performance vs. novelty, a well-established optimization paradigm.
- **Surrogate-based optimization**: Uses learned models to reduce evaluation cost, a technique from Bayesian optimization and sample-efficient design.

## Practical Applications
1. **Energy-efficient edge AI**: Deploy SNN models discovered by this method on neuromorphic chips or low-power devices (smartphones, IoT sensors, autonomous robots) where energy is the primary constraint.

2. **Real-time sequence processing**: SNNs excel at temporal data; discovered architectures could improve efficiency for speech recognition, EEG signal processing, or streaming sensor analytics on embedded hardware.

3. **Neuromorphic hardware optimization**: Use discovered architectures to drive neuromorphic accelerator design and firmware, since the method co-evolves structures with spike-based constraints.

4. **Autonomous system control**: Deploy energy-optimized sequence models on autonomous vehicles or drones where battery life and real-time response matter.

5. **Data center sustainability**: SNNs could reduce power consumption in large-scale inference servers, lowering operational cost and carbon footprint for cloud providers.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **NeuromorphicML-as-a-Service**: A cloud platform where users upload sequential data or a modeling task; the system automatically discovers and benchmarks energy-optimized SNN architectures, returning Pareto-optimal models for their hardware (neuromorphic chip, custom ASIC, or CPU).

2. **Edge AI Model Compiler**: A tool that takes standard PyTorch/TensorFlow models, auto-discovers energy-efficient SNN equivalents, and compiles them for specific neuromorphic hardware (Intel Loihi, Brainscales, etc.), quantifying energy savings.

3. **Federated Learning on Edge**: Package discovered architectures as lightweight models for federated learning on IoT/edge devices; the extreme energy efficiency enables on-device training without constant cloud uploads.

4. **Autonomous Vehicle Power Management**: Integrate auto-discovered SNNs for perception and control tasks in EVs, reducing computational power draw during autonomous driving and extending range.

5. **Bio-signal Wearable Analytics**: Develop wearable devices that run discovered SNN sequence models for real-time ECG, EMG, or EEG analysis on-device without cloud connectivity, powered by battery for weeks.

## Technical Limitations
1. **SNN training complexity**: SNNs are notoriously harder to train than ANNs (gradient flow through discrete spikes is non-smooth); the paper doesn't deeply address whether discovered architectures remain trainable at scale or only perform well under specific training recipes.

2. **Validation scope**: Evaluation focuses primarily on WikiText-103 (language modeling); generalization to other sequence tasks (speech, time-series, control) is unexplored.

3. **Hardware dependency**: Energy efficiency gains are claimed against a "dense Transformer baseline" but not validated on actual neuromorphic hardware; performance on real neuromorphic chips may differ due to mapping overhead, communication costs, and hardware-specific constraints.

4. **Surrogate model accuracy**: The method relies on learned performance predictors and novelty estimators; if these surrogates are poorly calibrated, the search may waste budget on unpromising candidates or miss high-value ones.

5. **Code search space unboundedness**: While open program space enables discovery, it also risks generating pathological or inefficient code; the spiking-projection constraints may not fully prevent degenerate solutions.

6. **Reproducibility of LLM-guided search**: LLM outputs are stochastic and depend on prompts and model version; exact replication of discovered architectures may be difficult without detailed hyperparameter logging.

## Open Questions
1. **Can discovered SNN architectures scale to industrial tasks?** Do NeuroGate and LoopMem remain competitive on larger datasets, longer sequences, or in multi-task scenarios?

2. **How does hardware map discovered code to physical implementations?** What is the gap between theoretical energy savings and real neuromorphic hardware performance, including communication and memory overhead?

3. **Does novelty estimation via behavioral fingerprints correlate with actual downstream performance?** Is the three-view representation sufficient, or should other representations be explored?

4. **Can the method discover architectures for other modalities?** The paper focuses on sequence modeling; what about vision, graphs, or multi-modal data?

5. **How sensitive is the search to LLM choice and prompting?** Would a different LLM or prompting strategy discover fundamentally different architectures?

6. **What is the relationship between architectural novelty and trainability?** Do more novel architectures require different optimizers, learning rates, or initialization schemes?

## Key Takeaway
LLMs can guide automated discovery of energy-efficient spiking neural networks by evolving architecture code, finding designs 50× more efficient than standard transformers.
