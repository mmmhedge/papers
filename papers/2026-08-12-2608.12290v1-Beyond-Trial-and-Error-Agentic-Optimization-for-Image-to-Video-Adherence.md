# Beyond Trial-and-Error: Agentic Optimization for Image-to-Video Adherence

**Authors:** Aman Tyagi, Hemanth Boinpally, Jonathan Chen, Douglas Gebert, Steven Hickson
**Published:** 2026-08-12 | **arXiv:** [2608.12290v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12290v1)

## Summary
This paper tackles a major pain point in AI video generation: modern image-to-video models produce wildly different outputs from small prompt changes, forcing users into tedious trial-and-error loops. The authors propose an "agentic self-improvement" framework that automatically optimizes prompts and generation parameters using a multimodal language model and Bayesian search, achieving 69% human preference over baseline outputs and making video synthesis more reliable for professional use.

## Theoretical Background
Image-to-video (I2V) models are neural networks that generate video sequences from static images and text descriptions. Modern I2V systems are black-box (their internals are proprietary) and highly stochastic — the same input produces different outputs due to randomness in the generation process. The paper builds on two key prior concepts: (1) **Davidsonian Scene Graphs (DSG)** — structured representations of scenes that capture objects, attributes, and relationships, used here to verify semantic consistency; (2) **Bayesian optimization** — a principled method for searching high-dimensional spaces efficiently by modeling uncertainty. The framework also leverages multimodal large language models (mLLMs) — models like GPT-4V that process both text and images — to iteratively refine prompts based on feedback.

## Core Contributions
1. **Two-stage optimization pipeline**: Separates the problem into prompt refinement (via mLLM feedback) and parameter tuning (via Bayesian optimization), rather than treating them as a single black-box search.
2. **Automated evaluation metrics**: Introduces Video-Text Adherence (VTA) score derived from DSG queries and Common Mistake Questions (CMQ) that detect artifacts without human intervention.
3. **Closed-loop agentic approach**: Frames I2V generation as a goal-directed optimization problem where an agent iteratively improves outputs, not random sampling.
4. **Empirical validation**: Demonstrates strong human preference wins (up to 69%) over unguided baselines on real video generation tasks.

## Connections to Other Work
This work relates to several threads: [[RLHF]] and preference optimization (using human feedback to improve model outputs), [[Prompt Engineering]] and in-context learning (refining text inputs to guide models), and general [[Black-box Optimization]] techniques. It also connects to recent work on evaluating generated videos without ground truth, though the novelty lies in coupling evaluation with iterative search. The use of scene graphs for semantic verification echoes prior work in vision-language understanding, but applies it in a novel way to close the feedback loop.

## Practical Applications
1. **Professional video production**: Studios using AI for concept animation, storyboarding, or asset generation can now reliably reproduce and refine outputs rather than re-rolling dozens of times.
2. **Marketing and advertising**: Automated generation of product demo videos or social media content with consistent aesthetic and messaging.
3. **Film and game development**: Rapid iteration on visual effects, character animations, or environment design constrained by narrative requirements.
4. **Content moderation and QA**: Systematically detect and filter out videos with unwanted artifacts or semantic drift before human review.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **"Prompt Studio"** — A paid SaaS tool wrapping popular I2V APIs (Runway, Pika) with this optimization framework, charging per video or monthly subscription. Target: indie creators, small production houses.
2. **"Video Compliance Engine"** — Audit tool for enterprises generating marketing videos; automatically flags outputs that violate brand guidelines or contain errors, with suggested refinements.
3. **"Storyboard Accelerator"** — Integrate with scriptwriting software to auto-generate rough storyboards from scripts, iteratively polishing them against semantic scene descriptions.
4. **"Visual QA as a Service"** — White-label DSG + CMQ evaluator for video generation platforms (Runway, Adobe) to embed reliability scoring directly into their UX.
5. **"Agentic Video Editor"** — Desktop or web app where users sketch what they want (text + rough images), and the system automatically generates, evaluates, and refines videos in an interactive loop.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Dependency on black-box model quality**: If the underlying I2V model is poor, optimization can only improve so much; garbage in → slightly less garbage out.
2. **mLLM evaluation brittleness**: DSG and CMQ assessments rely on vision-language model accuracy, which can fail on edge cases or unusual visual styles.
3. **Computational cost**: Multi-stage optimization (prompt iterations + Bayesian search over seeds/CFG) requires many forward passes; latency may be prohibitive for real-time workflows.
4. **Metric design specificity**: VTA score and CMQ are task-specific; generalizing to new domains (e.g., abstract art, non-photorealistic styles) requires retuning.
5. **Limited to parameter space**: Optimizes prompts and generation hyperparameters but cannot modify the base model weights; fundamental model limitations (e.g., poor temporal coherence) remain unfixed.

## Open Questions
1. How does performance scale to longer videos or more complex scenes? All experiments appear to be short clips.
2. Can the framework handle real-time feedback from users mid-optimization, or adapt to changing goals?
3. How sensitive is the method to the choice of mLLM? Would cheaper or open-source models degrade performance significantly?
4. Can DSG + CMQ evaluations be learned end-to-end (as a neural reward model) rather than using hand-crafted queries?
5. Does this approach generalize to other stochastic generative models (image diffusion, text generation) or is it I2V-specific?

## Key Takeaway
Agentic optimization with semantic feedback transforms video generation from trial-and-error into reliable, controllable production.
