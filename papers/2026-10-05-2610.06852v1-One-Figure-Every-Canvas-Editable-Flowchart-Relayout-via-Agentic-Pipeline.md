# One Figure, Every Canvas: Editable Flowchart Relayout via Agentic Pipeline

**Authors:** Shih-Chen Tseng, Chih-Hsuan Chen, Ryan Yang, Hsi-An Chen, Chun-Wei Tuan Mu et al.
**Published:** 2026-10-05 | **arXiv:** [2610.06852v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06852v1)

## Summary
This paper solves the practical problem of automatically adapting flowchart diagrams to different aspect ratios (e.g., paper columns, slides, social media formats) while preserving all connections and content. The authors propose an agentic pipeline that parses the flowchart, applies styling, and relayouts elements to fit new dimensions, using critic agents to catch and prevent common failures like broken edges or hallucinated content.

## Theoretical Background
The work combines three established research areas: layout optimization (arranging visual elements to satisfy geometric constraints), flowchart understanding (recognizing graph structure in raster images), and agentic systems (decomposing complex tasks into specialized agents with feedback loops). Prior approaches either use image-to-image models (which struggle with extreme aspect ratios), text-to-image generation (which hallucinates missing elements), or naive parse-then-render pipelines (which misroute edges). The paper's contribution is in the factorization: separating parsing, styling, and layout into distinct stages, each paired with a critic that combines deterministic constraint checks with visual language model (VLM) feedback.

## Core Contributions
1. **Problem formulation**: Framing aspect-ratio-adaptive flowchart relayout as a distinct, practically important task with clear success criteria (structural fidelity, no hallucinations, editability).
2. **Agentic pipeline architecture**: Three-stage decomposition (Parse → Style → Layout) where each stage pairs a main agent with a critic, rather than relying on end-to-end models or rigid rule-based systems.
3. **Hybrid constraint enforcement**: Combining deterministic checks (graph connectivity verification) with VLM visual feedback, preventing silent failures where broken connections go undetected.
4. **Editable output format**: Producing draw.io-compatible mxGraph XML rather than static raster images, enabling downstream editing.
5. **Benchmark and evaluation**: A curated dataset of 100 flowcharts at five aspect ratios, evaluated both by Gemini 3.1 Pro and human validators, achieving 68.6% Content Fidelity versus 11–41% for baselines.

## Connections to Other Work
This paper sits at the intersection of [[computer-vision]] (diagram understanding and layout), [[agents]] and multi-agent reasoning (factored pipeline with critic feedback), and [[generative-models]] (using VLMs to guide layout decisions). It implicitly relates to work on structured generation and grounded reasoning—the idea that agents should verify their outputs against explicit constraints rather than relying solely on learned priors. The parse-then-render approach echoes design practices in symbolic AI but pairs them with modern VLM critics for robustness.

## Practical Applications
- **Academic publishing**: Automatically adapt paper figures to different submission formats (single column, two column, conference posters, preprint servers).
- **Presentation software**: Enable one-click relayout of complex diagrams across slide sizes and orientations.
- **Social media scheduling**: Repurpose technical diagrams for different platform aspect ratios (LinkedIn, Twitter, Instagram, TikTok) without manual redrawing.
- **Scientific documentation**: Maintain consistency of system diagrams across whitepapers, blog posts, and video thumbnails.
- **Collaborative design tools**: Enable teams to adapt shared flowcharts to project-specific canvas constraints in real time.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **Diagram Auto-Reflow SaaS**: A web tool where researchers upload flowcharts and instantly generate optimized versions for 10+ standard formats (paper, slides, posters, social, mobile). Integrates with Overleaf and Google Slides.
2. **Technical Content Repurposing Platform**: Ingest a single machine learning paper figure; automatically generate social-media-ready versions, podcast-episode graphics, and interactive explainers for different platforms.
3. **Enterprise Diagram Standardization Tool**: Help large organizations enforce consistent architecture diagrams across documentation, intranet sites, and presentations by auto-adapting to brand-specific canvas sizes.
4. **AI-Powered Design System for Academics**: A Figma-like plugin that takes hand-drawn or rough digital flowcharts and auto-beautifies + relayouts them for publication quality across multiple formats.
5. **Embedded Component for Generative AI Platforms**: Offer this as an API/library for LLM output visualization—automatically reflow generated diagrams to match user's screen size or document template.

## Technical Limitations
- **Raster input only**: The method requires a raster (pixel-based) flowchart as input; it cannot directly handle vector source files, limiting applicability to digitally-born diagrams without OCR preprocessing.
- **Benchmark scale**: Evaluation on only 100 flowcharts is modest; generalization to highly idiosyncratic or novel diagram styles is unvalidated.
- **VLM dependency**: Reliance on commercial models (Gemini 3.1 Pro) for critic feedback introduces cost, latency, and potential drift if model behavior changes.
- **Extreme aspect ratios**: While the method handles five standard ratios, behavior at truly pathological ratios (e.g., 10:1) is unknown.
- **Complex annotations**: The paper focuses on structured flowcharts; handling of overlaid text, equations, or annotations is not addressed.
- **Editability assumptions**: Output is editable mxGraph XML, but downstream usability and how well users can manually refine auto-generated layouts is not evaluated.

## Open Questions
1. How does the method scale to very large flowcharts (100+ nodes) or hierarchical diagrams with nested subgraphs?
2. Can the critic stages be distilled into smaller, open-source models to reduce inference cost and avoid vendor lock-in?
3. What is the sensitivity to the parse stage? If parsing fails silently, can critic stages catch and recover?
4. How do users actually interact with the auto-relayouted diagrams—do they need heavy manual editing, or is the output "good enough" for most use cases?
5. Can this pipeline be extended to other diagram types (entity-relationship diagrams, Gantt charts, network graphs) without major redesign?
6. How does performance degrade when input flowcharts are low-resolution, noisy, or hand-sketched?

## Key Takeaway
An agentic pipeline automatically adapts flowchart diagrams to any aspect ratio while preserving structure and editability, with critic feedback preventing silent failures.
