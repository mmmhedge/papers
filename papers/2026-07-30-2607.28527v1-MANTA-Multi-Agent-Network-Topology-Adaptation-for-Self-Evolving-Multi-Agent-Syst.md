# MANTA: Multi-Agent Network Topology Adaptation for Self-Evolving Multi-Agent Systems

**Authors:** Mao-xun Huang, Jerry Wang, Yi-Cheng Lai, Zhengxin Zhang, Claire Cardie et al.
**Published:** 2026-07-30 | **arXiv:** [2607.28527v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28527v1)

## Summary
This paper introduces MANTA, a framework that lets multi-agent LLM systems automatically improve their communication structure during execution rather than using a fixed topology. Instead of deciding upfront how agents should talk to each other, MANTA watches how they collaborate and adapts the organization in real time—changing which agents communicate, when they act, and how they validate results. The work shows that this adaptive approach outperforms static designs on complex reasoning and planning tasks.

## Theoretical Background
Multi-agent LLM systems decompose hard problems by assigning specialized roles to different agents and having them exchange information. Most existing systems use a communication topology—a fixed pattern of who talks to whom—determined at design time or optimized offline. The paper builds on ideas that (1) LLMs can coordinate through structured task decomposition, (2) agent specialization improves performance on complex problems, and (3) inference-time adaptation has shown benefits in other contexts (like [[RLHF]] for single-agent improvement). MANTA extends this inference-time adaptation principle to the system's architecture itself: the graph of communication links and execution order.

## Core Contributions
- **Inference-time topology adaptation**: A mechanism to modify communication structures during deployment based on observed collaboration traces, not just offline optimization.
- **Bounded structural updates**: A constrained adaptation scheme that can modify agent roles, communication links, execution order, information visibility, and validation paths while keeping the task interface and agent budget fixed.
- **Task-conditioned initialization**: Starting from prior structural experience relevant to the current task, reducing cold-start adaptation.
- **Empirical validation across diverse tasks**: Demonstrates +5.8 percentage point improvement over baselines on five benchmarks (information seeking, tool use, planning, workflow execution, mathematical reasoning), with strongest gains on PlanCraft.

## Connections to Other Work
MANTA extends ideas from [[In-context Learning]] and [[few-shot adaptation]] by applying them to system topology rather than just agent behavior. It relates to:
- **Neural architecture search (NAS)** and **AutoML**, but in the discrete, structured domain of agent graphs rather than weight optimization.
- **Hierarchical and dynamic multi-agent coordination**: Prior work treats topology as static; MANTA makes it fluid.
- **Self-improving systems**: Similar spirit to [[Constitutional AI]] or retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), where systems improve their own mechanisms at inference time.
- **Graph neural networks** concepts, though MANTA's graphs are discrete and symbolic rather than learned embeddings.

## Practical Applications
- **Enterprise workflow automation**: Dynamically routing documents, approvals, and tasks to the right set of specialized agents (compliance, finance, technical) depending on what the system learns during execution.
- **Scientific discovery pipelines**: Adapting multi-agent research systems (literature review, experiment design, result synthesis) based on emerging findings.
- **Technical support systems**: Routing customer queries and escalations to the right combination of specialist agents, learning which connections work best per problem type.
- **Autonomous planning under uncertainty**: Systems that decompose a goal into subtasks and learn to reorganize task dependencies when early steps reveal unexpected constraints.
- **Code generation and debugging**: Multi-agent systems that generate, test, and refactor code, adapting communication patterns when certain validation paths fail repeatedly.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **DynamicOps**: A no-code/low-code platform for enterprises to define multi-agent workflows that self-optimize their communication topology. Users specify agent capabilities and tasks; the system learns routing and sequencing dynamically. Sell to enterprises automating document processing, claims handling, or customer support.

2. **ResearchMate Pro**: A SaaS tool for research teams that coordinates literature review, experiment design, and analysis with autonomous agents whose collaboration structure adapts as new data emerges. Target biotech, pharma, and ML research labs.

3. **AdaptiveAssembly**: A B2B service that deploys multi-agent systems for complex product assembly, supply chain coordination, or logistics—agents learn to restructure communication and task order based on real-time bottlenecks.

4. **IntelliStack**: An API/SDK for developers to plug MANTA-style topology adaptation into existing LLM frameworks (OpenAI Assistants, Claude API, etc.). Monetize per-call or tiered by system complexity.

5. **AuditFlow**: A specialized solution for financial compliance and audit workflows, where dynamic agent coordination adapts to client risk profiles and emerging red flags, reducing manual hand-offs.

## Technical Limitations
- **Bounded adaptation assumption**: The framework assumes that modifying topology without changing the task interface and agent budget is sufficient. In truly novel domains, this may be too constrictive.
- **Scalability**: Monitoring collaboration traces and computing structural updates may become expensive at very large numbers of agents (10s–100s+); the paper doesn't discuss this scaling regime.
- **Convergence and stability**: No formal guarantees that adapted topologies are stable or convergent; the system could oscillate or degrade if updates are poorly timed.
- **Generalization of "prior structural experience"**: MANTA initializes from task-conditioned priors; if a novel task has no similar prior, cold-start performance may suffer.
- **Validation and interpretability**: It's unclear how to validate that adapted topologies are genuinely better versus just different, or how to explain topology changes to users (transparency/explainability).
- **Agent budget assumption**: Fixing agent budget may not reflect real cost trade-offs (some agents more expensive than others).

## Open Questions
- How does MANTA scale to systems with dozens or hundreds of agents? Where is the breaking point?
- Can you formalize conditions under which adapted topologies are provably better (regret bounds, convergence guarantees)?
- How sensitive is the system to the quality and diversity of prior structural experience? What happens with poor priors?
- Does MANTA work in adversarial or noisy environments where collaboration traces are misleading?
- Can topology adaptation be made interpretable and explainable to end users and auditors?
- How does this compare to online learning approaches (e.g., bandit-based topology search)?
- Do adapted topologies transfer to related tasks, or must they be learned per task?

## Key Takeaway
LLM multi-agent systems can learn to rewire their communication structure in real time, improving collaboration beyond static designs.
