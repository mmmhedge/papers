# DSTNet: Dynamic Spectral Trajectory Network for Causal Multi-Horizon Financial Forecasting

**Authors:** Aashish Bohra, Lokendra Vishwakarm
**Published:** 2026-10-07 | **arXiv:** [2610.09654v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09654v1)

## Summary
This paper proposes DSTNet, a neural network that forecasts financial prices by treating wavelet spectral information as a dynamic trajectory over time rather than a static snapshot. The model combines spectral analysis (decomposing price signals into frequency components) with transformer attention and recurrent networks to predict stock indices and gold prices 1–10 days ahead. Despite marginal improvements over simple persistence models in standard metrics, the paper honestly reports that the method shows no practical edge in actual trading allocation decisions.

## Theoretical Background
Financial forecasting has long relied on two traditions: **technical analysis** (patterns in historical price and volume) and **time-series modeling** (statistical methods like ARIMA). Wavelet transforms decompose signals into frequency and time components simultaneously, offering a richer view than Fourier analysis alone. Recent work has applied [[Transformers]] (neural networks using attention mechanisms) to time-series forecasting by learning which parts of history matter most. **Causal filtering** — ensuring a model only uses information available *before* the forecast date — is essential in finance to avoid look-ahead bias. Most prior wavelet forecasters either use wavelets only for denoising or collapse the evolving spectral information into a single representation; DSTNet instead preserves how spectral magnitudes change over a trailing window.

## Core Contributions
1. **Dynamic Spectral Trajectory**: Retains the full temporal evolution of wavelet filter-bank magnitudes (from technical indicators) over a 20-day lookback using causal, one-sided Morlet wavelets with explicit boundary handling.
2. **Scale-Temporal Spectral Transformer**: A factorized attention mechanism that independently learns dependencies along time and frequency (filter-bank) axes, then fuses the result with a CNN-BiLSTM branch via learned gates.
3. **Multi-horizon single-pass forecasting**: Generates predictions for 1, 3, 5, and 10-day horizons simultaneously using horizon-specific output gates.
4. **Honest empirical evaluation**: Tests on seven equity indices plus gold under rigorous expanding-window backtesting and a held-out one-year period, directly comparing against persistence (the hardest-to-beat baseline in finance).

## Connections to Other Work
This work sits at the intersection of [[Deep Learning]] time-series methods and quantitative finance. It builds on the renewed interest in [[Transformers]] for forecasting (e.g., Informer, Autoformer) but adds domain-specific structure: wavelet analysis and technical indicators grounded in finance practice. The emphasis on **causal filtering** echoes work in [[RLHF]] and causality-aware learning. The paper's honest acknowledgment that statistical improvements don't translate to trading profits aligns with the efficient-market hypothesis debate and critiques of overfitting in financial ML. It relates to broader questions in algorithmic trading about whether price patterns are truly predictable or merely artifacts of model capacity.

## Practical Applications
1. **Intraday hedging decisions**: Fund managers could use 1–3 day forecasts to adjust portfolio exposure or hedge ratios, even if not profitable enough for pure directional bets.
2. **Risk limit monitoring**: Short-term forecasts feed into volatility estimation for position sizing and margin calculations.
3. **Statistical arbitrage**: Pairs-trading or index component rotation strategies that require modest directional edges in volatile markets.
4. **Central bank or treasury operations**: Forecasting FX and commodity price moves (gold) for policy or reserve management timing.
5. **Data product for hedge funds**: Package the model as a quant signal alongside other alpha sources (ensemble effect).

## Technical Limitations
1. **Modest edge over persistence**: Improvements of 0.7–0.9% at 1-day and 3.4–4.5% at 10-day MAE/MAPE sound large in percentage terms but are tiny in absolute returns after transaction costs (typically 5–10 basis points per round-trip).
2. **No statistical significance at 1-day horizon**: Paired testing is inconclusive against strong baselines and persistence, suggesting the gains may be noise or regime-dependent.
3. **No trading profitability demonstrated**: The allocation diagnostic explicitly fails to show a practical edge. The paper doesn't report Sharpe ratio, cumulative returns, or drawdown—the metrics that matter in practice.
4. **Seven-year or shorter backtest window**: Untouched hold-out is only one year; market regimes change (e.g., 2020 COVID shock, 2022 rate hikes). Robustness across longer, more diverse periods is unclear.
5. **Technical indicator dependence**: Using seven lagged indicators assumes their relevance is stable. In flash crashes or illiquid periods, technical patterns break down.
6. **Causal assumptions**: While one-sided wavelets are causal, the model still trains on 20-day windows; any omitted forward-looking information (e.g., earnings announcements, Fed decisions) will limit predictability.

## Open Questions
1. **Why does better statistical fit not translate to profits?** Is it market efficiency, transaction costs, or model overfitting to in-sample metrics (MAE/MAPE) that don't align with trading P&L?
2. **How does DSTNet perform across different market regimes** (trending, mean-reverting, high-volatility)? Does it fail in any regime?
3. **Can the spectral-trajectory idea generalize to ultra-high-frequency data** (tick-by-tick) or only daily/hourly?
4. **What if you add exogenous features** (volume, volatility term structure, macro indicators)? Does the pure price+technical-indicator approach leave money on the table?
5. **Ablation study**: Which component matters most—the dynamic trajectory, the factorized attention, the horizon-specific gates, or the fusion with CNN-BiLSTM? A systematic ablation would clarify.
6. **Cross-asset transfer**: Can a model trained on equity indices forecast commodities and FX with fine-tuning, or is each market idiosyncratic?

## Key Takeaway
Wavelet spectral trajectories with transformers improve short-term price forecasts modestly but don't generate tradeable profits after costs.
