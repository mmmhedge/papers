# Learning Bidirectional Causal Interactions with Heteroscedastic Neural Networks

**Authors:** Masahiro Tanaka
**Published:** 2026-07-24 | **arXiv:** [2607.22313v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22313v1)

## Summary
This paper proposes SEM-DNN, a neural network method for learning bidirectional causal relationships from observational data when the outcomes directly influence each other (simultaneity problem). The key insight is using heteroscedastic structure — variable variance across the feature space — to uniquely identify true causal effects without needing external instruments, something traditional econometric approaches struggle with.

## Theoretical Background
The paper builds on simultaneous-equation modeling (SEM), a classical econometric framework for handling endogeneity — when an outcome variable depends on another outcome that itself depends back on the first. Traditional SEM requires instrumental variables (exogenous predictors that affect one variable but not the other) for identification. This paper instead exploits heteroscedasticity (non-constant variance) as an identification source, an idea grounded in recent econometric theory but previously limited to parametric models. The authors extend this using neural networks, which can flexibly approximate nonlinear structural relationships and feature-dependent variances. The identification strategy relies on conditional covariance diagonalization: structural shocks with nonproportional conditional variances across features will only be uncorrelated when the true interaction coefficients are recovered.

## Core Contributions
1. **Heteroscedasticity-based identification without instruments**: Shows that conditional variance structure alone can identify bidirectional causal effects, removing the need for external instruments.
2. **Neural implementation (SEM-DNN)**: Develops a practical neural network estimator using diagonal Gaussian quasi-likelihood that jointly learns nonlinear structural means and feature-dependent variances while respecting the simultaneous-system Jacobian.
3. **Theoretical guarantees**: Establishes unique identification and proves positive-definite local curvature of the population criterion. Critically, shows that neural parameterizations can inherit this curvature despite network non-uniqueness.
4. **Empirical validation**: Demonstrates on simulated and real data (cereal scanner data) that SEM-DNN recovers structural effects more reliably than parametric, kernel, and separate-equation baselines, with diagnostic tools for assessing identification strength and residual structure.

## Connections to Other Work
This extends [[Causal inference]] from independent data to endogenous systems. It relates to broader efforts in [[causal machine learning]] that move beyond parametric assumptions. The heteroscedasticity identification strategy connects to recent econometric work on nonlinear IVs and semiparametric SEM. The neural approximation approach parallels [[debiased machine learning]] methods that use flexible models while preserving asymptotic validity. It also touches on [[disentanglement]] in representation learning — the goal of separating structural causal relationships from confounded correlations — though from a classical econometric perspective rather than a generative-model lens.

## Practical Applications
1. **Price-demand feedback in retail/e-commerce**: Understanding mutual causality between pricing and sales in scanner data, controlling for confounders like seasonality.
2. **Supply chain dynamics**: Estimating bidirectional effects between supplier costs and buyer orders in industrial datasets.
3. **Labor market equilibrium**: Learning simultaneous wage-employment dynamics in regional labor markets from observational employment records.
4. **Financial markets**: Estimating mutual causality between asset prices and trading volumes or bid-ask spreads.
5. **Health interventions**: Studying bidirectional relationships (e.g., depression ↔ substance use) from longitudinal observational health records.

## Product & Startup Ideas
1. **Causal analytics SaaS for e-commerce**: "CausalRetail" — a platform that ingests scanner or transaction data and automatically estimates price-sales feedback loops and intervention effects, helping merchants optimize pricing without A/B testing.
2. **Supply chain optimization tool**: Use SEM-DNN to learn supplier-buyer feedback mechanisms in procurement networks, enabling dynamic contract terms and risk assessment.
3. **Labor market intelligence API**: Analyze regional wage-employment dynamics and forecast labor market bifurcations for workforce planning tools and policy makers.
4. **Financial microstructure analytics**: Provide market makers and traders with estimates of liquidity-price feedback and volatility transmission without needing external instrument selection.
5. **Clinical decision support for comorbidities**: Build diagnostic tools that learn bidirectional disease relationships (depression ↔ chronic pain, diabetes ↔ hypertension) from EHR data to improve treatment prioritization.

## Technical Limitations
1. **Computational cost**: The method is explicitly more expensive than alternatives, limiting scalability to very high-dimensional data or real-time settings.
2. **Variance structure assumption**: Relies on nonproportional conditional heteroscedasticity; if shocks have homoscedastic variance, identification fails and the method collapses to reduced-form regression.
3. **Neural parameterization ambiguity**: Although theory guarantees local curvature, multiple network parameterizations may satisfy the criterion, and the paper does not fully characterize global optimization landscape or provide convergence rates.
4. **Autonomous mechanism assumption**: Causal interpretation requires that structural equations remain invariant under interventions — violated if mechanisms shift or are adaptive (e.g., in strategic environments).
5. **Small-sample validity unclear**: Asymptotic theory does not directly translate to finite-sample behavior with deep networks; Monte Carlo uses moderate sample sizes and it's unclear how well SEM-DNN performs with n < p scenarios.
6. **Diagnostics are post-hoc**: Methods for assessing identification strength and diagonalization quality are exploratory; no formal statistical tests are provided.

## Open Questions
1. **Global optimization**: Can you characterize the non-convex landscape or provide practical guarantees that local optima recover structural parameters? Does the method have failure modes in practice?
2. **Neural-profile conditions**: What exactly are "neural-profile compatibility conditions" and how restrictive are they? Can they be checked a priori?
3. **Causal sufficiency**: How does the method perform when the structural system is incomplete (unobserved confounders between the two endogenous variables)? Can it detect or bound this bias?
4. **Heteroscedasticity-free settings**: Can the identification strategy be extended to homoscedastic shocks via higher moments (skewness, kurtosis) or other structure?
5. **Adaptive mechanisms**: How should the method be modified for strategic or learning-based systems where structural parameters drift with history?
6. **Sample complexity**: What is the sample size required for reliable inference as a function of network depth, dimension, and nonlinearity strength?

## Key Takeaway
Neural networks can learn bidirectional causal effects from data without external instruments by exploiting varying noise levels across features.
